About the job

Overview

POSITION SUMMARY

The F&B Lead is the working leader of our outlets: a player-coach who performs the duties of F&B Attendants and Cooks and leads the team doing them. You are on the floor and on the line, keeping people on task, keeping service moving, and keeping every guest at the center of what the team does. When a station is behind, a table is waiting, or a guest is unhappy, the Lead is the person who sees it first and fixes it.

This role is built for someone who earns respect by doing the work alongside the team, holds people to a high standard with warmth and clarity, and cares deeply about the guest experience. It is the first step on the leadership path in Brighton F&B, and Leads are the people we look to first when we grow our managers and chefs.

OUR HOSPITALITY STANDARD

At Brighton, hospitality is not a department; it is how we operate. Every guest, from a first-time skier to a lifelong regular, should leave feeling genuinely cared for. We hold ourselves to Brighton's own standard of service, and we pursue excellence in the details: clean spaces, accurate orders, warm greetings, and teammates who have each other's backs.


Responsibilities

Leading the Shift & Keeping the Team on Task

• Run pre-shift huddles: set the plan for the day, assign positions and stations, review specials, 86'd items, expected volume, weather, and events, and make sure every team member knows their job and the standard.

• Stay visible and move constantly between the front of house and the kitchen; watch pace, quality, cleanliness, and morale, and redirect immediately when someone is off task or a standard slips.

• Deploy the team where it is needed most as volume changes: move people between stations, call for help early, and recommend adding or releasing staff to the manager.

• Coordinate breaks and coverage so that service never suffers and every team member, including those in cold outdoor positions, gets proper rest and hydration.

• Recognize great work in the moment and give clear, respectful, real-time feedback when expectations are not met; escalate ongoing performance, attendance, or conduct concerns to the manager promptly.

• Set the example in attitude, urgency, uniform, cleanliness, and phone discipline, every shift.

Guest Service Leadership

• Keep guest service the first priority of every decision: be present on the floor, greet guests, and model the warmth and attentiveness we expect from the whole team.

• Monitor wait times, lines, ticket times, and dining areas, and step in to run food, bus tables, expedite, or work a station to keep service moving.

• Own guest concerns from start to finish: listen, apologize sincerely, resolve the issue within resort guidelines, follow up, and bring in a manager when needed.

• Handle allergen, dietary, and special-request situations with extra care, working directly with the kitchen to get them right.

• Anticipate rushes such as powder days, holiday weekends, lunch peaks, and weather-driven surges, and have the team positioned before they hit.

Working the Floor & the Line

• Perform F&B Attendant duties as needed, including point of sale and guest payments, cafeteria and quick-service operations, busing and resetting tables, food running, and dishwashing.

• Perform Cook duties as needed, including prep, station setup, and cooking on the line to recipe, portion, and presentation standards.

• Fill any gap in the operation, whether it is a call-out, a surge, or a station that has fallen behind.

• Check food quality, temperature, and presentation before it reaches the guest.

Training, Coaching & Onboarding

• Train new F&B Attendants and Cooks on outlet procedures, systems, standards, and safety, and sign off on training checklists as directed.

• Cross-train team members across stations and between front of house and kitchen to build a flexible, capable team.

• Coach team members at their station, share what you see with the manager, and help identify team members ready for advancement.

Opening, Closing & Operational Readiness

• Open and close the outlet: confirm stations, equipment, and POS systems are ready, complete all checklists, and verify the outlet is clean, secure, and set up for the next service.

• Monitor stock and par levels, assist with ordering and receiving, and communicate shortages or equipment issues before they affect service.

• Run end-of-shift POS reports as directed and report discrepancies to the manager.

• Complete shift notes and pass-downs so that managers and the next shift know exactly where things stand.

Food Safety, Sanitation & Compliance

• Enforce food safety, sanitation, and allergen standards across the whole team; verify that temperature logs, labeling and dating, sanitizer checks, and cleaning schedules are complete and accurate.

• Keep the outlet ready for health inspection at all times.

• Oversee responsible alcohol service: confirm that team members verify ID, refuse service to minors and to guests showing signs of intoxication, and follow all state laws and resort policies.

• Wear the required uniform, slip-resistant footwear, and any assigned personal protective equipment (PPE), appropriate for indoor and outdoor conditions.

• Use and store cleaning chemicals only as trained, following Safety Data Sheet (SDS) guidance; never mix chemicals.

• Report injuries, near-misses, spills, equipment problems, and unsafe conditions to a supervisor immediately.

• Follow the resort's emergency, evacuation, and incident-reporting procedures.

• Report and document any guest or employee incident right away and see that the manager is informed.

Weather, Volume & Mountain Coordination

• Anticipate how snow, road conditions, delivery delays, and closures affect staffing and product, and communicate early with managers and support teams.

• Keep entries, walkways, patios, and service areas safe for guests and team members with mats, ice melt, wet-floor signage, and quick cleanup.

• Watch your team for signs of cold stress, fatigue, or altitude sickness, and adjust assignments to keep people safe.

Communication & Teamwork

• Serve as the link between the team and management: share what is working, what is not, and what the team needs.

• Communicate clearly and respectfully with front-of-house, kitchen, and support teams so that service runs as one team.

• Perform other duties as directed by the manager, Chef de Cuisine, or Sous Chef.


Qualifications

Required

• Age 21+ (required to oversee alcohol service)

• Previous food and beverage experience (typically 2+ years), including time in both front-of-house and kitchen roles

• Hands-on skill across attendant and cook duties, including POS operation, cafeteria and quick-service operations, and cooking on the line to outlet standards

• Proven ability to lead, motivate, and hold a team accountable; confident giving direction and feedback

• Strong guest-service orientation and the ability to resolve guest concerns calmly

• Strong communication, organization, and time-management skills; ability to stay composed under pressure

• Current food handler permit and required alcohol server training/certification, or ability to obtain within 30 days of hire

• Open availability, including weekends, evenings, and holidays

Preferred

• Prior lead, supervisor, or trainer experience in a restaurant, resort, or high-volume setting

• ServSafe Manager certification

• Experience in resort, cafeteria, quick-service, or banquet operations

• Ability to ski or snowboard

PHYSICAL DEMANDS

• Stand and walk for extended periods, up to 4 hours at a time, and lift and carry up to 50 lbs unassisted; use proper lifting technique and ask for help with heavier loads.

• Push, pull, bend, reach, squat, and climb stairs repeatedly throughout the shift, including while carrying product, trays, or equipment.

• Work in a range of temperatures, from walk-in coolers and freezers and hot cooking lines to outdoor winter cold, wind, and snow.

• Work in wet, noisy, and fast-paced environments with exposure to cleaning chemicals, steam, and hot surfaces, using required PPE.

• Use hands for repetitive motion, gripping, and fine-motor tasks; see and hear well enough to read tickets, communicate, and respond to guests and teammates.

WORK ENVIRONMENT & MOUNTAIN OPERATIONS

• Work in a mix of heated indoor spaces and cooler, semi-open or outdoor areas such as patios, decks, loading areas, and walkways, in all conditions from sub-zero winter days to warm summer events.

• Be comfortable working in cold, snow, wind, and high altitude. Dress in layers, stay hydrated, and tell a supervisor if you or a teammate show signs of cold stress or altitude sickness.

• Walk and carry product across snow- and ice-covered surfaces and stairs; use ice melt, mats, and wet-floor signage to keep guests and teammates safe, especially as guests enter in ski boots and wet gear.

• Operations shift with the weather and the day. Powder days, holiday weekends, road closures, delivery delays, and late-day rushes change the plan quickly, and we expect everyone to adapt with a positive, professional attitude.

• Seasonal operations include winter ski season and summer activity (including events and outdoor service), and duties, outlets, and hours may vary by season.

Commute & Transportation

Working at Brighton takes commitment and planning. The resort sits at the head of Big Cottonwood Canyon, and winter travel can be affected by snow, avalanche-control closures, road restrictions, and traction requirements. Employees are expected to plan ahead and arrive on time and ready to work, with reliable transportation that meets canyon requirements or by using the free UTA winter bus. Prepare for delays and build in extra time.
