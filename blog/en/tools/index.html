    Method

    Heights. Base, middle and top are the lowest and highest mapped lift or run of the ski area (OpenSkiMap / OpenStreetMap) and the height halfway between them.

    Temperature. Hourly 2 m air temperature forecast at each of the three heights from the weather models we use for the region (ECMWF IFS, DWD ICON and others via Open-Meteo, downscaled to the height), the median of the models, averaged over the next 24 hours.

    Snow today. Snow depth from the SnowScope snow model (daily analysis) at each height. Usual for this date and the season chart: the SnowScope past-winters archive, daily snow depth of 46 winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) from ERA5-Land, corrected to the height of the resort; percentiles p10, p25, p50 (median), p75 and p90 per calendar day. Last winter comes from the same archive, this winter from the snow model analysis and forecast.

    Fitted line. Ordinary least squares on the n heights with data: y = a + b·z, b = Σ(z−z̄)(y−ȳ) / Σ(z−z̄)², a = ȳ − b·z̄, R² = 1 − Σ(y−ŷ)² / Σ(y−ȳ)². b is shown in °C per km for temperature and in cm per 100 m for snow. With three heights R² says little: read the slope as a description of this day, not as a law.

    Limits. These are model values for an area, not measurements at a station. Temperature inversions (cold air pooled in valleys, common on calm winter nights) make the gradient weak or even positive. In the past-winters archive the height correction is part of the method, so the snow gradient of the usual values is partly built in; the snow model today is not tied to it in the same way. Snow on our map does not mean a resort is open.
