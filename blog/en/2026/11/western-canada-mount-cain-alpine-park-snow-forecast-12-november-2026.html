    Mount Cain Alpine Park: how much snow is coming? 37 cm by 15 November

    Western CanadaSnow forecastsPowder alertsPublished Thursday 12 November 2026 · 14:17 UTCData of 12 November3 min read

    How much snow is coming in Western Canada, and where? Between now and 15 November we expect 83 cm at Whistler Blackcomb and 37 cm at Mount Cain Alpine Park. A word on the numbers: resort values are our model's estimate for the upper slopes, while each station measures a single spot on the date given. In the field we always carry a probe; numbers like these tell you where to go, the probe tells you what is under your skis.

    What is on the ground

    Among the resorts in this report, Whistler Blackcomb has the deepest snow up high today, about 116 cm, ahead of Mount Cain Alpine Park 88 cm.

    Today 39 of the 57 resorts in Western Canada have snow on their upper slopes; in all, our model counts 255,860 km² under snow.

    Against past winters

    At about 1,403 m, Mount Cain Alpine Park has 28 cm today; a normal 12 November has 12 cm there.

    That is more than in 26 of the 46 past winters on this date.

    In past winters the best snow at Mount Cain Alpine Park usually came early January / mid-April.

    An early lead like this matters most for the lower and middle runs, which usually wait until December for this much snow.

    Day by day at Mount Cain Alpine Park: Thursday 12 November 6 cm; Friday 13 November 1 cm; Saturday 14 November 7 cm; Sunday 15 November 29 cm.

    Height by height at Mount Cain Alpine Park: 1,190 m 0 cm on the ground, 12 cm more in the next 7 days; 1,403 m 7 cm on the ground, 16 cm more in the next 7 days; 1,616 m 52 cm on the ground, 21 cm more in the next 7 days.

    Top, middle, bottom

    On 13 November the freezing level at Whistler Blackcomb climbs to 1,480 m to 2,680 m, higher than the top of the lifts (2,259 m): expect rain or wet snow on every slope.

    Warm air at Mount Cain Alpine Park on 13 November: the freezing level goes from 1,660 m to 2,160 m, above the resort's top (1,616 m), so rain or wet snow can reach every slope.

    At Whistler Blackcomb, the difference between top and bottom is clear: 85 cm at the top (2,259 m), 38 cm at mid-mountain (1,456 m) and 0 cm at the base (653 m).

    The days ahead

    The snow has pulled back since 10 November, from 281,250 km² to 255,860 km² today; for 15 November our forecast gives 245,900 km².

    Tips for skiers

    • Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.
    • Air above freezing over the slopes turns snow into slush and water. The highest lifts keep the driest snow.
    • The best powder lasts a morning, not a week. Go early, before the sun and the crowds get to it.
    • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
    • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Whistler Blackcomb gets fresh snow.

    About these numbers

    Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 12 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
