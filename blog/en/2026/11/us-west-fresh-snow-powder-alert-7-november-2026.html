Fresh snow in the US West: 13 in in 72 hours at Mt Baker (7 November)

US WestPowder alertsPublished Saturday 7 November 2026 · 20:00 UTCData of 6 November2 min read

Mt Baker got 13 in (32 cm) of new snow in the last 72 hours, 1 in (3 cm) of it in the last 24 hours. It now has about 17 in (44 cm) on its upper slopes, according to our snow model.

The new snow

Elsewhere, Buttermilk, Monarch Mountain and Ski Cooper got 6 in (15 cm), 4 in (11 cm) and 4 in (11 cm) in the last 72 hours.

136 of 195 resorts in the US West have snow on their upper slopes today, and our model counts 74,363 sq mi (192,600 km²) of snow-covered ground.

Height, slopes and snow line

Height matters: at Mt Baker, our model gives 8 in (21 cm) at the top (5,105 ft / 1,556 m), 4 in (11 cm) at mid-mountain (4,367 ft / 1,331 m) and 1 in (2 cm) at the base (3,629 ft / 1,106 m).

At Mt Baker, the freezing level reaches 5,971 ft (1,820 m) to 9,252 ft (2,820 m) on 7 November, above the top of the resort (5,105 ft / 1,556 m): rain or wet snow is possible on every slope that day.

What is coming next

Until 10 November, our forecast gives 21 in (53 cm) at Mt Baker.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Safety and avalanche level

After fresh snow, wind builds drifts on sheltered slopes. Off-piste, the first days after a snowfall need the most care.

Tips for skiers

  • Fresh snow is best in the first hours after it stops: go early, before the sun and the crowds.
  • Look for snow high up and on north-facing slopes: they keep it longest, while south-facing slopes lose it first in the sun.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Mt Baker gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 6 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
