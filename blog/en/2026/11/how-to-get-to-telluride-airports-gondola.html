How to get to Telluride: two airports, one mountain road and a free gondola

US WestGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 19:32 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

Telluride sits at the end of a box canyon in the San Juan Mountains, at 8,754 ft (2,668 m). That is part of its charm and the reason access needs a plan. There are two realistic airports, one main road and, at the end, a free gondola that does the last climb for you. Here they are one by one.

Option 1: Telluride Regional Airport (TEX)

The closest airport is on a mesa about 6 miles (9.7 km) west of town, at 9,078 ft (2,767 m), with a 1,000 ft (300 m) drop to the San Miguel River below the runway. Scheduled flights are run by Denver Air Connection to Denver and Phoenix. It is the fastest way in, but also the most exposed to the weather: in the past, about 20 % of the winter commuter flights had to divert to other airports. If you fly here, have a plan B through Montrose.

Option 2: Montrose Regional Airport (MTJ)

Most visitors land in Montrose, at 5,759 ft (1,755 m), which is busiest in winter precisely because of the skiers heading to Telluride. United, Southwest, American and Delta fly there, with year-round links to Denver and seasonal flights to hubs such as Chicago, Dallas/Fort Worth, Houston and Newark. Telluride is 67 miles (108 km) away, about an hour and a half by road. Shuttles and rental cars both work; on a storm day the drive takes longer.

Option 3: drive in on Highway 145

State Highway 145, part of the San Juan Skyway, is the main road into town. Colorado's traction law applies on state highways during winter storms or when conditions require: tyres with at least 3/16 inch of tread rated all-weather or winter (M+S, M/S or the mountain-snowflake symbol), or chains. Four-wheel or all-wheel drive is recommended. The fine is $100 plus a $33 surcharge, and $500 plus $157 if your car blocks a lane. Rental cars often have all-season tyres: ask before you collect the keys.

The last mile: the free gondola

Once you are in town, leave the car. The free gondola, opened on 20 December 1996, links the old town of Telluride with Mountain Village at the base of the ski area, by way of the San Sophia station at 10,540 ft (3,210 m). The ride takes about 13 minutes, and it is the only free gondola of its kind in North America. Stay in town and ride it in the morning, or stay in Mountain Village and ride it down for dinner.

Is the trip worth it? The snow

For the snow, yes, if you aim for the right height. Our model of 46 past winters gives a usual peak of about 67 in (169 cm) at mid-mountain, 10,620 ft (3,237 m), and about 106 in (268 cm) near the top, 12,507 ft (3,812 m), while the base at 8,734 ft (2,662 m) peaks at only about 31 in (80 cm). Mid-mountain keeps at least 20 in (50 cm) for 13 weeks in 9 winters out of 10. Winters change, though: last winter, 2025-26, was the thinnest of our record at mid-mountain, about 30 in (76 cm), and the median peak there fell from about 80 in (203 cm) in our first fifteen winters to about 63 in (160 cm) in the last fifteen.

Tips for skiers

  • Flying into Telluride: keep Montrose as plan B for weather diversions.
  • Driving: winter-rated tyres with 3/16 inch of tread or chains on Highway 145 during storms.
  • In town, use the free gondola instead of the car.
  • Snow on our map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and the gondola on the official websites.
