Michlifen snow comes in episodes: how to catch a ski day in the Middle Atlas

Atlas (Morocco)GuidesPublished Wednesday 11 November 2026 · 20:00 UTCData of 11 November3 min read

Michlifen is a small ski area in a volcanic crater of the Middle Atlas, close to Ifrane. People ask whether there is snow there in winter. The honest answer is: sometimes, for a few days at a time. That is not a reason to stay away. It is a reason to plan differently, and our record of 46 past winters shows how.

A season made of short visits

The ski area lies between about 1,800 m and 2,000 m, with five pistes in a crater surrounded by cedar forest. In our model, at 1,970 m, a typical winter has snow on the ground for about 24 days, and those days do not come in one block. The first snow usually arrives around mid-December and the last melts around mid-March, with dry spells in between. The deepest snow of a typical winter is only about 31 cm.

Why the usual depth is zero

Here is the odd part. If you take any date in January and ask what depth is usual there, the answer from our 46 winters is zero: on most days of most winters, there is no snow on the ground. Yet every one of those winters had snow at some point; even the poorest reached about 9 cm. The snow is real, it simply comes on different days each year. In the snowiest tenth of winters, a January day had about 25 cm or more at 1,970 m.

Good winters do happen

The best of our winters reached about 80 cm at 1,970 m and about 106 cm at 2,070 m. Last winter, 2025-26, was one of the good ones: about 51 cm at its peak, and about 48 days with snow on the ground, twice the usual. Over time the picture is mixed rather than simply worse: the median peak went from about 34 cm in our first fifteen winters to about 31 cm in the last fifteen, while the days with snow rose from about 24 to 29.

How to catch an episode

Watch the forecast, not the calendar. The national weather service, the Direction Générale de la Météorologie, publishes vigilance maps at least twice a day, and snowfall is one of the warnings. When a cold storm is announced for the Middle Atlas, plan to arrive in the first one to three days after it, when the snow is fresh and before the sun and mild air thin it. Go early in the day, check the road conditions before leaving, and carry warm clothes: Ifrane holds the record for the lowest temperature ever observed in Africa, about −23.9 °C, on 11 February 1935.

A note on other sources

Some sources describe snow at Michlifen from mid-November to the end of March. Our model is more cautious. Those dates are a fair outer frame, but inside it snow comes and goes. Plan for an episode, not for a season, and you will rarely be disappointed.

If the snow does not come

Ifrane, at more than 1,650 m and nicknamed 'Little Switzerland', is worth the trip anyway. The Middle Atlas around it has wide cedar forests and the Ifrane National Park, home of the endangered Barbary macaque. Snow can reach down to about 600 m in this range in winter, so a cold week can turn the whole landscape white, even when the crater has only a thin cover.

Tips for skiers

  • Follow the DGM vigilance maps: a snow warning for the Middle Atlas is your signal.
  • Arrive one to three days after the snowfall, early in the day.
  • Check the roads before you leave and bring warm clothes.
  • Snow on our map does not mean the ski area is open or the lifts are running: ask locally before you go.
