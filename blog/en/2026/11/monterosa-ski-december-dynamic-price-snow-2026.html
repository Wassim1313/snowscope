Monterosa Ski in December: from 40 EUR a day, if you ski high

AlpsGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 16:31 UTCData of 14 November4 min read

Monterosa Ski is one of the few big Italian ski areas where a December day can cost 40.00 EUR, because the price changes with the date. The catch is height: in our snow history the bottom of the lifts is almost bare in a usual December, while the top sits under deep snow.

One pass, many prices

Monterosa Ski does not sell one fixed day pass. Bought online, the adult 1-day pass changes with the date, from 40.00 EUR on the cheapest days to 71.00 EUR on the busiest. Early December, before the Christmas rush, is when the low end of that range is worth hunting for.

For that money you get a big mapped ski area: 72 km of runs and 37 lifts, from 1207 m up to 3030 m, a vertical of 1823 m. Check the price for your exact dates on the official page before you book anything else.

Bare at the bottom, deep at the top

No resort in this series shows a bigger gap between its lowest and its highest lifts. At 1207 m the usual December depth is just 6 cm, and even in a deep December, 1 winter in 10, it stays under 24 cm. Lasting snow does not usually settle down there before 1 December. Plan on taking the lift down at the end of the day.

Mid-mountain, at 2118 m, is better but not safe: 36 cm in a usual December, under 14 cm in a thin one and over 67 cm in a deep one. The top is another world. At 3030 m a usual December holds 124 cm, a thin one still 55 cm and a deep one 212 cm. On 15 December the usual depth up there is 127 cm.

Week by week: stay high

At mid-mountain, 30 cm or more was on the ground in 46 % of past winters in the first week of December, 59 % in the second, 67 % in the third and 80 % in the week of 22-28 December. For 50 cm or more the shares are only 20 %, 26 %, 28 % and 35 %.

At the top the picture is simple: 30 cm or more in 98 % of winters in the first week and in every one of our 46 winters after that, and 50 cm or more in 87 % to 96 % of winters through the month. At the bottom, 30 cm or more happened in 4 % of winters at best, in the last week. A December trip here means skiing the upper lifts and not counting on the lower runs.

This season has started slowly: on 13 November our estimate shows 1 cm at mid-mountain and 7 cm at the top, with about 26 cm and 42 cm of new snow in the next 7 days. The two-week outlook for mid-mountain and above gives 22 to 61 cm, a range rather than a promise.

Monterosa Ski or Pila

Pila, above Aosta, is the simpler plan: one adult day price of 60.00 EUR all season and 322.00 EUR for 6 days. Its mapped area is smaller, 56 km of runs and 16 lifts between 1533 m and 2723 m.

The snow is spread more evenly at Pila. Its base at 1533 m holds 34 cm in a usual December against 6 cm at Monterosa's 1207 m, and its mid-mountain (2128 m) 45 cm against 36 cm. But Pila's top at 2723 m usually has 80 cm, far less than the 124 cm at 3030 m on Monterosa. If you can catch a low-price day and you are happy to stay high, Monterosa Ski gives you more mountain for less money. If you want one fixed price and snow lower down, Pila is the easier choice.

Has December changed here?

Hardly, and that is unusual. Comparing the first 15 winters of our record (1980-81 to 1994-95) with the last 15 (2011-12 to 2025-26), the December average stayed at 37 cm at mid-mountain and at 126 cm at the top; at the bottom it went from 4 cm to 6 cm.

Single winters swing a lot. Last winter, December averaged 22 cm at mid-mountain and 87 cm at the top. The thinnest December, 2015-16, had 5 cm at mid-mountain and 38 cm at the top; the deepest, 1982-83, had 82 cm and 262 cm.

Tips for skiers

  • Compare several December dates on the official page: the online day price runs from 40.00 to 71.00 EUR.
  • Pick the week of 22-28 December if you want cover at mid-mountain: 30 cm or more there in 80 % of past winters.
  • In early December, stay on the lifts above 2118 m; the 1207 m base is usually close to bare.
  • Read the avalanche bulletin before any off-piste: the top carries deep snow while the lower slopes are thin.
