Deer Valley, Utah ski conditions: is there snow yet? (13 November 2026)

US WestGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 18:32 UTCData of 12 November3 min read

Is there snow in Deer Valley? Not yet, not in any useful amount. Our snow map shows a trace, 0.4 in (1 cm), near the top at 9,600 ft, and bare ground at mid-mountain and on the lower slopes. The weekend storm should change the high ground first: about 4 in (11 cm) near the top. The low end of the resort, by the Jordanelle Reservoir, sits under the snow level and gets mostly rain.

The mountain, from Empire down to Jordanelle

Deer Valley is the skiers-only resort next door to Park City, with no snowboards on its lifts. It has grown east: the new East Village side now reaches down towards the Jordanelle Reservoir. On our mapping that gives 117 mi (189 km) of runs and 37 lifts, from about 6,400 ft at the lowest point to 9,600 ft near the top of Empire Canyon and Bald Mountain, a drop of about 3,100 ft.

That low eastern end matters this weekend. It is the part of Deer Valley that is closest to the rain.

This weekend, side by side

The storm comes in from Friday night into Saturday. Our numbers for the next two days: about 4 in (11 cm) near the top, 3 in (7 cm) at mid-mountain around 8,000 ft, and nothing that stays at 6,400 ft. The forecasts we check give 3 to 9 in for the top, the official forecast about 3 in, so our number sits in the middle.

The snow level is the key line: about 6,700 to 7,600 ft. Above it, around Empire and Bald Mountain, the storm falls as snow. Below it, on the Jordanelle side and the lower runs, about two thirds of it should be rain.

The map, drawn for Sunday after the storm, shows something else that locals know well: the heaviest snow lands on the high Wasatch to the west, and Deer Valley sits on the eastern edge of the white. The crest takes the first share.

Next week: colder, and a wide range

After the weekend the air turns colder and the snow level drops to about 6,200 to 6,900 ft, close to the bottom of the resort. Over seven days our totals reach 11 in (29 cm) near the top, 7.5 in (19 cm) at mid-mountain and 2 in (5 cm) at the base.

Be ready for change. For the top, the forecasts we check range from 8 to 29 in over the week, a very wide spread for the days after the storm. Check again in a couple of days; the later storms are the ones that could build a real base.

What good conditions usually look like here

Take 20 in (50 cm) on the ground as the line for good skiing. In our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) the top of Deer Valley has that much for 19 weeks in 9 winters out of 10, mid-mountain for 14 weeks, and the lowest slopes for only 3. The best week near the top is week 12, in late March, when the middle depth is 93 in (237 cm).

November is early in that story: a usual November holds 13 in (33 cm) near the top, 8 in (21 cm) at mid-mountain and 4 in (10 cm) at the base, and the first lasting snow usually arrives around 12 October up high and 24 October at the bottom. December usually brings 38 in (96 cm) near the top and 15 in (37 cm) at the base. Last winter was the thinnest of our 46 at every height, with a peak of 57 in (145 cm) near the top and 12 in (30 cm) at the base.

Tips for skiers

  • Deer Valley is for skiers only: snowboarders head next door to Park City Mountain.
  • In the first weeks, the high runs and the snowmaking runs open first; the low East Village slopes need a colder pattern.
  • Before skinning uphill or leaving the resort, read the Utah Avalanche Center forecast: early snow on the Wasatch often turns into a weak layer.
