Where is the deepest snow in Western Canada? Manning Park Resort, with 110 cm (10 November)

Western CanadaWhere to skiPublished Tuesday 10 November 2026 · 14:19 UTCUpdated 10 November 2026Data of 10 November2 min read

Where is the deepest snow in Western Canada today? Deepest snow in Western Canada today: Manning Park Resort, about 110 cm on the upper slopes (model). Second Big White, 85 cm; third Whistler Blackcomb, 83 cm. Snow-covered ground in Western Canada: 260,060 km². Resorts with snow: 41 of 57. Confidence: good for the trend, lower for any single number.

Is this normal?

A strong opening at Big White: our model has 42 cm at 1,891 m, deeper than on this date in 40 of the past 46 winters, against a usual 26 cm.

Below par at Whistler Blackcomb: 55 cm at 1,456 m against a usual 77 cm, and only 12 of the past 46 winters had less on 10 November.

The forecast in short

More snow ahead: 53 cm at Whistler Blackcomb, 40 cm at Mount Cain Alpine Park and 38 cm at Tweedsmuir Ski Club by 13 November, if the forecast holds.

On 11 November the freezing level at Manning Park Resort sits at 2,800 m to 3,030 m, over the top of the resort (1,789 m): rain or wet snow could fall on all slopes.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Tips for skiers

  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
  • A rule of thumb that is rarely wrong: high and north-facing keeps snow, south-facing loses it first.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Manning Park Resort gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 10 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
