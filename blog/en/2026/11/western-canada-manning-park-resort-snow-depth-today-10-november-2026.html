Where is the deepest snow in Western Canada? Manning Park Resort, with 110 cm (10 November)

Western CanadaWhere to skiPublished Tuesday 10 November 2026 · 14:19 UTCData of 10 November2 min read

Where is the deepest snow in Western Canada today? The most snow in Western Canada is at Manning Park Resort: about 110 cm on the upper slopes, according to our snow model. Next come Big White with 85 cm and Whistler Blackcomb with 83 cm. Our snow model shows 260,060 km² of snow-covered ground in Western Canada today, with snow at 41 of 57 resorts.

Today's snow

The snow cover shrank from 270,720 km² on 8 November to 260,060 km² today, and our forecast gives 235,100 km² on 13 November.

Height matters: at Big White, our model gives 62 cm at the top (2,271 m), 10 cm at mid-mountain (1,891 m) and 0 cm at the base (1,512 m).

The next days

More snow is on the way: by 13 November our forecast gives 53 cm at Whistler Blackcomb, 40 cm at Mount Cain Alpine Park and 38 cm at Tweedsmuir Ski Club.

At Manning Park Resort, the freezing level reaches 2,800 m to 3,030 m on 11 November, above the top of the resort (1,789 m): rain or wet snow is possible on every slope that day.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Measured on the ground

Resort numbers are model estimates for the upper slopes; station numbers are measurements at one point, on the date shown.

Is it a good start?

This is a good start at Big White. At 1,891 m, our model has 42 cm today, deeper than on this date in 40 of the past 46 winters (usual: 26 cm).

At 1,456 m on Whistler Blackcomb the season is late: our model has only 55 cm today, below the usual 77 cm. Only 12 of the past 46 winters had less snow there on 10 November.

Tips for skiers

  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
  • Look for snow high up and on north-facing slopes: they keep it longest, while south-facing slopes lose it first in the sun.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Manning Park Resort gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 10 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
