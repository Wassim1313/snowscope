Tromsø snow depth: 6 cm in town, 44 cm in the Lyngen hills (13 November 2026)

ScandinaviaGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 13:29 UTCData of 13 November2 min read

Tromsø has a thin white cover this Friday. The weather station on the island, 114 m up the hill above the town centre, measured 6 cm of snow on Friday morning, down from 8 cm on Thursday. The real snow is across the fjords: a station at 710 m in the Lyngen Alps has 44 cm.

Town, fjords and hills

The cover in town is new: on Monday the station still had bare ground, then the snow came mid-week and settled a little on Friday. Along the coast north-east of Tromsø, Nordstraum in Kvænangen has 8 cm. Inland, Bardufoss has only 3 cm.

Higher up it is a different season. Gjerdvassbu in Lyngen, at 710 m, has had a deep cover all week: 38 cm on Monday, 44 cm now. Across the border, Kilpisjärvi in Finland, 100 km away, has 13 cm.

About normal for Tromsø

Our snow history for Tromsø near sea level covers 46 past winters. On 13 November there was snow on the ground in 32 of them, and the middle value is 8 cm, with up to about 17 cm in a snowy year. Today's 6 cm, measured a little higher up, sits right in that range.

Recent winters on this date ranged from bare ground (2022-23 and last winter) to 12 cm in 2023-24. By 1 December the usual depth is about 17 cm, by mid-December about 27 cm, and by mid-January about 50 cm.

The weekend: the cold arrives

Saturday is dry and cold, with temperatures between about -11 °C and -4 °C depending on the forecast. Sunday brings around 2 cm of new snow and then milder air, close to zero. Monday stays near freezing with little snow. The thin cover will stay, and crisp.

Tips for skiers

  • With 6 cm on the ground and dark afternoons, a headlamp and studded shoes make a town walk much easier this weekend.
  • If you are driving out of town towards Lyngen, the roads go from wet to icy as you climb.
  • Before skiing anywhere steep in the Lyngen Alps, read the avalanche warning on Varsom: 44 cm on bare, early-season ground is not a stable base.

About these numbers

Snow depth today: measured at weather stations on the morning of 13 November 2026 (MET Norway station reports in the international SYNOP format); a station reports the snow on its own small plot, so a few kilometres away and a hundred metres higher the ground can look different. Usual depth for the date: SnowScope snow history, 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26), a model of the past at the city's height, not a station record. Weekend: two weather models (ECMWF IFS and DWD ICON, read through Open-Meteo), given as a range. Places and rules: the official pages listed below.
