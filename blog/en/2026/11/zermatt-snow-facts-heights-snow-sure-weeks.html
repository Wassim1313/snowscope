Zermatt snow facts: heights, snow-sure weeks and the glacier

AlpsGuidesPublished Tuesday 10 November 2026 · 07:53 UTCData of 10 November3 min read

Zermatt is a resort of extremes: the village sits at 1,620 m, while the Klein Matterhorn, the highest point of the lift network, reaches 3,883 m. That height gap explains almost everything about the snow here, so this guide starts with the numbers and then turns them into plans for the slopes.

The fact file

Village: 1,620 m, closed to combustion-engine cars. Most visitors arrive on the rack-assisted train from Täsch, and trains also run from Visp and Brig further down the valley. Highest point: the Klein Matterhorn at 3,883 m. The ski area links with Cervinia and Valtournenche in Italy across the Plateau Rosa glacier, and the Theodul Glacier behind the Klein Matterhorn is used for summer skiing.

Our snow history covers 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) at three heights of the ski area: 1,559 m, 2,715 m and 3,872 m. These are model values for an area, corrected for height, not the resort's own snow reports.

What 46 winters say about each height

At 2,715 m, in the middle of the ski area, the deepest snow of a typical winter is about 143 cm. It builds slowly: about 54 cm in December, 79 cm in January, 94 cm in February and 102 cm in March, and it is usually deepest in April, with about 118 cm. In 9 winters out of 10 there are at least 50 cm at this height for 18 weeks of the season.

Low down the picture changes. At 1,559 m the peak of a typical winter is only about 35 cm, and no week of the season reliably reaches 50 cm. Near the top, at 3,872 m, the model peak is about 351 cm, with the deepest snow usually in May; part of the terrain up there is glacier, so read the top numbers as high-mountain snow, not as piste depth.

Snow-sure months

If you can choose, February to April is the safest window at mid-mountain. In a typical winter the first snow of the season reaches 2,715 m around 2 October and the last melts around 29 June, so early-season trips depend on the upper lifts. At 3,872 m the first snow usually comes around 24 September.

A slow change

Our record also shows a shift. In the first fifteen winters from 1980-81, the median peak at 2,715 m was about 156 cm; in the last fifteen, up to 2025-26, it was about 142 cm, and the days with snow on the ground fell from about 281 to 248. At the top the median peak went from about 394 cm to 342 cm. Last winter, 2025-26, the peak at mid-mountain was about 120 cm, below the long-term middle.

Using the numbers on the mountain

Beginners and early-season visitors: stay high, where the snow is deepest and the season longest, and ride a lift down rather than skiing the lowest runs on thin snow. Strong skiers: spring at altitude is the long game here, with the deepest snow of the year often arriving in April and May near the top. The glacier and the off-piste terrain around it are high-alpine ground, with crevasses as well as avalanches: go with a mountain guide.

Where to stay: the village is car-free and long, so a room near the station or near the lift you plan to use most saves a lot of walking with skis. Before any off-piste run, read the official avalanche bulletin of the Swiss institute WSL-SLF; for the Italian side, AINEVA lists the regional bulletins.

Tips for skiers

  • For the most reliable snow, plan for February to April and ski the upper half of the area.
  • Check lifts and opening dates on the resort's official website before you book: snow on our map does not mean a lift is running.
  • Off-piste or on the glacier: read the WSL-SLF avalanche bulletin and hire a guide.
  • Leave the car in Täsch: the village is reached by train.
