38 cm of new snow at Tweedsmuir Ski Club: the Western Canada powder report (11 November)

Western CanadaPowder alertsPublished Wednesday 11 November 2026 · 18:44 UTCData of 11 November2 min read

38 cm in 72 hours: that is the fresh snow at Tweedsmuir Ski Club. Tweedsmuir Ski Club gained 38 cm of new snow in the last 72 hours, 35 cm of it in the last 24 hours, so the snowpack there is growing. Its upper slopes now hold about 72 cm of snow, by our model. For the rivers downstream, how long the snow stays matters as much as how much falls.

New snow on the mountains

Across the region, Whistler Blackcomb and Mount Cain Alpine Park gained 29 cm and 14 cm in the last 72 hours.

Today 245,310 km² of ground in Western Canada is covered by snow, our model says, and 39 of 57 resorts have snow on their upper slopes.

Where snow stays, where it melts

Height changes everything at Tweedsmuir Ski Club: our model gives 58 cm at the base (1,588 m), 59 cm at mid-mountain (1,619 m) and 61 cm at the top (1,650 m).

At Tweedsmuir Ski Club the freezing level reaches 1,040 m to 1,870 m on 13 November, higher than the top (1,650 m): rain or wet snow is possible on all slopes, and some of the snow may turn to runoff.

What the gauges recorded

Two kinds of numbers here: model estimates for the upper slopes of each resort, and station measurements that are exact but cover one point on the date shown.

Safety first

After fresh snow, wind builds drifts on sheltered slopes. Off-piste, the first days after a snowfall need the most care.

Tips for skiers

  • Be on the first lift after a snowfall: by midday, sun and skiers have already changed the snow.
  • A rule of thumb that is rarely wrong: high and north-facing keeps snow, south-facing loses it first.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Tweedsmuir Ski Club gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 11 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
