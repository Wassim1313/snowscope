Early-season skiing in the Alps: how high do you need to go in December?

AlpsGuidesPublished Tuesday 10 November 2026 · 14:58 UTCData of 10 November5 min read

In the mountains of Morocco, people learn one rule early: if you want snow, go up. In the Atlas, a few hundred metres of height can be the difference between a white slope and a dusty one. The Alps in December follow the same rule, only with more snow. We checked 676 Alpine ski areas over 46 past winters to see how high you really need to be for a trip before Christmas.

The height ladder

We sorted the resorts by the height of their mid-mountain, the middle of their ski area, into six steps: below 1,200 m, then steps of 300 m, and a last step above 2,400 m. For each step we looked at three dates: 1 December, 15 December and 22 December. On each date we took the usual snow depth, the middle value of 46 winters, and then the middle resort of the step. The chart shows the result.

We use 30 cm as a simple test. It is roughly what you need on smooth grass slopes to ski without hitting the ground. Rocky terrain needs more. The red dashed line in the chart marks that 30 cm.

Below 1,500 m: wait for January

Resorts with their middle below 1,200 m usually have only 8 cm on 1 December, 12 cm on 15 December and 14 cm on 22 December. Not one of the 92 resorts in this step reaches 30 cm by mid-December in half of the winters. These are the places that depend on snowmaking and on luck in December.

One step higher, between 1,200 and 1,500 m, the usual depth goes from 15 cm on 1 December to 24 cm on 15 December and 27 cm on 22 December. Only 20 % of the 186 resorts in this step reach 30 cm by 15 December in a typical winter, and 41 % by 22 December.

KitzSki is a good example. Its mid-mountain sits at 1,378 m, and its usual snow there is 15 cm on 1 December and 26 cm on 22 December. In a poor winter, one of the worst in ten, it has only 10 cm on 22 December. Garmisch-Classic, at 1,387 m, looks almost the same.

1,500 to 2,100 m: a good bet from mid-December

Between 1,500 and 1,800 m, the usual depth crosses 30 cm around mid-December: 21 cm on 1 December, 32 cm on 15 December and 38 cm on 22 December. 57 % of these resorts reach 30 cm by 15 December in a typical winter.

Between 1,800 and 2,100 m, the picture changes. The usual depth is 28 cm on 1 December, 42 cm on 15 December and 49 cm on 22 December, and 80 % of the resorts in this step have 30 cm by 15 December in half of the winters. St. Anton (mid-mountain 1,962 m) usually has 42 cm on 1 December and 72 cm on 22 December.

There is a catch. "Half of the winters" also means that in the other half, the snow is thinner. Only 8 % of the resorts in the 1,800–2,100 m step reach 30 cm by 15 December in 9 winters out of 10.

Above 2,100 m: where December is reliable

Between 2,100 and 2,400 m, the usual depth is 34 cm on 1 December, 51 cm on 15 December and 58 cm on 22 December. 95 % of these resorts have 30 cm by mid-December in half of the winters, and 63 % have it on 22 December even in 9 winters out of 10.

Above 2,400 m, the step of the glacier ski areas and the highest resorts, the usual depth is already 56 cm on 1 December, 83 cm on 15 December and 90 cm on 22 December. By 22 December, 88 % of these resorts reach 30 cm in 9 winters out of 10.

Stubaier Gletscher (mid-mountain 2,450 m) usually has 63 cm on 1 December and 88 cm on 15 December. Val Thorens (2,474 m) has 56 cm and 88 cm. In the worst winter of ten, Val Thorens still has 30 cm on 1 December and 43 cm on 15 December.

Glacier or not: what the numbers do not show

A glacier helps in two ways that our snow numbers miss. The ice under the runs is smooth, so a thin cover is enough to ski. And the lifts on glaciers often open in autumn, long before the first village resort. Our numbers count only natural snow on the ground, at the middle of each ski area.

Snowmaking is the other big unknown. Many lower resorts open in December on machine-made snow on a few main runs. That can give you good piste skiing even when the usual natural snow is 15 cm. It does not give you off-piste snow, and it does not cover the whole area.

Hintertux shows that height is not everything. Its middle sits at 2,372 m, but its usual depth on 1 December is 40 cm, lower than Stubaier Gletscher at a similar height. Valleys, sun and wind change the snow from place to place, which is why each resort page in the app shows its own past winters.

How to use the ladder when you book

For a trip in the first week of December, book a resort with its middle above 2,100 m, or a glacier. For the week before Christmas, 1,800 m is usually enough in a normal winter, and above 2,100 m you are well protected even in a poor one.

If you love a lower resort, keep it for January, or book late, when the real snow is on the ground and visible on the live map.

In the Atlas we say the mountain decides, not the calendar. In the Alps in December it is the same: the calendar gives you a date, and the height gives you the snow.

Tips for skiers

  • Before mid-December, book above 2,100 m (mid-mountain) or on a glacier.
  • For the week before Christmas, 1,800 m is a good line in a normal winter.
  • Lower resorts can be fine on machine-made snow, but expect only a few open runs and no off-piste.
  • Early-season snow often sits on weak layers: check the official avalanche bulletin before skiing off-piste.

About these numbers

Snow numbers: SnowScope snow history, 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) at the middle height of 676 Alpine ski areas with at least 5 km of runs, a model of the past corrected for height (natural snow, no snowmaking, not resort reports). "Usual" = middle value of the 46 winters on that date; "9 winters out of 10" = the 10th percentile.
