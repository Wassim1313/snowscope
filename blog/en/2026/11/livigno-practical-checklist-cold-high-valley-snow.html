Livigno checklist: ten practical things to know about Italy's cold high valley

AlpsGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 19:48 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

Livigno is easy to love and easy to underestimate. The town stands at 1,816 m in a long valley of Lombardy close to the Swiss border, and it is cold. A little planning goes a long way. Here are ten practical points, in the order you will need them.

1. Pick your road before you pick your dates

Three roads lead to Livigno. The Foscagno pass, at 2,291 m, links it with the rest of Italy; the Munt la Schera tunnel connects it with Switzerland all year; and the Forcola di Livigno, at 2,315 m, is a summer-only road. In winter that leaves two ways in, and both are mountain routes, so check road conditions and carry chains or winter tyres.

2. Expect real cold

The valley has a subalpine climate, most of the winter is spent below freezing, and the record low is −37 °C. Pack a good mid-layer, a face cover and spare gloves; on a still January morning the valley floor can be colder than the slopes above it.

3. Know the two sides

The skiing is split between two sides of the valley: Carosello 3000 and Mottolino. Together they have about 115 km of pistes and 31 lifts. Plan your days by side rather than crossing the valley at lunch.

4. Height is on your side

Because the town is already so high, the snow starts close to your door. In our model of 46 past winters, the lowest point of the ski area, at 1,811 m, has a usual peak of about 65 cm. At 2,301 m, our middle point, it is about 116 cm, and near the top, at 2,791 m, about 207 cm.

5. Aim for February and March

At 2,301 m a typical winter has about 48 cm of snow in December, 74 cm in January, 91 cm in February, 96 cm in March and 92 cm in April. There are at least 50 cm there for 13 weeks in 9 winters out of 10, so the middle of the season is the safest bet.

6. Don't write off spring

Near the top, the snow keeps growing late: April is the deepest month at 2,791 m, with about 178 cm in a typical winter, and there are at least 50 cm for 22 weeks in 9 winters out of 10. On a sunny spring day ski the high runs before lunch.

7. Be careful with early December

The low runs are the weak point. At 1,811 m a typical winter has only about 29 cm in December, and in no week are there 50 cm in 9 winters out of 10. Early-season trips work best if you are happy to stay high.

8. Shop, but know the rules

Livigno has a long-standing tax-free status, with no value added tax on many goods. It goes back to around 1840, was confirmed by Italy around 1910 and again in 1960. If you plan to take goods home, check the customs allowances first.

9. A valley with an Olympic past

Livigno hosted the snowboard and freestyle skiing events of the 2026 Winter Olympics. Check on the official website which areas are open.

10. Read the snow, not just the forecast

The long-term picture here is steady at mid-mountain: our median peak at 2,301 m was about 117 cm in the first fifteen winters and about 122 cm in the last fifteen, although the days with snow on the ground fell from about 248 to 230. Last winter, 2025-26, was thin, with about 86 cm at mid-mountain. Check lifts on the official website and the regional avalanche bulletin before any off-piste day.

Tips for skiers

  • Check the Foscagno pass or the Munt la Schera tunnel before you drive; the Forcola is closed in winter.
  • Pack for deep cold: face cover, spare gloves, a warm mid-layer.
  • For early-season trips, plan to ski high.
  • Snow on our map does not mean a resort is open: check the lifts on the official website.
