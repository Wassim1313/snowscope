Why Baqueira's snow is different: the Atlantic side of the Spanish Pyrenees, explained and tested

PyreneesGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 11:38 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

Ask Spanish skiers why Baqueira-Beret is different and many will say one word: Aran. The resort lies in the Val d'Aran, a valley that belongs to Catalonia but faces the other way. We explain why that matters for snow, then test the idea against 46 past winters from our model.

A Catalan valley that faces the Atlantic

The Val d'Aran is the only part of Catalonia that lies on the northern side of the Pyrenees. The Garonne rises there, on the Pla de Beret, and flows out of Aran towards the Atlantic, so the valley's rivers are the only Catalan rivers that end in that ocean. Its climate follows the water: Atlantic, not Mediterranean. Even the language is different; Aranese is the local form of Occitan.

Why that should mean more snow

Storms from the Atlantic reach the northern slopes of the range first, while the southern valleys sit in their lee. Baqueira-Beret adds a westerly aspect to its northern position, and the resort is known for a good snow record because of this Atlantic climate. That is the theory. Here is how it looks in our numbers.

The test: Baqueira against La Molina

We compared mid-mountain at Baqueira, at 2,052 m, with mid-mountain at La Molina, at 2,085 m, a resort in the Cerdanya, in Girona, far to the east. In a typical winter Baqueira's snow peaks at about 91 cm there, La Molina's at about 75 cm, and both have snow on the ground for a similar time, about 150 and 146 days. The bigger difference is how long a useful depth lasts: in half of our 46 winters Baqueira had at least 50 cm for 8 weeks, La Molina for none. The Atlantic effect is real, but it shows more in the length of good snow than in the peak.

The other side of Atlantic snow: big swings

Atlantic storms come in series, and some winters miss them. At 2,052 m the poorest of our winters peaked at only about 26 cm and the best at about 152 cm. In February, the deepest month, the usual depth is about 67 cm, but the poor tenth of winters had only about 8 cm and the good tenth about 107 cm. Plan for the middle, but know the range.

What has changed

The valley floor has changed most. At 1,504 m, days with snow on the ground fell from about 133 in our first fifteen winters to about 86 in the last fifteen. At mid-mountain the median peak fell from about 91 cm to about 81 cm and the snow days from about 153 to 133; near the top, at 2,600 m, the peak fell from about 130 cm to about 109 cm. Last winter, 2025-26, went the other way: it was above normal, with peaks of about 101 cm at mid-mountain and about 149 cm at the top.

When to go, and how

February is the deepest month at every height: about 67 cm at mid-mountain and about 102 cm at the top in a typical winter, with January (about 44 cm at mid-mountain) and March (about 53 cm) on either side. The single best week in our record falls in early February. Baqueira-Beret is the largest and most visited ski resort in Spain, with 161 km of marked pistes and 35 lifts across the Baqueira, Beret and Bonaigua areas. The nearest airport is Toulouse, about two hours by road, and the Vielha tunnel, opened in 1948, links Aran to the south. Before skiing off the pistes, read the Aran avalanche bulletin, and check lifts and dates on the official website.

Tips for skiers

  • Aim for February: it is the deepest month at every height in a typical winter.
  • Expect big swings from year to year: watch the forecast before you book late.
  • Off-piste: read the Aran avalanche bulletin (Lauegi) first.
  • Snow on our map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and dates on the official website.
