Snow forecast in the US West: up to 13 in at Bear Valley by 16 November

US WestSnow forecastsPowder alertsPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 17:36 UTCData of 13 November2 min read

The snow is not done yet: we expect 13 in (34 cm) at Bear Valley, 13 in (34 cm) at Dodge Ridge and 13 in (33 cm) at Badger Pass by 16 November. A word on the numbers: resort values are our model's estimate for the upper slopes, while each station measures a single spot on the date given. Field work in dry mountains teaches one thing: the map is a start, the slope itself has the last word.

Snow on the slopes today

Of the places named here, Kirkwood leads with about 9 in (24 cm) on its upper slopes; Bear Valley 8 in (21 cm) and Dodge Ridge 6 in (14 cm) come next.

Which height to ski today

On 15 November the freezing level at Bear Valley climbs to 4,003 ft (1,220 m) to 10,302 ft (3,140 m), higher than the top of the lifts (8,484 ft / 2,586 m): expect rain or wet snow on every slope.

Warm air at Dodge Ridge on 15 November: the freezing level goes from 4,921 ft (1,500 m) to 10,564 ft (3,220 m), above the resort's top (8,143 ft / 2,482 m), so rain or wet snow can reach every slope.

On 15 November the freezing level at Badger Pass climbs to 6,857 ft (2,090 m) to 11,220 ft (3,420 m), higher than the top of the lifts (7,795 ft / 2,376 m): expect rain or wet snow on every slope.

From the base to the top of Bear Valley: 1 in (3 cm) at the base (6,640 ft / 2,024 m), 3 in (8 cm) at mid-mountain (7,562 ft / 2,305 m) and 9 in (22 cm) at the top (8,484 ft / 2,586 m).

The days ahead

And more is coming: by 16 November, the forecast brings 13 in (32 cm) at Kirkwood and 13 in (32 cm) at Mt. Shasta Ski Park.

Less ground is white now: 49,467 sq mi (128,120 km²) on 11 November, only 49,050 sq mi (127,040 km²) today, and 64,429 sq mi (166,870 km²) expected on 16 November.

Safety and avalanche level

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Tips for skiers

  • Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.
  • Air above freezing over the slopes turns snow into slush and water. The highest lifts keep the driest snow.
  • New snow does not stay new for long. Start early, while it is still soft and untracked.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Bear Valley gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 13 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
