Pyrenees: is the snow above or below normal? (6 November)

PyreneesSnow vs past wintersPublished Friday 6 November 2026 · 12:08 UTCData of 5 November2 min read

We compared today's snow at 4 resorts in the Pyrenees with the same date in each of the past 46 winters. 1 of them are clearly above normal.

Is it a good start?

At 2,060 m on Aramón Cerler, today's 0 cm is close to normal for the date: deeper than in 17 of the past 46 winters (usual: 1 cm).

This is a good start at Rasos de Peguera. At 1,931 m, our model has 4 cm today, deeper than on this date in 44 of the past 46 winters (usual: 0 cm).

At 2,050 m on Gavarnie-Gèdre, today's 0 cm is close to normal for the date: deeper than in 16 of the past 46 winters (usual: 1 cm).

At 1,900 m on Espot, today's 1 cm is close to normal for the date: deeper than in 19 of the past 46 winters (usual: 1 cm).

Today's snow

Of these resorts, Aramón Cerler has the most snow on its upper slopes today, about 21 cm; then come Rasos de Peguera 20 cm and Gavarnie-Gèdre 11 cm.

26 of 53 resorts in the Pyrenees have snow on their upper slopes today, and our model counts 660 km² of snow-covered ground.

Resort numbers are model estimates for the upper slopes; station numbers are measurements at one point, on the date shown.

The next days

More snow is on the way: by 9 November our forecast gives 2 cm at Gavarnie-Gèdre, 1 cm at Aramón Cerler and 1 cm at Espot.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Tips for skiers

  • Look for snow high up and on north-facing slopes: they keep it longest, while south-facing slopes lose it first in the sun.
  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Aramón Cerler gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 5 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
