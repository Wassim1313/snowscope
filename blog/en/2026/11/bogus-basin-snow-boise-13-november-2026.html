Bogus Basin snow: still bare, a wet weekend, colder next week (13 November 2026)

US WestGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 15:31 UTCData of 12 November3 min read

Boise's home hill is still brown. On our snow map Bogus Basin has no snow on the ground at the base (5,800 ft), at mid-mountain (6,700 ft) or at the summit (7,600 ft). This weekend's storm is a mild one: rain at the lodge and wet snow only near the top. The better news comes later in the week, when colder air brings the snow level down to about 5,000 ft.

Where things stand on the mountain

Bogus Basin is the nonprofit ski area that Boise drives up to after work: on our mapping it has 37 mi (59 km) of runs and 11 lifts over about 1,800 ft of vertical, and it is known for its night skiing. Today there is nothing to ski. The past week was dry at all three heights, and on the snow map the nearest white is far to the north-east, in the higher mountains of central Idaho.

That is about a week behind the usual calendar. In the middle of our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) the first lasting snow arrives at the summit around 3 November, at mid-mountain around 5 November and at the base around 8 November.

This weekend: rain at the lodge, wet snow on top

The storm that reaches southwest Idaho this weekend comes with mild air. The snow level sits between about 6,300 and 6,800 ft, which cuts right through the ski area. At the base, about three quarters of what falls should come as rain. Mid-mountain is on the line, with mixed rain and snow. Only the summit gets a coating: about 2 in (4 cm) on our numbers.

So no, this weekend will not open the season. It will wet the ground, which is not useless: the next snow sticks faster to cold, damp soil than to dry dirt.

Next week: the snow level drops

After the weekend, colder air follows. Over the full seven days the snow level falls to around 5,000 to 5,100 ft, below the base, so later showers fall as snow everywhere on the mountain. Our week totals: 3.5 in (9 cm) at the base, 6 in (16 cm) at mid-mountain and 8 in (21 cm) at the summit. The other forecasts we check go higher, from 8 to 15 in at the summit, so our number is on the careful side.

Eight inches is not a base. In a usual November the summit holds 7 in (17 cm), mid-mountain 6 in (14 cm) and the base 4 in (9 cm); in one November out of ten there is 24 in (62 cm) up top, and in another one out of ten nothing at all. Bogus Basin really comes alive in December, when a middle winter gives 41 in (104 cm) at the summit, 35 in (89 cm) at mid-mountain and 22 in (56 cm) at the base.

After the thinnest winter in our record

Locals will remember last season. In our record, 2025-26 was the thinnest of all 46 winters at every height: the summit peaked at 27 in (68 cm), the base at 13 in (33 cm), and the summit had snow on 105 days against a usual 154.

One poor winter tells you nothing about the next one; the chart below shows how wide the range is from year to year. What it does say is that the date the lifts start matters more than usual this year, so watch the snow level more than the totals.

Tips for skiers

  • Opening day is the ski area's call: follow the snow report and opening news on the Bogus Basin site.
  • The road up from Boise climbs fast and turns icy before the city sees any snow; carry chains or use snow tires once it turns cold.
  • Early-season snow on dirt hides rocks and stumps: wait for packed runs, and read the avalanche forecasts before heading into the backcountry.
