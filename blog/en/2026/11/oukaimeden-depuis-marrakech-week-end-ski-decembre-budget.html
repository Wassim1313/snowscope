Oukaïmeden from Marrakech: a budget December ski weekend

Atlas (Morocco)GuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 13:29 UTCData of 14 November4 min read

Oukaïmeden sits 50 km from Marrakech as the crow flies—close enough for a weekend, high enough to hold snow when the city bakes. But December is a gamble: a typical mid-month brings only 16 cm at mid-mountain, so you must chase fresh snow, not the date.

Why height matters, and why it doesn't

The resort spans from 2600 m at the base to 3258 m at the summit. You would expect the top to be much snowier than lower slopes, but December in the Atlas breaks that rule. On 15 December, a typical winter gives 15 cm at the base, 16 cm at mid-mountain (2929 m) and 17 cm at the top. The difference is barely a centimetre.

This happens because storms matter far more than metres. Snow falls across the whole mountain at once, then melts together between events. Early season, the sun and wind strip the lower slopes faster, so the mid and upper zones stay close in depth. Only from January onward does altitude create a real gap.

The first week of December: a poor bet

On 1 December, mid-mountain usually has 12 cm. That sounds like a start, but 28 % of winters are nearly bare—under 5 cm—on that date. Only 4 % of winters have built a 50 cm base by the first week of December. If you have a free weekend in early December, the mountain is beautiful to walk on (bring warm clothes and boots), but skiing is a lottery.

The first lasting snow typically arrives around 5 November, but November snow often vanishes within days. Plan a December trip only if you can wait for a fresh fall forecast and leave within two or three days.

Mid-December to Christmas: the odds improve

By 15 December, snow depth at mid-mountain climbs to 16 cm in an average winter. More importantly, 43 % of winters now have 25 cm or more—double the share on 1 December. Christmas Day sees 20 cm typical, and 41 % of winters cross the 25 cm line.

Even so, 20 % of Decembers are still nearly bare on the 15th. Only 11 % of winters have a proper 50 cm base by mid-December at mid-mountain. Last winter (2025-26) broke that pattern: 33 cm on 15 December, then a sharp rise to 78 cm by Christmas. That happens roughly one winter in ten.

New Year's Eve: your safest date

By 31 December, the typical depth reaches 24 cm at mid-mountain, and half of all winters have crossed 25 cm. At the summit (3258 m), 20 % of winters have built 50 cm by year-end, compared to just 15 % at mid-mountain. If you must pick a December date, New Year's Eve is statistically your best chance.

Still, no week in December is guaranteed. The pattern across 46 winters shows that only 13 % of years see a full week of average snow at 50 cm or more at mid-mountain. A single good weekend after a storm beats a full week of hoping.

The long view: December has not changed

Over four decades, December has stayed unpredictable. Mid-mountain snow on 15 December averaged 14 cm in winters 1980–81 to 1994–95 and 11 cm in winters 2011–12 to 2025–26. In both eras, about 40 % of winters reached 25 cm by mid-month. December now is as thin and as variable as it was forty years ago.

By March, the picture shifts: snow peaks then, with 53 cm typical at mid-mountain and 58 cm at the summit. If December fails, pushing your trip to late February or March means far higher odds of deep snow.

Logistics and what to pack

Lift passes and equipment rental are settled on the spot—there is no official online tariff. Ask locals or your accommodation for prices before you commit. Transport from Marrakech and a night in the mountains usually make up most of your budget.

Bring warm layers, walking boots (the mountain is worth visiting even without snow), and a headtorch if you drive after dark. The mountain road demands care, especially after a storm. Check road conditions before leaving Marrakech, and drive slowly.

Tips for skiers

  • Watch the forecast for snow in the Atlas, then leave within two or three days of a storm. That single window is more reliable than any calendar date.
  • If early December is bare, do not despair: walk the high terrain, enjoy the views, and plan to return in late February or March when snow is deepest.
  • On the drive up from Marrakech, leave early to reach the mountain in daylight. Mountain roads are steep and narrow, and visibility drops fast after sunset.
  • Pack warm clothes even if snow is thin: the wind is strong above 2600 m, and you can freeze quickly once the sun dips below the ridges.
  • Before renting skis or a snowboard, ask the price on site—there is no official tariff to compare online.

About these numbers
