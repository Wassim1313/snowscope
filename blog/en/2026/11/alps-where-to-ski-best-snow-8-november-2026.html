    50 cm at La Grave: where to ski in the Alps now (8 November)

    AlpsWhere to skiSatellite & 3DPublished Sunday 8 November 2026 · 21:27 UTCData of 8 November2 min read

    50 cm on the upper slopes: that is why La Grave heads our list. We combined today's snow depth, the new snow of the last 72 hours and the forecast to pick La Grave, Les Deux Alpes and Les Grands Montets. Of these resorts, Bonneval-sur-Arc has the most snow on its upper slopes today, about 52 cm; then come La Grave 50 cm and Les Deux Alpes 47 cm.

    Resorts worth a look

    La Grave got 12 cm of new snow in the last 72 hours. Height matters: at La Grave, our model gives 39 cm at the top (3,543 m), 39 cm at mid-mountain (3,369 m) and 32 cm at the base (3,196 m). Satellites saw La Grave snow-covered on 5 November, its last clear day.

    Les Deux Alpes got 8 cm of new snow in the last 72 hours. Height matters: at Les Deux Alpes, our model gives 39 cm at the top (3,511 m), 1 cm at mid-mountain (2,397 m) and 0 cm at the base (1,284 m). Satellites saw Les Deux Alpes snow-covered on 3 November, its last clear day.

    Les Grands Montets got 23 cm of new snow in the last 72 hours. Satellites saw Les Grands Montets snow-covered on 5 November, its last clear day.

    Snow line and slopes

    At La Grave, the freezing level reaches 2,870 m to 3,210 m on 10 November, so the lower slopes may get wet snow while the top stays wintry.

    Look for snow high up and on north-facing slopes: they keep it longest, while south-facing slopes lose it first in the sun.

    Forecast

    More snow is on the way: by 11 November our forecast gives 32 cm at La Grave, 28 cm at Les Deux Alpes and 18 cm at Les Grands Montets.

    Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

    Safety first

    The official avalanche danger level for the La Grave area today is 1 on the 5-level scale (Météo-France BERA).

    Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).

    Tips for skiers

    • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
    • Smaller resorts near the leaders often get the same snow with fewer people: compare them on the map.
    • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
    • Look for snow high up and on north-facing slopes: they keep it longest, while south-facing slopes lose it first in the sun.
    • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when La Grave gets fresh snow.

    About these numbers

    Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 8 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
