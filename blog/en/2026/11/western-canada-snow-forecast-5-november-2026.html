Snow forecast in Western Canada: up to 94 cm at Whistler Blackcomb by 8 November

Western CanadaSnow forecastsPowder alertsPublished Thursday 5 November 2026 · 10:27 UTCData of 5 November2 min read

More snow is on the way: by 8 November our forecast gives 94 cm at Whistler Blackcomb, 93 cm at Ski Callaghan and 70 cm at Tweedsmuir Ski Club. Keep in mind: resort values come from our snow model and describe an area, while stations measure one point.

Height, slopes and snow line

At Whistler Blackcomb, the freezing level reaches 1,690 m to 2,660 m on 7 November, above the top of the resort (2,259 m): rain or wet snow is possible on every slope that day.

At Ski Callaghan, the freezing level reaches 1,570 m to 2,550 m on 7 November, above the top of the resort (998 m): rain or wet snow is possible on every slope that day.

At Tweedsmuir Ski Club, the freezing level reaches 1,130 m to 1,690 m on 5 November, above the top of the resort (1,650 m): rain or wet snow is possible on every slope that day.

Height matters: at Whistler Blackcomb, our model gives 36 cm at the top (2,259 m), 22 cm at mid-mountain (1,456 m) and 0 cm at the base (653 m).

Today's snow

Of these resorts, Mount Cain Alpine Park has the most snow on its upper slopes today, about 75 cm; then come Whistler Blackcomb 65 cm and Tweedsmuir Ski Club 27 cm.

53 of 57 resorts in Western Canada have snow on their upper slopes today, and our model counts 314,170 km² of snow-covered ground.

Tips for skiers

  • Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.
  • Where the freezing level goes above the resort, plan for wet snow and use the highest lifts.
  • Fresh snow is best in the first hours after it stops: go early, before the sun and the crowds.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 5 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
