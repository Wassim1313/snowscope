Oukaïmeden notebook: skiing Morocco's Atlas when the snow comes

Atlas (Morocco)GuidesPublished Sunday 8 November 2026 · 20:53 UTCData of 8 November3 min read

Oukaïmeden is the High Atlas ski area closest to Marrakesh, with slopes between 2,600 m and 3,200 m. It is not an Alpine resort and does not try to be: snow comes and goes with each storm, and a good day here is one you plan for at short notice. These are the notes we would give a friend before a first visit.

Note 1: snow here is a series of storms, not a season

Our snow history covers 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) at three heights of the area, as model values corrected for height. At 2,929 m, the middle of the slopes, a typical winter has snow on the ground for about 138 days, from around 5 November to around 5 May. But the depth is modest and changes a lot from year to year: the deepest snow of a typical winter is about 92 cm, yet the thinnest winter in our record peaked at about 41 cm and the snowiest at about 207 cm.

There is no week in the season with at least 50 cm in 9 winters out of 10. Even the best week, at the end of February, reaches that depth in only about 59 % of winters. In other words: watch the forecast, and go when a storm has just passed.

Note 2: February and March are the best bets

Month by month, a typical winter at 2,929 m has about 18 cm in December, 28 cm in January, 44 cm in February and 53 cm in March, the deepest month, before the sun takes it quickly: about 11 cm in April. At the top, near 3,258 m, the March figure is about 58 cm. Visitors' guides agree that the best skiing in the Atlas is usually found in January to February. Our numbers lean slightly later, towards late February and March.

Note 3: go high and look north

The single chairlift climbs to 3,258 m at the peak of Jebel Attar, and the drag lifts serve the lower slopes; in all, the area has six ski lifts, and the longest run is about 3 km. Higher means more snow: at 2,600 m the typical peak is about 78 cm, at the top about 112 cm. Under the strong Atlas sun, slopes that face north keep their snow for days after a storm, while south-facing ones can turn bare or slushy by early afternoon.

Beginners should stay on the gentle lower slopes when they are covered and avoid them when rocks show. Confident skiers will find the best snow on the upper slopes in the morning, before it softens.

Note 4: what the last winters looked like

Recent winters have been a little thinner than the early ones. In our first fifteen winters from 1980-81, the median peak at mid-height was about 102 cm; in the last fifteen it was about 92 cm, and the snow season shortened from about 145 days to 128. Last winter, 2025-26, was a good one: a peak of about 132 cm and about 149 days with snow.

Note 5: getting there and staying

From Marrakesh, drive towards Setti Fatma in the Ourika valley and take the last exit before Setti Fatma at the foot of the mountain; the road up is paved and in good condition, but after snowfall check the conditions before you leave. Most visitors come for the day from Marrakesh; there are a few places to stay at the resort itself if you want the first lift.

Note 6: safety

Morocco has no regional avalanche bulletin like the European services, so off-piste and ski touring on the surrounding summits are for experienced, equipped people with local knowledge. Follow the official forecasts and weather warnings of the national weather service (DGM), and remember that snow on our map does not mean the lifts are running.

Tips for skiers

  • Plan at short notice: go a day or two after a storm, in February or March if you can choose.
  • Ski the upper, north-facing slopes in the morning, before the sun softens them.
  • Check the DGM weather warnings and the road conditions before driving up from Marrakesh.
  • Off-piste and touring: only with experience, equipment and someone who knows the mountain.
