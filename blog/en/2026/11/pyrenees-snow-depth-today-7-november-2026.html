    Pyrenees snow depth today: Estació d'esquí Port del Comte leads with 29 cm (7 November)

    PyreneesWhere to skiPublished Saturday 7 November 2026 · 10:26 UTCData of 7 November2 min read

    Estació d'esquí Port del Comte (Spain) has the deepest snow in the Pyrenees today, with about 29 cm on its upper slopes. Aramón Cerler follows with 21 cm, then Espace Cambre d'Aze with 21 cm. Our snow model shows 1,650 km² of snow-covered ground in the Pyrenees today, with snow at 37 of 53 resorts.

    At 2,060 m on Aramón Cerler, today's 0 cm is close to normal for the date: deeper than in 16 of the past 46 winters (usual: 1 cm).

    This is a good start at Espace Cambre d'Aze. At 2,004 m, our model has 8 cm today, deeper than on this date in 44 of the past 46 winters (usual: 0 cm).

    The next days

    At Estació d'esquí Port del Comte, the freezing level reaches 3,080 m to 3,720 m on 8 November, above the top of the resort (2,122 m): rain or wet snow is possible on every slope that day.

    Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

    Tips for skiers

    • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
    • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
    • Look for snow high up and on north-facing slopes: they keep it longest, while south-facing slopes lose it first in the sun.
    • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
    • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Estació d'esquí Port del Comte gets fresh snow.

    About these numbers

    Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 7 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
