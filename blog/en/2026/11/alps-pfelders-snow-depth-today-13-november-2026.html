Snow depth in the Alps compared: Pfelders ahead with 133 cm (13 November)

AlpsWhere to skiPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 13:02 UTCData of 13 November2 min read

The snow-covered area has grown from 39,090 km² on 11 November to 76,100 km² today, and our forecast gives 57,910 km² on 16 November: more ground is storing water as snow. Pfelders has the deepest snowpack in the Alps today, about 133 cm up high, followed by Monterosa Ski with 108 cm and Saint-Luc with 98 cm. Our model spreads today's snow over 76,100 km² in the Alps, reaching 829 of 1,179 resorts. Snow depth is only half the story: dense, older snow holds more water than the same depth of new snow.

At Monterosa Ski (2,118 m) the season has not begun: no snow today against a usual 12 cm, a start as dry as only 2 of the past 46 winters.

The next days in the forecast

Until 16 November, the forecast adds 12 cm at Les Arcs, 6 cm at Crans-Montana and 3 cm at Pfelders; amounts several days out can still change.

At Pfelders the freezing level reaches 1,870 m to 2,240 m on 14 November: lower down the snow may turn wet, higher up it stays cold.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Measured snow

The Weißseespitze station (3,499 m) reported a snow depth of 264 cm on 13 November. Measured, not modelled: the Sonnblick station (3,109 m) recorded 55 cm of snow on the ground on 13 November, with 23 cm of new snow in 24 hours. The Lanzada Passo Marinelli station (3,032 m) reported a snow depth of 52 cm on 13 November, 19 cm of it from the last 24 hours.

A note on measurement: resort values are modelled for the upper slopes, station values are measured at one place on the date given, and both carry some uncertainty.

Tips for skiers

  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
  • Shade protects the snowpack. High, north-facing slopes get less sun, so their snow lasts longer than on the south side.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Pfelders gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 13 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
