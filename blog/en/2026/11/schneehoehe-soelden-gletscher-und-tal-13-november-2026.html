Sölden snow depth: 9 cm on the glacier, the valley still green (13 November 2026)

AlpsGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 13:02 UTCData of 12 November3 min read

If you are looking up the snow depth in Sölden this week, the answer depends on where you stand. Up at the glacier end of the ski area, at 3245 m, our snow map gives about 9 cm on the ground today. Around the middle stations at 2295 m it is about 5 cm. Down in the village at 1346 m the meadows are still green. In November two seasons share one valley here, and this year both are running late.

Two heights, two seasons

Sölden lives on its vertical: 1899 m between the lowest and the highest mapped lift. In mid-November that number matters more than any other. The glacier road leads to the only slopes where it is really winter now. They took 18 cm in the last seven days, 8 cm of it in the last 24 hours, and sun and wind have already pressed that down to 9 cm.

At mid-mountain the week brought 10 cm, and a weather station at that height measures the same thin layer we show. The village has nothing on the ground and saw nothing fall this week. If you arrive now, you drive up through autumn to reach winter.

This week: snow above about 1620 m

The next seven days look more useful. We expect about 25 cm at the top, 19 cm at mid-mountain and 9 cm at valley height, with only 3 cm up high in the first 24 hours, so most of it comes later in the week. Other forecasts we compare with range from 14 to 40 cm at the top, so take 25 cm as the middle of the road, not a promise.

The snow line sits around 1620 m. In the village most of it will fall as rain or wet sleet, and only the upper half of the mountain gains a real layer.

How far behind a usual November?

In our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26), a usual November holds about 75 cm at the top, 29 cm at mid-mountain and 7 cm in the valley. Even a lean November, one winter in ten, keeps around 21 cm at the top. Today's 9 cm is below that, so by Sölden's own standard this is a slow start. December usually climbs to 140 cm on the glacier and 62 cm at mid-mountain; that is the month to watch.

The good news: the top of Sölden has not lost snow over the decades. The peak of a middle winter was 349 cm in the first 15 of our winters and 349 cm in the last 15. Mid-mountain slipped from 170 to 146 cm and the valley from 53 to 44 cm. Height is Sölden's insurance.

When the whole mountain turns white

At mid-mountain, 17 weeks of the winter have 50 cm or more in 9 of 10 winters; on the glacier it is 26 weeks. The deepest snow at the top usually comes in April (277 cm), at mid-mountain in March (133 cm). Last winter was thin: it peaked at 203 cm at the top, 110 cm at mid-mountain and 37 cm in the valley, well under a middle winter's 343, 152 and 48 cm. The mapped ski area counts 103 km of runs and 39 lifts.

Tips for skiers

  • Plan November ski days on the glacier slopes only: the runs down to the village need many more snowfalls.
  • Expect rain in the village this week; above about 1620 m the glacier road needs winter tyres and chains in the car.
  • New snow on windy glacier slopes builds slabs: read the Tyrol avalanche report before you leave the piste, and keep out of crevassed terrain without a guide.
