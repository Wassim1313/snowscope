Where should you ski in the US West this weekend? Telluride, Wolf Creek and Purgatory Resort

US WestWhere to skiSatellite & 3DPublished Wednesday 11 November 2026 · 18:44 UTCData of 11 November2 min read

Where should you go skiing in the US West? Three inputs, one shortlist: today's snow depth, the new snow of the last 72 hours and the forecast point to Telluride, Wolf Creek and Purgatory Resort. Top of this list: Wolf Creek, about 34 in (87 cm) up high. Then Telluride 34 in (86 cm) and Purgatory Resort 22 in (57 cm). Confidence: good for the trend, lower for any single number.

The forecast in short

Next round by 14 November: 4 in (9 cm) at Telluride, 3 in (8 cm) at Wolf Creek and 5 in (12 cm) at Purgatory Resort, according to our forecast.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Snow line check

On 11 November the freezing level at Telluride sits at 11,483 ft (3,500 m) to 13,484 ft (4,110 m), over the top of the resort (12,507 ft / 3,812 m): rain or wet snow could fall on all slopes.

A rule of thumb that is rarely wrong: high and north-facing keeps snow, south-facing loses it first.

Avalanche danger and safety

The official rating for the Telluride area is 1 on the 5-level scale today (Colorado Avalanche Information Center).

Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).

Tips for skiers

  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Small ski areas next to the big names often get the same storm and shorter lift lines. Check them on the map first.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
  • Satellite images show it every winter: snow stays longest high up and on north-facing slopes, and goes first on south-facing ones.
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Telluride gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 11 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
