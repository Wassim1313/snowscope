Ruka in December: a EUR 265 ski week, and which week to pick

ScandinaviaGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 16:44 UTCData of 14 November3 min read

A 6-day adult pass at Ruka costs EUR 265 in the 2026-27 season, one of the lowest fixed prices for a week away. The snow is trickier: early December is unreliable, but from mid-month onwards the odds shift sharply in your favour, peaking at 85% of winters with 30 cm or more during Christmas week.

When to go for the best snow odds

Ruka's December snow builds through the month. In the first week (1-7 December) only 39% of winters reached 30 cm. By 8-14 December that climbs to 61%, and by mid-month (15-21 December) you are at 72%. Christmas week (22-28 December) is safest: 85% of winters had 30 cm or more by then.

The usual first day to find 30 cm on the ground is 5 December, but this varies year to year. If early December is your only option, check the snow map before you book and prepare for thin cover.

Depth and thickness through the month

On a typical 15 December, Ruka holds 36 cm across the whole ski area. By 24 December that rises to 40 cm, and by New Year's Eve you are at 46 cm. These are the medians from 46 winters of history. In a thin year (1 in 10), expect only 20 cm on 15 December; in a deep year, 46 cm.

What you will not find here is powder depth. Only 4% of Christmas weeks reach 60 cm or more. What Ruka gives instead is consistent, cold, groomed snow that stays through the season. In 46 winters, the ground has never been bare on 15 December.

Height makes almost no difference

Ruka is a low, rounded fell with 24 lifts spread across 25 km of runs between 286 m and 494 m. Because the terrain is smooth and not very high, our snow history treats it as one zone. The top is not measurably snowier than the base.

This uniformity is typical for Lapland fells. Far north, even a warmer winter stays cold enough to keep a base. The real driver of snow depth is latitude and time of year, not altitude on these gentle slopes.

Cost and timing for a budget week

The EUR 265 price for 6 days is fixed, with no discounts through the season. The same ticket list applies at Pyhä-Luosto, 50 km to the north-west, which also offers 30 cm by mid-December in 72% of winters. The saving lies not in a cheaper ticket but in choosing dates outside the Christmas holidays, when accommodation is easier and sometimes less dear.

A week from 15-21 December, or 8-14 December if you are flexible, can still find good value on a bed while keeping snow odds well above a coin flip.

What to pack and expect

December in Finnish Lapland is dark. By mid-month the sun barely rises, and for part of the month it does not clear the horizon at all. The light turns blue and pink for a few hours in the afternoon, and much skiing happens in twilight or dim daylight.

The cold is dry and penetrating. Bring a good headlamp for walks between runs, warm mittens, face protection, and proper insulated boots. The quiet of the fells and the strange light make December skiing here unlike anywhere else.

Tips for skiers

  • Book a week from 15 December on: 72% of winters had 30 cm that week, against only 39% in the first week of December.
  • Christmas week (22-28 Dec) is the safest bet; 85% of winters reached 30 cm or more. Plan for a full week if possible to spread the value of your EUR 265 ticket.
  • Budget EUR 265 for the lift pass, then focus on finding affordable accommodation before the peak Christmas rush.
  • Pack cold-weather gear suited to 46-48°N at sea level in winter; the cold is severe but dry. A headlamp is essential because daylight is minimal.
  • Check the SnowScope map a few days before you travel; December can still surprise, and thin cover in early month may hide rocks.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes on the date shown; they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
