58 cm of New Snow Forecast for La Grave by Saturday

Snow regionsSnow forecastsPublished Wednesday 11 November 2026 · 18:44 UTCData of 11 November3 min read

Our snow model predicts significant snowfall across the Alps and parts of North America. La Grave in France is expected to receive the most, with 58 cm of new snow by Saturday, November 14.

Alps: Heavy Snowfall Expected

The French Alps are set to see the heaviest snow this week. La Grave is forecast to get 58 cm of new snow over the next 72 hours. Nearby Les Deux Alpes could see 51 cm. These amounts are estimated for the upper slopes, with the snow line expected around 2900 m for La Grave and 2600 m for Les Deux Alpes by Saturday.

Italian resorts also show good prospects. Ponte di Legno is forecast to receive 44 cm of new snow, and Livigno 42 cm, both by Saturday. In Switzerland, Diavolezza-Lagalb could get 40 cm. These figures represent the total accumulation over the next three days, as estimated by our snow model on November 11.

US West and Northeast: Snow on the Way

In the US West, Mt. Shasta Ski Park in California is forecast to receive 17 in (43 cm) of new snow by Saturday. Kirkwood, also in California, is expected to get 15 in (39 cm). The snow line for these areas is predicted to be high, around 7,200 ft (2200 m) for Mt. Shasta Ski Park and 9,200 ft (2800 m) for Kirkwood.

The US Northeast will also see some fresh snow. Okemo Mountain in Vermont is forecast to get 15 in (38 cm) of new snow by Saturday. Most of this snow is expected to fall over the next 72 hours, with 3 in (8 cm) coming in the next 24 hours. The snow line here is much lower, around 1,000 ft (300 m).

Western Canada: Whistler Blackcomb Leads

Western Canada is also expecting new snow. Whistler Blackcomb is forecast to receive 34 cm of new snow by Saturday, with 16 cm expected in the next 24 hours. The snow line for Whistler Blackcomb is estimated to be around 2400 m.

Mount Cain Alpine Park is also forecast to get 22 cm of new snow by Saturday. These amounts are based on our snow model run on November 11 and represent the estimated snowfall on the upper slopes.

Understanding Snow Forecasts

Snowfall forecasts, especially early in the season, can change. The amounts we report are estimates from our snow model for the upper slopes of each resort. The actual snow depth can vary locally due to wind and terrain.

Early season snow can be thin, and rocks may be hidden just below the surface. Always be aware of conditions and potential hazards. The snow line indicates the approximate height where snow is expected to fall and accumulate, roughly 300 m below the freezing level.

Tips for skiers

  • For the Alps, focus on higher elevations, especially above 2900 m in the French Alps, where the snow line will be highest.
  • In the US West, the best snow will be at higher altitudes, above 7,200 ft (2200 m), particularly in California.
  • In the US Northeast, Okemo Mountain offers the most significant new snow forecast, with a lower snow line around 1,000 ft (300 m).
  • Always check the official avalanche bulletin if you plan to go off-piste.
  • Remember that early season conditions mean thin cover; ski with caution.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 11 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
