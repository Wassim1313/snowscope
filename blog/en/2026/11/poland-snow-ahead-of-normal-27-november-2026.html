Poland: Karpacz holds three times the normal snow for late November

Europe snow by countryPublished Friday 27 November 2026 · 12:54 UTCData of 27 November3 min read

Polish ski areas have built snow cover far faster than usual this year. Karpacz, in the Karkonosze, holds 31 cm—nearly three times what a normal 27 November would bring—following heavy snow across all three main mountain ranges in the past 72 hours.

Three times the usual depth at this date

Karpacz sits at 31 cm today, which is 282 percent of the typical depth for this date in past winters. The mountain has gained 6 cm in the past three days. This early-season surplus matters: snow accumulation now means more water stored in the mountains as we head toward winter. At this pace, the Karkonosze are tracking toward a strong season.

Across Poland's ski areas, the story is similar. The highest peaks—above 1500 m—already hold 27 cm on average. Even mid-elevation slopes between 1000 m and 1500 m carry 11 cm, with continuous cover. The snow line sits at 500 m, so nearly all skiable terrain is white.

How much is unusually early

One week ago, only 720 km² of Poland was snow-covered. Today, 11,060 km² lies under snow—a leap of 10,340 km². This jump in a few days shows how much weather changed between late November 20 and now. The snowiest day in this sequence was 26 November, when several areas received up to 11 cm in 72 hours.

Where the mountains are deepest

The Tatras and the Karkonosze lead with 10 cm of new snow in three days. Both ranges sit higher and catch moisture well. Kasprowy Wierch, at 1958 m in the Tatras, now holds 35 cm—the deepest anywhere in Poland. The Beskids trail slightly with 8 cm added over 72 hours but still sit ahead of normal. Karpacz at 1350 m (31 cm) outpaces the usual for this date by a wide margin, while lower resorts like Hajduk, Sami Swoi and Stacja narciarska Suche all hold 24 cm, up 6–7 cm in three days.

What comes next

The pace will slow. SnowScope shows only 1 cm of new snow likely in the next three days and 2 cm through seven days ahead. The snowiest single day in the next week is forecast for 4 December, bringing 2 cm. Early December will see light snow at best. The mountains will not build depth quickly again until a larger weather system arrives, so the current cover will settle and consolidate over the coming weeks.

Snow by elevation: depth grows with height

The pattern is clear and typical for mountains. Between 500 m and 1000 m, the mean depth is 7 cm but cover is complete. From 1000 m to 1500 m, 11 cm lies across the board. Above 1500 m, depths jump to 27 cm. This is how mountain snow storage works: higher, colder elevations trap and hold moisture. Skiers heading up will find thicker, more stable bases as they climb.

Seven days of steady snow

The past week has been unusually consistent. Kasprowy Wierch has held the lead throughout, moving between 33 cm and 36 cm as fresh falls arrived and older snow settled. On 24 November, 9 cm arrived in three days; on 26 November, 11 cm fell. Even the smaller swings—7–8 cm—kept all major resorts building cover. This steady rhythm has let water accumulate in the mountains at an early-season pace that is rare.

Tips for skiers

  • Check the official avalanche bulletin before any off-piste skiing, especially on steep slopes above 1500 m where snow is thickest and new layers are still settling.
  • Early-season snow can hide rocks and dense brush. Stay on marked terrain at resorts with 24–31 cm until the base thickens further.
  • Head to higher resorts like Kasprowy Wierch (35 cm) or Karpacz (31 cm) for the most reliable cover over the next 7–10 days.
  • Expect light snow only through early December; use the next week to ski the depth you have rather than wait for new snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes on the date shown; they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
