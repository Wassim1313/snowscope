Oulu snow depth: from bare to 7 cm overnight (13 November 2026)

ScandinaviaGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 13:29 UTCData of 13 November4 min read

Oulu saw its first real snow cover of the season arrive overnight on 13 November. The city station measured 7 cm—well above the typical depth for mid-November—after bare ground just 24 hours before. The snow came as part of a wider event that touched the whole coast of the Bothnian Bay.

A sudden change across the coast

The snow did not fall on Oulu alone. Hailuoto island to the northwest measured 5 cm on the same morning, while Siikajoki Ruukki inland recorded 8 cm. These stations had bare ground on 12 November; by Friday they all held fresh cover. The uniformity across this 40 km stretch—no two readings far apart—shows a steady snow event that swept the region in a single pulse overnight.

This kind of sudden shift from brown to white is typical for Finnish autumn. The conditions align: cold air moves in, moisture is ready, and within hours the ground changes from bare to blanketed. For anyone who had not checked the forecast, Friday morning brought a surprise.

Above normal, but arrival is late

Oulu's snow history spans 46 past winters. Snow on the ground on 13 November is common—it lay there in 33 of those winters—but it is usually thin. The middle value is 2 cm, and only one winter in four reaches 5 cm or more. Today's 7 cm sits in the top quarter for this date, well above the median.

Yet the timing is worth noting. Half of past winters had lasting snow cover by 28 October; this year, it arrived two weeks later. So Oulu gained a substantial depth, but the season itself started slowly. The highest depth ever recorded on 13 November was 24 cm, so there is room for far deeper snow in mid-November if conditions are cold enough.

Comparisons with recent seasons

In the past five seasons, Oulu on 13 November has seen: 1 cm (2021–22), bare ground (2022–23), 5 cm (2023–24), bare ground (2024–25) and 1 cm (2025–26). This year's 7 cm is the highest of that sequence—not a record, but a solid start. The variation shows how unpredictable early November snow can be in this region.

What matters more is the shape of winter ahead. By 1 December, the typical depth here is 9 cm. By mid-December, 14 cm. By mid-January, about 27 cm. So the 7 cm that fell on 13 November is the foundation; the real accumulation builds through December and into the new year.

What the next few days will add

Friday brings around 2 cm more snow, with temperatures just below zero. Saturday stays cold, around -3 °C at its lowest, and mostly dry. Sunday warms slightly, reaching about 3 °C, with little fresh snow. By Monday, rain moves in—between 2 and 8 mm depending on exact timing—and temperatures climb to 1 to 4 °C.

Early-season snow is fragile. It often sits loose on bare ground or moss, without a firm base beneath. Rain will soak into it, bond it together, and then freeze it again when cold returns. The result is likely to be crust and slush rather than powder, but it will not vanish entirely this week.

What this means for the week ahead

Saturday is the best window for snow that feels fresh and clean. Temperatures stay below zero, and no rain has fallen yet. By Sunday afternoon, things soften. Monday's rain will mark a shift: the cover will thin, and waterlogged snow will cling to shoes and clothes.

This first substantial snow tells little about how deep or long-lasting the winter will be. But it marks a real change from autumn to the quieter, colder season. After two weeks of waiting since late October, Oulu's winter has finally begun.

Tips for skiers

  • Saturday is your day: temperatures stay around -3 °C, fresh snow does not accumulate much more, and rain has not yet started. Walk on paths and riverside routes in the morning light.
  • Fresh snow hides the first ice patches on bridges and near water. Test each step, especially where shade pools beneath trees.
  • Monday's rain will turn the new cover to slush. Wear waterproof shoes for commuting; ordinary canvas or leather will soak through.
  • Early snow on bare moss compacts and ices easily. If you cycle or run outdoors, choose high-visibility gear and reduce speed on slopes and bends.
  • By next week, check for official avalanche bulletins if you plan to walk or ski in hills or forest: even shallow snow can slide on steep terrain when soaked by rain and then frozen again.
