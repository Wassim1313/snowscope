Sillian ski map: where the snow is on the Hochpustertal slopes (13 November 2026)

AlpsGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 14:31 UTCData of 12 November3 min read

If you search for a Sillian ski map, you get the lifts and the runs. What it will not tell you is where the snow actually lies today. So we laid our snow map over the Hochpustertal: on 13 November the white starts high on the mountain, with 8.6 cm at the top (2399 m), almost nothing at mid-mountain (1739 m) and green grass at the bottom station by the village (1080 m).

The ski area on the map

Sillian sits on the floor of the upper Drau valley in East Tyrol, a few minutes from the Italian border. The lifts climb from the edge of the village up to the Thurntaler, and on our mapping the ski area covers 24 km of runs and 7 lifts between 1080 m and 2399 m, a drop of 1319 m (OpenSkiMap figures, not the resort's own count).

On the snow map the pin marked Hochpustertal sits on the ski area. Lienz is the town to the east, down the valley; San Candido, Innichen in German, is the first town across the border to the west. The grey band that joins them is the valley floor, where no snow lies yet.

What the colours say today

Blue means snow, from 1 cm in the palest shade to 250 cm in the darkest. Around Sillian the blue begins on the upper slopes: our numbers give 8.6 cm at the top, most of it new, with 7 cm in the last 24 hours and 13 cm over the past week. Mid-mountain has a trace, 0.7 cm, and the bottom station is bare.

The darker blue to the north is higher ground, the Villgraten mountains; the patchy blue south of the valley is the Carnic side, where the snow is thinner today.

This week, a line across the mountain

The coming days bring mixed rain and snow. At the top, the forecasts we check give between 10 and 22 cm over the week, and 4 to 5 cm of that in the next two days. Below about 1,800 to 2,000 m the same weather falls as rain.

That line runs straight across the ski area. Mid-mountain, at 1739 m, sits just under it: our number there is 13 cm for the week, but in the references more than half of what falls at that height comes as rain, so expect much less on the ground. The village end of the map stays grey, with 5 cm at most and rain most of the time.

When the map usually fills in

In the middle of our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) the top of the ski area has 36 cm in November, mid-mountain 9 cm and the bottom 1 cm. The first lasting snow usually reaches the top around 17 September, mid-mountain around 8 October and the valley station around 1 November, so the lower half is running late this year.

December fills the map: a usual December has 74 cm at the top, 32 cm at mid-mountain and 13 cm at the bottom. The lowest runs never get deep here, 26 cm in a usual February, and last winter the valley station had only 79 days with snow against a usual 152. The top is a safer bet: 50 cm or more for 19 weeks in 9 winters out of 10, and in week 13, at the end of March, every one of our winters had that much, with a middle depth of 147 cm.

Tips for skiers

  • Read a ski map and a snow map together: the runs are drawn every year, the snow is not.
  • Early in the season, the upper runs open first; plan on riding the lift back down to Sillian.
  • Before going off-piste, read the Euregio Tyrol avalanche bulletin, which covers East Tyrol.
