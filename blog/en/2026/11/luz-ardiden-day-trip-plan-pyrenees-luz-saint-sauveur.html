A day at Luz-Ardiden: a small Pyrenees ski trip from Luz-Saint-Sauveur, planned hour by hour

PyreneesGuidesPublished Wednesday 11 November 2026 · 20:08 UTCData of 11 November3 min read

Luz-Ardiden is not a resort you move into for a week. It is a mountain you drive up for the day, from Luz-Saint-Sauveur or further down the valley, ski hard and come back down for dinner. Here is that day, step by step, with the snow of 46 past winters from our model at 1,690 m, 2,087 m and 2,485 m.

The evening before: choose the day

A day trip lives or dies on the weather, so pick the day late. Read the Météo-France mountain forecast and the avalanche bulletin for the Pyrenees, and look at the road. In our record the best single week here falls in mid-February: at mid-mountain, 2,087 m, it had at least 50 cm in about 85 % of winters, with a usual depth of about 103 cm.

Morning: the road up

The main road into Luz-Saint-Sauveur is the D 921, the gorges road. From the village, the D 12 climbs about 12 km to the ski area, the same climb the Tour de France has used since 1985; the bike stage covers 14.7 km and 1,010 m of ascent. In winter that means hairpins and, on snowy mornings, chains or winter tyres. Leave early: the car park and the first lifts reward the people who arrive first.

First runs: Béderet or Aulian

The area has two linked sectors, Béderet, at about 1,680 m, and Aulian, at about 1,730 m, with 26 pistes in all: 4 green, 4 blue, 13 red and 5 black. It is a red-run mountain at heart. Start on the side that catches the morning sun if the snow is hard, and move to shadier slopes as it softens. The skiing reaches about 2,500 m at the Col de la Cloze.

Midday: why the top matters

The higher you ski, the more snow you get. In a typical winter the snow peaks at about 102 cm at 1,690 m, about 139 cm at 2,087 m and about 182 cm at 2,485 m. In February, the deepest month, the usual depth at mid-mountain is about 99 cm; in March about 90 cm and in April about 49 cm. In spring, spend the afternoon higher up, where the snow lasts.

Afternoon: last runs and the drive down

Stop before the light goes flat and your legs go soft; the drive down the hairpins needs attention too. Back in Luz-Saint-Sauveur there are the Saint-Sauveur thermal baths and the Pont Napoléon over the Gave. Gavarnie is about half an hour away and Barèges about twenty minutes, if you want a different mountain the next day.

Which weeks to choose

Snow comes in storms here, so years differ a lot: at 2,087 m the thinnest of our winters peaked at about 59 cm and the deepest at about 234 cm. In half of our winters there were at least 50 cm at mid-mountain for 15 weeks, from about late January to early April. The good news is stability: the median peak at mid-mountain was about 141 cm both in our first fifteen winters and in the last fifteen, although the days with snow on the ground fell from about 182 to 162. Last winter, 2025-26, was generous, with a peak of about 167 cm. The area first opened on 16 January 1975; check the official website for this season's lifts and dates.

Tips for skiers

  • Choose the day late, after the Météo-France mountain forecast and the avalanche bulletin.
  • Carry chains or fit winter tyres for the hairpins of the D 12.
  • February is the deepest month; late January to early April is the usual good window.
  • Snow on our map does not mean a resort is open: check the lifts on the official website.
