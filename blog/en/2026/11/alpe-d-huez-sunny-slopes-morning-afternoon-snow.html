Alpe d'Huez and the sun: what to ski in the morning, at noon and after lunch

AlpsGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 11:38 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

Alpe d'Huez is known as a sunny resort, and the reason is simple: most of its slopes face south. That is a gift on a cold January day and a challenge in March. The trick is to ski the mountain by the clock, moving with the sun rather than against it.

Why the sun changes everything here

A slope that faces south takes the sun from mid-morning to late afternoon. Overnight it freezes hard; by late morning it softens; by mid-afternoon, late in the season, it can turn heavy and wet. The ski area is large, about 249 km of pistes and 84 lifts, rising to Pic Blanc at 3,330 m, so there is always a better choice somewhere if you know where to look. The other key is height: the sun works on every slope, but the deep cold snow of the high mountain resists it far longer.

9 am: start high, while the snow is firm

First lifts are the moment for the upper mountain. In our model of 46 past winters, the top of the area, at 3,306 m, holds a usual peak of about 440 cm, and the snow there keeps building until April, when a typical winter has about 372 cm. Up high, a cold morning gives firm, grippy snow that suits fast, clean turns.

11 am: drop to mid-mountain

As the sun climbs, the middle of the mountain becomes the best place to be. At 2,215 m a typical winter has about 62 cm of snow in December, 110 cm in January, 150 cm in February and 154 cm in March, and there are at least 50 cm for 18 weeks in 9 winters out of 10. In mid-winter these slopes stay pleasant until lunch; in March they soften into easy spring snow from late morning, which many intermediates find the most enjoyable skiing of the week.

2 pm: choose the cool side, or stop

After lunch the south faces near the resort can become slow and rutted, especially from March. Look for runs that turn away from the sun or for higher ground, and keep the lowest slopes for the morning. At 1,124 m, the lowest point of our record near the linked villages of Vaujany, Oz, Villard-Reculas and Auris, the usual peak is only about 52 cm and in no week are 50 cm there in 9 winters out of 10. Snowmaking helps, but low runs are a morning affair in a mild spell.

Month by month, the clock moves

In December and January the sun is low and the snow stays cold for most of the day, so timing matters little. From February the window shortens, and by April the pattern is clear: ski high early, follow the softening snow down and finish before the slush. A typical season at mid-mountain sees its first snow around 18 October and its last around 9 June.

The long view is mixed. Our median peak at 2,215 m is slightly higher in the last fifteen winters, about 200 cm, than in the first fifteen, about 186 cm, but the days with snow on the ground fell from about 231 to 210. At the top the median peak dropped from about 452 to 405 cm. Last winter was above normal at mid-mountain, with a peak of about 211 cm.

Beyond the slopes

The resort has a sporting history away from skiing too: it hosted the bobsleigh events of the 1968 Winter Olympics, and its road with 21 numbered hairpins has been a Tour de France finish since 1952. In winter, check road conditions on the climb from Le Bourg-d'Oisans before you drive.

Tips for skiers

  • Ski the top first, mid-mountain at late morning, and the cool or high slopes after lunch.
  • In March and April, plan a short afternoon: the south faces soften early.
  • Low runs near the linked villages are best in the morning; check them on the official website.
  • Off-piste: read the Météo-France avalanche bulletin; warming in the afternoon raises the risk on sunny slopes.
