Mt. Baker in December: the snowiest cheap-ticket road trip in Washington

US WestGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 13:07 UTCData of 14 November3 min read

If you only get one December ski trip in the Northwest, point the car up the Mount Baker Highway. In our snow history of 46 winters, mid-mountain at Mt. Baker had at least 20 in (50 cm) of snow on 15 December in 93 % of them, and an off-peak adult ticket is $98.80, bought at the window on the day.

The last town is Glacier, and then it gets white

Mt. Baker is not a resort town. You drive east from Bellingham, fill up in Glacier, and climb to a base at about 3,630 ft (1,106 m). The mapped terrain is compact: 10 lifts and about 14 mi (23 km) of runs, topping out near 5,100 ft (1,556 m). Low numbers on paper, but this corner of the North Cascades collects snow like few places in the Lower 48.

In a typical winter on our history, lasting snow arrives at mid-mountain in late October. By the first days of December the lodge is usually already in a real winter.

What a typical December looks like

On 1 December, the middle winter of our record has 31 in (79 cm) at mid-mountain, about 4,370 ft (1,331 m). By 15 December that grows to 28 in (72 cm) at the base, 46 in (116 cm) at mid-mountain and 69 in (176 cm) at the top. By Christmas Day, mid-mountain sits at 57 in (145 cm).

The week-by-week picture is just as steady. The share of winters with good snow (a weekly mean of 20 in, 50 cm, or more) at mid-mountain is 85 % in the first week of December, 91 % in the second, 98 % in the third and 100 % in the week of Christmas.

The ticket: old-school, and cheaper Monday to Thursday

Mt. Baker keeps things simple. Adult tickets (ages 16-59) are $115.42 on peak days, Friday to Sunday and holidays, and $98.80 off-peak, Monday to Thursday outside holidays, tax not included. There is no online sale, no pre-purchase and no multi-day ticket: you buy on the day.

That makes the budget move obvious. A Tuesday or Wednesday trip saves you the gap between the two prices and also gives you an emptier parking lot and a calmer drive up the highway.

How it stacks up against White Pass and Timberline

White Pass, south of Mount Rainier, is the price challenger: its online adult ticket starts at $69 off-peak and $89 on peak days, but climbs as days sell, up to $99 and $119. Its mid-mountain had 40 in (102 cm) on a typical 15 December, with 20 in (50 cm) or more in 87 % of winters.

Timberline on Mt. Hood has a deeper mid-mountain on paper, 52 in (132 cm) on a typical 15 December, but reached 20 in (50 cm) in only 78 % of winters, and a full-day adult ticket there is $129 off-peak and $161 on peak days. For snow certainty per dollar in early December, Mt. Baker is hard to beat.

The honest part: lean years happen

A lean winter, the kind you get one year in ten, still leaves about 25 in (63 cm) at mid-mountain on 15 December. Last winter was one of the thin ones: 16 in (42 cm) at mid-mountain on that date.

Recent winters also start a little slower than the old ones. In the first 15 winters of our record (1980-81 to 1994-95), the middle value on 15 December was 52 in (131 cm) at mid-mountain, and every one of them had 20 in or more. In the last 15 winters (2011-12 to 2025-26), it was 41 in (104 cm), with 87 % above 20 in. Still a very good bet, just not a blank check.

Tips for skiers

  • Plan a Monday-to-Thursday trip: it is the cheaper ticket and the quieter day.
  • Bring a card or cash and buy at the window: tickets are sold on the day only.
  • Carry chains or use snow tires on the Mount Baker Highway, and check the road report before leaving Glacier.
  • Aim for mid-December or later: by the third week, nearly every past winter had good snow at mid-mountain.
  • Going beyond the ropes? Read the avalanche forecast first: deep early snow can be unstable.
