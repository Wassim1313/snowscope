Ischgl snow depth: thin on the Silvretta ridge, up to 42 cm this week (13 November 2026)

AlpsGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 12:40 UTCData of 12 November3 min read

In Ischgl you do not ski in the village but on a high ridge above it, where the Paznaun valley meets the Swiss border and Samnaun sits on the other side. In mid-November that ridge is where to look for snow. Our map today shows about 5 cm at the top of the ski area (2868 m), about 4 cm at mid-mountain (2110 m) and nothing at valley level (1352 m). A thin start, but a snowy week is on its way: about 42 cm up high.

A thin layer on the ridge

The last seven days brought 17 cm to the top and 9 cm to mid-mountain, 9 cm and 3 cm of it in the last 24 hours. Wind on an open ridge does not leave early snow alone, and only about 5 cm is lying up there now. The valley has had no snow at all this week.

The week: rain in the Paznaun, snow above about 1570 m

For the next seven days we expect about 42 cm at the top, 31 cm at mid-mountain and 19 cm at valley height, with about 5 cm up high in the first 24 hours. Other forecasts we compare with give 20 to 41 cm at the top and 16 to 35 cm at mid-mountain, so our number sits at the generous end; 30 cm on the ridge is a fair bet.

The snow line is around 1570 m. In Ischgl itself expect mostly rain, and white ground only starts a few hundred metres up the slopes.

Valley and ridge: two different trends

Our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) show why the lifts climb away from the village so fast. At valley height, the peak of a middle winter fell from 90 cm in the first 15 winters to 65 cm in the last 15. On the ridge the loss is smaller: from 329 to 303 cm at the top, from 205 to 179 cm at mid-mountain.

A usual November holds 6 cm in the valley, 24 cm at mid-mountain and 67 cm at the top. Today's cover is below that at all three heights.

When Ischgl is deep

December usually takes the top to 128 cm and mid-mountain to 61 cm, January to 170 and 95 cm. At mid-mountain 18 weeks of the winter have 50 cm or more in 9 of 10 winters, at the top 26 weeks; the surest week at mid-mountain is week 13, at the end of March. Up top the snow usually lasts until about 30 June.

Last winter was below the middle: peaks of 221 cm at the top, 147 cm at mid-mountain and 64 cm in the valley, against 313, 184 and 74 cm in a middle winter. The mapped area across both countries counts 163 km of runs and 46 lifts.

Tips for skiers

  • Judge Ischgl by the ridge, not the valley: the village stays green long after the top has turned white.
  • Wait for the resort's opening date and piste report: 5 cm on the ridge is not a base yet.
  • Wind-blown new snow on the border ridge forms slabs: read the Tyrol avalanche report before any off-piste plan, and the Swiss SLF bulletin on the Samnaun side.
