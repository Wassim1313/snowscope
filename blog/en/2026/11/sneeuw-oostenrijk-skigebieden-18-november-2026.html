    Where is the best snow in Austria? Kaprun, 59 cm on the upper slopes (18 November)

    AlpsPublished Wednesday 18 November 2026 · 11:39 UTCData of 18 November1 min read

    Kaprun has 59 cm on its upper slopes this Wednesday, the most in Austria.

    Across all 12 resorts on this list, the middle value is 31 cm on the upper slopes.

    Behind the leader: Sölden (46 cm), Hintertuxer Gletscher (43 cm), Obergurgl-Hochgurgl (37 cm) and Hochzillertal-Hochfügen (31 cm).

    Sölden reports 46 cm up top; the lifts reach 3,247 m. Fresh snow: 11 cm in three days, forecast 16 cm by 21 November.

    Hintertuxer Gletscher reports 43 cm up top; the lifts reach 3,245 m. Fresh snow: 10 cm in three days, forecast 12 cm by 21 November.

    Obergurgl-Hochgurgl reports 37 cm up top; the lifts reach 3,024 m. Fresh snow: 10 cm in three days, forecast 14 cm by 21 November.

    Fresh snow, last 72 hours

    The last three days brought Ischgl 14 cm, Serfaus Fiss Ladis 13 cm and Sölden 11 cm.

    The next three days

    Coming up by 21 November: Ischgl 22 cm, Serfaus Fiss Ladis 19 cm and Sölden 16 cm.

    Pick for a short trip

    Best value for two days on skis: Sölden, with 46 cm now and 16 cm more expected by 21 November.

    For a weekend, the German uplands are within a day trip by car; for Austria, plan at least three nights.

    Beyond the groomers

    Off the groomers the snow is old and firm for now. For touring, the upper slopes with 59 cm give the best base; check the avalanche bulletin before you go.

    The snow you find now is mostly the snow of the last storm, so height and north-facing slopes matter most.

    Tips for skiers

    • Early season: wax your skis, the snow is often abrasive.
    • Off-piste only after reading the avalanche bulletin.

    About these numbers

    Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 18 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
