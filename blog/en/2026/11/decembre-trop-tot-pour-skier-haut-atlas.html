Is December too early to ski in the High Atlas?

Atlas (Morocco)GuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 13:29 UTCData of 14 November3 min read

December is early in the High Atlas. At mid-mountain around 2929 m, Oukaimeden holds a typical depth of 18 cm in December, against 53 cm in March when snow peaks. But thin does not mean closed: the right timing and the right height can deliver skiable snow.

How snow builds across the season

The Atlas receives snow in bursts. Rather than steady weekly falls, one storm can drop half a season's load, then weeks of sun and wind carve it back. This rhythm means December often arrives before the main snow pack has formed.

Depth climbs by height and by month. At the base, 2600 m, typical December snow is 16 cm. At mid-mountain, 2929 m, it reaches 18 cm. At the top, 3258 m, it climbs to 20 cm. Jump ahead to March and the same heights hold 46 cm, 53 cm and 58 cm. The upper slopes always snow first and hold it longest.

When December can work

Late December offers better odds than early. By 1 December, only 2 % of past winters had 50 cm or more at the base, and 26 % had at least 25 cm. By 25 December, those shares rise to 7 % and 37 %. By 31 December, they reach 9 % and 37 %. The pattern holds at mid-mountain too: 4 % of winters reach 50 cm by 15 December, rising to 13 % by 25 December.

The upper slopes stay ahead. At 3258 m on 15 December, 15 % of past winters held 50 cm or more, climbing to 20 % by 25 December. If you have a choice of height, go higher in December.

The thin-snow risk

Rocks and exposed ground are real hazards in early December. Last winter's Christmas showed both extremes: mid-mountain held 78 cm on 25 December 2025, but the base was bare. This year's pattern will follow the same rule: depth climbs steeply with elevation.

Groomed runs and upper terrain are safer bets than lower slopes or off-piste in December. Watch the SnowScope map for actual depth before you book.

February and March: the safe bet

If your dates are flexible, shift them forward. The best week of the entire season at mid-mountain is week 9, late February: 59 % of past winters carried 50 cm or more, with a typical depth of 59 cm. March peaks at 53 cm typical depth, reached in roughly half of all winters.

The season runs long: a typical winter sees 138 days with snow on the ground. But the first weeks are the thinnest. Last winter topped out at 132 cm at mid-mountain and 181 cm at the top, but that peak arrived after January.

What last winter teaches us

The 2025-26 season showed how variable the Atlas is. By 15 December, mid-mountain held 33 cm, slightly above the 16 cm typical for that date. By Christmas, it had grown to 78 cm, far ahead of the 20 cm norm. Yet other Decembers are bare: in 1 of 5 winters on 15 December, the mid-mountain lies nearly snow-free, with less than 5 cm.

The lesson: watch the actual forecast before you pack. Early-season trips to the High Atlas demand either flexibility or luck.

Tips for skiers

  • If December is your only option, book late December after checking the SnowScope map for recent snowfall.
  • Always choose mid-mountain or the top of the resort over the base in early season; depth gains 2–5 cm per 300 m of elevation.
  • Aim for February week 9 (late February) or any week in March for the most reliable snow.
  • Early-season runs are thin: stay on groomed terrain, watch for rocks, and avoid off-piste until SnowScope shows 50 cm or more.
  • Check the two-week forecast and the daily snow map before booking flights or transport; the Atlas can flip from bare to good after a single storm.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes on the date shown; they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
