La Grave myths and facts: off-piste skiing and snow under La Meije

AlpsGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 17:16 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

La Grave is a small village at 1,520 m facing La Meije, a peak of 3,982 m. Skiers talk about it with a mix of respect and legend, and some of what is said is wrong. Here are five common ideas, checked against the sources below and against our own snow history.

Myth: 'La Grave is a ski resort like any other'

Fact: it is not. A two-stage gondola climbs to the Col des Ruillans at 3,200 m, and a button lift reaches the Girose Glacier at 3,550 m. Below that, the terrain is unpisted. It is patrolled, but there is no formal avalanche control. The vertical drop is about 2,150 m, and up to 2,300 m if you ski below the village. In practice this is high-mountain ground: glaciers, cliffs and avalanche slopes, with no groomed runs to fall back on.

Myth: 'Experts can go alone'

Fact: even very good skiers hire a mountain guide here, at least for the first days. The routes cross glaciers with crevasses, the right line changes with the snow, and a wrong turn in fog can end above a cliff. Read the Météo-France avalanche bulletin (BERA) every morning, carry a transceiver, shovel and probe, and know how to use them. Beginners and nervous intermediates should choose another resort.

Myth: 'Snow up there is always deep'

Fact: it usually is, but not always. Our snow history follows the high terrain served by the lifts, at about 3,369 m, over 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26); these are model values for an area, not snow measurements on the routes. The deepest snow of a typical winter there is about 367 cm. But the thinnest winter in our record peaked at only about 242 cm and the snowiest at about 520 cm, and depth says nothing about wind crusts, ice or fresh slabs, which matter more for safety.

Myth: 'Spring is too late'

Fact: spring is often the best time. A typical winter has about 151 cm in December, 230 cm in January, 267 cm in February and 282 cm in March, and the snow is usually deepest in April, with about 317 cm. In 9 winters out of 10 there are at least 50 cm for 25 weeks of the season. The first snow typically arrives around 5 October and the last melts around 18 June. Longer days and more settled snow make March and April popular, but warm afternoons raise the risk of wet-snow avalanches: start early and finish early.

Myth: 'Nothing has changed in forty years'

Fact: the snow has thinned even here. The median peak was about 400 cm in our first fifteen winters from 1980-81 and about 362 cm in the last fifteen, up to 2025-26, and the days with snow on the ground fell from about 248 to 225. Last winter, 2025-26, was close to normal, with a peak of about 359 cm.

Getting there and staying

La Grave lies on the road over the Col du Lautaret, between Grenoble and Briançon, so in winter check road conditions before you set off. The village is small; stay close to the gondola. Snow on our map does not mean the lift is running: check the operating days on the official website before you travel. Who will enjoy La Grave most? Strong skiers who are happy on steep, ungroomed snow in changing conditions, ski tourers and mountaineers. Families and skiers looking for easy, groomed slopes will be happier in a resort with pistes, and can keep La Grave for one guided day of big scenery.

Tips for skiers

  • Go with a mountain guide; La Grave has no groomed pistes and no formal avalanche control.
  • Read the Météo-France avalanche bulletin every morning and carry transceiver, shovel and probe.
  • In spring, start early and be off the steep sunny slopes before the afternoon warmth.
  • Check the Col du Lautaret road and the lift's operating days before you travel.
