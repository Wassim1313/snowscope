High Atlas: rain up to about 3,710 m – only the highest summits turn white

Atlas (Morocco)The physics behind the weatherSnow forecastsPublished Sunday 8 November 2026 · 10:05 UTCData of 7 November2 min read

Rain is coming to the High Atlas, but the air is warm. From 8 to 12 November the snow line should stay near 3,710 m. Oukaïmeden (top 3,258 m) gets 20.1 mm, all of it as rain; only the highest summits, with Toubkal at 4,167 m, should turn white.

A snow line above the ski area

Over the wet hours the snow line across the range stays in a narrow band, between 3,640 and 3,800 m. Our chart by height makes the cut very clear: at 3,500 m, 86 % of the precipitation falls as rain; at 3,750 m almost all of it is wet snow or sleet; at 4,000 m, 97 % is snow.

That leaves the whole ski area of Oukaïmeden below the snow line. Imlil, at 1,740 m in the valley, gets 19.5 mm of rain.

Toubkal: the wettest and the whitest

The summit area of Toubkal gets the most: 78.6 mm of precipitation from 8 to 12 November. During the wet hours the 0 °C level there stands near 4,150 m, so the snow line is near 3,850 m and about 88 % of it should fall as snow on the very top.

Is it late for snow? Not yet. Our model gives 0 cm at 2,929 m at Oukaïmeden on 7 November, and in the middle of 46 past winters there was also 0 cm on this date. Last winter the snow there peaked at 132.3 cm on 24 January 2026.

Why the snow line is so high

Air cools by about 6.5 °C per kilometre of height. With a warm air mass over Morocco, the 0 °C level climbs close to the highest summits. Snowflakes that fall through warmer air melt and usually turn to rain about 300 m below the 0 °C level.

The map applies this rule to the real terrain, hour by hour: blue where it should snow, lilac for wet snow, green for rain. In a colder storm the same map turns blue much lower down.

What it means for you

  • Hiking towards Toubkal: wet snow and ice near the summit, rain and swollen streams lower down.
  • Forecasts change: look at the live snow map again before you go.
  • In the mountains, follow the official weather warnings.

About these numbers

Forecast of 7 November (ECMWF IFS via Open-Meteo): hourly precipitation and the temperature profile on 8 pressure levels at 54 points over the High Atlas. Snow line = 0 °C level minus 300 m, wet snow within 150 m of it. Snow depth: SnowScope snow model; past winters from a reanalysis archive since 1980, adjusted to the height.
