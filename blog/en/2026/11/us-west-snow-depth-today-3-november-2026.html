US West snow depth today: Kendall Mountain leads with 134 cm (3 November)

US WestPublished Tuesday 3 November 2026Data of 3 November3 min read

The most snow in the US West is at Kendall Mountain: about 134 cm (53 in) on the upper slopes, according to our snow model. Next come Telluride with 130 cm (51 in) and Crested Butte with 98 cm (39 in). 143 of 195 resorts in the US West have snow on their upper slopes today, and our model counts 247,680 km² of snow-covered ground.

Today's snow

The snow cover shrank from 265,320 km² on 1 November to 247,680 km² today, and our forecast gives 202,830 km² on 6 November.

Height matters: at Kendall Mountain, our model gives 13 cm (5 in) at the top (2,904 m / 9,528 ft), 12 cm (5 in) at mid-mountain (2,869 m / 9,413 ft) and 11 cm (4 in) at the base (2,835 m / 9,301 ft).

Measured on the ground

The Rat Creek station (3,560 m / 11,680 ft) measured 66 cm (26 in) of snow on the ground on 1 November, with 23 cm (9 in) of new snow in 24 hours. The Beartown station (3,530 m / 11,581 ft) measured 66 cm (26 in) of snow on the ground on 1 November, with 5 cm (2 in) of new snow in 24 hours. The Red Mountain Pass station (3,371 m / 11,060 ft) measured 61 cm (24 in) of snow on the ground on 1 November, with 28 cm (11 in) of new snow in 24 hours.

Keep in mind: resort values come from our snow model and describe an area, while stations measure one point.

Is it a good start?

At 2,869 m (9,413 ft) on Kendall Mountain, today's 9 cm (4 in) is close to normal for the date: deeper than in 27 of the past 46 winters (usual: 5 cm (2 in)).

This is a good start at Telluride. At 3,237 m (10,620 ft), our model has 35 cm (14 in) today, deeper than on this date in 38 of the past 46 winters (usual: 10 cm (4 in)).

At 3,135 m (10,285 ft) on Crested Butte, today's 6 cm (2 in) is close to normal for the date: deeper than in 16 of the past 46 winters (usual: 12 cm (5 in)).

The next days

More snow is on the way: by 6 November our forecast gives 7 cm (3 in) at Crested Butte, 5 cm (2 in) at Telluride and 5 cm (2 in) at Lee's Ski Hill.

At Kendall Mountain, the freezing level reaches 2,310 m (7,579 ft) to 3,500 m (11,483 ft) on 3 November, above the top of the resort (2,904 m / 9,528 ft): rain or wet snow is possible on every slope that day.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Tips for skiers

  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
  • Look for snow high up and on north-facing slopes: they keep it longest, while south-facing slopes lose it first in the sun.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Kendall Mountain gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 3 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
