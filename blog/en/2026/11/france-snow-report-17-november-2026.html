Snow is coming in France: up to 87 cm in the week ahead

Europe snow by countryPublished Tuesday 17 November 2026 · 12:53 UTCData of 17 November4 min read

The week ahead could bring up to 87 cm to the snowiest slopes in France, 72 cm of it in the next three days. The biggest day looks like Friday 20 November, with up to 33 cm.

The snow on its way

Forecasts this far out move. The timing is usually more reliable than the totals, so keep a flexible plan and check again two days before you go.

8,720 km² of snow-covered ground in France this Tuesday, down 730 km² on yesterday. Roughly 10 % of the country's mapped mountains and hills are covered.

Best resorts right now

The deepest snow of the country's resorts is at La Grave: 113 cm.

Then come Orelle (108 cm), Val Thorens (108 cm), Les Deux Alpes (91 cm) and Alpe d'Huez Grand Domaine (78 cm).

At La Grave: 79 cm of fresh snow over three days, 92 cm more expected by Friday 20 November.

Orelle picked up 74 cm in the last three days and could add 102 cm by Friday 20 November.

Snow lies on the upper slopes of 104 out of 224 resorts in France.

The rest of the shortlist: Val Thorens: 108 cm on top, 74 cm in three days, 102 cm expected; Les Deux Alpes: 91 cm on top, 67 cm in three days, 68 cm expected; Alpe d'Huez Grand Domaine: 78 cm on top, 72 cm in three days, 85 cm expected.

The latest snowfall

Three-day totals peak at 47 cm, around the Mont Blanc massif, the southern French Alps and the northern French Alps.

The last 24 hours alone brought up to 23 cm.

At least 5 cm fell over some 8,300 km².

How deep it is, height by height

Climb and the snow gets deeper: 1,000-1,500 m: on average under 1 cm (1 % of the ground white); 1,500-2,000 m: on average 1 cm (27 % of the ground white); 2,000-2,500 m: on average 7 cm (70 % of the ground white); above 2,500 m: on average 31 cm (94 % white).

How much of the country is white

Below about 1,900 m the slopes are mostly green or brown. A week ago it was 6,860 km², so the cover has grown by 1,860 km².

Compared with past winters

Here is how the deepest resorts compare with their usual 17 November: La Grave: 64 cm at about 3,370 m, against 74 cm in a normal year; Orelle: 42 cm at about 2,780 m, against 53 cm in a normal year; Val Thorens: 71 cm at about 2,474 m, against 37 cm in a normal year; Les Deux Alpes: 14 cm at about 2,398 m, against 26 cm in a normal year.

1 of these 4 resorts are above their usual depth for the date.

These depths are for one height on each mountain, below the top, so they read lower than the upper-slope numbers further down.

In past winters the best snow at La Grave usually came mid-April / late April.

The neighbours today

For comparison, the neighbours on the same day: Switzerland: 48 % white, deepest Zermatt with 47 cm, up to 129 cm in the week ahead; Italy: 19 % white, deepest Prali with 51 cm, up to 85 cm in the week ahead.

Planning a day on the snow

Above 2,500 m the ground is almost fully white (94 % covered), so runs up there have a continuous base.

Between 1,500 m and 2,500 m the cover is patchy, about 27 % white: expect grass and stones between the snow.

Below 1,500 m the slopes are still almost bare.

If you can choose the day, go after Friday 20 November: the fresh snow then has time to settle and the roads to be cleared.

What it means on the slopes

The new snow freshens the upper slopes; lower down it may already be heavy or wet.

Keep an eye on the forecast: the coming snow could change the picture quickly.

Tips for skiers

  • Roads to the higher resorts may need winter tyres or chains.
  • Read the local avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
  • Morning snow is firm after a clear night: wait for the sun on south faces.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 17 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
