Weeks 10-11 best at Bonneval: 52 cm upper slopes, here's your gear

AlpsGear for the conditionsWhere to skiPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 11:38 UTCUpdated 10 November 2026Data of 8 November4 min read

Bonneval-sur-Arc's upper slopes show 52 centimetres—firm, early-season snow that demands stiff skis, sharp edges, and multiple layers plus sunscreen and goggles. With 11 centimetres forecast here and heavier snow arriving at La Grave and Les Deux Alpes, pack insulated jackets, neck warmers, and hand warmers for the storms coming.

Peak snow arrives in weeks 10 and 11

Satellite data from past winters shows that weeks 10 and 11—late February and early March—are when Bonneval's snow pack runs deepest. The resort sits at 15 centimetres on the base station today, which is 94 per cent of normal for this date, so the season is building right on track. Early snow is thinner and more reactive to thaw and wind than the dense pack you find in peak winter. Gear yourself accordingly.

Layer for 52 centimetres of consolidated snow

Upper slopes at 52 centimetres mean you are on firm snow and crust, not powder. Bring a wicking base layer (merino wool or synthetic), an insulating mid-layer (fleece or synthetic down), and a shell jacket that stops wind and water. At 2900 metres and above, air is cold and dry—gloves, neck warmer, and face protection are essential, not optional. Stiff skis with sharp edges will reward you far more than soft all-mountain boards in firm conditions.

Storm packing: 11 cm at Bonneval, 32 at La Grave

The model forecasts 11 centimetres at Bonneval in the next few days. La Grave and Les Deux Alpes show 32 and 28 centimetres on the horizon—these storms are real and warrant serious preparation. Pack an insulated jacket (down or synthetic), multiple base layers, and high-coverage goggles for wind-blown visibility. Hand warmers, a neck warmer, and a balaclava are not optional; they are tools to stay safe and warm for a full day in moving air.

Freeze-thaw surface and hidden ice

The freezing level bounces between 2500 and 3000 metres over the next three days, creating a classic thaw-freeze pattern. Morning snow is hard and icy; afternoon sun softens the upper slopes. This means high-SPF sunscreen and lip balm are as important as your jacket. Melt-freeze crust will form on exposed ridges, especially at Kühtai where a warm day without much fresh snow is likely. Sharp skis help; dull edges do not.

Early-season snow is thin: plan your terrain

52 centimetres is enough to ski, but thin enough that rocks break through on the edges of runs and in wind-scoured zones. Stick to the fall line early in the day. As sun and wind work the surface by afternoon, gullies and shaded slopes hold cleaner snow. Bring swappable goggles—one for sun, one for flat light and poor visibility. A small repair kit, water, and an extra layer should be in your pack every run.

Why peak weeks 10-11 shape your trip timing

The Alps today have 883 resorts with snow. By 11 November, that number is forecast to drop to 687—a significant retreat. This tells us a thaw or strong wind event is coming soon, so the snow window is tightening. If you are planning a trip in the next few weeks, the data backs late February through early March as the stronger bet. Right now, layer carefully, check the official avalanche bulletin if you venture off-piste, and remember that early-season snow teaches edge control faster than deep powder ever does.

Tips for skiers

  • Pack for thaw-freeze: bring high-SPF sunscreen, a stiff-edged ski for firm consolidated snow, and multiple base layers for a cold morning that will soften by afternoon. The freezing level sits around 2500-3000 metres, so your top runs will be icy.
  • Avoid thin-snow zones at the start of the day. 52 centimetres is enough to ski, but rocks break through on the edges and in wind-scoured terrain. Ski the fall line early, then move to gullies and shaded slopes as afternoon sun softens the surface.
  • Storm gear is essential: 11 centimetres is forecast at Bonneval, with 32 at La Grave and 28 at Les Deux Alpes. Pack an insulated jacket, neck warmer, and hand warmers. Wind speed matters as much as snow depth in moving conditions.
  • Check the official avalanche bulletin before you go off-piste. Early-season slopes have variable stability, especially where melt-freeze crust forms on ridges. Kühtai is at risk of rain-crust problems if warming accelerates.
  • Bring swappable goggles and a pocket repair kit. November light is deceptive, flat light arrives fast, and binding adjustments happen in cold hands. These are insurance, not extras.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 8 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
