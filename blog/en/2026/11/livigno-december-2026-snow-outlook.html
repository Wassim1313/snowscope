Livigno in December 2026: the safest white Christmas in our Italian list

AlpsGuidesPublished Tuesday 10 November 2026 · 12:19 UTCData of 10 November2 min read

Livigno is high and cold: in each of the last 45 winters there were at least 5 cm of snow at mid-mountain on 25 December. December 2026 looks warmer and wetter than usual, and the odds of a white Christmas stay at 100 %.

Snow today

The latest map shows 4 cm at mid-mountain (2301 m) and 4 cm at the top (2791 m), with a bare base at 1811 m. 83 % of the 60 km around Livigno is white. The next seven days could bring about 15 cm at mid-mountain and 23 cm at the top.

Snowier or leaner?

Around Livigno, Bormio and the Engadin, 80 % of the mountain area shows no clear signal and 20 % leans leaner; none leans snowier.

At mid-mountain the chance of more new snow than in a usual December is 43 %. A usual December brings 36 cm, the outlook is 32 cm; snow on 31 December is usually 62 cm, the outlook 58 cm, most likely between 41 and 83 cm.

What the European forecast says

The 51 runs make December 1.6 °C warmer than usual over the area, 80 % of them warmer, and precipitation 13 % above a usual December of 57 mm, with 59 % of the runs wetter. At Livigno's height more precipitation still means snow, and warmth costs only a little (correlation -0.26).

Christmas

A white Christmas at mid-mountain happened in all 45 past winters, and the forecast-weighted chance is still 100 %. A usual 25 December has 57 cm of snow there; the outlook is 54 cm. The past Decembers most like this forecast are 2013, 2019 and 1994.

Tips for skiers

  • If Christmas snow is the priority, Livigno's height is the safest choice in Italy.
  • The best snow sits above mid-mountain; the base can stay thin early in December.
  • Check the 7-day forecast and the SnowScope map for the next storm before you drive over the passes.

About these numbers

Method. Temperature and precipitation: the seasonal forecast of the European weather centre (ECMWF SEAS5, 51 runs started 1 November 2026, anomalies against its own 1993-2016 reforecasts, about 100 km grid). Snow: for every mountain cell of about 10 km we take our model of the past (46 winters, snow depth corrected for height) and the weather of each December from 1981 to 2025 (ERA5); past Decembers whose temperature and precipitation look like each forecast run count more, and their snow gives the odds. Snow today: the SnowScope snow map (500 m). A monthly outlook only tilts the odds: it cannot forecast a storm or a date.
