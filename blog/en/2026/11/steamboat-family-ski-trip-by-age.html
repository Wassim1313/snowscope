Steamboat with kids: a family ski trip planned by age

US WestGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 22:11 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

Steamboat sells itself as a family mountain, and the town has the record to back it up. But a good family trip depends on more than ski school: it depends on which height of the mountain each child skis, and on the month you choose. Here is the trip planned by age, with our snow history (a model of 46 past winters, not resort reports) as the guide.

Why families pick Steamboat

The resort is big, with 3,741 acres and 182 trails, but its terrain is not only for experts: about 12 % of the trails are rated beginner and 40 % intermediate. The town of Steamboat Springs calls itself Ski Town USA and has produced more Winter Olympians than any other town in North America, so skiing is part of everyday life there. The resort opened in January 1963, and night skiing on a few trails has been offered since the 2013-14 season.

Toddlers and first-timers (under 6)

The youngest skiers spend their days near the base, and that is where the snow is thinnest. At 6,896 ft (2,102 m) our model gives a usual season peak of about 37 in (95 cm), and at least 20 in (50 cm) lies there for only 6 weeks in 9 winters out of 10. For a first week on skis, that means one thing: come in mid-winter. Snowmaking and grooming make the beginner area work earlier, but the natural snow is most reliable from late January to March.

School-age kids (6 to 12)

Once children can ride a chairlift, the middle of the mountain becomes their playground. At 8,717 ft (2,657 m) a typical winter has about 26 in (67 cm) in December, 40 in (101 cm) in January, 48 in (122 cm) in February and 52 in (131 cm) in March. There are at least 20 in (50 cm) for 17 weeks in 9 winters out of 10, and in the record the safest week there is early March, with enough snow in every winter. If your school holidays fall in February or March, you have picked well.

Teens

Teenagers want speed, trees and terrain parks, and they usually want to ski without their parents. The upper mountain gives them the deepest snow: near the top, at 10,541 ft (3,213 m), the usual peak is about 94 in (240 cm) and it often comes in April. Agree on a meeting point and a time before they leave, and make sure they know that the trees and the steep upper runs need a partner, not a solo run.

Parents

Book lessons early for the children and give yourselves a morning together on the upper mountain. If the snow is thin at the base, ride the gondola down at the end of the day instead of skiing the lowest runs with tired kids.

Which winter to expect

Winters vary a lot. Last winter, 2025-26, was the thinnest of our record at mid-mountain, with a peak of about 35 in (89 cm), and the median peak there has fallen from about 64 in (162 cm) in our first fifteen winters to about 57 in (146 cm) in the last fifteen. A family trip in late February or March remains the best bet; for December, keep your plans flexible.

Tips for skiers

  • Plan the family week from late January to March for the most reliable snow at every height.
  • Little ones: the base area has the thinnest snow, so mid-winter dates matter most for them.
  • Teens on the upper mountain: a partner in the trees, a meeting point and a time.
  • Snow on our map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and lessons on the official website.
