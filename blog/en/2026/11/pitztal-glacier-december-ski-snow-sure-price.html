Pitztal Glacier in December: the high one that did not lose its snow

AlpsGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 14:54 UTCData of 14 November5 min read

Pitztaler Gletscher bucks the trend. While many Alpine resorts show falling December snow depth over recent decades, this glacier in Tyrol held steady or improved: mid-mountain went from 77 cm average in the 1980s-90s to 85 cm in the last 15 winters. That stability makes December here a rare bet worth taking.

What the glacier terrain bought you

Height and ice matter. The top of Pitztaler Gletscher sits at 3424 m, well above the December rain line. At that altitude every single winter in our 46-year record had 30 cm or more in mid-December, and 100 % held at least 60 cm by Christmas week. The mid-mountain station at 2576 m shows the same perfect record: not one winter dropped below 30 cm in the week of 15–21 December.

On a typical 15 December the mid-station holds 87 cm and the top 171 cm. By Christmas the mid-station sits around 92 cm. In 85 % of winters, that mid-height had 60 cm or more by Christmas week—the deep snow zone, not the scraping zone.

The base: a different story

The valley station at 1728 m tells a different tale. On a typical 15 December it has 32 cm—above the 30 cm threshold, but only just. Only 59 % of winters reach 30 cm in the week before Christmas there, and the usual first day to find 30 cm on the ground is 10 December.

This matters for families or skiers who want to warm up at lower altitude. The plan here is to ride up early and ski from mid-mountain or the top, then download by lift. Rock may show through at the base in early December. Last winter (2025–26) is a case in point: on 15 December there was only 16 cm at the base, though the mid-station had 50 cm.

December this winter versus past winters

The long-term trend is your friend. The old debate about whether December is getting snowier or thinner does not apply here. At the top, the December average rose from 142 cm in the first 15 winters to 174 cm in the last 15. Even the base improved: from 31 cm to 35 cm. This is a corner of the Alps where the glaciated high ground still turns winter moisture into snow, not rain.

In Christmas week every one of the last 15 winters had 30 cm or more at mid-mountain. The share with 30 cm at the base rose from 60 % to 67 %. That is not a guarantee—no season is—but it is as close as December gets in the Alps.

Cost and ticket options

The 2026-27 season uses flat pricing: EUR 77 for an adult day pass, EUR 410 for 6 days. No pre-Christmas discount applies, so December is not cheaper than January or February. The trade-off is snow reliability, not price.

One bonus worth using: a 2-day pass or longer includes one day at Kaunertal Glacier, Winterberg Fendels or Hochzeiger at no extra charge. The Rifflsee area, run separately, opens on 12 December 2026 if you want a low-cost second area nearby.

How it compares: Stubai Glacier next door

The Stubaier Gletscher, one valley to the east, has almost identical mid-mountain snow in December: 86 cm on a typical 15 December and 100 % of winters with 30 cm in the week before Christmas. Its base is slightly deeper at 40 cm. Stubai also offers a 6-day pass for EUR 355—about EUR 55 less than Pitztal.

Pitztal's edge is height. At 3424 m its top is 216 m higher than Stubai's 3208 m, which means more guaranteed terrain above the weather line and a longer season into spring. If your group is happy on mid-mountain runs, Stubai offers the same snow for less. If you want maximum elevation and the extra day on other glaciers, Pitztal justifies the cost.

When to book within December

Early December (1–7 December) is a 37 % shot at the base and 100 % at the top—a climb-and-download trip. Mid-December (8–14 December) is safer: 50 % of winters reach 30 cm at the base by then. The week of 15–21 December is the zone: 59 % at the base, 100 % at mid and top. Christmas week is the best bet: 67 % of winters have 30 cm at the base and near-certain snow from mid-mountain up.

Last winter (2025–26) is a warning: even a weak season left 16 cm at the base on 15 December and 50 cm at mid-mountain. Thin, but not bare. If you must come early December and cannot adapt plans, ask the resort about artificial cover on lower runs.

Tips for skiers

  • Book a 2-day pass or longer: the ticket includes one free day at Kaunertal Glacier, Winterberg Fendels or Hochzeiger. Use it to hedge against one poor-snow day.
  • Ski from mid-mountain (2576 m) or the top (3424 m) in early December and descend by lift; the base may have thin snow or bare patches until mid-month.
  • December on the glacier gets cold: at 3424 m expect well below freezing. Pack hand warmers, face protection and goggles suited to flat light and wind.
  • If you want off-piste terrain or ski touring, check the Avalanche.report – Euregio Tyrol bulletin before you go, and hire a local guide. Glacier crevasses are a hazard year-round.
  • If Pitztal's price feels steep and your group is content on mid-mountain, compare Stubaier Gletscher: same December snow depth at mid-height for about EUR 55 less per 6-day pass.
