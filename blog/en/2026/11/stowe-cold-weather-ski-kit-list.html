Stowe ski kit list: what to wear and pack for cold, wind and ice on Mount Mansfield

US NortheastGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 17:43 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

Stowe skis on the east side of Mount Mansfield, Vermont's highest mountain at 4,395 ft (1,340 m). The skiing is classic New England: real cold, wind on the upper lifts, and snow that can be soft one day and firm the next. Good kit makes the difference between a short day and a great one. Here is the list, item by item, with our snow history (a model of 46 past winters, not resort reports) to explain why.

1. Layers, not one thick jacket

Mansfield is a cold mountain: its record low is −39 °F, and snow has fallen on the summit in every month of the year. Wear a thin base layer that dries fast (wool or synthetic, never cotton), a warm mid layer and a windproof shell. Layers let you open up on the long run down and close up again on the chairlift.

2. Something for your face

Wind matters more than the thermometer. The US National Weather Service explains that at a wind chill of −19 °F, exposed skin can freeze in 30 minutes. A neck gaiter or balaclava and a helmet that covers your ears keep your skin out of the wind. Check your children's cheeks and noses on the lift: the weather service asks for extra care with children.

3. Goggles for flat light

Grey days are common in northern Vermont. Bring goggles with a lens for low light, plus a second lens or a pair of sunglasses for bright spring days. On the upper mountain, flat light hides the bumps and the icy patches.

4. Sharp edges

The snow at Stowe is often firm, and that is an equipment question. Have your edges tuned before the trip, or rent skis that were serviced recently. Our model explains why the surface varies so much: even at mid-mountain, 2,480 ft (756 m), the usual season peak is only about 37 in (93 cm), and at the base, 1,312 ft (400 m), about 26 in (66 cm). With a thinner snowpack, a thaw and a freeze turn the surface hard quickly.

5. Hand and toe warmers, and a dry pair of gloves

Pack chemical warmers and spare gloves in a small backpack. Wet gloves freeze on the chairlift; a dry pair at lunch makes the afternoon possible.

6. The right dates in your bag

The best kit is the right week. At mid-mountain a typical winter has about 10 in (26 cm) in December, 19 in (48 cm) in January and about 28 in (71 cm) in February and March. At least 20 in (50 cm) is there for 11 weeks in half of all winters, and the best single week, in late February, has that much in 83 % of winters. Near the top, 3,648 ft (1,112 m), the usual peak is about 48 in (122 cm), reached in March. Unlike many western resorts, Stowe has not lost snow in our record: the median peak at mid-mountain was about 34 in (86 cm) in our first fifteen winters and about 34 in (87 cm) in the last fifteen. Last winter, 2025-26, was a good one, with about 43 in (109 cm) at mid-mountain.

About the resort

The first trails on Mansfield were cut in 1933 by the Civilian Conservation Corps. Today the resort spreads over Mount Mansfield and Spruce Peak, with 116 trails, 12 lifts including 2 gondolas, and a vertical drop of 2,360 ft (719 m), the fifth largest in New England. Vail Resorts has run the ski area since 21 February 2017.

Tips for skiers

  • Wool or synthetic base layer, warm mid layer, windproof shell; no cotton.
  • Cover your face and ears when the wind blows on the upper lifts, and check the children.
  • Tune your edges: firm snow is normal in New England.
  • Snow on our map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts on the official website.
