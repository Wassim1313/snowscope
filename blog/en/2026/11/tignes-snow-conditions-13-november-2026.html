Tignes snow conditions: 42 cm up on the glacier side, about a metre due this week (13 November 2026)

AlpsGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 11:00 UTCData of 12 November4 min read

Search for Tignes snow conditions this week and you will find two different winters on one mountain. Up on the Grande Motte side our latest snow update gives 42 cm at the top of the lifts (3447 m). At 2503 m there are 12 cm, almost all of it from the last day. Down at 1559 m, roughly the height of Les Brévières, the ground is still bare. The coming week could close that gap quickly: 99 cm are forecast at the top and 80 cm at 2503 m.

Glacier side, lake villages, valley

The top of the ski area is the part that already looks like winter. It holds 42 cm after 14 cm in the last 24 hours and 28 cm over the week. This is the glacier sector, the one Tignes keeps for autumn skiing, and the snow there falls on old ice and cold ground, so it stays.

The lake villages, Val Claret, Tignes le Lac and Le Lavachet, sit at 2100 m, between our two lower heights. At 2503 m we count 12 cm, and the 12 cm that fell in the last day are the whole story there. At 1559 m there is nothing on the ground and only a trace fell this week. So the honest summary is simple: white above the lakes, patchy around them, green below.

These are estimates for the area, not stakes on the pistes. A sheltered hollow will hold more than our figure, a ridge in the wind less.

The week ahead, and where rain gets in

The next two days are quiet, about 2 cm at the top. The real storm comes after that: over seven days we have 99 cm at the top, 80 cm at 2503 m and about 32 cm at 1559 m.

Before we publish we compare our numbers with other forecasts. For the top they are even higher, from 124 to 172 cm, so read our 99 cm as the careful end of the range. The weak point is low down. The snow line in those forecasts sits around 1700 m, and at 1559 m about three quarters of the water arrives as rain. Expect mixed rain and snow at Les Brévières and on the road up, and snow only from roughly 1700 m upwards, which still puts the lake villages on the right side of the line.

Is this normal for the middle of November?

Not quite yet. In our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) a usual November holds 76 cm at the top, 33 cm at 2503 m and 13 cm at 1559 m, so today Tignes is thinner than usual at every height. The spread is wide, though: at the top one November in ten has less than 13 cm and one in ten more than 168 cm.

Snow usually arrives early here. The first lasting snow lies at the top by 28 September in a middle winter, at 2503 m by 5 October and at 1559 m by 22 October. A usual December then climbs to 161 cm at the top, 80 cm at 2503 m and 42 cm at the bottom. If this week's storm comes in near the forecast, Tignes jumps from below normal to roughly December depths at 2503 m in one go.

Why Tignes is a safe bet on height

Height is the reason people pick Tignes for an early or a late trip. At the top the ground is white for 241 days in a middle winter, at 2503 m for 234 days. At 2503 m, 21 weeks of the winter have 50 cm or more in 9 winters out of 10, and at the top 25 weeks do.

The long view is not flat, and it would be dishonest to hide it. The middle peak at the top was 401 cm over the first 15 winters of our record and 377 cm over the last 15; snow days at the top fell from 267 to 237. Last winter peaked at 310 cm at the top, 222 cm at 2503 m and 141 cm at 1559 m. The mapped Tignes - Val d'Isère area counts 211 km of runs and 84 lifts, so there is plenty of terrain once the lower half fills in.

Tips for skiers

  • The glacier sector opens first: check the resort's own opening page before you book a weekend in November.
  • Below about 1700 m this week, plan for rain or wet snow on the road and carry chains or winter tyres.
  • A metre of new snow on a thin, young base hides rocks and builds avalanche slabs: read the Météo-France bulletin before any off-piste.
