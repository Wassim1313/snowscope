Is this normal?

Wolf Creek, 11,010 ft (3,356 m): 26 in (66 cm) today. That beats this date in 32 of the past 46 winters (usual: 8 in (20 cm)). A good start.

Near average at Telluride: 8 in (20 cm) at 10,620 ft (3,237 m), deeper than in 21 of the past 46 winters on this date (usual: 9 in (24 cm)).

A strong opening at Silverton Mountain: our model has 31 in (79 cm) at 10,643 ft (3,244 m), deeper than on this date in 42 of the past 46 winters, against a usual 7 in (17 cm).

Purgatory Resort, 9,820 ft (2,993 m): 4 in (10 cm), close to normal (usual: 6 in (14 cm)); deeper than in 19 of the past 46 winters.

Near average at Lost Trail: 6 in (16 cm) at 7,306 ft (2,227 m), deeper than in 24 of the past 46 winters on this date (usual: 6 in (15 cm)).

The forecast in short

Forecast to 14 November: 5 in (12 cm) at Purgatory Resort, 4 in (9 cm) at Telluride and 3 in (8 cm) at Wolf Creek. Treat the later days with care.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Tips for skiers

  • South-facing slopes take the most sun and melt first; high, north-facing slopes keep their snow the longest.
  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Wolf Creek gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 11 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
