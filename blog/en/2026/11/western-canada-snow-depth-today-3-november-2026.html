Western Canada snow depth today: Big White leads with 91 cm (3 November)

Western CanadaPublished Tuesday 3 November 2026Data of 3 November2 min read

The most snow in Western Canada is at Big White: about 91 cm (36 in) on the upper slopes, according to our snow model. Next come Silver Star with 84 cm (33 in) and Harper Mountain with 74 cm (29 in). 51 of 57 resorts in Western Canada have snow on their upper slopes today, and our model counts 329,280 km² of snow-covered ground.

Today's snow

The snow cover shrank from 370,180 km² on 1 November to 329,280 km² today, and our forecast gives 302,380 km² on 6 November.

Height matters: at Big White, our model gives 58 cm (23 in) at the top (2,271 m / 7,451 ft), 8 cm (3 in) at mid-mountain (1,891 m / 6,204 ft) and 0 cm at the base (1,512 m / 4,961 ft).

Is it a good start?

This is a good start at Big White. At 1,891 m (6,204 ft), our model has 39 cm (15 in) today, deeper than on this date in 43 of the past 46 winters (usual: 13 cm (5 in)).

This is a good start at Silver Star. At 1,516 m (4,974 ft), our model has 9 cm (4 in) today, deeper than on this date in 39 of the past 46 winters (usual: 2 cm (1 in)).

This is a good start at Harper Mountain. At 1,283 m (4,209 ft), our model has 53 cm (21 in) today, deeper than on this date in 46 of the past 46 winters (usual: 2 cm (1 in)).

What is coming next

Until 6 November, our forecast gives 31 cm (12 in) at Mount Cain Alpine Park, 25 cm (10 in) at Whistler Blackcomb and 3 cm (1 in) at Silver Star.

At Big White, the freezing level reaches 1,120 m (3,675 ft) to 2,600 m (8,530 ft) on 3 November, above the top of the resort (2,271 m / 7,451 ft): rain or wet snow is possible on every slope that day.

Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.

Tips for skiers

  • Snow on the map does not mean a resort is open: check lifts and opening dates on the resort's website.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Stay on open, groomed pistes: rocks and grass can hide just under the surface.
  • Look for snow high up and on north-facing slopes: they keep it longest, while south-facing slopes lose it first in the sun.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Big White gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 3 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
