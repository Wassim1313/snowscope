Kirkwood snow outlook: in line for 16 in by 15 November (US West)

US WestSnow forecastsPowder alertsPublished Thursday 12 November 2026 · 16:48 UTCData of 12 November2 min read

The next few days look decidedly unsettled in the US West, with the heaviest snow concentrated in a handful of resorts. Until 15 November, the forecast adds 23 in (58 cm) at Mt. Shasta Ski Park, 16 in (40 cm) at Kirkwood and 15 in (38 cm) at Boreal; amounts several days out can still change. A model value covers an area, a station covers one point, and the snow can change a lot between the two. Every number here is an estimate with some uncertainty; the trend over several days is usually more reliable than one value.

Where snow stays, where it melts

On 12 November the freezing level at Mt. Shasta Ski Park rises to 8,333 ft (2,540 m) to 11,220 ft (3,420 m), above the top of the resort (7,539 ft / 2,298 m): precipitation can fall as rain or wet snow, and rain on snow speeds up the melt.

At Kirkwood the freezing level reaches 9,777 ft (2,980 m) to 12,205 ft (3,720 m) on 12 November, higher than the top (9,462 ft / 2,884 m): rain or wet snow is possible on all slopes, and some of the snow may turn to runoff.

On 12 November the freezing level at Boreal rises to 9,678 ft (2,950 m) to 12,139 ft (3,700 m), above the top of the resort (7,700 ft / 2,347 m): precipitation can fall as rain or wet snow, and rain on snow speeds up the melt.

Tips for skiers

  • Forecasts a few days ahead can change: look at the map again the day before you travel.
  • When the freezing level rises above the resort, part of the snow melts as it falls: expect wet snow and stay near the top.
  • Fresh snow has a short best-before time: the first hours. Early starts win.
  • Before you ski off-piste or go touring, read the official avalanche bulletin (links below).
  • Get a powder alert in the SnowScope app to know when Mt. Shasta Ski Park gets fresh snow.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes from the SnowScope snow model (about 1 km, run of 12 November); they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Station values are measurements on the date shown. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
