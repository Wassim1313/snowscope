St. Moritz from A to Z: a short glossary of Corviglia, with the snow behind each word

AlpsGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 11:38 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

St. Moritz has its own vocabulary. Visitors hear names like Chantarella, Marguns or Piz Nair on the first morning and nod politely. This glossary explains them in the order you are likely to meet them, and adds what our snow history, a model of 46 past winters, says about the height each word stands for.

Engadin

The high valley in Graubünden where St. Moritz stands at about 1,800 m. Height is the theme of this whole glossary: the valley floor is already higher than many Alpine ski areas reach. Our lowest point for Corviglia, at 1,733 m, has a usual peak of only about 60 cm, though, so the valley is a place to start from, not a place to rely on for snow.

Chantarella

The middle station of the St. Moritz to Corviglia funicular. It is a useful word to know because it marks the step from town to mountain. Above it, the snow changes quickly.

Corviglia

The ski mountain itself, on the eastern slopes of Piz Nair above the town, at 2,486 m. Our middle point is close by, at 2,377 m. There, a typical winter has about 52 cm of snow in December, 82 cm in January, 98 cm in February, 105 cm in March and 108 cm in April, so the deepest month is late in the season. In 9 winters out of 10 there are at least 50 cm for 16 weeks. Corviglia hosted the alpine events of the 1948 Olympic Games and the World Championships of 1974 and 2003.

Marguns

The area above Celerina, linked to Corviglia. Together they form the largest skiing area in the Engadin. When you hear someone say they will ski down to Marguns, they mean the Celerina side of the same mountain.

Piz Nair

The summit above Corviglia, at 3,057 m, reached by an aerial tramway. This is the snow-sure end of the mountain. Near the top of our record, at 3,021 m, the usual peak is about 256 cm; in April a typical winter has about 211 cm and in May still about 203 cm. There are at least 50 cm there for 23 weeks in 9 winters out of 10.

Corvatsch

The other big ski mountain of the valley, rising to about 3,300 m with a piste of about 8 km down to St. Moritz-Bad. Worth a day when you want a change of view.

Bob Run

The natural bobsleigh run, a 1.72 km ice channel built again each winter. St. Moritz hosted the Winter Olympics twice, in 1928 and 1948, and the World Championships in 2017 too.

White Turf

Horse races on the frozen Lake St. Moritz, part of a calendar of lake events that also includes snow polo, held since 1985.

Sunshine

The town is said to enjoy over 300 days of sunshine a year. It is one reason the low slopes melt sooner than the cold upper ones, and why spring skiing here is best on the high ground.

Trend

Not a local word, but a useful one. At 2,377 m our median peak has hardly changed, about 130 cm in the first fifteen winters and about 133 cm in the last fifteen, but the days with snow on the ground fell from about 255 to 243. Near the top the median peak went from about 270 to 254 cm. Last winter, 2025-26, was thin: about 100 cm at mid-mountain and about 171 cm near the top.

Tips for skiers

  • For the surest snow, plan your days around Piz Nair and the upper Corviglia slopes.
  • Late season is a real option here: April is the deepest month at mid-mountain in our record.
  • Read the SLF avalanche bulletin before any off-piste run.
  • Snow on our map does not mean a resort is open: check the lifts on the official website.
