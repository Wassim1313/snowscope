Snow depth in Chamonix: a bare valley, 12 cm at Brévent, 5 to 24 cm up on the Grands Montets (13 November 2026)

AlpsGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 13:00 UTCData of 12 November6 min read

Snow depth in Chamonix in mid-November depends almost entirely on one thing: how high you stand. On the valley floor the ground is bare. Our numbers find the first snow that lies at about the height of the Grands Montets middle station, 1 cm at 2001 m, then 12 cm at the top of Brévent-Flégère (2486 m) and somewhere between 5 and 24 cm at the top of the Grands Montets lifts (2768 m). A big storm is lined up for the coming week, but it starts as rain in the valley.

The depth ladder, from the river to the top lifts

Here is the valley read from bottom to top, using the three ski areas we follow around Chamonix. At 978 m, the foot of Les Houches, nothing. At 1047 m, the valley floor below Brévent, nothing. At 1234 m, the bottom of the Grands Montets at Argentière, nothing. At 1432 m and 1887 m on Les Houches, still nothing lying, even after 6 cm fell at the top there this week. At 1766 m on the Flégère side, nothing.

The line is crossed only above that. At 2001 m on the Grands Montets there is 1 cm, after 8 cm fell over the week. At 2486 m, the top of Brévent-Flégère, there are 12 cm after 8 cm in the last day and 18 cm in seven days. At 2768 m, the top of the Grands Montets, the cover is real but its exact depth is uncertain, which deserves its own paragraph.

So the honest one-line answer to 'how much snow is in Chamonix' today: none in town, a thin start from 2001 m, the Grands Montets middle station, upwards, and the best of it on the north-facing slopes of the Grands Montets.

Why the Grands Montets top is a range

Our own estimate at 2768 m is 24 cm, built from 15 cm of snow in the last day and 35 cm over the week. The closest high snow station, in the Swiss Val Ferret about 10 km away at 2800 m, last reported 5 cm, on 2 November, before most of this week's snow. Our resort page leans on that measured 5 cm; the ground today is most likely somewhere between the two.

Wind adds to the doubt. The upper Grands Montets are open ridges and glacier bowls, and early snow gets blown off the crests and packed into the hollows. A skier can find knee-deep drifts and bare rock within a few turns. Read 5 to 24 cm as a fair range for the top, not as a promise for any single slope.

Above the lifts: what nobody can tell you from a map

The Aiguille du Midi cable car climbs from 1035 m in Chamonix to 3842 m, far above the highest point we give a depth for. Up there the snow sits on glaciers that keep a cover all year, and the depth that matters for the Vallée Blanche is the snow bridging the crevasses, not a number on a chart. We do not publish a depth for that terrain, and you should not trust anyone who gives one from a map. The guides' office and the avalanche bulletin are the only sources to use for it.

The week ahead: a lot of snow up high, rain down low

Over seven days we have about 100 cm at the top of the Grands Montets, 63 cm at 2001 m and 8 cm at 1234 m. On the other side of the valley Brévent-Flégère gets 80 cm at the top and 47 cm at 1766 m; Les Houches gets 64 cm at 1887 m and 26 cm at 1432 m.

Before publishing we compare these with other forecasts. At the top of the Grands Montets they range widely, from 89 to 211 cm, so our figure sits in the middle. The snow line in those forecasts lies between about 1500 and 1800 m. In the valley, at 1047 to 1234 m, almost all of the water falls as rain; at 1432 m on Les Houches rain is still the larger share, and at 1766 m on the Flégère side close to half. In plain words: rain in Chamonix town, mixed rain and snow at middle heights, snow only from roughly 1800 m up. By the end of the week the depth ladder should start much lower than today, but the valley floor will probably stay green or slushy.

How deep Chamonix usually gets

Our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) show a valley of extremes. On the valley floor below Brévent (1047 m) a middle winter peaks at only 85 cm, with 58 cm in the leanest winter and 131 cm in the richest; a usual November holds 5 cm there and December 30 cm. At the top of the Grands Montets the middle peak is 427 cm, between 294 and 638 cm, and the deepest months are March and April (352 and 355 cm). Few places in the Alps have that much difference in snow depth within a short drive.

Mid-November is normally thin here as well: a usual November has 21 cm at 2001 m and 58 cm at the top of the Grands Montets, though one November in ten has 130 cm at the top. December then builds to 65 cm at 2001 m and 155 cm at the top, January to 114 and 245 cm.

The trend is clear at every height. On the valley floor the middle peak fell from 98 cm over the first 15 winters of our record to 82 cm over the last 15, and snow days from 197 to 172. At the top of the Grands Montets it fell from 490 to 419 cm. Last winter was a good one up high: 466 cm at the top of the Grands Montets, 357 cm at the top of Brévent, 88 cm on the valley floor.

Reading Chamonix snow depth for a trip

If you plan to ski back to the valley, look at the bottom number, not the top: on the valley floor only 3 weeks of the winter have 50 cm or more in 9 winters out of 10. If you plan to stay high, the top of the Grands Montets has 26 such weeks, and its best week in our record, the fourteenth of the year (early April), has a middle depth of 381 cm. For most visitors the sweet spot is between those two: the middle of the ski areas, which usually has a solid cover from the turn of the year.

Tips for skiers

  • Check the depth at the height where you will ski: in Chamonix the valley and the top can differ by more than a metre in the same week.
  • This week, expect rain in town and on the valley roads and snow only from roughly 1800 m; carry chains for the drive to the higher car parks.
  • Glacier terrain above the lifts needs a guide, and fresh snow on a thin base is an avalanche trap: read the Météo-France bulletin before any off-piste.
