How accurate is our snow depth map? First check against 2,012 stations

Snow regionsGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 19:55 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

A snow map helps skiers only if the numbers are honest. We now check ours every day against real station measurements across the Alps, Pyrenees and North America, and publish the results. Here is the first score from 9 November 2026.

Overall accuracy: the first score card

On 9 November, SnowScope compared its map with 2,012 stations that reported a snow depth (data came from 2,516 stations across 16 networks). The map was 0.5 cm too shallow on average. The typical error was 7.7 cm, and the map correctly showed snow or no snow (5 cm or more) 88% of the time.

Early November makes some things easier: many mountain stations have no snow yet, and the map agrees with that. But at 780 stations where either the station or the map showed snow, the story was harder. The typical error there was 12.4 cm, with the map running 1.3 cm too shallow and each site varying about 3.3 cm from the forecast.

Three regions, three stories

The US West (1,063 SNOTEL and similar stations) was the best match: 0.1 cm bias, 4.6 cm typical error, 89% agreement on snow or no snow. The Alps (917 stations) had a 1.1 cm shallow bias and 10.3 cm typical error, with 86% agreement. The Pyrenees are still small: only 14 reporting stations so far, with a 3.0 cm typical error.

One Alps site skews the numbers: a glacier at 3,499 m measured 320 cm while the map shows 28 cm. Glaciers hold old snow and ice that a seasonal ski map should ignore, so we flag such outliers rather than chase them into the forecast.

Where the map still struggles

Early-season thin snow is hard. When a station measured snow, the map found snow only 61% of the time. When the map said snow, 23% of those stations reported none. A few centimetres scattered across a hillside at 500 m resolution is the first problem we are fixing.

Warm storms also caught us out this week. Our map was turning rain into snow when the air was too warm to freeze it. On 12 November in the southern Alps at 2,300 m, that mistake meant forecasting 2.7 cm of new snow when reality was 0.1 cm. The good news: new snow over 24 hours already scores well, with a typical error of just 1.0 cm across 1,890 stations.

Why these numbers matter—and their limits

This check tells you how well the finished map fits the measurements, not an independent test. The same stations help build the map, so it is a final fit check, not a blind forecast. We are planning a separate test using held-out stations.

A single station shows one spot; the map averages 500 m grid squares. Snow varies sharply over a few hundred metres because of wind, forest shelter and sun exposure. That gap between point and area is real, not an error we can fix. We are working on a 100 m grid to narrow it, and we will add a 'likely range' so you know when the map is less sure.

What you will see next

This check now runs twice daily and keeps a history, so you will watch the numbers shift through the winter season. By spring you will see how the map performs on deep snow, steep terrain and changing weather.

Coming soon: a sharper 100 m resolution map, more stations in Spain and Norway, a smarter rule for thin patchy snow at low elevations, and a forecast range around each depth value so you know how confident the map is.

Tips for skiers

  • Use the map to compare different slopes and heights at your resort rather than trusting a single point. Cross-check with the resort's own snow report before you go.
  • Turn on the 'Measured snow' layer in the app to see where the real stations are and how they compare with the map around you.
  • Early in the season, believe the high north-facing slopes first. Expect thin or patchy snow lower down, especially in valleys and on south-facing terrain.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes on the date shown; they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
