Gourette or Cauterets? Two Pyrenees ski resorts compared on snow

PyreneesGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 15:24 UTCData of 9 November4 min read

Gourette and Cauterets are two small, old ski resorts of the French Pyrenees, a valley apart. Gourette sits at 1,400 m with lifts up to 2,450 m; Cauterets is a spa town at 1,000 m whose ski area, the Cirque du Lys, lies between 1,700 m and 2,500 m. Which one gives you better snow? We compared them winter by winter.

Two resorts in brief

Gourette, in the commune of Eaux-Bonnes in the Ossau valley, claims one of the longest skiing histories in the range: skis were first tried here in 1903, and the first international ski competition in the Pyrenees was held on 15 and 16 February 1908. Today it has 36 trails and 42 km of marked slopes. Cauterets lies 32 km south-west of Lourdes; a cable car built in 2005 lifts skiers from the town to the 25 pistes of the Cirque du Lys. Both suit families, beginners and intermediates; neither is a big-mountain resort.

Snow at mid-mountain

Our snow history covers 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26), as model values for an area corrected for height. At mid-mountain the two are close: at 1,903 m in Gourette the deepest snow of a typical winter is about 134 cm, at 2,072 m in Cauterets about 140 cm. Month by month, a typical Gourette winter has about 32 cm in December, 66 cm in January, 92 cm in February and 88 cm in March; Cauterets has about 34, 72, 100 and 93 cm.

Neither resort has a single week with at least 50 cm at mid-mountain in 9 winters out of 10, which is honest Pyrenean reality: snow comes in big Atlantic storms with dry or mild spells between them. In half of the winters, though, both have at least 50 cm for 15 weeks of the season.

At the top

Near the top the difference is small again: about 193 cm at 2,453 m in Gourette, about 180 cm at 2,420 m in Cauterets. The usual peak comes in February in both, with about 139 cm at the top of Gourette in February and 138 cm in March. Lower down, at 1,353 m, Gourette's base has only about 82 cm in a typical winter, while the bottom of the Cirque du Lys, at 1,725 m, has about 108 cm: if you are a beginner who cannot ride the lifts down, Cauterets' higher base is an advantage.

Year-to-year swings and the trend

These resorts are about luck as much as height. At mid-mountain Gourette's peak has ranged from about 53 cm in its thinnest winter to about 231 cm in its snowiest; Cauterets from about 60 cm to about 242 cm. Over time the peaks have held up better than in many Alpine resorts: the median at mid-mountain in Gourette moved from about 139 cm in the first fifteen winters to 134 cm in the last fifteen, while Cauterets stayed at about 144 cm. The snow season has become shorter in both, though: from about 187 days to 169 in Gourette, and from about 181 to 162 in Cauterets. Last winter, 2025-26, was generous for both, with peaks of about 159 cm and 170 cm.

Getting there and staying

Gourette is reached from Laruns in the Ossau valley through Eaux-Bonnes. The road over the Col d'Aubisque, at 1,709 m just above the resort, is generally closed from December to June, so in winter come from the Laruns side. Cauterets is reached from Lourdes, and the town itself is the place to stay: the cable car takes you to the slopes. Check lifts and opening dates on each resort's official website: snow on our map does not mean the lifts are running.

Our verdict

On snow alone it is close to a draw, with a slight edge to Cauterets for its higher base and its town below. Gourette has the deeper top in a typical winter and an easy approach from Pau's side of the mountains. Either way, the best weeks are usually in February: in both resorts the week with the best record has at least 50 cm at mid-mountain in about 87 % of winters. Before any off-piste run, read the Météo-France avalanche bulletin for the Pyrenees.

Tips for skiers

  • Aim for February: in both resorts it is usually the deepest and most reliable month.
  • Beginners in a thin winter: Cauterets' higher lower slopes hold snow better than Gourette's base.
  • In winter, drive to Gourette from Laruns: the Col d'Aubisque road is generally closed.
  • Off-piste: read the Météo-France avalanche bulletin (BERA) first.
