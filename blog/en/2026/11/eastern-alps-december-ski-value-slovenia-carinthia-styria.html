Eastern Alps in December: Slovenia vs Carinthia, Styria and Lower Austria

AlpsGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 17:54 UTCData of 14 November3 min read

Prices drop as you move east along the Alps, and so does December snow. Slovenia has the cheapest lift passes in this comparison, from EUR 45 a day, but at Krvavec, Vogel, Rogla and Mariborsko Pohorje no more than 24 % of our winters had 30 cm at mid-mountain in the week of 15-21 December. The Austrian side costs more and delivers more.

The short answer

If you need natural snow in December, go to the higher Austrian areas: the Mölltaler Gletscher (8), Lachtal (7) or the Hochkar (9). If you live near Ljubljana or Maribor and can decide on the day, the Slovenian hills are great value when their own snow reports are good.

One note on scope: we only compare resorts where we hold 46 winters of snow history. Ski areas in Czechia, Slovakia, Poland, Romania and Bulgaria are not in our history yet, so they are not ranked here.

Slovenia: cheap, and honest about the snow

Krvavec (1) is the highest of the four, with lifts up to 1963 m, and the most popular from Ljubljana. Its day pass is EUR 47 and its 6-day pass EUR 205. Mid-mountain had 30 cm in mid-December in 24 % of our winters, and only 13 % of recent Christmas weeks.

Rogla (3) is similar: EUR 49 a day (EUR 44 early season), EUR 210 to 237 for 6 days, and 22 % of winters with 30 cm at mid-mountain in mid-December. Vogel (2), above Lake Bohinj, costs EUR 49 a day and EUR 230 for 6 days; its top had 30 cm in 30 % of winters, its middle in 9 %. Mariborsko Pohorje (4), the lowest, costs EUR 45 a day and had 30 cm at mid-mountain in none of our winters in that week.

Carinthia: one glacier and two gambles

The Mölltaler Gletscher (8) is the only sure thing in this region: 128 cm at mid-mountain on a typical 15 December and 30 cm there in every one of our winters in mid-December. Its 6-day pass cost EUR 332 in the low season on its latest published list (2025-26).

Nassfeld (5), on the Italian border, is large (70 km of mapped runs) but low at the bottom: its top had 30 cm in mid-December in 63 % of winters, its middle in 22 %. Six days cost EUR 353 early in the season and EUR 381 later. The Turracher Höhe (6) is similar on snow (39 % at mid-mountain) and price (EUR 353 for 6 days in its low season).

Styria and Lower Austria: the middle way

Lachtal (7) in Styria had 30 cm at mid-mountain in mid-December in 72 % of winters, for EUR 62 a day or EUR 293.50 for 6 days off-peak. The Hochkar (9) in Lower Austria had 63 %, with dynamic day prices seen from EUR 32.90 on quiet December dates.

Both lost a lot of December snow over the years, so treat them as places to go when the forecast is good, not as fixed bookings.

How to choose

Fixed pre-Christmas week, no snow risk: Mölltaler Gletscher. Cheap long weekend decided late: Lachtal or the Hochkar. Day trips from Slovenian cities: Krvavec or Rogla on days when their pistes are reported open and covered. For everything else in Slovenia, January and February are the safer months.

Tips for skiers

  • In Slovenia buy day passes on good days rather than a December week in advance.
  • For a fixed December week in this region, the Mölltaler Gletscher is the only near-certain snow.
  • Use the snow map a few days before: the eastern hills change fast with each storm.
  • Read the Slovenian, Carinthian or Styrian avalanche bulletin before leaving the piste.
