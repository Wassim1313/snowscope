Snowfall in Val Thorens: 27 cm this week at the top, 86 cm more on the way (13 November 2026)

AlpsGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 13:02 UTCData of 12 November4 min read

Snowfall in Val Thorens counts for more than almost anywhere else in Les 3 Vallées, because the village sits at 2300 m and what lands there is nearly always snow, not rain. Over the last seven days our numbers add up to 27 cm at the top (3175 m), 17 cm at 2498 m, close to village height, and 3 cm at 1822 m, the end of the lowest run. What is lying on the ground today is a different figure: 50 cm at the top, 8 cm at 2498 m and nothing at 1822 m.

Fallen against lying: the week in one ledger

Most of this week's snow came in one go: 16 cm at the top and 10 cm at 2498 m in the last 24 hours, 18 cm at the top over two days. Add up the week and you get 27 cm at the top for a depth of 50 cm, so the summit already had a base from earlier storms.

At 2498 m the arithmetic runs the other way: 17 cm fell, 8 cm lie. New snow is fluffy and settles fast, the ground in November is still warm enough to eat the first layer, and the wind on the open slopes around the village moves the rest. That is why a snowfall total and a snow depth should never be read as the same number.

The next seven days

Tomorrow stays quiet, barely 2 cm. After that a long run of snow days is lined up: 86 cm at the top, 73 cm at 2498 m and 52 cm at 1822 m over the week.

We check our forecast against other forecasts before writing it down. They agree on a big week and go higher than we do: 96 to 148 cm at the top and 94 to 130 cm at 2498 m. Their snow line sits between about 1500 and 1700 m, well below the village, so in Val Thorens this is a snow event from top to bottom. Only at the lowest run end, 1822 m, does roughly a third of the water come as rain in those forecasts. Our 86 cm is the cautious figure; the true total at the top could be well above a metre.

How the snowfall season usually builds

Across our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) the snow that lasts arrives at the top by 27 September in a middle winter, at 2498 m by 4 October and at 1822 m by 20 October. A usual November leaves 34 cm on the ground at 2498 m, though one November in ten has only 7 cm and one in ten 79 cm. December brings that to 90 cm, January to 144 cm.

Once winter is in, it stays in: the ground near village height is white for 232 days in a middle winter, and 21 weeks have 50 cm or more in 9 winters out of 10. The middle peak there is 238 cm, with 164 cm in the leanest winter and 316 cm in the richest.

When the snowfall pays off

The deepest snow at the top usually comes late, in April (289 cm), long after the busy weeks. Near the village the best single week in our record is the tenth of the year, early March, with a middle depth of 209 cm. Last winter peaked at 305 cm at the top, 237 cm at 2498 m and 165 cm at 1822 m.

One honest note on the trend: the middle peak at the top fell from 386 cm over the first 15 winters to 342 cm over the last 15, and snow days near the village from 255 to 225. Val Thorens still loses less than lower resorts, simply because it starts higher. The mapped area counts 115 km of runs and 27 lifts between 1822 m and 3175 m, with Cime de Caron at 3200 m above it.

Tips for skiers

  • Read the forecast as snow from the village up: the rain line this week stays far below 2300 m.
  • A week with this much new snow means closed roads and slow lifts on the heaviest days; plan the drive up for a quiet day.
  • Fresh snow loaded by wind is the classic early-season avalanche trap: read the Météo-France bulletin before leaving the pistes.
