Les Menuires snow report: 28 cm at the top, the 1850 m village still waiting (13 November 2026)

AlpsGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 12:20 UTCData of 12 November3 min read

Today's Les Menuires snow report is short, because the snow has only reached the top so far. At 2838 m there are 28 cm on the ground, 15 cm of them from the last day. Lower down the slopes are still waiting: nothing lies at 2112 m, and nothing at 1386 m, the bottom of the lowest mapped run. The resort itself sits at 1850 m, between those two heights, so the village is not white yet.

The report, top to bottom

Top, 2838 m: 28 cm lying, 15 cm new in 24 hours, 25 cm over seven days. This is the ridge that links towards Val Thorens and Méribel, and the only part of the resort with a real cover.

Middle, 2112 m: 6 cm fell over the week but none of it stayed. A trace on the grass, not a base.

Bottom, 1386 m: dry all week, bare ground.

Treat these as area estimates rather than a stake reading. North-facing slopes above the village will hold a little more than this, the sunny side of the valley less.

Same valley, 450 m higher: why Val Thorens is ahead

Drive up the Belleville valley from Les Menuires and you reach Val Thorens at 2300 m, 450 m higher. Our numbers today show what those metres are worth: at 2498 m in Val Thorens there are 8 cm on the ground and 50 cm at its top, against nothing at 2112 m and 28 cm at the top here. Same storms, same week; the difference is height, not luck.

The gap shows up in the long record too. At the lowest run end of Val Thorens (1822 m), 13 weeks of the winter have 50 cm or more in 9 winters out of 10. At the bottom of Les Menuires (1386 m) only 6 weeks do, and 14 in half the winters. The lower slopes here are the fragile part of the resort, and they are the part to watch in every report.

What the next seven days bring

The coming week is a different story. Our forecast gives 80 cm at the top, 62 cm at 2112 m and 24 cm at 1386 m. The other forecasts we compare before publishing agree on a big week: 96 to 148 cm at the top and 87 to 100 cm at 2112 m, so we sit on the low side.

Rain is the catch at the bottom. The snow line in those forecasts lies around 1500 to 1700 m, and at 1386 m about 80 % of the water comes as rain. So: mixed rain and snow at the lowest runs, wet snow possible around the village at 1850 m on the mildest day, and snow only higher up. For the village, the week should still end white.

How November usually looks here

Across 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) a usual November has 9 cm at 1386 m, 25 cm at 2112 m and 52 cm at the top; December rises to 36, 73 and 124 cm. The first lasting snow lies at the top by 29 September, at 2112 m by 10 October and at the bottom by 27 October in a middle winter, so this year the middle and the bottom are a little behind.

Winter here peaks late at height and early down low: February is the deepest month at the bottom (88 cm), March at 2112 m (171 cm) and April at the top (260 cm). A middle winter peaks at 104 cm at the bottom, 203 cm at 2112 m and 310 cm at the top; last winter reached 118, 204 and 281 cm. The mapped area counts 120 km of runs and 33 lifts, and the best single week at 2112 m is the tenth of the year, early March, at a middle depth of 177 cm.

Tips for skiers

  • For an early trip, judge Les Menuires by its 2112 m figure, not by the top: that is where most of the skiing lies.
  • Below about 1700 m this week, expect rain mixing in; the valley road can be slushy rather than white.
  • New snow over a bare or thin base is an avalanche and rock trap: read the Météo-France bulletin before leaving the marked runs.
