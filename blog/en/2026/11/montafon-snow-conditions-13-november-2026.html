Montafon snow conditions: snow up high, rain in the valley (13 November 2026)

AlpsGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 14:11 UTCData of 12 November3 min read

The Montafon is living two seasons at once. On the top of Silvretta Montafon, at 2421 m, our snow map shows 4.5 cm on the ground after 5 cm of fresh snow in the last 24 hours. Halfway down, at 1555 m, and in the valley at 689 m, there is nothing yet. The coming week brings about 40 cm to the top and 27 cm to mid-mountain, but the valley gets rain: the snow line sits somewhere between 1,300 and 1,500 m.

Three floors, three different days

Silvretta Montafon is one of the tallest ski areas in Vorarlberg: the mapped lifts and runs go from 689 m in the valley to 2421 m on the top, a drop of 1732 m. That height difference is the whole story of the conditions in mid-November.

Top floor, around the Hochjoch and the ridges above Schruns and St. Gallenkirch: 4.5 cm of snow, 9.5 cm fallen over the past week, 5 cm more in the next 24 hours. Middle floor, at 1555 m: bare ground, with 3 cm due in the next day. Ground floor, the valley around Schruns at 689 m: bare, and it will stay mostly green this week.

Where the snow line sits this week

Rain and snow are coming in waves through the next seven days. Our numbers give 40 cm at the top, 27 cm at mid-mountain and 7.5 cm in the valley. The forecasts we compare against agree on the top, with 28 to 49 cm, so the high ground should turn properly white.

Lower down it is a different matter. The snow line, the height where rain turns into snow, sits between about 1,300 and 1,500 m this week. In the valley that means rain. At mid-mountain it is close: in the references about 45 % of what falls at 1555 m comes as rain, so expect wet, heavy snow there rather than powder.

Is this late for the Montafon?

A little. In the middle of our 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) the top of the ski area holds 41 cm in November, mid-mountain 11 cm and the valley 3 cm. In one winter out of ten the top has only 12 cm at this time of year; in another one out of ten it has 84 cm. So 4.5 cm is thin, but a 40 cm week would put the top back in the middle of the usual range.

The calendar agrees. The first lasting snow usually reaches the top around 16 September, mid-mountain around 3 October and the valley around 13 October. The top is on time; the lower two floors are waiting for colder air.

The valley runs need December

The long runs back to the villages are the last part to fill in. A usual December gives 18 cm in the valley and 35 cm at mid-mountain, and the valley reaches its deepest in February, with 40 cm in a middle winter. On thin years the valley runs depend on snowmaking.

That lower floor has also lost the most over the decades. The middle peak in the valley fell from 59 cm in the first 15 of our winters to 42 cm in the last 15, and the snow days there from 188 to 166 a winter. Up high the picture is much safer: the top has 50 cm or more for 23 weeks in 9 winters out of 10, and its best week, week 14 at the start of April, had that much in every one of our winters, with a middle depth of 199 cm.

Tips for skiers

  • This week, plan on the top floor only: ride the lifts down rather than counting on the valley runs.
  • Fresh snow on bare ground hides rocks and drainage ditches; keep to prepared runs once the lifts turn.
  • Before leaving the marked runs, read the Vorarlberg avalanche bulletin: early snow on wind-loaded ridges is often the weak layer of the whole winter.
