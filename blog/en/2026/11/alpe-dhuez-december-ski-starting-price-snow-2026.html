Alpe d'Huez in December: fair prices and snow odds?

AlpsGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 18:11 UTCData of 14 November4 min read

Planning a student group trip for December 2026 and looking at Alpe d'Huez? We checked the official ticket prices and our snow history of 46 past winters to see if this famous French resort gives you good value.

Is the starting price fair for a big resort?

Organising a trip for a student group means watching every euro you spend. At Alpe d'Huez, the adult 1-day pass starting price for winter 2026-27 is 58.00 EUR. If you stay longer, the adult 6-day pass costs 336.50 EUR.

The mapped ski area has 163 km of mapped runs and 82 mapped lifts. You get a lot of terrain for your money, but let us look at the snow odds before you book.

Snow depth at the bottom, middle and top in December

Our snow history of 46 past winters shows a big difference between the bottom of the lifts at 1124 m and the high top at 3306 m. At the bottom, the usual December depth is 14 cm. On 15 December, the usual depth is 13 cm. In a thin December, it drops to 0 cm. The share of winters with 30 cm or more at the bottom is only 4 percent from 1-7 December, rising to 28 percent from 22-28 December.

Things improve fast as you go higher. At mid-mountain at 2215 m, the usual December depth is 60 cm, and the usual depth on 15 December is 56 cm. In a thin December, you get 25 cm, while a deep December brings 107 cm. The share of winters with 30 cm or more at mid-mountain is 70 percent in the first week, and reaches 96 percent from 22-28 December.

At the top of the lifts at 3306 m, the usual December depth is 159 cm, with 151 cm on 15 December. In a thin December, you still find 70 cm of snow, and a deep December reaches 278 cm. The share of winters with 30 cm or more here is 98 percent in the first week and 100 percent for the rest of the month.

Current snow conditions and the two-week outlook

Right now, the snow depth at the bottom of the lifts at 1124 m is 0 cm, with 0 cm of new snow expected in the next 7 days. Mid-mountain at 2215 m has 0 cm right now, but expects 59 cm of new snow in the next 7 days. At the top of the lifts at 3306 m, the current depth is 58 cm, with 90 cm expected in the next 7 days. For mid-mountain and above, the two-week new snow outlook gives a middle of 92 cm with a range from 73 cm to 110 cm.

Alpe d'Huez or Les 2 Alpes

How does Alpe d'Huez compare to Les 2 Alpes across the valley? Les 2 Alpes has 108 km of mapped runs and 46 mapped lifts, with a lowest lift at 1284 m and highest lift at 3511 m. Its adult 6-day Grand Domaine pass costs 266.50 EUR, while the flexible-date 1-day Freedom ticket is 68.00 EUR.

In our snow history, Les 2 Alpes has a usual December depth of 16 cm at the base, 75 cm at mid-mountain, and 176 cm at the top. On 15 December, Les 2 Alpes usually has 14 cm at the base, 73 cm at mid-mountain, and 160 cm at the top. Both resorts have reliable snow high up, and Les 2 Alpes is the cheaper of the two for a 6-day pass: 266.50 EUR against 336.50 EUR.

First snow, last winter, and long term trends

At the bottom of Alpe d'Huez, the usual first lasting snow day is November 12. The usual season peak is 52 cm, with 159 usual snow days per winter. At mid-mountain, the usual first lasting snow day is October 18, with a season peak of 184 cm and 218 snow days. At the top, snow usually arrives by September 30, reaching a season peak of 440 cm over 232 snow days.

Looking at the long term trend, the December average depth at mid-mountain was 50 cm in the first 15 winters from 1980-81 to 1994-95, and rose to 65 cm in the last 15 winters from 2011-12 to 2025-26. At the top, the average went from 133 cm to 165 cm between the same periods. Last winter in December 2025, the average depth was 10 cm at the bottom, 49 cm at mid-mountain, and 132 cm at the top. The thinnest December on record at the bottom was 0 cm in 1989-90, while the deepest was 42 cm in 1981-82.

Tips for skiers

  • Book your 6-day pass on the official site for 336.50 EUR.
  • Plan your skiing for mid-mountain and top areas above 2215 m in December, because the bottom base is often thin with a usual depth of only 14 cm.
  • Travel later in December if possible, as the share of winters with 30 cm or more snow at the bottom increases from 4 percent in the first week to 28 percent in the last week.
  • Always check the local avalanche bulletin before heading off-piste, even when the top depths reach 159 cm.
