December Alps and Pyrenees Ranked for Snow and Budget

AlpsGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 20:11 UTCData of 14 November6 min read

French and Italian border resorts top our December 2026 value ranking for low-cost trips. Les 2 Alpes leads with 75 cm on its mid-mountain in a usual December and a 6-day pass at 266.50 EUR, while Livigno charges 258.50 EUR before 18 December. Pyrenean areas sit last with thin snow and day passes that are no bargain.

How we compared the ten ski areas

We ranked ten resorts across France, Switzerland, Italy, Andorra and Spain using our snow history of 46 past winters. Every resort is checked at the bottom of the lifts, mid-mountain and top of the lifts, alongside its mapped runs and mapped lifts. Pass prices come from each official site for 2026-27, except La Thuile, where no price was checked on the official site. The snow numbers split the ten into three clear groups.

Snow-safe leaders and border pockets

Les 2 Alpes tops our table with a mapped ski area of 108 km of runs and 46 lifts reaching 3511 m. Its 1284 m base is thin, averaging 16 cm (0 cm thin, 39 cm deep) and hitting 30 cm or more in 2 % of past winters in 1-7 December and 37 % in 22-28 December. Stay at mid-mountain (2397 m), which averages 75 cm (30 cm thin, 140 cm deep) and reaches 50 cm or more in 46 % of past winters in 1-7 December, 72 % in 8-14 December, 87 % in 15-21 December and 91 % in 22-28 December, while the 3511 m top averages 176 cm (72 cm thin, 302 cm deep). Passes cost 68.00 EUR for a flexible 1-day ticket, 266.50 EUR for 6 days, or 42 EUR per day on 28-29 November 2026; mid-mountain snow grew from 63 cm in the first 15 winters to 77 cm in the last 15 winters.

Saas-Fee (48 km of mapped runs, 30 lifts) reaches 3556 m with 68 cm usual at mid-mountain (2668 m; 31 cm thin, 113 cm deep; dipping from 70 cm to 63 cm), though passes cost CHF 86 for 1 day and CHF 419 for 6 days. On snow, two border resorts stand out: Montgenèvre (62 km, 24 lifts) and La Thuile (79 km, 17 lifts). Montgenèvre averages 56 cm at its 1795 m base (22 cm thin, 102 cm deep) and hits 30 cm or more in 89 % of past winters by 15-21 December; mid-mountain rose from 49 cm in the first 15 winters to 76 cm in the last 15 winters, and a 1-day pass is 45.50 EUR until 18 December 2026 (56.00 EUR from 19 December, or 301.00 EUR for 6 days in high season). La Thuile averages 44 cm at its 1468 m base (17 cm thin, 92 cm deep) and 67 cm at mid-mountain, rising from 64 cm to 73 cm.

Good-value middle for mid-December

Valloire-Valmeinier and Vars-Risoul both have 131 km of mapped runs at mid-range French prices. Valloire-Valmeinier (34 lifts, 2738 m top) costs 54.90 EUR for 1 day, 278.50 EUR for 6 days, or 20 EUR on 5-6 December 2026 with ice-rink access. Its 1406 m base averages 29 cm (12 cm thin, 58 cm deep), while mid-mountain (2072 m) averages 66 cm (31 cm thin, 120 cm deep; rising from 55 cm in the first 15 winters to 69 cm in the last 15 winters) and reaches 50 cm or more in 43 % of past winters in 1-7 December, 67 % in 8-14 December, 80 % in 15-21 December and 87 % in 22-28 December. Vars-Risoul (38 lifts, 2722 m top) charges 58 EUR for 1 day and 298 EUR for 6 days, with 51 cm usual at mid-mountain (20 cm thin, 97 cm deep; up from 42 cm to 62 cm).

In Italy, Livigno (83 km, 37 lifts) has the lowest checked 6-day pass at 258.50 EUR from 28 November to 18 December 2026 (51.50 EUR per day then, 66.00 EUR on 19 to 25 December, 74.50 EUR from 26 December). Its 1811 m base averages 28 cm (6 cm thin, 60 cm deep) and mid-mountain (2301 m) averages 48 cm (up from 39 cm in the first 15 winters to 52 cm in the last 15 winters), so stick to the 2791 m top (85 cm usual). Pila (56 km, 16 lifts) charges 60.00 EUR for 1 day and 322.00 EUR for 6 days, averaging 45 cm at mid-mountain (up from 42 cm to 49 cm) and 80 cm at 2723 m.

Pyrenees: thin snow, no real price advantage

Grandvalira (137 km of mapped runs, 53 lifts) and Baqueira Beret (154 km, 36 lifts) have large mapped areas, yet December snow is thin and the passes are no cheaper than most of the Alpine resorts here. Grandvalira's 1674 m base averages 9 cm (0 cm thin, 28 cm deep) and hits 30 cm or more in only 7 % of past winters from 8-14 December onward. Mid-mountain (2146 m) averages 27 cm (2 cm thin, 63 cm deep; up from 21 cm to 25 cm), while the 2619 m top averages 45 cm. Its 1-day pass for Monday 14 December 2026 is 62.50 EUR online or 72.00 EUR at the ticket office.

Baqueira Beret averages 8 cm at its 1504 m base (0 cm thin, 37 cm deep), 20 cm at mid-mountain (2 cm thin, 56 cm deep; up from 17 cm to 20 cm) and 30 cm at its 2600 m top, reaching 30 cm or more in 39 % of past winters in 1-7 December and 59 % in 22-28 December. Passes cost 74,00 € for 1 day (70,00 € with the rechargeable card) or 359,00 € for 6 days.

Choosing your week and height

For early December on a budget, book Livigno before 18 December at 258.50 EUR for 6 days and ski its upper lifts, or pick Montgenèvre at 45.50 EUR per day with strong 1795 m base cover. For 15-21 December or 22-28 December, Les 2 Alpes (266.50 EUR for 6 days) and Valloire-Valmeinier (278.50 EUR) deliver deeper mid-mountain snow for less than Saas-Fee.

Tips for skiers

  • Pick 15-21 December or 22-28 December for mid-mountain skiing: Valloire-Valmeinier jumps from 43 % of past winters with 50 cm or more in 1-7 December to 80 % and 87 % later in the month.
  • Ski mid-mountain (2397 m) or higher at Les 2 Alpes in December, because its 1284 m base averages just 16 cm.
  • Lock in promotional passes on the official site before 18 December 2026 at Montgenèvre (45.50 EUR per day) or Livigno (258.50 EUR for 6 days), and buy Grandvalira passes online (62.50 EUR) instead of at the ticket office (72.00 EUR).
  • Check the local avalanche bulletin before heading off-piste on high, wind-loaded slopes.

About these numbers

Snow numbers: SnowScope snow history, 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26) at three heights of each resort (a model of the past corrected for height: an estimate for an area, not resort measurements); December figures computed from the daily depths of those winters. Snow now and the two-week outlook: SnowScope forecasts (outlook from ECMWF and GFS, a range, not a promise). Lifts and runs: OpenSkiMap mapping.
