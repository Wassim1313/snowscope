Ukraine's Carpathians gain 3,080 km² of snow cover in one week

Europe snow by countryPublished Saturday 21 November 2026 · 13:46 UTCData of 21 November4 min read

Ukraine's ski mountains have transformed in one week. Snow cover grew by 3,080 km² as cold air moved in, and the highest peaks now hold meaningful water reserves as the winter season begins.

Драгобрат leads a mountain-wide build-up

Драгобрат stands ahead of all Ukrainian resorts with 17 cm of snow depth and 19 cm of new fall in the past 72 hours. This tells us the high peaks—above 1,500 m—are starting to store water properly. The Carpathian ridge is waking up.

Of 20 resorts tracked, only 9 have measurable snow on the ground today. The others remain bare. But the pattern is clear: elevation matters. Where mountains rise above 1,500 m, snow is accumulating. Where they stay lower, the ground is still mostly dark.

One week changed everything

Seven days ago, only 6,040 km² of Ukraine's mountain terrain held snow. Today it is 9,120 km². That jump of 3,080 km² in one week marks the shift from autumn to winter. Yesterday alone added 8,440 km², driven by a major snowfall event across the range.

The highest mountain band—above 1,500 m—now carries full snow cover. The mid-band, between 1,000 and 1,500 m, also reached 100% coverage with a mean depth of 3 cm. Only below 1,000 m is snow patchy, sitting on just 34% of the lower slopes.

The wet band is thickening fast

Between 1,500 and 2,000 m, our estimates a mean depth of 12 cm. This is the zone where water accumulates on the bedrock and feeds springs, streams and reservoirs through the winter and spring melt. That 12 cm represents stored water—not deep, but real.

The snow line sits at 800 m today. Rain and melt dominate below that height, so winter runoff will be thin at low elevations. But the high peaks are now holding the season's water supply in solid form, where it will stay cold until late spring.

Chornohora and the Gorgany show the steepest rise

Three hotspots stand out in the past 72 hours. Chornohora received 21 cm of new snow, the Gorgany 20 cm, and the Borzhava ridge 13 cm. These massifs are higher and colder—they caught the heaviest of the recent storms. Chornohora and the Gorgany are where snow will accumulate fastest over the next week.

Драгобрат, perched at 1,706 m in the Chornohora complex, reflects this advantage. Its 19 cm of new snow in 72 hours ranks it first among ski areas. Lower resorts such as Буковель (1,370 m) and Pylypets (1,165 m) received only 8 cm and 6 cm respectively, because they sit where the air is warmer.

More snow is coming—a lot

Over the next three days, the forecast shows up to 13 cm of new snow across the mountains. November 23 will be the wettest day, with 7 cm falling. By next week, the forecast extends to 17 cm of total new snow through day seven.

This matters for water storage. Each centimetre of snow on the high peaks is water locked away from evaporation. By early December, if the forecast holds, the 1,500–2,000 m band could easily double its current 12 cm mean depth. That would be significant for spring runoff.

The view from the slopes

Today, 52% of Ukraine's ski terrain—across the mapped area—is white. That is a dramatic shift from the scattered patches of a week ago. For skiers, it means the high mountains are now rideable on their upper sections, though lower slopes remain thin or bare.

Драгобрат's 17 cm is enough to ski on the upper peak. Буковель and Pylypets, both under 6 cm, still face thin coverage and rock hazard. By November 24, fresh snow should add 1–2 cm to lower resorts, but the mountains above 1,500 m will remain the focus through this period. Tips for skiers

  • Ski above 1,500 m where snow depth now exceeds 10 cm. Below 1,000 m, only 34% of terrain is covered; rocks remain a hazard.
  • Драгобрат and resorts in the Chornohora range have the most snow today. Lower areas like Slavske (2–3 cm) are not yet skiable.
  • Expect 13 cm more snow over the next three days. By November 23, lower resorts may gain 2–3 cm and become more rideable.
  • The snow line sits at 800 m; expect rain below that height. Any water runoff will drain to valleys, not accumulate as snow.
  • If you go off-piste, consult the official avalanche bulletin. Early-season snow on steep slopes can slide on bare rock beneath.

About these numbers
