Verbier snow forecast next week (23–29 November 2026): 24 cm at the top

AlpsGuidesPublished Monday 16 November 2026 · 11:31 UTCData of 16 November3 min read

Next week Verbier receives fresh snow across all heights, but the upper mountain above 2900 m gains the most. The forecast adds 24 cm there (low case 5 cm, high case 42 cm), with Thursday 26 November as the snowiest day.

What comes before next week matters

The real story starts this week. Between 17 and 20 November, Verbier's upper slopes collect 74 cm (range 56–91 cm), a solid early-season base. The base at village level stays dry: 0 cm. That depth sits there waiting.

Next week's snow falls on top of a prepared bed. When the second round arrives, mid-mountain already holds 58 cm and the top sits under 110 cm, so each new centimetre counts.

Breaking down next week by slope height

Upper slopes at 2900 m: 24 cm total (5–42 cm range). Mid-mountain around 2100 m: 22 cm (5–38 cm). The base near 900 m: 9 cm (1–17 cm). Snow gets heavier and wetter lower down; expect rain mixing in at the village.

The upper mountain does most of its work in four days. Tuesday 24 November adds 2 cm (up to 5), Wednesday 25 gives 5 cm (up to 7), Thursday 26 delivers 7 cm (up to 13), and Sunday 29 closes the week with 4 cm (up to 8). Between those dates, only flurries fall.

The weak point: lower runs and the valley

At the base, 9 cm is roughly half the top's total. Warmer air at lower altitude means heavier, wetter snow that settles fast. Watch for a snow line that may climb back toward mid-mountain on the mildest day, leaving Le Châble and Savoleyres runs vulnerable.

Pack for rain in the village. The 9 cm figure masks significant liquid water content in the base layers. Thin early-season coverage means rock hazard on the lowest slopes.

How Verbier stacks up regionally

Nearby resorts show similar totals at their upper elevations: Avoriaz (37 km north) 29 cm, Chamonix (32 km south-east) 27 cm, Cervinia (35 km south-east) 23 cm, Verbier 24 cm. All are within forecast uncertainty, so the region is fairly even.

Verbier belongs to the 4 Vallées domain, which spreads across Nendaz, Veysonnaz and Thyon as well. The high peaks of Mont Fort and Mont Gelé catch the most, while valley-floor terrain lags.

Thursday 26: when to go high

Thursday 26 November is the peak snow day for the upper mountain: 7 cm in the mid-range forecast (up to 13 cm). That is a fresh powder coat, not a rescue operation. The morning after—Friday 27—may offer the best skiing once lifts and avalanche work are done.

Go to Mont Fort and its glacier for the most reliable depth and the safest snow. The shaded north-facing Mont Gelé and Tortin slopes stay cold and preserve fresh powder longest. Avoid lower Savoleyres and Le Châble terrain until the upper slopes are tracked out.

Forecast uncertainty: when to decide

The range at the top is wide: 5 to 42 cm for the week. One scenario brings a decent early-season snow event; another delivers almost nothing. Storm timing usually firms up first; total accumulation keeps shifting until a day or two before arrival.

Check the forecast again on Monday or Tuesday of next week before booking guides or planning a trip. Early November snow is thin enough that a few centimetres make a real difference to skiability.

Tips for skiers

  • Go high to Mont Fort or the shaded Mont Gelé and Tortin slopes; treat the lower Savoleyres runs and Le Châble as secondary until upper slopes are tracked.
  • Thursday 26 November is the peak snow day. Plan your visit for Friday 27 morning if you can, after lifts and avalanche control are complete.
  • The Swiss avalanche bulletin is essential reading if you plan to ski off-piste; new snow brings new instability.
  • Pack wet-weather gear for the valley: the 9 cm at the base is mostly rain-on-snow; boots and waterproofs matter as much as thermal layers.
  • Watch the forecast mid-week. The 5–42 cm range means conditions could shift; confirm before committing transport and accommodation.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes on the date shown; they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
