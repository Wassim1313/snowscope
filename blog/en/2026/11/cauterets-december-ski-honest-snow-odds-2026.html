Is Cauterets worth the risk for a December ski trip?

PyreneesGuidesPublished Saturday 14 November 2026 · 17:41 UTCData of 14 November4 min read

Cauterets is a tempting choice for a budget trip, but the snow in early December is often thin. If you want the best conditions, our history shows you should wait until the final week of the month. While the prices are low, you are making a gamble on the natural cover in the French Pyrenees.

The honest answer first

Cauterets is a small, easy ski area: the mapped area has 25 km of runs and 9 lifts, with a vertical of 695 m. A strong skier who wants new runs every day will know it quickly, but it is a relaxed place to get your ski legs back.

The most important thing to know is that the lowest lift starts at 1725 m. This is quite high for the Pyrenees, which helps when the weather is warm. However, the usual December depth at this base height is only 23 cm. In a thin winter, like in 1989-90, that depth can drop as low as 2 cm, which makes skiing impossible at the bottom.

Early vs late December in numbers

The timing of your trip changes everything in Cauterets. In the first week, from 1 to 7 December, the base at 1725 m has 30 cm of snow in only 28 % of past winters. If you go during the week of 15 to 21 December, that chance stays at 37 %. It is a nervous wait for the snow to arrive.

The numbers look much better if you book for the very end of the month. From 22 to 28 December, the chance of having 30 cm or more at the base rises to 54 %. At the top of the lifts, which reaches 2420 m, the chance for 30 cm of cover is 72 % during that same final week. The usual depth on 15 December at the top is 34 cm, so the higher you stay, the safer your trip will be.

What the lift pass costs

One thing that has not changed is that Cauterets remains very affordable for a winter holiday. An adult 1-day pass is 46 EUR, which is a fair price for 25 km of mapped runs. If you are staying for a full week, the adult 6-day pass costs 276 EUR. These prices make it much easier to return to the sport without spending a fortune.

Today is 13 November 2026, and the mountain is currently waiting for winter. There is 0 cm of snow at the base and middle heights. The top of the lifts at 2420 m also has 0 cm, but the forecast shows 8 cm of new snow in the next 7 days. Between 13 November and 26 November, the two-week outlook for the middle and above shows a range of 9 cm to 39 cm, so the season is just starting to wake up.

Cauterets or Grand Tourmalet

When comparing Cauterets to Grand Tourmalet, you have to choose between size and height. Grand Tourmalet is much larger with 54 km of runs and 29 lifts. It also costs more, with an adult 1-day pass at 55 EUR and a 6-day pass at 294 EUR. However, bigger is not always better in a usual December.

The lowest lift at Grand Tourmalet is at 1459 m, which is much lower than the 1725 m base at Cauterets. Because of this, the usual December depth at the Grand Tourmalet base is only 15 cm. Even at the top, Cauterets usually has more snow, with 42 cm compared to 36 cm at the top of Grand Tourmalet. For a December trip, the higher base at Cauterets is a safer bet.

Has December got worse?

Our snow history of 46 past winters shows a clear trend that we cannot ignore. In the first 15 winters from 1980-81 to 1994-95, the middle of the mountain at 2072 m had a December average of 35 cm. In the last 15 winters from 2011-12 to 2025-26, that average has dropped to 23 cm.

Last winter, in 2025-26, the December average at the base was only 17 cm. While the usual season peak at the top can reach 180 cm, that usually happens much later than December. In a usual winter, the first lasting snow arrives at the top on 1 November and at the base on 4 November. It is a slow start, so being honest about the numbers is the only way to plan a good trip.

Tips for skiers

  • Book your trip for the week of 22-28 December when the chance of 50 cm at the top is 59 %.
  • Stick to the runs near the top of the lifts at 2420 m where the snow is usually 42 cm deep in December.
  • Choose Cauterets over Grand Tourmalet if you want a cheaper 46 EUR daily pass and a higher base area.
  • Check the avalanche bulletin before you think about leaving the 25 km of mapped runs.
  • Look for the 276 EUR 6-day pass on the official site to keep your trip low-cost.
