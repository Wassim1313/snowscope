Snow in Oslo today: 4 cm in town, 37 cm up at Tryvann (13 November 2026)

ScandinaviaGuidesPublished Friday 13 November 2026 · 18:29 UTCData of 13 November2 min read

Oslo woke up to two different winters on Friday. In town, the Blindern weather station measured 4 cm of snow on the ground, after 1 cm the day before. Up the hill at Tryvannshøgda, 528 m above the sea, the depth jumped from 5 cm to 37 cm in one night. If you want white ground, go up, not out.

What the stations measured this morning

Five stations around the city reported a snow depth today. Blindern (97 m, by the university): 4 cm. Asker (163 m): 4 cm. Hakadal (170 m, north of Nittedal): 15 cm. Bjørnholt (360 m, deep in Nordmarka): 31 cm. Tryvannshøgda (528 m): 37 cm.

The pattern is simple. Every hundred metres of height adds snow, and the forest north of the city got far more than the streets. Most of the 37 cm at Tryvann fell in the last day: the station had 5 cm on Thursday.

Is this early for Oslo?

A little. Our snow history for the city at Blindern's height covers 46 past winters. On 13 November there was snow on the ground in 20 of them, and the middle value for the date is under 1 cm. In half of the winters the snow cover had started by 17 November, so this year is a few days ahead, not a record.

The last five winters were mostly green on this date: only 2023-24 had a few centimetres. By 1 December the usual depth in town is about 5 cm, by mid-December about 12 cm, and by mid-January about 28 cm.

The weekend: cold Saturday, milder Sunday, rain on Monday

Saturday looks dry in Oslo with frost at night, down to about -4 °C, and only a few degrees above zero in the afternoon. The thin snow in town should hold.

Sunday turns milder, up to about 6 °C, and both forecasts we read bring around 10 mm of rain to the city on Monday. In the streets and parks that will most likely be the end of this first snow. Up in Nordmarka, with 30 cm or more on the ground, the cover has a much better chance.

Tips for skiers

  • For a white walk this weekend, take the metro up to Frognerseteren or Holmenkollen rather than staying in the city parks.
  • Thin early snow on forest roads hides ice and puddles: good shoes with grip beat sneakers.
  • Groomed ski tracks need much more than 30 cm on the ground; do not expect them to be prepared from this snow alone.

About these numbers

Snow depth today: measured at weather stations on the morning of 13 November 2026 (MET Norway station reports in the international SYNOP format); a station reports the snow on its own small plot, so a few kilometres away and a hundred metres higher the ground can look different. Usual depth for the date: SnowScope snow history, 46 past winters (1980-81 to 2025-26), a model of the past at the city's height, not a station record. Weekend: two weather models (ECMWF IFS and DWD ICON, read through Open-Meteo), given as a range. Places and rules: the official pages listed below.
