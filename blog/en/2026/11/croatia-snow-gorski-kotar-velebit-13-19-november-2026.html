Croatia snow: Gorski kotar and Velebit turned white on 13 November, then it melted

Europe snow by countryGuidesPublished Thursday 19 November 2026 · 16:39 UTCData of 19 November4 min read

Croatia's mountains had a short but real taste of winter last week. On Friday 13 November about 30 cm of snow fell on the upper slopes of northern Velebit, 29 cm on Bjelolasica and 19 cm on Platak and Risnjak. Four days later it had melted: on Thursday 19 November no Croatian ski area or ski mountain has snow on the ground. Here is how the week went, what the weather stations measured, and when the next snow is due.

Friday 13 November: one day of snow

Almost all of the week's snow fell in a single day, along the high ridges between Rijeka and the Lika plateau. Zavižan, at the top of northern Velebit (1,676 m), picked up about 30 cm on its upper slopes. Bjelolasica, the old ski mountain of Gorski kotar at 1,534 m, got 29 cm. Platak, the ski area above Rijeka, and the Risnjak massif next to it each had about 19 cm.

Inland the fall was much thinner. Sljeme above Zagreb only saw a dusting of about 3 cm, and the hills of Hrvatsko zagorje stayed green. Fresh snow always settles, so the depth on the ground never reached the amount that fell: on Velebit, 30 cm of new snow became a cover of about 10 to 11 cm within two days.

What the weather stations measured

The weather station at Zavižan, at 1,597 m on northern Velebit, is Croatia's highest. It had 16 cm of snow on the ground on Saturday 14 and Sunday 15 November, 5 cm on Monday 16 November, and bare ground on Tuesday 17 November. In Gorski kotar, the station at Parg (863 m), near Čabar, had 8 cm on Saturday morning, 2 cm on Sunday and nothing on Monday. Gospić, in Lika at 565 m, woke up to 2 cm on Saturday, gone by the next morning.

On Medvednica, the station at Puntijarka (988 m), just below Sljeme, measured bare ground every morning of the week. Zagreb never saw lying snow at all.

The ski areas, day by day

The snow lasted three to four days on the upper slopes. Rudnik Tršće, the small ski slope near Čabar with lifts up to 1,019 m, had the deepest cover in the country: 29 cm on Saturday 14 November, 27 cm on Sunday and 17 cm on Monday, then nothing on Tuesday. Platak, top lift at 1,354 m, went from 9 cm on Saturday to 14 cm on Sunday and 10 cm on Monday, and was bare by Tuesday.

Bjelolasica kept its snow the longest, with 12 cm on its upper slopes on Monday 16 November. On Zavižan the upper slopes peaked at 11 cm on Sunday. Since Wednesday 18 November every one of them has been bare, and today, Thursday 19 November, is a dry day with no new snow anywhere in Croatia's mountains.

Is a green mid-November normal?

Yes. At Platak's middle station, around 1,220 m, bare ground on 19 November is the usual picture: in past winters the ground there was white on this date in only 34% of years. On Sljeme, at 882 m, it was white in just 11% of years. Croatian skiing usually starts once December brings colder air and lift operators can add their own snow, so a melt after one early storm is nothing unusual.

What last week did show is how fast the high ground of Gorski kotar and Velebit can turn white when a cold northern flow meets moist Adriatic air. That is the pattern skiers are waiting for.

When does snow come back?

Not before the start of next week. Friday 20, Saturday 21 and Sunday 22 November look mostly dry, with at most 3 cm on Platak on Sunday in the highest forecast. The next real chance is Monday 23 November: Bjelolasica gets 2 to 9 cm on its upper slopes, with 7 cm in the middle forecast; Zavižan 2 to 5 cm, middle 4 cm; Platak 0 to 8 cm, middle 2 cm.

Another light round is possible on Thursday 26 November, up to 6 to 7 cm in the highest forecasts for Platak, Zavižan and Bjelolasica, but the middle forecast keeps it dry. Over the whole week from 20 to 26 November that adds up to about 7 cm for Bjelolasica (2 to 16 cm), 4 cm for Zavižan (2 to 11 cm), 3 cm for Platak (0 to 17 cm) and close to nothing for Sljeme (0 to 4 cm).

Across the border

If you need snow this weekend, look north-west. Slovenia's high ski mountains are white again today: the upper slopes of Kanin have 28 cm and Kranjska Gora 20 cm. Bosnia and Herzegovina is in the same position as Croatia, bare now with snow forecast later next week, as our Bosnia and Herzegovina report explains.

For Croatia itself, the best plan is to watch the forecast for Monday 23 November and check Platak's own channels before driving up: a few centimetres on grass is not enough to open a lift.

Tips for skiers

  • Snow on Velebit and in Gorski kotar melted within four days: do not plan a ski day on last weekend's pictures.
  • Next chance of snow: Monday 23 November, a few centimetres on Bjelolasica, Zavižan and Platak.
  • Check each ski area's own channels for opening news before you go.
