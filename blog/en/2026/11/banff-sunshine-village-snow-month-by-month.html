Banff Sunshine month by month: snow depth from November to May

Western CanadaGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 14:51 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

Banff Sunshine Village sits high on the Continental Divide, and its season is one of the longest in Canada. Rather than a list of runs, this guide walks through the winter month by month, with the published facts of the resort and our snow history of 46 past winters (a model, not resort reports).

The setting

Sunshine Village lies in Banff National Park, about 15 km southwest of the town of Banff and 140 km from Calgary, and straddles the Continental Divide between Alberta and British Columbia. The top is at 2,730 m and the base at 1,660 m, a vertical of 1,070 m over 13.6 km² of terrain. An eight-person gondola carries skiers from the parking lot up to the mountain in about 10 minutes. The season usually runs from early November to late May, with about 914 cm of snowfall a year.

October and November: an early start

In our model, the first snow on the ground near the top usually comes around 18 September and at mid-mountain around 28 September. By November the usual depth is about 34 cm at mid-mountain, around 2,173 m, and about 42 cm near the top, around 2,686 m. That is enough for groomed runs, not for rocky or steep terrain. Early-season trips are realistic here, but expect a thin cover.

December and January: building the base

The usual depth at mid-mountain grows to about 68 cm in December and 84 cm in January; near the top to about 78 cm and 94 cm. These are cold months at this height, so the snow tends to stay dry. Short days and low temperatures are the price; dress for the cold, especially on the lifts.

February and March: the heart of the season

February brings about 95 cm at mid-mountain and March about 100 cm, close to the usual peak of the season, about 113 cm. Near the top the usual peak is about 136 cm. In 9 of 10 winters, mid-mountain had at least 50 cm on the ground for 18 weeks of the season, and these months are the core of them. This is also when the freeride zones such as Delirium Dive and Wild West are most often in good shape; entry requires an avalanche transceiver.

April and May: the long spring

April keeps about 97 cm at mid-mountain and is the deepest month near the top, about 116 cm. May still has about 71 cm at mid-mountain and 99 cm near the top. Spring days are longer and warmer; ski the high, north-facing slopes in the afternoon and the sunny ones from late morning, when the surface softens.

A mountain bucking the trend

Unlike many resorts, our model shows more snow here in recent winters. Comparing the first 15 winters of the record with the last 15, the usual peak at mid-mountain rose from about 108 cm to about 130 cm, and near the top from about 129 cm to about 154 cm. Last winter, 2025-26, peaked at about 150 cm at mid-mountain. The numbers are modest compared with coastal resorts, so the cold, dry snow matters more than the depth.

Staying and safety

Most visitors stay in the town of Banff and drive or take a shuttle to the gondola. Beyond the boundary, and inside the freeride zones, read the daily Avalanche Canada forecast for the Banff, Yoho and Kootenay region, carry a transceiver, probe and shovel, and travel with trained partners.

Tips for skiers

  • For the best mix of depth and cold, dry snow, plan February or March.
  • Early-season trips are realistic, but stay on groomed runs while the cover is thin.
  • Pack for the cold: at this height January can be harsh on the lifts.
  • Carry a transceiver and read the Avalanche Canada forecast before any freeride zone.
