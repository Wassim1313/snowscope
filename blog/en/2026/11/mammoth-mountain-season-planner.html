Mammoth Mountain season planner: when the snow is best, month by month

US WestGuidesPublished Monday 9 November 2026 · 20:36 UTCData of 9 November3 min read

Mammoth Mountain has one of the longest seasons in California, but its winters swing between huge and lean. This planner splits the season into parts and tells you what to expect in each, from the published facts of the mountain and our snow history of 46 past winters (a model, not resort reports).

The mountain in brief

The summit is at 11,059 ft (3,371 m). The area has more than 3,500 acres of skiable terrain, 3,100 ft (940 m) of vertical and 25 lifts, and it gets about 400 in of snow a year with sunshine on about 300 days. Dave McCoy founded it in 1953. Our model looks at three heights: the lower slopes near 8,061 ft (2,457 m), mid-mountain near 9,541 ft (2,908 m) and the upper mountain near 11,024 ft (3,360 m).

Before Christmas: a gamble

In our model the first snow on the ground at mid-mountain usually comes around 24 October, but the cover grows slowly: about 6 in (14 cm) in November and 28 in (71 cm) in December in a usual winter. Early-season trips can work in a good year, but book flexible, ski the groomed runs and expect thin snow off-piste.

January and February: the season fills in

The usual depth at mid-mountain reaches about 50 in (128 cm) in January and 64 in (162 cm) in February. These are the months of the big Pacific storms, which can bring heavy snow in a few days and then long sunny spells. Plan a few flexible days around a storm if you can.

March: the safest bet

March is the deepest month in our model, about 74 in (187 cm) at mid-mountain, and the usual peak of the season is about 93 in (235 cm) at mid-mountain and 109 in (276 cm) near the top. In 9 of 10 winters, mid-mountain had at least 20 in (50 cm) on the ground for 13 weeks of the season, most of them from February to April.

April to June: the long spring

Snow lasts. At mid-mountain the usual depth is still about 57 in (145 cm) in April and 21 in (54 cm) in May; near the top it is about 74 in (189 cm) in April and 30 in (77 cm) in May. The last snow on the ground at mid-mountain usually goes around 11 June. Spring snow is best from late morning, once the sun has softened the frozen surface, and the upper lifts keep the best of it.

Big winters, small winters

Mammoth's numbers swing more than most. At mid-mountain the season peak ranged from about 33 in (84 cm) in the leanest of the 46 winters to about 209 in (531 cm) in the deepest; in March, 9 winters out of 10 fall between about 41 in (105 cm) and 133 in (337 cm). Last winter, 2025-26, was a big one, with a peak of about 118 in (300 cm) at mid-mountain. Comparing the first 15 winters of the record with the last 15, the usual peak barely changed at mid-mountain, 91 in (230 cm) then and 89 in (225 cm) now, and near the top it is slightly higher, 109 in (278 cm) against 103 in (262 cm).

Getting there, staying and safety

The ski area is reached from the town of Mammoth Lakes by State Route 203, which leaves U.S. Route 395. Mammoth Yosemite Airport has limited scheduled service. In winter Tioga Pass is closed to through traffic, so from the west you drive around the Sierra; carry chains and check the roads. Most beds are in the town, linked to the lifts by road.

Beyond the boundary, read the forecast of the Eastern Sierra Avalanche Center and carry a transceiver, probe and shovel. Inside the area, respect closures after storms.

Tips for skiers

  • For the deepest and most reliable cover, plan for February to early April.
  • Before Christmas, keep your booking flexible: early snow is a gamble.
  • In spring, ski the upper lifts from late morning when the snow has softened.
  • Do not count on Tioga Pass in winter; carry chains on the drive.
