Cortina d'Ampezzo snow forecast 23–29 November 2026: only 5–21 cm, a light refresh

AlpsGuidesPublished Monday 16 November 2026 · 11:51 UTCData of 16 November3 min read

Next week brings a quiet spell to Cortina d'Ampezzo. The upper slopes will see around 13 cm of fresh snow between 23 and 29 November, with the range stretching from 5 to 21 cm depending on where the heaviest falls land. The base elevation, by contrast, will stay nearly bare.

When the snow arrives

Most of the week's total comes in a two-day push: Wednesday 25 November brings 5 cm (up to 7 cm) and Thursday 26 November delivers 6 cm (up to 9 cm) at the summit. These are the days when skiers will notice the refresh, though the amount is modest.

Before that, 17–20 November delivers just 4 cm across the top of the resort. The timing means the second half of the week matters far more than the first.

Height makes the difference

Cortina d'Ampezzo sits at a crossroads between valley and peak. At 2700 m you get 13 cm for the week; drop to mid-mountain around 1800 m and the total shrinks to 12 cm. At the base near 1100 m almost nothing falls—closer to 0 cm.

This spread tells the story: snow is reliable only once you are high. Lower runs risk rain or staying bare, so check conditions on the lower slopes before you plan your morning.

Where to find the best surface

Thursday 26 November is the day to ski fresh snow. At the summit, expect 6 cm in the middle of the forecast (possibly 9 cm in a wetter outcome). That is a good top-up for the groomers—a fresh coat that makes a difference on a prepared run.

The Tofane, Faloria and Cristallo areas hold snow better than the lower terrain around town. Focus on the north-facing Tofana runs above Pocol, where aspect and altitude combine to preserve what falls. Treat Cortina itself as the weak link: lower elevation and south-facing slopes mean snow lasts shortest there.

Uncertainty in the forecast

The range of 5 to 21 cm is wide. The middle value of 13 cm is only the halfway point, and the actual total could land anywhere within it. Early-season snow is also thinner and more prone to compression, so do not expect depth to feel generous even at the summit.

Storm timing tends to firm up faster than total amounts. By mid-week, you will have a clearer sense of which day brings the most snow.

How the Dolomites stack up

Cortina d'Ampezzo is not alone in this quiet week. Val Gardena, 30 km away, gets 11 cm at the top. Hintertux in Austria, 71 km away, sees 14 cm. Mayrhofen, 75 km away, edges ahead with 17 cm. Cortina sits in the middle at 13 cm.

The Dolomites as a region receive less snow from north-west storms than the western Alps, and this pattern holds this week. No single resort stands out, so your choice can rest on terrain preference rather than chasing the snowiest area.

What this means for your plans

This is a maintenance week, not a base-building week. If you are chasing powder or deep turns, Cortina d'Ampezzo is not the place to be. If you want a tidy groom with fresh corduroy on top of existing snow, Thursday 26 is your window.

Before you book, check the resort's opening status and lift operations. Fresh snow on the mountain does not guarantee lifts are running early in the season.

Tips for skiers

  • Head for Tofane and the north-facing runs above Pocol; skip lower terrain around town where snow is marginal.
  • Thursday 26 November is the snowiest day—plan to ski then if the forecast holds.
  • Off the groomed slopes, new snow creates avalanche risk: consult the regional avalanche bulletin (bollettino valanghe) before going off-piste.
  • With 13 cm at the summit, this is not a week for deep powder—it is a week for fresh corduroy and maintained runs.
  • The base elevation gets almost no snow; rain is likely on the lowest runs, so check resort conditions before heading down.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes on the date shown; they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
