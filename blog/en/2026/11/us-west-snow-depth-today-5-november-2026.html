US West snow depth today: Kendall Mountain leads with 51 in (5 November)

US WestWhere to skiPublished Thursday 5 November 2026 · 10:27 UTCData of 5 November3 min read

Kendall Mountain has the deepest snow in the US West right now: 51 in (130 cm) on the upper slopes. Telluride trails by just 1 in (4 cm) with 50 in (126 cm), showing that the region's two highest resorts are pulling away from the rest.

Which height has the most snow

Altitude is everything in early November. At Telluride, the top at 12,507 ft (3,812 m) holds 43 in (108 cm), but drop to mid-mountain at 10,620 ft (3,237 m) and you find only 11 in (29 cm). The base at 8,734 ft (2,662 m) has almost none: 1 in (3 cm). Kendall Mountain's upper slopes sit lower at 9,528 ft (2,904 m), so the snow depth falls off faster with every metre down.

Three mountain stations measured the ground on 1 November. Rat Creek at 11,680 ft (3,560 m) reported 26 in (66 cm), with 9 in (23 cm) in the last 24 hours. Red Mountain Pass at 11,060 ft (3,371 m) had 24 in (61 cm), but that one spot saw 11 in (28 cm) fall in a single day. Beartown at 11,581 ft (3,530 m) also held 26 in (66 cm), though only 2 in (5 cm) arrived in the previous 24 hours.

How the region's snow cover is shrinking

Snow-covered ground in the US West shrank from 97,800 sq mi (253,300 km²) on 3 November to 91,746 sq mi (237,620 km²) today. The forecast shows another drop to 71,911 sq mi (186,250 km²) by 8 November. Resorts with any snow on upper slopes fell from 150 to 145 today and are expected to drop to 133 by next weekend.

This shrinkage is normal for early November as lower elevations warm and lose thin cover. The question is whether new snow arrives to replace what melts. For now, the two leaders at high altitude are holding on.

How today compares with past years

Kendall Mountain is tracking near average for the date. At 9,413 ft (2,869 m), today's 4 in (9 cm) sits deeper than in 25 of the past 46 winters. The usual depth on this date is 2 in (5 cm), so this is about average.

Telluride is well ahead of normal. At 10,620 ft (3,237 m), SnowScope shows 14 in (36 cm) today—deeper than on this date in 36 of the past 46 winters. Normal for early November is 4 in (11 cm). That 10 in (25 cm) surplus means skiers have real choices this week.

What happens next

The forecast adds 1 in (2 cm) across the region by 8 November. Kendall Mountain, Telluride and Lee's Ski Hill will all see small gains over the next few days, though the pace has slowed since the initial storms.

Watch the freezing level on 7 November. At Kendall Mountain it swings from 5,610 ft (1,710 m) to 11,647 ft (3,550 m)—well above the summit at 9,528 ft (2,904 m). That means rain or wet snow is possible everywhere that day, with potential for surface softening on south-facing slopes and near the top.

Tips for skiers

  • Ski high: 50 in (126–130 cm) at Kendall Mountain and Telluride sits on slopes above 9,500 ft (2,900 m). Below that, snow thins fast.
  • North-facing slopes hold snow longer than south-facing ones, especially early in the season when days are sunny.
  • Early-season snow is thin. Rocks and grass hide just under shallow cover. Stay on groomed runs unless you are sure of snow depth.
  • Before you go off-piste or touring, check the official avalanche bulletin for the area.
  • Check the SnowScope app the day before you travel: forecasts can shift, and by 8 November the snow-covered area will be noticeably smaller.

About these numbers

Resort snow depths are the deepest snow on the resort's upper slopes on the date shown; they are estimates for an area, not a measurement at one point. Forecasts can change. Always read the official avalanche bulletin before going off-piste.
