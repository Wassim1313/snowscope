Editorial policy

How the snow numbers, the resort pages and the snow reports on SnowScope are made and checked.

The snow numbers

Forecasts combine several weather models and satellite observations. Every day we compute the snow on the ground and the fresh snow over the mountains, then check them against snow measured on the ground. Where the numbers disagree, the measured snow wins. When the models disagree with each other we show a range, never the single most extreme value.

Resort pages

Each resort page is built from that day's snow numbers for the resort's own slopes (not the valley or the summit above the lifts), from the resort's heights, pistes and lifts, and from announced season dates when the resort has published them. Heights and dates are corrected by hand when a resort or a reader tells us they are wrong.

Snow reports and guides

  • Daily reports are written from the day's numbers. Before a report is published every number in it is checked against the data it comes from; a report that fails the check is not published.
  • A report is only published when there is something to say: quiet days get no report, or a short one that is kept out of search engines.
  • Daily reports are news: after a few days they point readers to the region's living page, which always shows the latest situation.
  • Guides and analyses are chosen by the team and written from our own snow records and from prices we have checked; their numbers go through the same check before they are published.
  • We do not write about places we cannot cover well, and we never publish anything we know to be wrong.

Corrections

Found a mistake? Write to us (see contact). We fix errors as soon as we can and correct the page itself.

Independence

Advertising and partner links pay for SnowScope, but they never decide what we write or how we rank resorts. Partner links are marked “Partner”; ads are marked as advertising.
