    The snow at Copper Mountain, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Copper Mountain (11,017 ft (3,358 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 31 in (80 cm) of cover (22 in (55 cm) to 38 in (97 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 49 in (125 cm), between 41 in (104 cm) and 59 in (151 cm) in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 60 in (153 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 74 % of winters, and by 1 January in 89 %.

    Best winter on record: 1983-84, peaking at 93 in (236 cm). Worst: 1980-81, which never passed 39 in (98 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to early June (206 days).

    Common questions

    Can you ski Copper Mountain at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 31 in (80 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 89 % of 46 winters. Right now: 17 in (44 cm).

    What is the best time to ski Copper Mountain?

    March: the snow peaks at about 60 in (153 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June.

    How deep does the snow get at Copper Mountain?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 60 in (153 cm) in late March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 93 in (236 cm) (1983-84), the thinnest 39 in (98 cm) (1980-81).

    What altitude is Copper Mountain at?

    Its highest lift reaches 3,756 m; the lowest starts at 2,959 m (797 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Copper Mountain have?

    About 115 km of pistes and 23 lifts.
