    About Plymouth Notch

    Plymouth Notch has about 0.2 km of pistes. The ski area spans the mountain from 686 m up to 726 m. Its nearest neighbour is Killington Resort, 9.7 km north, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    When the snow is good

    At about 706 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 19 January and lasts to 28 March: about 10 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 22 cm (lean years 5 cm, snowy years 45 cm). The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 22 cm and 80 cm. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. A normal season tops out near 49 cm, around 4 March.

    How Plymouth Notch is laid out

    The vertical drop is 40 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Because the lifts start as low as 686 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. The only lift is a chairlift. The skyline is dominated by Shrewsbury Peak, 1,122 m high and 6.8 km north-west of the resort.

    Plymouth Notch: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Plymouth Notch?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from mid-January to late March.

    How much snow does Plymouth Notch have at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 22 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    How high do the lifts go at Plymouth Notch?

    Between 686 m and 726 m, so 40 m of vertical drop.

    How many km of pistes does Plymouth Notch have?

    About 0.2 km of pistes and 1 lift.
