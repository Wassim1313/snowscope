    About Boží Dar

    Boží Dar is a ski area. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,034 m up to 1,037 m. The closest other ski area is Skiareál Novako (0.3 km, west).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 1,036 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. For the best snow, aim for early February and mid-February. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. The snow is usually deepest around 21 February, with about 27 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 10 cm (snow lay here in nearly every winter), with 45 cm in the best years. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 1 cm to 72 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Boží Dar gives 3 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The only lift is a moving carpet. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes.

    The snow right now

    Up high, Boží Dar has roughly 8 cm of snow on the ground today. The last seven days brought 4 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. An ordinary winter would have around 6 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track.

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Boží Dar?

    Aim for early February and mid-February.

    Can you ski at Boží Dar at Christmas?

    It is a gamble: only nearly every winter had snow at mid-mountain on Christmas Day (typically 10 cm).

    How high do the lifts go at Boží Dar?

    The top is at 1,037 m and the base at 1,034 m.
