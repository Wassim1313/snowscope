    What 46 winters say about Orelle

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Orelle (about 2,780 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 135 cm; half of the winters sit between 107 cm and 180 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 222 cm (171 cm–252 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 244 cm.

    By 15 December 93 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 98 %.

    Best winter on record: 1994-95, peaking at 365 cm. Worst: 2004-05, which never passed 201 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early November to mid-June (224 days).

    So far this winter: 87 cm on 27 November (usual: 67 cm).

    About Orelle

    Neither huge nor tiny, Orelle offers about 23 km of pistes (number 270 of 1,056 in the Alps). The ski area spans the mountain from 2,338 m up to 3,221 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Val Thorens, 3.4 km to the north.

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 24 graded runs, about 23 km in total. Most of the length is blue (50%, about 12 km). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. 1.6 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    This week's snow

    Snow on the ground at Orelle today: about 146 cm up high. The last seven days brought 121 cm of new snow. The next days look mild and dry; the cover is expected to settle to about 140 cm by Monday. An ordinary winter would have around 85 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track.

    The mountain at Orelle

    From top to bottom that makes 883 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. At 2,338 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Its top at 3,221 m is the third highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 2 gondolas, 3 chairlifts and 1 moving carpet. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. Above the ski area rises Aiguille de Péclet (3,561 m), 4.7 km to the north-east, about 340 m higher than the top lift.

    Snow at Orelle through a normal winter

    Count on about 29 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 2,780 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from early November to late May or later. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. Most winters land between 153 cm and 265 cm by mid-February. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 225 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. The snow is usually deepest around 9 March, with about 244 cm. For about 25 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 135 cm, from 72 cm in a poor year to 201 cm in a big one.
