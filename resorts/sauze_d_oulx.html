    What 46 winters say about Sauze D'Oulx

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Sauze D'Oulx (2,036 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 52 cm of cover (40 cm to 72 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 97 cm (80 cm–120 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 109 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 41 % of winters, and by 1 January in 59 %.

    Best winter on record: 2017-18, peaking at 204 cm. Worst: 2004-05, which never passed 73 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late November to mid-May (172 days).

    So far this winter: 30 cm on 27 November (usual: 23 cm).

    Questions about Sauze D'Oulx

    Can you ski Sauze D'Oulx at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 52 cm, and only 59 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 41 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Sauze D'Oulx?

    March: the snow peaks at about 109 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to mid-May.

    How deep does the snow get at Sauze D'Oulx?

    About 109 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 73 cm in the leanest winter (2004-05) and 204 cm in the biggest (2017-18).

    When does Sauze D'Oulx open for the 2026/27 season?

    The announced opening day is 5 December 2026; the last ski day is planned for 11 April 2027.

    What altitude is Sauze D'Oulx at?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,687 m; the lowest starts at 1,385 m (1,302 m of vertical).

    Who is Sauze D'Oulx best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 70% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    About Sauze D'Oulx

    With some 57 km of runs, Sauze D'Oulx belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 1,385 m and 2,687 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Pragelato, 5.7 km south-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 105 runs and around 57 km to ski. Most of the length is red (70%, about 40 km). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. The steepest pistes add up to about 3.6 km.

    The mountain at Sauze D'Oulx

    1,302 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Starting at 1,385 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. Its 18 lifts break down into 10 chairlifts, 3 button lifts and 5 moving carpets. 5 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Monte Motta, 2,823 m high and 7.3 km south-east of the resort.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 23 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 2,036 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from early December to mid-May. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 109 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 78 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 60 cm to 134 cm in mid-February in most winters. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 52 cm, with 98 cm in the best years. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 4 weeks.
