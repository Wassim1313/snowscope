    What 46 winters say about Montgenèvre

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Montgenèvre (about 2,188 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 76 cm of cover (55 cm to 100 cm in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 138 cm, between 108 cm and 160 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 144 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 63 % of winters, and by 1 January in 89 %.

    Best winter on record: 2017-18, peaking at 254 cm. Worst: 2004-05, which never passed 96 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 190 days.

    Today's 44 cm compares with a usual 37 cm for 24 November.

    About Montgenèvre

    With some 62 km of runs, Montgenèvre belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,795 m up to 2,581 m. The closest other ski area is Claviere (3.6 km, east).

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 114 marked runs cover roughly 61 km. Most of the length is blue (43%, about 26 km). With 65% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    At about 2,188 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 21 November and lasts to 25 May: about 27 weeks. For about 15 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. By mid-April the typical depth is 119 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March.     How Montgenèvre is laid out

    786 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,795 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Getting up the mountain: 3 gondolas, 2 chair-gondolas, 9 chairlifts, 4 T-bars, 3 drag lifts and 3 moving carpets, 24 lifts in all. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 3 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Mont Chaberton (3,132 m), 4.6 km to the north-east, about 551 m higher than the top lift.
