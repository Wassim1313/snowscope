    Stubai in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Stubai (2,084 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 50 cm; half of the winters sit between 40 cm and 62 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 88 cm (68 cm–98 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 94 cm.

    By 15 December 39 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 61 %.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (153 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2024-25 (72 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early November to early June, about 223 days.

    Stubai: quick answers

    Can you ski Stubai at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 50 cm at Christmas, and 61 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 42 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Stubai?

    March: the snow peaks at about 94 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early November to early June.

    How snowy is Stubai?

    About 94 cm at the peak of a normal winter (late March); between 72 cm in the leanest winter (2024-25) and 153 cm in the biggest (2000-01).

    How high do the lifts go at Stubai?

    The top is at 3,208 m and the base at 959 m.

    How many km of pistes does Stubai have?

    About 95 km of pistes and 46 lifts.

    About Stubai

    Stubai is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 95 km of pistes put it at number 54 of 1,056. Skiing here happens between 959 m and 3,208 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Sonnenbergbahn (0.6 km, north-west).

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 2,249 m of vertical, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Below about 1,159 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point.
