    The snow at Courchevel, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Courchevel (about 1,976 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 69 cm of cover (48 cm to 90 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 129 cm (106 cm–146 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 139 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 54 % of winters, and by 1 January in 83 %.

    The deepest winter was 1994-95 (227 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2010-11 (118 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 187 days.

    Questions about Courchevel

    Is there snow in Courchevel at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 69 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 83 % of 46 winters. Right now: 149 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Courchevel?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 139 cm). For a sure base, late November to late May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Courchevel?

    About 139 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 118 cm in the leanest winter (2010-11) and 227 cm in the biggest (1994-95).

    What altitude is Courchevel at?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,695 m; the lowest starts at 1,258 m (1,437 m of vertical).

    How big is Courchevel?

    About 151 km of pistes and 59 lifts.

    About Courchevel

    Around 151 km of marked runs place Courchevel among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 28 by length. The slopes start at 1,258 m and reach 2,695 m at the top. Méribel is the next resort, 5.3 km away to the south-west.

    Snow this week

    Up high, Courchevel has roughly 149 cm of snow on the ground today. 3 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. The coming days should add about 17 cm by Friday.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Courchevel gives 1,437 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Les Trois Vallées, for one, climbs to 3,221 m.
