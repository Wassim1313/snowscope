    What 46 winters say about Okemo Mountain

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Okemo Mountain (2,238 ft (682 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 8.3 in (21 cm) of cover (3.9 in (10 cm) to 12 in (31 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 17 in (44 cm); the middle half of winters range from 11 in (29 cm) to 23 in (59 cm).

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 19 in (47 cm).

    By 15 December 2 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 9 %.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (46 in (118 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2015-16 (6.7 in (17 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from early January to late March, about 95 days.

    About Okemo Mountain

    About 70 km of pistes make Okemo Mountain one of the ten biggest ski areas of US Northeast (number 6). Skiing here happens between 353 m and 1,012 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Plymouth Notch (13 km, north).

    Lifts and terrain

    659 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. The lowest slopes are at only 353 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Its top at 1,012 m is the third highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km.
