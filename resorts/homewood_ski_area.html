    The snow at Homewood, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Homewood (7,064 ft (2,153 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 28 in (72 cm); half of the winters sit between 16 in (40 cm) and 44 in (112 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 53 in (134 cm) (38 in (96 cm)–69 in (174 cm)).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 61 in (155 cm).

    57 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 76 % by 1 January.

    2022-23 was the big one (129 in (328 cm)); 2014-15 the lean one (28 in (71 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs late November to early May (168 days).

    So far this winter: 10 in (26 cm) on 27 November (usual: 7.5 in (19 cm)).

    Homewood: quick answers

    Does Homewood have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 28 in (72 cm) at Christmas, and 76 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 33 in (83 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Homewood?

    March: the snow peaks at about 61 in (155 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late November to early May.

    How deep does the snow get at Homewood?

    About 61 in (155 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (mid-March); between 28 in (71 cm) in the leanest winter (2014-15) and 129 in (328 cm) in the biggest (2022-23).

    How high is Homewood?

    Between 1,903 m and 2,403 m, so 500 m of vertical drop.

    Is Homewood good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 11 km of its graded pistes (31%) are difficult.

    Is there a larger resort near Homewood?

    Heavenly, 27 km to the south-east, with about 85 km of pistes.

    About Homewood

    Neither huge nor tiny, Homewood offers about 35 km of pistes (number 68 of 175 in US West). The ski area spans the mountain from 1,903 m up to 2,403 m. Its nearest neighbour is Homewood, 0.2 km east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    This week's snow

    Today there are about 83 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Homewood. The last seven days brought 1 cm of new snow. The coming days should add about 13 cm by Monday. So far the season is running close to normal, which is about 29 cm at mid-mountain.

    What there is to ski at Homewood

    Counting every graded piste, there are 70 runs and around 35 km to ski. Most of the length is blue square (53%, about 18 km). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Advanced skiers have about 11 km of black or expert runs.

    Snow at Homewood through a normal winter

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 2,153 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 5 December and keeps it until about 9 May, roughly 22 weeks. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 95 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 155 cm. For about 14 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. For the best snow, aim for early January and early March. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 68 cm and 227 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 72 cm, with 143 cm in the best years.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 500 m: runs of a moderate length. At 1,903 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Among the 8 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Heavenly reaches 3,037 m. Getting up the mountain: 4 chairlifts, 2 button lifts and 1 moving carpet, 7 lifts in all. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. The skyline is dominated by Twin Peaks, 2,701 m high and 6.6 km north-west of the resort.
