    The snow at Homewood, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Homewood (7,064 ft (2,153 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 28 in (72 cm); half of the winters sit between 16 in (40 cm) and 44 in (112 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 53 in (134 cm) (38 in (96 cm)–69 in (174 cm)).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 61 in (155 cm).

    57 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 76 % by 1 January.

    2022-23 was the big one (129 in (328 cm)); 2014-15 the lean one (28 in (71 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs late November to early May (168 days).

    Homewood: quick answers

    Does Homewood have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 28 in (72 cm) at Christmas, and 76 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 38 in (96 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Homewood?

    March: the snow peaks at about 61 in (155 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late November to early May.

    How deep does the snow get at Homewood?

    About 61 in (155 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (mid-March); between 28 in (71 cm) in the leanest winter (2014-15) and 129 in (328 cm) in the biggest (2022-23).

    How high is Homewood?

    Between 1,903 m and 2,403 m, so 500 m of vertical drop.

    Is there a larger resort near Homewood?

    Heavenly, 27 km to the south-east, with about 85 km of pistes.

    How big is Homewood?

    About 35 km of pistes and 7 lifts.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 500 m: runs of a moderate length. At 1,903 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Among the 8 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Heavenly reaches 3,037 m.
