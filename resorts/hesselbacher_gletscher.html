    About Hesselbacher Gletscher

    With roughly 1.0 km of runs, Hesselbacher Gletscher sits in the middle of the size table of Central Europe. Skiing here happens between 408 m and 495 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Eichholzkopf, 5.5 km to the south-west.

    Snow at Hesselbacher Gletscher through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 452 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. Year to year the snow is steady, with 0 cm to 15 cm in mid-February in most winters. At Christmas there was snow here in 5 winters out of ten, typically 1 cm, from 0 cm in a poor year to 12 cm in a big one. Depth normally peaks in late January at roughly 5 cm. For the best snow, aim for late January and early February.

    How Hesselbacher Gletscher is laid out

    87 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Because the lifts start as low as 408 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Familienskigebiet Sahnehang, for one, climbs to 825 m. To get up there are 1 T-bar and 1 moving carpet. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes.
