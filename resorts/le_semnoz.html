    The snow at Le Semnoz, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Le Semnoz (about 1,552 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 74 cm; half of the winters sit between 22 cm and 128 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 125 cm, between 75 cm and 170 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 136 cm.

    41 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 65 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1981-82, peaking at 500 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 46 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late November to early May (151 days).

    Today's 10 cm compares with a usual 14 cm for 28 November.

    Questions about Le Semnoz

    Is there snow in Le Semnoz at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 74 cm at Christmas, and 65 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 38 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Le Semnoz?

    March: the snow peaks at about 136 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early May.

    How snowy is Le Semnoz?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 136 cm in late March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 500 cm (1981-82), the thinnest 46 cm (2022-23).

    When does Le Semnoz have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from early December to late April.

    How high is Le Semnoz?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,693 m; the lowest starts at 1,412 m (281 m of vertical).

    Is Le Semnoz suitable for families?

    Yes: 63% of the graded pistes, about 10 km, are easy.

    About Le Semnoz

    Neither huge nor tiny, Le Semnoz offers about 16 km of pistes (number 367 of 1,056 in the Alps). Its lifts climb from 1,412 m to 1,693 m. The closest other ski area is Montmin (11 km, east).

    Pistes and who they suit

    The piste map lists 27 graded runs, about 16 km in total. Most of the length is green (beginner) (46%, about 7.5 km). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children.

    How Le Semnoz is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 281 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With its base at 1,412 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Its top at 1,693 m is the third highest of the 8 ski areas within 50 km. Its 9 lifts break down into 1 chair-gondola, 1 chairlift and 7 button lifts. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Roc des Boeufs (1,774 m), Crêt de Châtillon (1,702 m) and Crêt de l'Aigle (1,645 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    At mid-mountain (about 1,552 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 8 December and lasts to 30 April: about 21 weeks. For about 11 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 74 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 156 cm in the best years. The snow is usually deepest around 27 March, with about 136 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 96 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Year to year the snow is steady, with 40 cm to 196 cm in mid-February in most winters. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March.
