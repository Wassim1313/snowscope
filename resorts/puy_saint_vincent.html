    What 46 winters say about Puy-Saint-Vincent

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Puy-Saint-Vincent (about 2,038 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 69 cm; half of the winters sit between 47 cm and 86 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 117 cm (97 cm–133 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 120 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    50 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 80 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 2017-18, peaking at 200 cm. Worst: 2004-05, which never passed 72 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May, about 181 days.

    Skiing Puy-Saint-Vincent: the practical side

    The pistes run from 1,411 m to 2,664 m (1,253 m of vertical), with 42 km of marked runs and 12 lifts.

    About Puy-Saint-Vincent

    Puy-Saint-Vincent is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 42 km of pistes put it at number 140 of 1,056. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,411 m up to 2,664 m. Pelvoux-Vallouise is the next resort, 5.4 km away to the north.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Puy-Saint-Vincent gives 1,253 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. With its base at 1,411 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Its top at 2,664 m is the third highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km.

    Snow this week

    The upper slopes of Puy-Saint-Vincent hold around 93 cm of snow today. 7 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. The coming days should add about 32 cm by Friday.
