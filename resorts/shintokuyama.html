    About Shintokuyama

    With roughly 3.0 km of runs, Shintokuyama sits in the middle of the size table of Japan. The slopes start at 188 m and reach 437 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Sahoro, 9.8 km north, and 7 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Shintokuyama has 7 graded runs adding up to about 2.5 km. Green runs make up the biggest part: 1.7 km, or 68%. Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    Snow this week

    Today there are about 42 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Shintokuyama. The last seven days brought 16 cm of new snow. The next days look mild and dry; the cover is expected to settle to about 21 cm by Tuesday.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    249 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 188 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. The only lift is a chairlift.

    Shintokuyama: common questions

    What altitude is Shintokuyama at?

    Between 188 m and 437 m, so 249 m of vertical drop.

    Is Shintokuyama good for beginners?

    Yes: 68% of the graded pistes, about 1.7 km, are easy.

    Is there a larger resort near Shintokuyama?

    Sahoro, 9.8 km to the north, with about 24 km of pistes.

    How many km of pistes does Shintokuyama have?

    About 3.0 km of pistes and 1 lift.
