    What 46 winters say about Hemsedal skisenter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Hemsedal skisenter (1,040 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 79 cm; half of the winters sit between 65 cm and 96 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 113 cm; the middle half of winters range from 101 cm to 127 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 122 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 87 % of winters, and by 1 January in 98 %.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (186 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1995-96 (84 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early November to early June (211 days).

    Today's 17 cm compares with a usual 49 cm for 26 November.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    799 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. The highest named summit within 8 km is Skurvefjellet at 1,741 m, standing 302 m above the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,040 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 7 November and keeps it until about the end of May, roughly 30 weeks. The snow is usually deepest around 15 March, with about 122 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 79 cm, with 114 cm in the best years. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 17 weeks. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 87 cm and 144 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-March and late March. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 118 cm, plenty for sunny spring days.
