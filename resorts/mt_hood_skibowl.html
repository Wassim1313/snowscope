    The snow at Mt. Hood Skibowl, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Mt. Hood Skibowl (4,331 ft (1,320 m)), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 27 in (68 cm), with a normal range of 11 in (27 cm)–44 in (113 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 47 in (120 cm); the middle half of winters range from 25 in (64 cm) to 65 in (166 cm).

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 53 in (135 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 48 % of winters, and by 1 January in 63 %.

    Best winter on record: 2007-08, peaking at 186 in (473 cm). Worst: 1991-92, which never passed 17 in (43 cm).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from early December until late April in a normal winter, roughly 145 days.

    Common questions

    Is there snow in Mt. Hood Skibowl at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 27 in (68 cm) at Christmas, and 63 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 1.6 in (4 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Mt. Hood Skibowl?

    February: the snow peaks at about 53 in (135 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from early December to late April.

    How snowy is Mt. Hood Skibowl?

    About 53 in (135 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (late February); between 17 in (43 cm) in the leanest winter (1991-92) and 186 in (473 cm) in the biggest (2007-08).

    What altitude is Mt. Hood Skibowl at?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,528 m; the lowest starts at 1,113 m (415 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Mt. Hood Skibowl have?

    About 26 km of pistes and 9 lifts.
