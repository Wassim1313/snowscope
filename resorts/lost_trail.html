    The snow at Lost Trail, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Lost Trail (7,310 ft (2,228 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 33 in (83 cm); half of the winters sit between 24 in (60 cm) and 39 in (100 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 46 in (118 cm); the middle half of winters range from 40 in (102 cm) to 56 in (141 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 54 in (138 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 72 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 93 %.

    1996-97 was the big one (87 in (222 cm)); 2000-01 the lean one (36 in (92 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May, about 187 days.

    This winter so far: 5.9 in (15 cm) on 22 November, against a usual 9.8 in (25 cm) for the date.

    About Lost Trail

    With roughly 36 km of runs, Lost Trail sits in the middle of the size table of US West. Its lifts climb from 1,960 m to 2,495 m.

    The pistes of Lost Trail

    In all, 55 marked runs cover roughly 35 km. Most of the length is blue square (57%, about 20 km). Intermediate runs make up 57% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. The steepest pistes add up to about 7.7 km.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 2,228 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 21 November and lasts to 24 May: about 27 weeks. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 126 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 83 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 112 cm, a dry one 50 cm. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 84 cm and 154 cm. For about 16 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late March and early April. A normal season tops out near 138 cm, around 28 March.

    The mountain at Lost Trail

    Skied top to bottom, Lost Trail gives 535 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Even the lowest lift starts at 1,960 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Its 8 lifts break down into 5 chairlifts and 3 rope tows. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Saddle Mountain (2,572 m) and Hughes Point (2,501 m).
