    The snow at Schnalstal, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Schnalstal (about 2,602 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 93 cm, with a normal range of 69 cm–130 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 160 cm; the middle half of winters range from 129 cm to 193 cm.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 172 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 76 % of winters, and by 1 January in 96 %.

    Best winter on record: 2000-01, peaking at 292 cm. Worst: 1995-96, which never passed 120 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from late October until mid-June in a normal winter, roughly 240 days.

    Schnalstal: quick answers

    Can you ski Schnalstal at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 93 cm at Christmas, and 96 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 133 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Schnalstal?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 172 cm). For a sure base, late October to mid-June is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Schnalstal?

    About 172 cm at the peak of a normal winter (mid-April); between 120 cm in the leanest winter (1995-96) and 292 cm in the biggest (2000-01).

    What altitude is Schnalstal at?

    The top is at 3,200 m and the base at 2,005 m.

    How many km of pistes does Schnalstal have?

    About 33 km of pistes and 11 lifts.

    About Schnalstal

    With some 33 km of runs, Schnalstal belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 2,005 m up to 3,200 m. Ramudla is the next resort, 9.1 km away to the south-west.

    This week's snow

    Today there are about 133 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Schnalstal. The last seven days brought 33 cm of new snow. More is on the way: about 23 cm of new snow is expected by Friday.

    The mountain at Schnalstal

    From top to bottom that makes 1,195 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. Even the lowest lift starts at 2,005 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 1 reaches higher.
