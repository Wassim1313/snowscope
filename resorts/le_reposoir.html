    Le Reposoir in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Le Reposoir (about 1,030 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 23 cm, with a normal range of 10 cm–36 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 46 cm, between 36 cm and 57 cm in a normal winter.

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 47 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 2 % of winters, and by 1 January in 9 %.

    Best winter on record: 1983-84, peaking at 90 cm. Worst: 1992-93, which never passed 23 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late December to early April, about 101 days.

    Questions about Le Reposoir

    Is there snow in Le Reposoir at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 23 cm, and only 9 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is no snow.

    What is the best time to ski Le Reposoir?

    February: the snow peaks at about 47 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late December to early April.

    How much snow does Le Reposoir get?

    About 47 cm at the peak of a normal winter (mid-February); between 23 cm in the leanest winter (1992-93) and 90 cm in the biggest (1983-84).

    What altitude is Le Reposoir at?

    Between 978 m and 1,081 m, so 103 m of vertical drop.

    About Le Reposoir

    Le Reposoir is a small ski area by the standards of the Alps, with about 1.0 km of pistes. Skiing here happens between 978 m and 1,081 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Le Reposoir Chalet Neuf, 2.3 km to the west.

    Le Reposoir and the resorts around it

    For more terrain, Le Grand Massif (12 km east) has about 174 km of pistes against 1.0 km here. Le Grand Massif reaches 2,484 m, the most of the resorts listed.

    How Le Reposoir is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Le Reposoir gives 103 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 978 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Among the 9 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Le Grand Massif reaches 2,484 m.
