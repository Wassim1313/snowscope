    About Tannheim

    With roughly 7.0 km of runs, Tannheim sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,099 m up to 1,803 m. Füssener Jöchle-Grän is the next resort, 3.9 km away to the north-east.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 21 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 1,451 m in an ordinary winter, from mid-December to mid-May. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 37 cm, from 17 cm in a poor year to 61 cm in a big one. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. By mid-April the typical depth is 52 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 40 cm and 94 cm. A normal season tops out near 68 cm, around 28 February.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 704 m: enough height for proper long runs. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. To get up there are 1 gondola, 1 chairlift, 3 T-bars and 1 drag lift. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. Above the ski area rises Leilachspitze (2,274 m), 5.8 km to the south, about 471 m higher than the top lift.

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Tannheim?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to mid-May.

    How much snow does Tannheim have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 37 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Tannheim?

    Between 1,099 m and 1,803 m, so 704 m of vertical drop.

    What level of skier is Tannheim best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 58% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    Is there a larger resort near Tannheim?

    Hindelang Oberjoch, 9.2 km to the west, with about 22 km of pistes.
