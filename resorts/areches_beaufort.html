    The snow at Arêches Beaufort, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Arêches Beaufort (about 1,670 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 78 cm; half of the winters sit between 51 cm and 111 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 154 cm; the middle half of winters range from 130 cm to 183 cm.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 167 cm.

    By 15 December 65 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 87 %.

    The deepest winter was 1994-95 (286 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2010-11 (123 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until early June in a normal winter, roughly 195 days.

    So far this winter: 50 cm on 19 November (usual: 21 cm).

    Arêches Beaufort: quick answers

    Can you ski Arêches Beaufort at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 78 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 87 % of 46 winters. Right now: 40 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Arêches Beaufort?

    March: the snow peaks at about 167 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early June.

    How much snow does Arêches Beaufort get?

    About 167 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 123 cm in the leanest winter (2010-11) and 286 cm in the biggest (1994-95).

    When does Arêches Beaufort have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and early April; the cover is usually good from late November to late May.

    How high is Arêches Beaufort?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,319 m; the lowest starts at 1,022 m (1,297 m of vertical).

    Is Arêches Beaufort good for beginners?

    Yes: 60% of the graded pistes, about 22 km, are easy.

    About Arêches Beaufort

    With some 38 km of runs, Arêches Beaufort belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,022 m up to 2,319 m. The closest other ski area is Espace Diamant (13 km, north).

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 64 runs and around 37 km to ski. Most of the length is blue (57%, about 21 km). With 60% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.

    Snow at Arêches Beaufort through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 1,670 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to late May or later, about 27 weeks of good cover. A normal season tops out near 167 cm, around 3 March. For about 17 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. By mid-April the typical depth is 137 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and early April. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 101 cm and 206 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 78 cm (lean years 33 cm, snowy years 132 cm).

    The mountain at Arêches Beaufort

    Skied top to bottom, Arêches Beaufort gives 1,297 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Among the 7 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Paradiski reaches 3,205 m. To get up there are 1 gondola, 4 chairlifts, 8 button lifts and 2 moving carpets. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. First-timers get 2 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The skyline is dominated by Le Grand Mont, 2,686 m high and 3.4 km south of the resort.

    Other resorts close by
