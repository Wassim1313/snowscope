    About Salmo

    Salmo is a small ski area by the standards of Canada West, with about 6.0 km of pistes. The ski area spans the mountain from 696 m up to 1,027 m. Whitewater is the next resort, 30 km away to the north.

    The pistes of Salmo

    Salmo has 5 graded runs adding up to about 5.8 km. The largest share is blue square, with 2.7 km (47%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. The steepest pistes add up to about 1.1 km.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 331 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 696 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. The lift system counts 2 lifts: 1 T-bar and 1 rope tow.

    How much snow Salmo usually gets

    At mid-mountain (about 862 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 1 December and lasts to 17 April: about 20 weeks. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 38 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. For the best snow, aim for late January and early February. A normal season tops out near 90 cm, around 25 February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 56 cm, with 83 cm in the best years. Year to year the snow is steady, with 54 cm to 107 cm in mid-February in most winters.

    Salmo: common questions

    When does Salmo have the most snow?

    Aim for late January and early February; the cover is usually good from early December to mid-April.

    Is there snow at Salmo at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 56 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Salmo?

    The top is at 1,027 m and the base at 696 m.

    Who is Salmo best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 47% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How many km of pistes does Salmo have?

    About 6.0 km of pistes and 2 lifts.
