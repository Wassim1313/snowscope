    Orsa Grönklitt in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Orsa Grönklitt (476 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 34 cm; half of the winters sit between 22 cm and 43 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 56 cm; the middle half of winters range from 44 cm to 67 cm.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 57 cm.

    By 15 December 11 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 24 %.

    1987-88 was the big one (119 cm); 1995-96 the lean one (33 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to mid-April, about 127 days.

    Today's 21 cm compares with a usual 14 cm for 24 November.

    About Orsa Grönklitt

    With some 13 km of runs, Orsa Grönklitt belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of Scandinavia. The ski area spans the mountain from 393 m up to 560 m.

    What there is to ski at Orsa Grönklitt

    Orsa Grönklitt has 28 graded runs adding up to about 13 km. Most of the length is blue (48%, about 6.5 km). With 73% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    The snow right now

    Today there are about 36 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Orsa Grönklitt. The last seven days brought 5 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. That is close to a normal winter for the date (about 16 cm at mid-mountain).

    The mountain at Orsa Grönklitt

    167 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. To get up there are 1 chairlift, 5 T-bars, 8 button lifts and 1 moving carpet. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Hällbergsklacken (599 m) and Grönklitt (562 m).

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 476 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to mid-April, about 17 weeks of good cover. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 34 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 11 cm, snowy years 54 cm). The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 31 cm and 78 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 32 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. For the best snow, aim for early February and mid-February. A normal season tops out near 57 cm, around 10 February.
