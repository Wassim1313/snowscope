    About Izver

    Izver has about 0.3 km of pistes. Skiing here happens between 534 m and 592 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Rudnik Tršće, 21 km south, and 4 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    When the snow is good

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 563 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April. The snow is usually deepest around 27 January, with about 4 cm. For the best snow, aim for late January and early February. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 0 cm to 33 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in 6 winters out of ten; the usual depth was 2 cm (lean years 0 cm, snowy years 17 cm).

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Izver gives 58 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Because the lifts start as low as 534 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Platak, for one, climbs to 1,354 m. The only lift is a drag lift. Above the ski area rises Veliki vrh (1,153 m), 5.3 km to the south-east, about 561 m higher than the top lift.

    Beds near the slopes

    Among the closest beds to the pistes are Planinski dom na Travni gori (mountain hut), Koča na Kamnem griču (mountain hut), Gostilna in picerija Harlekin (guesthouse) and Turistični dom na Grmadi (mountain hut). Several of them are mountain huts, reached on skis or on foot.

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at Izver?

    Aim for late January and early February.

    How much snow does Izver have at Christmas?

    It is a gamble: only 6 winters out of ten had snow at mid-mountain on Christmas Day (typically 2 cm).

    How high is Izver?

    The top is at 592 m and the base at 534 m.

    How many km of pistes does Izver have?

    About 0.3 km of pistes and 1 lift.
