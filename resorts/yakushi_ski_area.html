    About Yakushi

    Compact and local, Yakushi has about 1.0 km of runs, small for Japan. The ski area spans the mountain from 136 m up to 257 m. Its nearest neighbour is Koide, 3.9 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Yakushi and the resorts around it

    For more terrain, Muikamachi Hakkaisan (13 km south) has about 11 km of pistes against 1.0 km here. The highest nearby is Muikamachi Hakkaisan, topping out at 1,168 m.

    The pistes of Yakushi

    In all, 4 marked runs cover roughly 1.2 km. Most of the length is black (60%, about 0.7 km). With 60% of the pistes graded difficult, it suits strong skiers looking for steep runs.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 121 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Because the lifts start as low as 136 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. Its summit is modest for the area; the highest lifts nearby, at Muikamachi Hakkaisan, reach 1,168 m. The only lift is a chairlift.

    Yakushi: common questions

    What altitude is Yakushi at?

    Its highest lift reaches 257 m; the lowest starts at 136 m (121 m of vertical).

    Does Yakushi have steep runs?

    Yes: about 0.7 km of its graded pistes (60%) are difficult.

    Which bigger ski area is near Yakushi?

    Muikamachi Hakkaisan, 13 km to the south, with about 11 km of pistes.

    How many km of pistes does Yakushi have?

    About 1.0 km of pistes and 1 lift.
