    Crystal Mountain in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Crystal Mountain (about 5,604 ft (1,708 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 69 in (176 cm); half of the winters sit between 51 in (129 cm) and 85 in (215 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 119 in (302 cm); the middle half of winters range from 96 in (245 cm) to 142 in (361 cm).

    The snowiest month is April, when the pack peaks at about 150 in (380 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 93 % of winters, and by 1 January in 98 %.

    The deepest winter was 1998-99 (244 in (620 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2004-05 (59 in (150 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to mid-June, about 221 days.

    Today's 3.5 in (9 cm) compares with a usual 17 in (43 cm) for 22 November.

    About Crystal Mountain

    Neither huge nor tiny, Crystal Mountain offers about 39 km of pistes (number 52 of 175 in US West). The ski area spans the mountain from 1,344 m up to 2,072 m. The closest other ski area is White Pass (35 km, south).

    The pistes of Crystal Mountain

    Counting every graded piste, there are 85 runs and around 38 km to ski. Most of the length is blue square (50%, about 19 km). Intermediate runs make up 50% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. Advanced skiers have about 13 km of black or expert runs.

    Run colours follow the North American system: green circles are easiest, blue squares intermediate, black diamonds difficult.

    The mountain at Crystal Mountain

    Skied top to bottom, Crystal Mountain gives 728 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,344 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Its 11 lifts break down into 1 gondola, 9 chairlifts and 1 moving carpet. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Silver King (2,133 m), Silver Queen (2,129 m) and Chinook Peak (2,104 m).

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 1,708 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-November to late May or later, about 28 weeks of good cover. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 176 cm; a snowy December gave 263 cm, a dry one 104 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 368 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. The best weeks for snow are usually late March and early April. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 380 cm. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 26 weeks. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 191 cm and 408 cm.
