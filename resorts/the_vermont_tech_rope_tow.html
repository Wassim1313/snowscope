    About The Vermont Tech Rope Tow

    The Vermont Tech Rope Tow is a ski area. Skiing here happens between 400 m and 438 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Harrington Hill, 22 km to the south-east.

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow at about 419 m, the middle of the ski area reaches a solid 30 cm base around 19 January and keeps it until about 25 March, roughly 10 weeks. Year to year the snow is steady, with 19 cm to 72 cm in mid-February in most winters. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and late February. Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 45 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 20 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 44 cm, a dry one 5 cm.

    The mountain at The Vermont Tech Rope Tow

    From top to bottom that makes 38 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lowest slopes are at only 400 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. The only lift is a rope tow. The highest named summit within 8 km is Old Hurricane Hill at 575 m, standing 137 m above the top lift.

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at The Vermont Tech Rope Tow?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from mid-January to late March.

    How much snow does The Vermont Tech Rope Tow have at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 20 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    What altitude is The Vermont Tech Rope Tow at?

    Its highest lift reaches 438 m; the lowest starts at 400 m (38 m of vertical).
