    What 46 winters say about San Pellegrino

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at San Pellegrino (1,864 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 29 cm; half of the winters sit between 15 cm and 44 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 44 cm (34 cm–63 cm).

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 51 cm.

    11 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 20 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 2013-14 (155 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2016-17 (32 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early December to mid-April (123 days).

    This winter so far: 33 cm on 26 November, against a usual 11 cm for the date.

    About San Pellegrino

    With some 53 km of runs, San Pellegrino belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 1,216 m and 2,513 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Passo Rolle, 7.3 km to the south.

    The pistes of San Pellegrino

    San Pellegrino has 82 graded runs adding up to about 53 km. Most of the length is red (48%, about 25 km). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. The steepest pistes add up to about 6.5 km.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, San Pellegrino gives 1,297 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Its top at 2,513 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. The lift system counts 18 lifts: 2 cable cars, 1 gondola, 9 chairlifts, 4 button lifts and 2 moving carpets. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Cima della Vezzana (3,192 m), 7.7 km to the south, about 679 m higher than the top lift.

    In this group of 7, San Pellegrino has the most pistes. If snow is the priority, Marmolada does better, with about 24 weeks of a good base instead of 16. The highest nearby is Marmolada, topping out at 3,235 m.

    How much snow San Pellegrino usually gets

    Count on about 16 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,864 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from late December to mid-April. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 31 cm and 79 cm. The snow is usually deepest around 2 March, with about 51 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 29 cm, and the lower runs are often thin by then. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 29 cm, from 4 cm in a poor year to 58 cm in a big one.
