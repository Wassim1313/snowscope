    About Val Pelens

    Val Pelens is a ski area. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,594 m up to 1,682 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Val d'Allos, 14 km to the north-west.

    How Val Pelens is laid out

    88 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Starting at 1,594 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Val d'Allos, for one, climbs to 2,592 m. All 2 lifts here are button lifts. Above the ski area rises Roche Grande (2,752 m), 7.4 km to the north-east, about 1,070 m higher than the top lift.

    Snow at Val Pelens through a normal winter

    At mid-mountain (about 1,638 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 18 January and lasts to 18 March: about 9 weeks. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 42 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 16 cm (lean years 2 cm, snowy years 41 cm). Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 9 cm, so the season is over at mid-mountain by then. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and late February. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 8 cm to 81 cm.

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Val Pelens?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from mid-January to mid-March.

    How much snow does Val Pelens have at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 16 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high do the lifts go at Val Pelens?

    Between 1,594 m and 1,682 m, so 88 m of vertical drop.
