    The snow at Les Sybelles, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Les Sybelles (1,835 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 67 cm of cover (43 cm to 83 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 127 cm; the middle half of winters range from 105 cm to 142 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 130 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 41 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 74 %.

    2019-20 was the big one (201 cm); 2022-23 the lean one (104 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until mid-May in a normal winter, roughly 174 days.

    Getting to Les Sybelles and what it costs

    Questions about Les Sybelles

    Is there snow in Les Sybelles at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 67 cm at Christmas, and 74 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 63 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Les Sybelles?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 130 cm). For a sure base, late November to mid-May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Les Sybelles?

    About 130 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 104 cm in the leanest winter (2022-23) and 201 cm in the biggest (2019-20).

    How high do the lifts go at Les Sybelles?

    The top is at 2,587 m and the base at 1,083 m.

    How many km of pistes does Les Sybelles have?

    About 153 km of pistes and 73 lifts.

    About Les Sybelles

    Les Sybelles is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 153 km of pistes put it at number 26 of 1,056. Skiing here happens between 1,083 m and 2,587 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Albiez-Montrond, 9.2 km to the east.

    This week's snow

    Up high, Les Sybelles has roughly 63 cm of snow on the ground today. 5 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. The coming days should add about 73 cm by Tuesday.

    The mountain at Les Sybelles

    1,504 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher.
