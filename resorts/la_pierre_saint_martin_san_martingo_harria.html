    The snow at La Pierre Saint-Martin, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at La Pierre Saint-Martin (1,832 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 21 cm of cover (8 cm to 37 cm in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 52 cm, between 27 cm and 79 cm in a normal winter.

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 54 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 15 % of winters, and by 1 January in 22 %.

    The deepest winter was 2012-13 (181 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1989-90 (31 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-December until mid-April in a normal winter, roughly 114 days.

    Today's 0 cm compares with a usual 2 cm for 21 November.

    La Pierre Saint-Martin: quick answers

    Is there snow in La Pierre Saint-Martin at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 21 cm, and only 22 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 6 cm.

    What is the best time to ski La Pierre Saint-Martin?

    February: the snow peaks at about 54 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to mid-April.

    How much snow does La Pierre Saint-Martin get?

    About 54 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 31 cm in the leanest winter (1989-90) and 181 cm in the biggest (2012-13).

    When does La Pierre Saint-Martin have the most snow?

    Aim for early February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-January to early April.

    When does La Pierre Saint-Martin open for the 2026/27 season?

    The announced opening day is 5 December 2026; the last ski day is planned for 29 March 2027.

    How high do the lifts go at La Pierre Saint-Martin?

    Between 1,519 m and 2,146 m, so 627 m of vertical drop.

    About La Pierre Saint-Martin

    La Pierre Saint-Martin is a mid-sized ski area with about 25 km of pistes, number 27 of 49 in the Pyrenees by length. The slopes start at 1,519 m and reach 2,146 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Candanchu (27 km, south-east).

    Pistes and who they suit

    La Pierre Saint-Martin has 53 graded runs adding up to about 25 km. The largest share is blue, with 14 km (55%). With 67% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, La Pierre Saint-Martin gives 627 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Starting at 1,519 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. The lift system counts 13 lifts: 5 chairlifts, 4 button lifts, 1 rope tow and 3 moving carpets. 3 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Pic d'Anie (2,504 m), 4.0 km to the south-east, about 358 m higher than the top lift.

    Snow this week

    Up high, La Pierre Saint-Martin has roughly 6 cm of snow on the ground today. 2 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 18 cm of new snow is expected by Tuesday. That is close to a normal winter for the date (about 10 cm at mid-mountain). The 2026/27 season at La Pierre Saint-Martin is announced to start on 5 December 2026 and to run until 29 March 2027.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 12 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,832 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from mid-January to early April. A normal season tops out near 54 cm, around 5 March. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 7 cm to 106 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 21 cm, with 70 cm in the best years. For the best snow, aim for early February and early March. By mid-April the typical depth is 20 cm; by then it pays to ski early, before the sun softens it.
