    Mount Timothy in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Mount Timothy (1,512 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 64 cm, with a normal range of 50 cm–81 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 89 cm, between 73 cm and 106 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 98 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 63 % of winters, and by 1 January in 83 %.

    2021-22 was the big one (152 cm); 1997-98 the lean one (72 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late November to early May (166 days).

    Common questions

    Can you ski Mount Timothy at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 64 cm at Christmas, and 83 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 9 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Mount Timothy?

    March: the snow peaks at about 98 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early May.

    How much snow does Mount Timothy get?

    About 98 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early April); between 72 cm in the leanest winter (1997-98) and 152 cm in the biggest (2021-22).

    How high do the lifts go at Mount Timothy?

    The top is at 1,650 m and the base at 1,374 m.

    About Mount Timothy

    Neither huge nor tiny, Mount Timothy offers about 26 km of pistes (number 23 of 53 in Canada West). The slopes start at 1,374 m and reach 1,650 m at the top.

    The mountain at Mount Timothy

    Skied top to bottom, Mount Timothy gives 276 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The bottom station at 1,374 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months.
