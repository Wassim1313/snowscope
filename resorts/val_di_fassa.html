    Val di Fassa in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Val di Fassa (about 1,883 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 25 cm, with a normal range of 13 cm–40 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 42 cm; the middle half of winters range from 33 cm to 54 cm.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 45 cm.

    9 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 20 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 2013-14, peaking at 128 cm. Worst: 2016-17, which never passed 24 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to late April, about 127 days.

    Common questions

    Can you ski Val di Fassa at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 25 cm, and only 20 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 30 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Val di Fassa?

    February: the snow peaks at about 45 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to late April.

    How much snow does Val di Fassa get?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 45 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 128 cm (2013-14), the thinnest 24 cm (2016-17).

    How high is Val di Fassa?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,435 m; the lowest starts at 1,331 m (1,104 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Val di Fassa have?

    About 78 km of pistes and 37 lifts.

    About Val di Fassa

    Val di Fassa is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 78 km of pistes put it at number 72 of 1,056. Skiing here happens between 1,331 m and 2,435 m above sea level. Buffaure is the next resort, 2.2 km away to the south-east.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Val di Fassa gives 1,104 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. With its base at 1,331 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March.
