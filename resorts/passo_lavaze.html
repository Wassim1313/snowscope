    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Passo Lavazè (1,800 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 27 cm, with a normal range of 12 cm–43 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 41 cm, between 31 cm and 57 cm in a normal winter.

    About Passo Lavazè

    Compact and local, Passo Lavazè has about 1.0 km of runs, small for the Alps. Its lifts climb from 1,763 m to 1,837 m. Its nearest neighbour is Jochgrimm, 3.0 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How much snow Passo Lavazè usually gets

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,800 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 27 December and keeps it until about 23 April, roughly 17 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 27 cm (snow lay here in nearly every winter), from 4 cm in a poor year to 59 cm in a big one. By mid-April the typical depth is 33 cm, and the lower runs are often thin by then. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 22 cm and 72 cm. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 46 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March.

    The snow right now

    Snow on the ground at Passo Lavazè today: about 25 cm up high. The last seven days brought 35 cm of new snow. The next days look mild and dry; the cover is expected to settle to about 16 cm by Monday. An ordinary winter would have around 18 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Passo Lavazè gives 74 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The bottom station at 1,763 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. To get up there are 1 button lift and 1 moving carpet. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. The skyline is dominated by Cimon del Latemar - Diamantiditurm, 2,842 m high and 7.5 km north-east of the resort.
