    About Saddleback Mountain

    Saddleback Mountain is one of the bigger ski areas of US Northeast: about 36 km of pistes put it at number 19 of 93. Its lifts climb from 655 m to 1,218 m.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Saddleback Mountain gives 563 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The lowest slopes are at only 655 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Getting up the mountain: 4 chairlifts and 1 T-bar, 5 lifts in all. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Saddleback Mountain (1,231 m), Potato Nubble (893 m) and Oakes Nubble (786 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 19 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 936 m) in an ordinary winter, from mid-December to late April. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and early March. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 82 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 42 cm, from 15 cm in a poor year to 66 cm in a big one. The snow is usually deepest around 23 March, with about 92 cm. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 54 cm and 114 cm.

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Saddleback Mountain?

    Aim for mid-February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to late April.

    Is there snow at Saddleback Mountain at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 42 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    What altitude is Saddleback Mountain at?

    Between 655 m and 1,218 m, so 563 m of vertical drop.

    Is Saddleback Mountain good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 11 km of its graded pistes (32%) are difficult.

    How many km of pistes does Saddleback Mountain have?

    About 36 km of pistes and 5 lifts.
