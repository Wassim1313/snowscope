    What 46 winters say about Donner Ski Ranch

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Donner Ski Ranch (7,395 ft (2,254 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 32 in (81 cm) of cover (15 in (39 cm) to 54 in (137 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 62 in (158 cm) (43 in (108 cm)–83 in (212 cm)).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 76 in (194 cm).

    59 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 78 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1982-83, peaking at 165 in (418 cm). Worst: 2014-15, which never passed 33 in (83 cm).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from late November until mid-May in a normal winter, roughly 174 days.

    Donner Ski Ranch: quick answers

    Is there snow in Donner Ski Ranch at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 32 in (81 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 78 % of 46 winters. Right now: 49 in (125 cm).

    What is the best time to ski Donner Ski Ranch?

    March: the snow peaks at about 76 in (194 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late November to mid-May.

    How deep does the snow get at Donner Ski Ranch?

    About 76 in (194 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (mid-March); between 33 in (83 cm) in the leanest winter (2014-15) and 165 in (418 cm) in the biggest (1982-83).

    How high do the lifts go at Donner Ski Ranch?

    The top is at 2,364 m and the base at 2,145 m.

    How many km of pistes does Donner Ski Ranch have?

    About 18 km of pistes and 8 lifts.

    About Donner Ski Ranch

    Neither huge nor tiny, Donner Ski Ranch offers about 18 km of pistes (number 104 of 175 in US West). The ski area spans the mountain from 2,145 m up to 2,364 m. Its nearest neighbour is Boreal, 1.5 km north-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Donner Ski Ranch gives 219 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. A high base at 2,145 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter.
