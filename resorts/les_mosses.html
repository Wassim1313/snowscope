    Les Mosses in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Les Mosses (1,625 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 51 cm of cover (31 cm to 68 cm in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 105 cm, between 86 cm and 126 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 112 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 33 % of winters, and by 1 January in 61 %.

    Best winter on record: 1981-82, peaking at 198 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 72 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early December to mid-May (163 days).

    So far this winter: 46 cm on 23 November (usual: 17 cm).

    Les Mosses: quick answers

    Does Les Mosses have snow over Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 51 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 61 % of 46 winters. Right now: 43 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Les Mosses?

    March: the snow peaks at about 112 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to mid-May.

    How snowy is Les Mosses?

    About 112 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 72 cm in the leanest winter (2022-23) and 198 cm in the biggest (1981-82).

    How high do the lifts go at Les Mosses?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,871 m; the lowest starts at 1,379 m (492 m of vertical).

    Can beginners ski at Les Mosses?

    Yes: 51% of the graded pistes, about 13 km, are easy.

    About Les Mosses

    With some 26 km of runs, Les Mosses belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,379 m and reach 1,871 m at the top. Château-d'Oex is the next resort, 6.2 km away to the north-east.

    What there is to ski at Les Mosses

    The piste map lists 39 graded runs, about 26 km in total. Most of the length is blue (51%, about 13 km). With 51% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,625 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to mid-May, about 23 weeks of good cover. Most winters land between 68 cm and 145 cm by mid-February. The snow is usually deepest around 2 March, with about 112 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 51 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 80 cm, a dry one 20 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and late February. For about 8 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 93 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered.

    Les Mosses today and the next days

    The upper slopes of Les Mosses hold around 43 cm of snow today. 20 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. The coming days should add about 20 cm by Thursday. For the time of year this is an unusually snowy start: mid-mountain normally has about 23 cm now.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Les Mosses gives 492 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. With its base at 1,379 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. The lift system counts 14 lifts: 4 T-bars, 7 button lifts, 2 rope tows and 1 moving carpet. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. The skyline is dominated by La Tarent, 2,548 m high and 4.4 km south-east of the resort.

    Staying nearby

    Places to stay close to the slopes of Les Mosses include Le Relais Alpin (hotel), Perle des Alpes (guesthouse), Pra Cornet (mountain hut) and Cabane des Monts-Chevreuils (mountain hut). Several of them are mountain huts, reached on skis or on foot.
