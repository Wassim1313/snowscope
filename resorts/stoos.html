    Stoos in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Stoos (about 1,590 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 49 cm; half of the winters sit between 34 cm and 58 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 84 cm, between 70 cm and 100 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 101 cm.

    28 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 61 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 176 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 60 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to late May, about 165 days.

    Today's 12 cm compares with a usual 17 cm for 28 November.

    Stoos: quick answers

    Can you ski Stoos at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 49 cm at Christmas, and 61 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 31 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Stoos?

    March: the snow peaks at about 101 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to late May.

    How deep does the snow get at Stoos?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 101 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 176 cm (1998-99), the thinnest 60 cm (2022-23).

    When does Stoos have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from early December to mid-May.

    How high do the lifts go at Stoos?

    Between 1,253 m and 1,928 m, so 675 m of vertical drop.

    Does Stoos have steep runs?

    About Stoos

    With roughly 19 km of runs, Stoos sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,253 m and reach 1,928 m at the top. Ibergeregg-Handgruobi is the next resort, 6.1 km away to the north-east.

    The pistes of Stoos

    In all, 30 marked runs cover roughly 19 km. Most of the length is red (64%, about 12 km). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Experts can count on some 5.0 km of difficult runs.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    675 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. Its top at 1,928 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 1 cable car, 17 funiculars, 3 chairlifts, 5 T-bars, 2 rope tows and 2 moving carpets. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. For small children there are 2 conveyor-belt lifts. Above the ski area rises Chaiserstock (2,515 m), 6.9 km to the south-east, about 587 m higher than the top lift.

    Snow at Stoos through a normal winter

    At mid-mountain (about 1,590 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 9 December and lasts to 20 May: about 23 weeks. A normal season tops out near 101 cm, around 5 March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 49 cm; a snowy December gave 65 cm, a dry one 21 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 74 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. Year to year the snow is steady, with 58 cm to 115 cm in mid-February in most winters. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March.
