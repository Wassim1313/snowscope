    About See

    See is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 33 km of pistes put it at number 190 of 1,056. Skiing here happens between 1,037 m and 2,567 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Tobadill, 6.1 km north-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    When the snow is good

    At about 1,802 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 29 November and lasts to the end of May: about 26 weeks. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 94 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and late March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 54 cm, with 78 cm in the best years. For about 3 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Most winters land between 65 cm and 126 cm by mid-February. A normal season tops out near 104 cm, around 21 March.

    Lifts and terrain

    1,530 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. To get up there are 3 gondolas, 2 chairlifts, 1 button lift and 4 moving carpets. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. 4 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Hexenkopf, 3,034 m high and 5.7 km south of the resort.

    What there is to ski at See

    In all, 71 marked runs cover roughly 36 km. Most of the length is red (73%, about 26 km). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Experts can count on some 2.7 km of difficult runs.

    Questions about See

    When is the best time to ski at See?

    Aim for early March and late March; the cover is usually good from late November to late May.

    Can you ski at See at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 54 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    How high is See?

    The top is at 2,567 m and the base at 1,037 m.

    Who is See best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 73% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How many km of pistes does See have?

    About 33 km of pistes and 9 lifts.
