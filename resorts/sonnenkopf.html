    The snow at Sonnenkopf, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Sonnenkopf (1,642 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 70 cm, with a normal range of 49 cm–86 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 133 cm; the middle half of winters range from 109 cm to 165 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 147 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    52 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 80 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 287 cm. Worst: 2024-25, which never passed 97 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May, about 191 days.

    Sonnenkopf: quick answers

    Can you ski Sonnenkopf at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 70 cm at Christmas, and 80 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 53 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Sonnenkopf?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 147 cm). For a sure base, mid-November to late May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Sonnenkopf?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 147 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 287 cm (1998-99), the thinnest 97 cm (2024-25).

    How high is Sonnenkopf?

    The top is at 2,287 m and the base at 998 m.

    Which bigger ski area is near Sonnenkopf?

    Ski Arlberg, 11 km to the north-east, with about 272 km of pistes.

    How many km of pistes does Sonnenkopf have?

    About 31 km of pistes and 12 lifts.

    About Sonnenkopf

    Sonnenkopf is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 31 km of pistes put it at number 199 of 1,056. Its lifts climb from 998 m to 2,287 m. Its nearest neighbour is Kristberg-Silbertal, 5.9 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    This week's snow

    Today there are about 53 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Sonnenkopf. 1 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 8 cm of new snow is expected by Saturday.

    The mountain at Sonnenkopf

    Skied top to bottom, Sonnenkopf gives 1,289 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. With the bottom at 998 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry.

    Other resorts close by

    The big name nearby is Ski Arlberg: about 272 km of pistes, 11 km to the north-east. The highest nearby is Ski Arlberg, topping out at 2,642 m.
