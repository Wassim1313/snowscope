    About Ski amadé

    Only 1 of the 1,056 ski areas of the Alps is larger than Ski amadé, which has some 605 km of runs. The slopes start at 667 m and reach 2,642 m at the top. Flachau is the next resort, 2.5 km away to the north.

    How Ski amadé is laid out

    The vertical drop is 1,975 m: one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. The lowest slopes are at only 667 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Its top at 2,642 m is the highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 3 cable cars, 2 funiculars, 50 gondolas, 2 chair-gondolas, 76 chairlifts, 35 T-bars, 32 button lifts, 3 drag lifts, 15 rope tows and 21 moving carpets. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 21 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Kraxnkogel (2,436 m), Ennskraxn (2,410 m) and Tagweidegg (2,135 m).

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 1,020 runs and around 600 km to ski. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 294 km, or 49%. No single level dominates; beginners and good skiers both find runs to suit them. The steepest pistes add up to about 34 km. 1.5 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Snow at Ski amadé through a normal winter

    At mid-mountain (about 1,654 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 1 December and lasts to 12 May: about 23 weeks. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 54 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 19 cm, snowy years 90 cm). A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 9 weeks. The snow is usually deepest around 3 March, with about 133 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 87 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Year to year the snow is steady, with 69 cm to 151 cm in mid-February in most winters.
