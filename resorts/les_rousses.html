    Les Rousses in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Les Rousses (1,362 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 21 cm; half of the winters sit between 2 cm and 54 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 50 cm (10 cm–65 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 50 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 22 % of winters, and by 1 January in 28 %.

    1981-82 was the big one (341 cm); 1992-93 the lean one (19 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-December until early April in a normal winter, roughly 98 days.

    Questions about Les Rousses

    Does Les Rousses have snow over Christmas?

    Not every year: about 21 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 28 % of 46 winters. Right now: no snow.

    What is the best time to ski Les Rousses?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 50 cm). For a sure base, mid-December to early April is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How much snow does Les Rousses get?

    About 50 cm at the peak of a normal winter (mid-February); between 19 cm in the leanest winter (1992-93) and 341 cm in the biggest (1981-82).

    How high do the lifts go at Les Rousses?

    The top is at 1,663 m and the base at 1,060 m.

    About 32 km of pistes and 20 lifts.

    About Les Rousses

    Around 32 km of marked runs place Les Rousses among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 193 by length. Skiing here happens between 1,060 m and 1,663 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Les Rousses (4.3 km, south).

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 603 m: runs of a moderate length. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point.
