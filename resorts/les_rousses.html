    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Les Rousses (1,362 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 21 cm; half of the winters sit between 2 cm and 54 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 50 cm (10 cm–65 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 50 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-December until early April in a normal winter, roughly 98 days.

    About Les Rousses

    Around 32 km of marked runs place Les Rousses among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 193 by length. Skiing here happens between 1,060 m and 1,663 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Les Rousses (4.3 km, south).

    The pistes of Les Rousses

    Les Rousses has 93 graded runs adding up to about 32 km. Freeride runs make up the biggest part: 9.9 km, or 32%. It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes. 9.9 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    How much snow Les Rousses usually gets

    Count on about 8 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 1,362 m in an ordinary winter, from mid-January to mid-March. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early February and mid-February. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 2 cm to 91 cm. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 21 cm, with 78 cm in the best years. The snow is usually deepest around 15 February, with about 50 cm.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 603 m: runs of a moderate length. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. The lift system counts 18 lifts: 1 cable car, 4 chairlifts, 5 button lifts, 4 drag lifts, 2 rope tows and 2 moving carpets. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Named peaks within a few kilometres include La Dôle (1,678 m), Pointe de Poêle Chaud (1,628 m) and Le Noirmont (1,567 m).
