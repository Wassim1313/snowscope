    What 46 winters say about Northstar at Tahoe Resort

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Northstar at Tahoe Resort (7,457 ft (2,273 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 24 in (62 cm); half of the winters sit between 10 in (26 cm) and 42 in (107 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 45 in (115 cm); the middle half of winters range from 30 in (76 cm) to 62 in (158 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 54 in (138 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 46 % of winters, and by 1 January in 63 %.

    The deepest winter was 2022-23 (125 in (318 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2014-15 (26 in (67 cm)).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from early December until early May in a normal winter, roughly 152 days.

    This winter so far: 18 in (45 cm) on 27 November, against a usual 3.9 in (10 cm) for the date.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Northstar at Tahoe Resort gives 704 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. At 1,921 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. The lift system counts 20 lifts: 2 gondolas, 11 chairlifts, 1 button lift and 6 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. First-timers get 6 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Mount Pluto (2,625 m), Mount Watson (2,566 m) and Lookout Mountain (2,466 m).

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow at about 2,273 m, the middle of the ski area reaches a solid 30 cm base around 9 December and keeps it until about 3 May, roughly 21 weeks. For about 11 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. The snow is usually deepest around 16 March, with about 138 cm. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 51 cm and 211 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 73 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 62 cm, with 140 cm in the best years.
