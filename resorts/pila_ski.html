    About Pila ski

    Pila ski is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 56 km of pistes put it at number 103 of 1,056. Its lifts climb from 1,533 m to 2,723 m. The closest other ski area is Cogne (9.4 km, south-east).

    The pistes of Pila ski

    The piste map lists 96 graded runs, about 55 km in total. Red runs make up the biggest part: 38 km, or 69%. Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. The steepest pistes add up to about 7.7 km.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 1,190 m: enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,533 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. Its top at 2,723 m is the highest of the 7 ski areas within 50 km. The lift system counts 16 lifts: 1 cable car, 6 gondolas, 6 chairlifts and 3 moving carpets. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. For small children there are 3 conveyor-belt lifts. The skyline is dominated by Monte Emilius, 3,559 m high and 5.9 km east of the resort.

    How much snow Pila ski usually gets

    In a typical winter the snow at about 2,128 m, the middle of the ski area reaches a solid 30 cm base around 3 December and keeps it until about 25 May, roughly 25 weeks. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 93 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Most winters land between 69 cm and 116 cm by mid-February. The snow is usually deepest around 7 March, with about 100 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 55 cm, from 30 cm in a poor year to 83 cm in a big one. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March.
