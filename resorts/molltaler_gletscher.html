    About Mölltaler Gletscher

    With roughly 21 km of runs, Mölltaler Gletscher sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 2,103 m and 3,084 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Sportgastein, 5.5 km north-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How much snow Mölltaler Gletscher usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 2,594 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from early November to late May or later, about 30 weeks of good cover. The snow is usually deepest around 25 March, with about 263 cm. Most winters land between 148 cm and 283 cm by mid-February. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 249 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late March and mid-April. For about 25 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 131 cm, with 196 cm in the best years.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 981 m: enough height for proper long runs. At 2,103 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. To get up there are 2 funiculars, 1 gondola, 4 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 1 button lift. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. The skyline is dominated by Hocharn, 3,254 m high and 7.7 km north-west of the resort.

    What there is to ski at Mölltaler Gletscher

    Mölltaler Gletscher has 41 graded runs adding up to about 21 km. The largest share is red, with 11 km (54%). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Advanced skiers have about 5.0 km of black or expert runs.
