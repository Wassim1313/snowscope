    About Grand Ballon

    Grand Ballon is a mid-sized ski area with about 6.0 km of pistes, number 617 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,065 m up to 1,307 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Le Markstein, 7.2 km to the north-west.

    Runs by difficulty

    Grand Ballon has 13 graded runs adding up to about 6.2 km. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 2.9 km, or 46%. It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    242 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 1 reaches higher. Getting up the mountain: 3 drag lifts and 1 rope tow, 4 lifts in all. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. Above the ski area rises Grand Ballon (1,424 m), 1.1 km to the north-west, about 117 m higher than the top lift.

    How much snow Grand Ballon usually gets

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,186 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 0 cm to 49 cm. Depth normally peaks in late January at roughly 9 cm. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. For the best snow, aim for early January and early February. At Christmas there was snow here in 6 winters out of ten, typically 3 cm, with 28 cm in the best years.

    Questions about Grand Ballon

    When does Grand Ballon have the most snow?

    Aim for early January and early February.

    How much snow does Grand Ballon have at Christmas?

    Sometimes. Christmas snow at mid-mountain came in 6 winters out of ten, usually about 3 cm, so check before you go.

    How high do the lifts go at Grand Ballon?

    Between 1,065 m and 1,307 m, so 242 m of vertical drop.

    Is Grand Ballon good for beginners?

    Yes: 51% of the graded pistes, about 3.2 km, are easy.

    Is there a larger resort near Grand Ballon?

    La Bresse, 18 km to the north-west, with about 24 km of pistes.
