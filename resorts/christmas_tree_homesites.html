    Snow at Christmas Tree Homesites through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 2,216 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to mid-May, about 23 weeks of good cover. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 78 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 10 cm, snowy years 170 cm). By mid-April the typical depth is 106 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early January and early March. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 15 weeks. Most winters land between 62 cm and 265 cm by mid-February. The snow is usually deepest around 16 March, with about 179 cm.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 418 m: runs of a moderate length. At 2,007 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. The highest named summit within 8 km is Castle Peak at 2,773 m, standing 348 m above the top lift.

    Christmas Tree Homesites: common questions

    When does Christmas Tree Homesites have the most snow?

    Aim for early January and early March; the cover is usually good from early December to mid-May.

    Can you ski at Christmas Tree Homesites at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 78 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high do the lifts go at Christmas Tree Homesites?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,425 m; the lowest starts at 2,007 m (418 m of vertical).
