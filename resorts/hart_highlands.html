    About Hart Highlands

    About 2.0 km of pistes make Hart Highlands one of the smaller places to ski in Canada West. Its lifts climb from 713 m to 759 m. Tabor Mountain is the next resort, 22 km away to the east.

    Nearby alternatives compared

    In this group of 3, Hart Highlands is number 3 for piste length. For more reliable snow, Purden Ski Village usually keeps a 30 cm base for about 24 weeks, against 13 here. The highest nearby is Purden Ski Village, topping out at 1,365 m.

    The mountain at Hart Highlands

    The vertical drop is 46 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 713 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Cranbrook Hill (701 m) and Cranbrook Hill (639 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    At about 736 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 21 December and lasts to 22 March: about 13 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually early February and mid-February. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 24 cm and 76 cm. A normal season tops out near 53 cm, around 5 February. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 31 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 17 cm in a poor year to 53 cm in a big one. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow.

    Questions about Hart Highlands

    What is the best month to ski at Hart Highlands?

    Aim for early February and mid-February; the cover is usually good from late December to late March.

    How much snow does Hart Highlands have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 31 cm deep.

    How high is Hart Highlands?

    Between 713 m and 759 m, so 46 m of vertical drop.

    How big is Hart Highlands?

    About 2.0 km of pistes and 3 lifts.
