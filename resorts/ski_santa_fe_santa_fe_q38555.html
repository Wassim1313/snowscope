    What 46 winters say about Ski Santa Fe

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Ski Santa Fe (11,214 ft (3,418 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 17 in (43 cm); half of the winters sit between 8.7 in (22 cm) and 46 in (116 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 52 in (131 cm) (13 in (34 cm)–85 in (217 cm)).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 60 in (153 cm).

    By 15 December 41 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 54 %.

    The deepest winter was 1982-83 (229 in (582 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2025-26 (22 in (56 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late November to early May, about 171 days.

    Common questions

    Can you ski Ski Santa Fe at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 17 in (43 cm), and only 54 % of the past 46 winters had 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 7.5 in (19 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Ski Santa Fe?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 60 in (153 cm)). For a sure base, late November to early May is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How snowy is Ski Santa Fe?

    About 60 in (153 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 22 in (56 cm) in the leanest winter (2025-26) and 229 in (582 cm) in the biggest (1982-83).

    What altitude is Ski Santa Fe at?

    The top is at 3,686 m and the base at 3,151 m.

    How many km of pistes does Ski Santa Fe have?

    About 29 km of pistes and 7 lifts.

    About Ski Santa Fe

    Neither huge nor tiny, Ski Santa Fe offers about 29 km of pistes (number 82 of 175 in US West). Its lifts climb from 3,151 m to 3,686 m. Terrain Park is the next resort, 0.8 km away to the west.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 535 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. A high base at 3,151 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter.
