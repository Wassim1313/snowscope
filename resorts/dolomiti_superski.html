    About Dolomiti Superski

    No ski area of the Alps has more pistes than Dolomiti Superski, with about 1,100 km. Skiing here happens between 848 m and 3,235 m above sea level. Alta Badia is the next resort, 2.4 km away to the north.

    How Dolomiti Superski is laid out

    2,387 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. With the bottom at 848 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. Its 485 lifts break down into 17 cable cars, 28 funiculars, 97 gondolas, 2 chair-gondolas, 188 chairlifts, 95 button lifts, 3 drag lifts, 3 rope tows and 52 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. For small children there are 52 conveyor-belt lifts. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Piz Boè (3,152 m), Cresta Strenta (3,125 m) and Piz dles Cunturines (3,064 m).

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 21 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 2,042 m in an ordinary winter, from early December to early May. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 42 cm and 102 cm. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. By mid-April the typical depth is 62 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 40 cm, with 76 cm in the best years. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 68 cm.
