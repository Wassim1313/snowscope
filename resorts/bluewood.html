    The snow at Bluewood, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Bluewood (5,108 ft (1,557 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 23 in (58 cm) of cover (11 in (27 cm) to 40 in (101 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 35 in (88 cm) (16 in (40 cm)–56 in (142 cm)).

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 37 in (94 cm).

    By 15 December 46 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 63 %.

    The deepest winter was 2001-02 (177 in (450 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2025-26 (13 in (34 cm)).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from early December until mid-April in a normal winter, roughly 130 days.

    Questions about Bluewood

    Is there snow in Bluewood at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 23 in (58 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 63 % of 46 winters. Right now: 5.9 in (15 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Bluewood?

    February: the snow peaks at about 37 in (94 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from early December to mid-April.

    How deep does the snow get at Bluewood?

    About 37 in (94 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (late February); between 13 in (34 cm) in the leanest winter (2025-26) and 177 in (450 cm) in the biggest (2001-02).

    What altitude is Bluewood at?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,728 m; the lowest starts at 1,386 m (342 m of vertical).

    How big is Bluewood?

    About 17 km of pistes and 4 lifts.

    About Bluewood

    Neither huge nor tiny, Bluewood offers about 17 km of pistes (number 108 of 175 in US West). The ski area spans the mountain from 1,386 m up to 1,728 m.

    Lifts and terrain

    342 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The bottom station at 1,386 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months.
