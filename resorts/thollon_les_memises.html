    Thollon-les-Mémises in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Thollon-les-Mémises (1,476 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 83 cm of cover (48 cm to 113 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 111 cm (80 cm–131 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 148 cm.

    59 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 83 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1981-82 (360 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2022-23 (90 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late November to mid-May (165 days).

    This winter so far: 38 cm on 23 November, against a usual 22 cm for the date.

    About Thollon-les-Mémises

    Thollon-les-Mémises is a mid-sized ski area with about 19 km of pistes, number 331 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,078 m up to 1,873 m. The closest other ski area is Thollon-les-Memises (2.0 km, north-west).

    The pistes of Thollon-les-Mémises

    Thollon-les-Mémises has 52 graded runs adding up to about 19 km. Most of the length is red (57%, about 11 km). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Advanced skiers have about 2.3 km of black or expert runs.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 1,476 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to mid-May, about 24 weeks of good cover. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-March and late March. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 16 weeks. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 83 cm; a snowy December gave 138 cm, a dry one 22 cm. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 148 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 130 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Most winters land between 63 cm and 152 cm by mid-February.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 795 m: enough height for proper long runs. To get up there are 1 gondola, 3 chairlifts, 8 button lifts, 2 drag lifts, 1 rope tow and 1 moving carpet. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. 1 moving carpet serves the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Les Cornettes de Bise, 2,432 m high and 7.1 km south-east of the resort.
