    The snow at Attitash, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Attitash (1,450 ft (442 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 11 in (29 cm) of cover (5.9 in (15 cm) to 15 in (37 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 24 in (62 cm), between 18 in (46 cm) and 30 in (76 cm) in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 26 in (65 cm).

    By 15 December 2 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 17 %.

    2007-08 was the big one (56 in (143 cm)); 2005-06 the lean one (14 in (35 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs late December to mid-April (109 days).

    This winter so far: 0 in (0 cm) on 27 November, against a usual 2 in (5 cm) for the date.

    Attitash: quick answers

    Does Attitash have snow over Christmas?

    Not every year: about 11 in (29 cm) at Christmas on average, with a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 17 % of 46 winters. Right now: no snow.

    When is the best month to ski Attitash?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 26 in (65 cm)). For a sure base, late December to mid-April is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How much snow does Attitash get?

    About 26 in (65 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 14 in (35 cm) in the leanest winter (2005-06) and 56 in (143 cm) in the biggest (2007-08).

    How high do the lifts go at Attitash?

    Between 190 m and 695 m, so 505 m of vertical drop.

    Is Attitash good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 10 km of its graded pistes (26%) are difficult.

    About Attitash

    Around 39 km of marked runs place Attitash among the larger resorts of US Northeast, number 15 by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 190 m up to 695 m. Cranmore is the next resort, 11 km away to the east.

    What there is to ski at Attitash

    In all, 87 marked runs cover roughly 39 km. The largest share is blue square, with 21 km (54%). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Experts can count on some 10 km of difficult runs.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 15 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 442 m) in an ordinary winter, from late December to early April. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 21 cm, and the lower runs are often thin by then. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 65 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 35 cm to 102 cm in mid-February in most winters. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 29 cm, with 50 cm in the best years.

    How Attitash is laid out

    505 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. The lowest slopes are at only 190 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Its 8 lifts break down into 7 chairlifts and 1 moving carpet. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. Above the ski area rises Bear Mountain (976 m), 7.4 km to the south-west, about 281 m higher than the top lift.
