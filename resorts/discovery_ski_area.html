    About Discovery

    With roughly 41 km of runs, Discovery sits in the middle of the size table of US West. The slopes start at 1,743 m and reach 2,486 m at the top.

    What there is to ski at Discovery

    In all, 50 marked runs cover roughly 41 km. Most of the length is blue square (49%, about 20 km). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Experts can count on some 11 km of difficult runs.

    Snow at Discovery through a normal winter

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 2,114 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 22 November and keeps it until about 25 May, roughly 27 weeks. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 15 weeks. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late March and early April. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 155 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 137 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 74 cm; a snowy December gave 112 cm, a dry one 41 cm. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 87 cm and 170 cm.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Discovery gives 743 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,743 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. To get up there are 7 chairlifts and 1 moving carpet. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. The highest named summit within 8 km is Red Lion Mountain at 2,662 m, standing 176 m above the top lift.

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Discovery?

    Aim for late March and early April; the cover is usually good from late November to late May.

    Can you ski at Discovery at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 74 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high do the lifts go at Discovery?

    The top is at 2,486 m and the base at 1,743 m.

    Is Discovery good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 11 km of its graded pistes (27%) are difficult.

    How many km of pistes does Discovery have?

    About 41 km of pistes and 8 lifts.
