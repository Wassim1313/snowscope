    About Wallberg

    Wallberg is a ski area. Its lifts climb from 791 m to 1,616 m. Its nearest neighbour is Hirschberglifte, 3.4 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,204 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 10 January and keeps it until about 17 April, roughly 13 weeks. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 26 cm, from 5 cm in a poor year to 54 cm in a big one. The snow is usually deepest around 1 March, with about 53 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and early March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 23 cm to 84 cm in mid-February in most winters. By mid-April the typical depth is 30 cm; by then it pays to ski early, before the sun softens it.

    How Wallberg is laid out

    The vertical drop is 825 m: enough height for proper long runs. With the bottom at 791 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 1,616 m. The only lift is a gondola. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. The highest named summit within 8 km is Halserspitz at 1,862 m, standing 246 m above the top lift.

    Questions about Wallberg

    When does Wallberg have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and early March; the cover is usually good from early January to mid-April.

    How much snow does Wallberg have at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 26 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    What altitude is Wallberg at?

    Between 791 m and 1,616 m, so 825 m of vertical drop.
