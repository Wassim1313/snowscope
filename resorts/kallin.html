    About Kallin

    Compact and local, Kallin has about 1.0 km of runs, small for Scandinavia. Skiing here happens between 16 m and 65 m above sea level.

    The pistes of Kallin

    In all, 5 marked runs cover roughly 1.1 km. Most of the length is blue (69%, about 0.8 km). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    The snow right now

    Up high, Kallin has roughly 22 cm of snow on the ground today. The last seven days brought 7 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. That is close to a normal winter for the date (about 9 cm at mid-mountain).

    The mountain at Kallin

    The vertical drop is 49 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The only lift is a T-bar.

    How much snow Kallin usually gets

    Halfway up the slopes, around 40 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 2 January and lasts to 25 April: about 16 weeks. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 56 cm. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 34 cm and 71 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 45 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 25 cm; a snowy December gave 40 cm, a dry one 8 cm.

    Questions about Kallin

    When does Kallin have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from early January to late April.

    Is there snow at Kallin at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 25 cm deep.

    How high is Kallin?

    Between 16 m and 65 m, so 49 m of vertical drop.

    Is Kallin good for beginners?

    Yes: 69% of the graded pistes, about 0.8 km, are easy.

    How big is Kallin?

    About 1.0 km of pistes and 1 lift.
