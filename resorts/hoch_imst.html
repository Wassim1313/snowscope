    About Hoch-Imst

    Neither huge nor tiny, Hoch-Imst offers about 7.0 km of pistes (number 576 of 1,056 in the Alps). The slopes start at 1,047 m and reach 2,022 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Galtwiesenlift, 9.1 km south-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How much snow Hoch-Imst usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 1,534 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to mid-May, about 22 weeks of good cover. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 68 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 55 cm and 108 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 46 cm, from 21 cm in a poor year to 65 cm in a big one. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 92 cm.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Hoch-Imst gives 975 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. The lift system counts 4 lifts: 2 gondolas, 1 T-bar and 1 rope tow. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. Above the ski area rises Große Schlenkerspitze (2,827 m), 6.0 km to the west, about 805 m higher than the top lift.

    Quick answers

    When does Hoch-Imst have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from early December to mid-May.

    Is there snow at Hoch-Imst at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 46 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Hoch-Imst?

    The top is at 2,022 m and the base at 1,047 m.

    What level of skier is Hoch-Imst best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 66% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How big is Hoch-Imst?

    About 7.0 km of pistes and 4 lifts.
