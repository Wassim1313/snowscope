    The snow at 4 Vallées, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of 4 Vallées (about 2,104 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 64 cm, with a normal range of 49 cm–84 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 112 cm (98 cm–133 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 124 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 52 % of winters, and by 1 January in 85 %.

    Best winter on record: 1994-95, peaking at 199 cm. Worst: 2010-11, which never passed 99 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late November to early June (197 days).

    This winter so far: 58 cm on 27 November, against a usual 30 cm for the date.

    About 4 Vallées

    4 Vallées is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 175 km of pistes put it at number 18 of 1,056. Its lifts climb from 905 m to 3,304 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Lourtier, 9.2 km to the south.

    How 4 Vallées is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 2,399 m of vertical, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Below about 1,105 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. To get up there are 6 cable cars, 1 funicular, 12 gondolas, 5 chair-gondolas, 16 chairlifts, 5 T-bars, 18 button lifts, 1 drag lift, 2 rope tows and 10 moving carpets. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 10 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Mont Fort (3,329 m), Bec des Etagnes (3,232 m) and Bec des Rosses (3,222 m).

    What there is to ski at 4 Vallées

    4 Vallées has 348 graded runs adding up to about 173 km. Red runs make up the biggest part: 81 km, or 47%. Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Advanced skiers have about 16 km of black or expert runs. 13 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Snow at 4 Vallées through a normal winter

    Count on about 27 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 2,104 m in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May or later. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 124 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 116 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Year to year the snow is steady, with 76 cm to 144 cm in mid-February in most winters. For about 14 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and early April. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 64 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 101 cm in the best years.
