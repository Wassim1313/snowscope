    The snow at 4 Vallées, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of 4 Vallées (about 2,104 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 64 cm, with a normal range of 49 cm–84 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 112 cm (98 cm–133 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 124 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 52 % of winters, and by 1 January in 85 %.

    Best winter on record: 1994-95, peaking at 199 cm. Worst: 2010-11, which never passed 99 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late November to early June (197 days).

    Questions about 4 Vallées

    Does 4 Vallées have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 64 cm at Christmas, and 85 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 56 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at 4 Vallées?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 124 cm). For a sure base, late November to early June is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How much snow does 4 Vallées get?

    About 124 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 99 cm in the leanest winter (2010-11) and 199 cm in the biggest (1994-95).

    How high is 4 Vallées?

    Between 905 m and 3,304 m, so 2,399 m of vertical drop.

    How many km of pistes does 4 Vallées have?

    About 4 Vallées

    4 Vallées is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 175 km of pistes put it at number 18 of 1,056. Its lifts climb from 905 m to 3,304 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Lourtier, 9.2 km to the south.

    How 4 Vallées is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 2,399 m of vertical, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Below about 1,105 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point.
