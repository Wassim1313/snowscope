    Kirkwood in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Kirkwood (8,638 ft (2,633 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 43 in (109 cm) of cover (22 in (57 cm) to 62 in (158 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 74 in (189 cm), between 56 in (142 cm) and 102 in (259 cm) in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 87 in (221 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 67 % of winters, and by 1 January in 80 %.

    The deepest winter was 2022-23 (194 in (493 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2014-15 (40 in (101 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to late May (194 days).

    Common questions

    Does Kirkwood have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 43 in (109 cm) at Christmas, and 80 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 35 in (90 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Kirkwood?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 87 in (221 cm)). For a sure base, mid-November to late May is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How much snow does Kirkwood get?

    About 87 in (221 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (mid-March); between 40 in (101 cm) in the leanest winter (2014-15) and 194 in (493 cm) in the biggest (2022-23).
