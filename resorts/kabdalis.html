    About Kåbdalis

    Kåbdalis is a mid-sized ski area with about 6.0 km of pistes, number 112 of 262 in Scandinavia by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 350 m up to 535 m.

    Snow at Kåbdalis through a normal winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 442 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 23 November and lasts to 14 May: about 25 weeks. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and late February. By mid-April the typical depth is 71 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Most winters land between 53 cm and 95 cm by mid-February. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 49 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 74 cm in the best years. The snow is usually deepest around 8 February, with about 78 cm.

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 7 marked runs cover roughly 5.6 km. Red runs make up the biggest part: 2.5 km, or 44%. The mix is balanced: easy slopes to learn on and enough steeper runs to keep good skiers busy. Experts can count on some 1.2 km of difficult runs.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    185 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Its 4 lifts break down into 1 chairlift and 3 drag lifts. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Goabddálisvárre (520 m) and Gáttekvárre (493 m).

    Kåbdalis: common questions

    When does Kåbdalis have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from late November to mid-May.

    Is there snow at Kåbdalis at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 49 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    What altitude is Kåbdalis at?

    Between 350 m and 535 m, so 185 m of vertical drop.

    Who is Kåbdalis best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 44% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How big is Kåbdalis?

    About 6.0 km of pistes and 4 lifts.
