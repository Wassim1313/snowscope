    About Simonhöhe

    With roughly 7.0 km of runs, Simonhöhe sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,046 m and reach 1,338 m at the top. Hochrindl is the next resort, 17 km away to the north-west.

    Runs by difficulty

    In all, 9 marked runs cover roughly 6.6 km. The largest share is blue, with 4.1 km (62%). With 62% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    292 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Among the 8 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Turracher Höhe reaches 2,193 m. To get up there are 6 T-bars, 2 button lifts, 1 rope tow and 1 moving carpet. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Hocheck (1,338 m), Schneebauerberg (1,338 m) and Paulsberg (1,333 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,192 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 8 cm: spring snow is mostly gone at this height. The snow is usually deepest around 14 March, with about 28 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 10 cm to 48 cm in mid-February in most winters. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 14 cm (lean years 4 cm, snowy years 32 cm).

    Simonhöhe: common questions

    When does Simonhöhe have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March.

    How much snow does Simonhöhe have at Christmas?

    Sometimes. Christmas snow at mid-mountain came in nearly every winter, usually about 14 cm, so check before you go.

    How high is Simonhöhe?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,338 m; the lowest starts at 1,046 m (292 m of vertical).

    Is Simonhöhe good for beginners?

    Yes: 62% of the graded pistes, about 4.1 km, are easy.

    Is there a larger resort near Simonhöhe?

    Bad Kleinkirchheim, 30 km to the west, with about 68 km of pistes.
