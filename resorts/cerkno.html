    What 46 winters say about Cerkno

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Cerkno (1,118 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 13 cm, with a normal range of 6 cm–32 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 28 cm (14 cm–56 cm).

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 29 cm.

    By 15 December 9 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 15 %.

    Best winter on record: 2005-06, peaking at 141 cm. Worst: 2001-02, which never passed 14 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early January to late March, about 83 days.

    Questions about Cerkno

    Does Cerkno have snow over Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 13 cm, and only 15 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is no snow.

    What is the best time to ski Cerkno?

    February: the snow peaks at about 29 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early January to late March.

    How much snow does Cerkno get?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 29 cm in early February. The deepest of 46 winters reached 141 cm (2005-06), the thinnest 14 cm (2001-02).

    How high do the lifts go at Cerkno?

    The top is at 1,290 m and the base at 946 m.

    How big is Cerkno?

    About 9.0 km of pistes and 6 lifts.

    About Cerkno

    Cerkno is a mid-sized ski area with about 9.0 km of pistes, number 507 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 946 m up to 1,290 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Soriška planina, 8.6 km to the north-west.

    Lifts and terrain

    344 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 946 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry.
