    About Schönfeld

    Schönfeld is a small ski area by the standards of the Alps, with about 2.0 km of pistes. The slopes start at 1,735 m and reach 2,088 m at the top. Innerkrems is the next resort, 2.3 km away to the south-west.

    When the snow is good

    Halfway up the slopes, around 1,912 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 13 December and lasts to 5 May: about 21 weeks. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 66 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and late February. A normal season tops out near 76 cm, around 26 February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 39 cm (lean years 15 cm, snowy years 65 cm). Year to year the snow is steady, with 45 cm to 98 cm in mid-February in most winters.

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 2 runs and around 2.0 km to ski. Most of the length is blue (70%, about 1.4 km). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    How Schönfeld is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 353 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. Starting at 1,735 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. All 2 lifts here are T-bars. The highest named summit within 8 km is Kilnprein at 2,408 m, standing 320 m above the top lift.

    Schönfeld: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Schönfeld?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from mid-December to early May.

    How much snow does Schönfeld have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 39 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Schönfeld?

    The top is at 2,088 m and the base at 1,735 m.

    Is Schönfeld suitable for families?

    Yes: 70% of the graded pistes, about 1.4 km, are easy.

    Which bigger ski area is near Schönfeld?

    Katschberg-Aineck, 14 km to the north-west, with about 52 km of pistes.
