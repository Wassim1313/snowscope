    What 46 winters say about Saint-Lary

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Saint-Lary (about 2,028 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 29 cm; half of the winters sit between 11 cm and 48 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 74 cm; the middle half of winters range from 38 cm to 102 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 80 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 24 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 37 %.

    The deepest winter was 2013-14 (183 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1992-93 (36 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-December until mid-April in a normal winter, roughly 124 days.

    Questions about Saint-Lary

    Is there snow in Saint-Lary at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 29 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 37 % of 46 winters. Right now: 28 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Saint-Lary?

    February: the snow peaks at about 80 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to mid-April.

    How snowy is Saint-Lary?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 80 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 183 cm (2013-14), the thinnest 36 cm (1992-93).

    What altitude is Saint-Lary at?

    Between 1,595 m and 2,462 m, so 867 m of vertical drop.

    How big is Saint-Lary?

    About 72 km of pistes and 27 lifts.
