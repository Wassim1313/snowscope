    La Plagne in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at La Plagne (2,138 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 97 cm, with a normal range of 74 cm–130 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 175 cm, between 143 cm and 200 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 184 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 78 % of winters, and by 1 January in 98 %.

    1994-95 was the big one (298 cm); 2004-05 the lean one (156 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early November to early June, about 209 days.

    This winter so far: 84 cm on 27 November, against a usual 45 cm for the date.

    About La Plagne

    La Plagne is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 188 km of pistes put it at number 14 of 1,056. The slopes start at 1,217 m and reach 3,058 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Paradiski, 4.9 km north-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 268 graded runs, about 188 km in total. The largest share is blue, with 117 km (62%). With 63% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served. 1.7 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Snow this week

    Today there are about 60 cm of snow on the upper slopes of La Plagne. The last seven days brought 98 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. An ordinary winter would have around 55 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track. The 2026/27 season at La Plagne is announced to start on 12 December 2026 and to run until 24 April 2027.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 1,841 m: one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Its top at 3,058 m is the third highest of the 8 ski areas within 50 km. Getting up the mountain: 2 cable cars, 12 gondolas, 29 chairlifts, 13 button lifts, 10 drag lifts, 5 rope tows and 4 moving carpets, 75 lifts in all. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 4 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Sommet de Bellecôte, 3,417 m high and 7.0 km east of the resort.

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 2,138 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 14 November and keeps it until about the end of May, roughly 29 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 97 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 53 cm in a poor year to 149 cm in a big one. Most winters land between 119 cm and 218 cm by mid-February. For the best snow, aim for early March and early April. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 184 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 175 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 21 weeks.
