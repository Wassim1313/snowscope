    La Plagne in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at La Plagne (2,138 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 97 cm, with a normal range of 74 cm–130 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 175 cm, between 143 cm and 200 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 184 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 78 % of winters, and by 1 January in 98 %.

    1994-95 was the big one (298 cm); 2004-05 the lean one (156 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early November to early June, about 209 days.

    Skiing La Plagne: the practical side

    Lifts: 75. Pistes: 188 km. Heights: 1,217 m at the bottom, 3,058 m at the top (1,841 m of vertical).

    Questions about La Plagne

    Does La Plagne have snow over Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 97 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 98 % of 46 winters. Right now: 168 cm.

    When is the best month to ski La Plagne?

    March: the snow peaks at about 184 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early November to early June.

    How snowy is La Plagne?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 184 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 298 cm (1994-95), the thinnest 156 cm (2004-05).

    How high is La Plagne?

    Its highest lift reaches 3,058 m; the lowest starts at 1,217 m (1,841 m of vertical).

    How big is La Plagne?

    About 188 km of pistes and 75 lifts.

    About La Plagne

    La Plagne is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 188 km of pistes put it at number 14 of 1,056. The slopes start at 1,217 m and reach 3,058 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Paradiski, 4.9 km north-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Snow this week

    Today there are about 168 cm of snow on the upper slopes of La Plagne. 3 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. The coming days should add about 13 cm by Friday.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 1,841 m: one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Its top at 3,058 m is the third highest of the 8 ski areas within 50 km.
