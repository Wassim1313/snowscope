    About Axamer Lizum

    Axamer Lizum is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 38 km of pistes put it at number 162 of 1,056. Its lifts climb from 892 m to 2,334 m. Its nearest neighbour is Sonnenlift Axams, 4.2 km north-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 1,442 m: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. The base at 892 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. Its top at 2,334 m is the highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. Getting up the mountain: 3 funiculars, 4 gondolas, 4 chairlifts, 2 T-bars, 2 button lifts and 3 moving carpets, 18 lifts in all. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 3 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Schlicker Seespitze at 2,808 m, standing 474 m above the top lift.

    How much snow Axamer Lizum usually gets

    Count on about 21 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 1,613 m) in an ordinary winter, from mid-December to mid-May. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 53 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. A normal season tops out near 62 cm, around 24 March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 34 cm, from 16 cm in a poor year to 50 cm in a big one. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 38 cm and 75 cm.

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 51 runs and around 38 km to ski. The largest share is red, with 20 km (53%). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Experts can count on some 4.5 km of difficult runs. 2.4 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.
