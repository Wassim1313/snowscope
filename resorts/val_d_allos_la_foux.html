    What 46 winters say about Val d'Allos

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Val d'Allos (2,147 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 53 cm; half of the winters sit between 37 cm and 77 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 101 cm, between 74 cm and 123 cm in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 109 cm.

    By 15 December 33 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 61 %.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (210 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1992-93 (43 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early December to early May (161 days).

    Common questions

    Is there snow in Val d'Allos at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 53 cm at Christmas, and 61 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 34 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Val d'Allos?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 109 cm). For a sure base, early December to early May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Val d'Allos?

    About 109 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 43 cm in the leanest winter (1992-93) and 210 cm in the biggest (2000-01).

    What altitude is Val d'Allos at?

    The top is at 2,592 m and the base at 1,702 m.

    How big is Val d'Allos?

    About 96 km of pistes and 18 lifts.
