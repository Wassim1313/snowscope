    What 46 winters say about Val d'Allos

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Val d'Allos (2,147 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 53 cm; half of the winters sit between 37 cm and 77 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 101 cm, between 74 cm and 123 cm in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 109 cm.

    By 15 December 33 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 61 %.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (210 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1992-93 (43 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early December to early May (161 days).

    This winter so far: 41 cm on 28 November, against a usual 18 cm for the date.

    How deep does the snow get at Val d'Allos?

    About Val d'Allos

    With some 96 km of runs, Val d'Allos belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,702 m and reach 2,592 m at the top. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Espace Lumière, 3.8 km to the north.

    Pistes and who they suit

    The piste map lists 183 graded runs, about 96 km in total. Most of the length is freeride (40%, about 38 km). Good skiers will like it here; 49% of the runs are steep. Experts can count on some 9.4 km of difficult runs. 38 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Snow at Val d'Allos through a normal winter

    Count on about 22 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 2,147 m in an ordinary winter, from early December to early May. By mid-April the typical depth is 68 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 109 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 53 cm (lean years 20 cm, snowy years 111 cm). The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and early March. Most winters land between 50 cm and 144 cm by mid-February. For about 4 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    890 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. With its base at 1,702 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point. The lift system counts 18 lifts: 8 chairlifts, 3 button lifts, 4 drag lifts, 1 rope tow and 2 moving carpets. First-timers get 2 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The highest named summit within 8 km is Tête de l'Estrop at 2,961 m, standing 369 m above the top lift.
