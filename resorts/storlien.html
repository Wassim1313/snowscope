    The snow at Storlien, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Storlien (686 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 68 cm of cover (54 cm to 83 cm in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 115 cm, between 92 cm and 141 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 141 cm.

    By 15 December 65 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 85 %.

    Best winter on record: 1996-97, peaking at 228 cm. Worst: 2025-26, which never passed 82 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs mid-November to late May (192 days).

    Today's 15 cm compares with a usual 29 cm for 19 November.

    Questions about Storlien

    Does Storlien have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 68 cm at Christmas, and 85 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 17 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Storlien?

    March: the snow peaks at about 141 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May.

    How snowy is Storlien?

    About 141 cm at the peak of a normal winter (late March); between 82 cm in the leanest winter (2025-26) and 228 cm in the biggest (1996-97).

    How high do the lifts go at Storlien?

    The top is at 782 m and the base at 589 m.

    Is Storlien good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 1.6 km of its graded pistes (37%) are difficult.

    Which bigger ski area is near Storlien?

    Åre, 47 km to the east, with about 116 km of pistes.

    About Storlien

    With roughly 4.0 km of runs, Storlien sits in the middle of the size table of Scandinavia. Its lifts climb from 589 m to 782 m. There are 1 other ski area within 40 km, the closest being Meråker alpinsenter, 26 km to the north-west.

    Snow at Storlien through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 686 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to late May, about 27 weeks of good cover. Year to year the snow is steady, with 73 cm to 158 cm in mid-February in most winters. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 68 cm, from 41 cm in a poor year to 110 cm in a big one. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 141 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 137 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. For about 14 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. The best weeks for snow are usually late March and early April.

    The mountain at Storlien

    From top to bottom that makes 193 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Of the 5 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. All 9 lifts here are drag lifts. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. The skyline is dominated by Litjkluken, 941 m high and 6.0 km south-west of the resort.
