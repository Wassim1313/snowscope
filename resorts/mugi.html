    About Mugi

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Mugi gives 40 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 497 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Braunwald, for one, climbs to 1,900 m. The only lift is a rope tow. Above the ski area rises Vrenelisgärtli (2,904 m), 6.5 km to the south, about 2,367 m higher than the top lift.

    How much snow Mugi usually gets

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 517 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. The best weeks for snow are usually early February and mid-February. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 4 cm to 31 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 8 cm (snow lay here in 8 winters out of ten), with 16 cm in the best years. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April. The snow is usually deepest around 6 February, with about 16 cm.

    Questions about Mugi

    When is the best time to ski at Mugi?

    Aim for early February and mid-February.

    How much snow does Mugi have at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in 8 winters out of ten, typically 8 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    How high do the lifts go at Mugi?

    The top is at 537 m and the base at 497 m.

    How many km of pistes does Mugi have?

    About 0.2 km of pistes and 1 lift.
