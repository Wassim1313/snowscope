    About Wolzenalp

    Wolzenalp is a mid-sized ski area with about 8.0 km of pistes, number 563 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. Its lifts climb from 728 m to 1,461 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Wolzenalp (Krummenau), 3.0 km to the east.

    What there is to ski at Wolzenalp

    Wolzenalp has 7 graded runs adding up to about 8.3 km. The largest share is red, with 5.5 km (66%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Advanced skiers have about 2.8 km of black or expert runs.

    Lifts and terrain

    733 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. Below about 928 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. Its top at 1,461 m is the second highest of the 8 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 1 chairlift, 2 T-bars, 2 rope tows and 1 moving carpet. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. 1 moving carpet serves the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Speer at 1,950 m, standing 489 m above the top lift.

    Wolzenalp and the resorts around it

    In this group of 7, Wolzenalp has the most pistes. Amden Mattstock has the longer snow season: around 22 weeks of solid cover, compared with 11 at Wolzenalp.

    Snow at Wolzenalp through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,094 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-January to early April, about 11 weeks of good cover. Most winters land between 17 cm and 60 cm by mid-February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 20 cm, from 6 cm in a poor year to 35 cm in a big one. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 28 cm, and the lower runs are often thin by then. The snow is usually deepest around 13 February, with about 37 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March.

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Wolzenalp?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-January to early April.

    How much snow does Wolzenalp have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 20 cm deep.

    How high is Wolzenalp?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,461 m; the lowest starts at 728 m (733 m of vertical).

    Does Wolzenalp have steep runs?

    Yes: about 2.8 km of its graded pistes (34%) are difficult.

    How many km of pistes does Wolzenalp have?

    About 8.0 km of pistes and 6 lifts.
