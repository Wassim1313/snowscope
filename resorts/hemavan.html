    Hemavan in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Hemavan (799 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 93 cm; half of the winters sit between 81 cm and 110 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 146 cm; the middle half of winters range from 123 cm to 165 cm.

    April is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 165 cm.

    By 15 December 96 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 100 %.

    Best winter on record: 1996-97, peaking at 265 cm. Worst: 2005-06, which never passed 133 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from early November until mid-June in a normal winter, roughly 225 days.

    Today's 14 cm compares with a usual 50 cm for 19 November.

    Common questions

    Is there snow in Hemavan at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 93 cm at Christmas, and 100 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 20 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Hemavan?

    The deepest snow comes in April (about 165 cm). For a sure base, early November to mid-June is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Hemavan?

    About 165 cm at the peak of a normal winter (late March); between 133 cm in the leanest winter (2005-06) and 265 cm in the biggest (1996-97).

    What altitude is Hemavan at?

    The top is at 1,136 m and the base at 462 m.

    Is Hemavan suitable for families?

    Yes: 79% of the graded pistes, about 30 km, are easy.

    About Hemavan

    About 37 km of pistes make Hemavan one of the ten biggest ski areas of Scandinavia (number 10). The slopes start at 462 m and reach 1,136 m at the top. There are 1 other ski area within 40 km, the closest being Tärmaby, 14 km to the south-east.

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 47 graded runs, about 37 km in total. Most of the length is blue (61%, about 23 km). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Hemavan gives 674 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Its 9 lifts break down into 1 chair-gondola, 4 button lifts and 4 drag lifts. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. Above the ski area rises Govretjåhke (1,484 m), 7.8 km to the east, about 348 m higher than the top lift.

    Snow at Hemavan through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 799 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from early November to late May or later, about 30 weeks of good cover. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 93 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 123 cm, a dry one 70 cm. For the best snow, aim for late March and early April. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 165 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 163 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. For about 22 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 111 cm and 196 cm.
