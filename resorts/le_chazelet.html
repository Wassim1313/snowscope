    About Le Chazelet

    Neither huge nor tiny, Le Chazelet offers about 9.0 km of pistes (number 519 of 1,056 in the Alps). The ski area spans the mountain from 1,730 m up to 2,159 m. The closest other ski area is La Grave (4.1 km, south).

    Runs by difficulty

    In all, 27 marked runs cover roughly 9.4 km. Red runs make up the biggest part: 3.4 km, or 36%. Mixed groups do well here, with gentle slopes for learners and steeper pistes for the experts. Experts can count on some 1.8 km of difficult runs.

    Nearby alternatives compared

    For more terrain, Alpe d'Huez Grand Domaine (17 km north-west) has about 163 km of pistes against 9.0 km here. If snow is the priority, La Grave does better, with about 30 weeks of a good base instead of 25. For higher slopes, La Grave climbs to 3,543 m.

    The snow right now

    Today there are about 47 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Le Chazelet. 8 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. The coming days should add about 31 cm by Thursday. That is close to a normal winter for the date (about 34 cm at mid-mountain).

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 1,944 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to mid-May, about 25 weeks of good cover. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 13 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 81 cm; a snowy December gave 124 cm, a dry one 43 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and early March. Most winters land between 100 cm and 190 cm by mid-February. The snow is usually deepest around 6 March, with about 158 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 87 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered.

    How Le Chazelet is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Le Chazelet gives 429 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The bottom station at 1,730 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. To get up there are 1 chairlift, 4 button lifts and 2 rope tows. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. The skyline is dominated by Grand Pic de la Meije, 3,982 m high and 6.1 km south of the resort.

    Staying nearby

    Places to stay close to the slopes of Le Chazelet include Relais d'Emparis (hotel), Soldanelle (guesthouse), Le Chardon Bleu (guesthouse) and Jacotte (guesthouse).

    Questions about Le Chazelet

    What is the best month to ski at Le Chazelet?

    Aim for mid-February and early March; the cover is usually good from late November to mid-May.

    Can you ski at Le Chazelet at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 81 cm deep.

    What altitude is Le Chazelet at?

    The top is at 2,159 m and the base at 1,730 m.

    Can beginners ski at Le Chazelet?

    Yes: 46% of the graded pistes, about 4.3 km, are easy.

    Is there a larger resort near Le Chazelet?

    Alpe d'Huez Grand Domaine, 17 km to the north-west, with about 163 km of pistes.
