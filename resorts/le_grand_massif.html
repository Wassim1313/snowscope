    Le Grand Massif in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Le Grand Massif (1,586 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 104 cm of cover (69 cm to 139 cm in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 196 cm, between 170 cm and 227 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 208 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 76 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 93 %.

    Best winter on record: 2019-20, peaking at 321 cm. Worst: 2010-11, which never passed 155 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June, about 196 days.

    Common questions

    Can you ski Le Grand Massif at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 104 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 93 % of 46 winters. Right now: 142 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Le Grand Massif?

    March: the snow peaks at about 208 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June.

    How deep does the snow get at Le Grand Massif?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 208 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 321 cm (2019-20), the thinnest 155 cm (2010-11).

    How high is Le Grand Massif?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,484 m; the lowest starts at 687 m (1,797 m of vertical).

    How big is Le Grand Massif?

    About 174 km of pistes and 69 lifts.

    About Le Grand Massif

    Around 174 km of marked runs place Le Grand Massif among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 19 by length. The slopes start at 687 m and reach 2,484 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Romme, 8.6 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    The mountain at Le Grand Massif

    The vertical drop is 1,797 m: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Because the lifts start as low as 687 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. Its top at 2,484 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km.
