    Christmas week typically brings 104 cm of cover (69 cm to 139 cm in the middle half of winters).

    About Le Grand Massif

    Around 174 km of marked runs place Le Grand Massif among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 19 by length. The slopes start at 687 m and reach 2,484 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Romme, 8.6 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    The mountain at Le Grand Massif

    The vertical drop is 1,797 m: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Because the lifts start as low as 687 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. Its top at 2,484 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. Its 69 lifts break down into 7 gondolas, 21 chairlifts, 22 button lifts, 3 drag lifts and 16 moving carpets. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. First-timers get 16 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The highest named summit within 8 km is Pointe de Chardonnière at 2,723 m, standing 239 m above the top lift.

    The pistes of Le Grand Massif

    Counting every graded piste, there are 324 runs and around 174 km to ski. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 80 km, or 46%. With 58% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 27 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 1,586 m) in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May or later. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 20 weeks. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 128 cm and 255 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 104 cm, from 46 cm in a poor year to 164 cm in a big one. The snow is usually deepest around 2 March, with about 208 cm. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 157 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.
