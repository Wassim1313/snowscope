    About Schneeland Wenigzell

    Schneeland Wenigzell is a small ski area by the standards of the Alps, with about 1.0 km of pistes. Its lifts climb from 948 m to 1,121 m. The closest other ski area is Strallegg (4.1 km, west).

    How Schneeland Wenigzell compares nearby

    Skiers after mileage can drive 18 km north to Stuhleck, with about 22 km of runs. If snow is the priority, Stuhleck does better, with about 10 weeks of a good base instead of 0. For higher slopes, Stuhleck climbs to 1,767 m.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Schneeland Wenigzell gives 173 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 948 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Stuhleck, for one, climbs to 1,767 m. The only lift is a drag lift. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Floisenkogel (1,217 m), Eggberg (1,215 m) and Ochsenkopf (1,191 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 1,034 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 2 cm to 27 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 6 cm (lean years 1 cm, snowy years 19 cm). Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 11 cm. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and late February.

    Questions about Schneeland Wenigzell

    What is the best month to ski at Schneeland Wenigzell?

    Aim for mid-February and late February.

    Can you ski at Schneeland Wenigzell at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in nearly every winter, typically 6 cm; the higher slopes do better.
