    What 46 winters say about Sörenberg

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Sörenberg (about 1,688 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 45 cm, with a normal range of 30 cm–54 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 84 cm (71 cm–99 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 95 cm.

    20 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 48 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 150 cm. Worst: 1989-90, which never passed 70 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to mid-May, about 163 days.

    Today's 37 cm compares with a usual 17 cm for 26 November.

    About Sörenberg

    With some 38 km of runs, Sörenberg belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 1,119 m to 2,257 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Mörlialp, 6.3 km to the east.

    The pistes of Sörenberg

    Counting every graded piste, there are 48 runs and around 38 km to ski. The largest share is blue, with 16 km (43%). No single level dominates; beginners and good skiers both find runs to suit them. Advanced skiers have about 5.5 km of black or expert runs.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 1,138 m: enough height for proper long runs. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. Getting up the mountain: 1 cable car, 1 gondola, 3 chairlifts, 10 T-bars, 1 button lift and 1 moving carpet, 17 lifts in all. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Brienzer Rothorn (2,348 m), Schongütsch (2,319 m) and Tannhorn (2,221 m).

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,688 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 13 December and keeps it until about 20 May, roughly 23 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 45 cm, with 61 cm in the best years. Most winters land between 60 cm and 110 cm by mid-February. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and early March. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 95 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 84 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.
