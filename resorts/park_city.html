    What 46 winters say about Park City

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Park City (8,435 ft (2,571 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 32 in (81 cm); half of the winters sit between 24 in (62 cm) and 45 in (114 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 56 in (143 cm); the middle half of winters range from 43 in (110 cm) to 69 in (176 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 64 in (163 cm).

    By 15 December 72 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 89 %.

    2022-23 was the big one (120 in (304 cm)); 2025-26 the lean one (31 in (79 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to mid-May (175 days).

    Today's 13 in (33 cm) compares with a usual 14 in (36 cm) for 24 November.

    About Park City

    With about 256 km of pistes, Park City is the largest ski area of US West. The ski area spans the mountain from 2,101 m up to 3,041 m. The closest other ski area is Deer Valley Resort (5.7 km, south-east).

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 361 runs and around 253 km to ski. The largest share is blue square, with 138 km (55%). Intermediate runs make up 55% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. The steepest pistes add up to about 94 km.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 940 m: enough height for proper long runs. At 2,101 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Getting up the mountain: 2 funiculars, 5 gondolas, 32 chairlifts, 1 rope tow and 3 moving carpets, 43 lifts in all. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. First-timers get 3 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The skyline is dominated by Clayton Peak, 3,258 m high and 7.6 km south of the resort.

    How much snow Park City usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 2,571 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to mid-May, about 25 weeks of good cover. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 15 weeks. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. The snow is usually deepest around 13 March, with about 163 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 122 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 92 cm and 199 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 81 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 151 cm in the best years.
