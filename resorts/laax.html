    What 46 winters say about Laax

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Laax (2,028 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 67 cm of cover (48 cm to 86 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 120 cm (94 cm–140 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 129 cm.

    By 15 December 57 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 80 %.

    Best winter on record: 2000-01, peaking at 222 cm. Worst: 1995-96, which never passed 99 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs mid-November to early June (195 days).

    Common questions

    Does Laax have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 67 cm at Christmas, and 80 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 28 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Laax?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 129 cm). For a sure base, mid-November to early June is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Laax?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 129 cm in late March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 222 cm (2000-01), the thinnest 99 cm (1995-96).

    How high is Laax?

    The top is at 2,974 m and the base at 1,082 m.

    How big is Laax?

    About 152 km of pistes and 37 lifts.

    About Laax

    With some 152 km of runs, Laax belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 1,082 m to 2,974 m. Elm is the next resort, 9.7 km away to the north-west.

    The mountain at Laax

    Skied top to bottom, Laax gives 1,892 m of descent: one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 2,974 m.
