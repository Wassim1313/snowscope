    What 46 winters say about Stowe

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Stowe (2,480 ft (756 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 14 in (36 cm) of cover (7.1 in (18 cm) to 19 in (49 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 29 in (74 cm), between 20 in (50 cm) and 35 in (88 cm) in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 30 in (75 cm).

    15 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 28 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (56 in (141 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2015-16 (14 in (35 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-December to early April (113 days).

    This winter so far: 0 in (0 cm) on 23 November, against a usual 4.7 in (12 cm) for the date.

    Stowe: quick answers

    Does Stowe have snow over Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 14 in (36 cm), and only 28 % of the past 46 winters had 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 1.6 in (4 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Stowe?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 30 in (75 cm)). For a sure base, mid-December to early April is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Stowe?

    About 30 in (75 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (mid-February); between 14 in (35 cm) in the leanest winter (2015-16) and 56 in (141 cm) in the biggest (2000-01).

    When does Stowe have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to early April.

    About Stowe

    About 67 km of pistes make Stowe one of the ten biggest ski areas of US Northeast (number 7). Skiing here happens between 400 m and 1,112 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Smugglers' Notch Resort, 4.9 km north, and 7 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 137 runs and around 67 km to ski. The largest share is blue square, with 38 km (57%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Experts can count on some 12 km of difficult runs.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 756 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to early April, about 16 weeks of good cover. A normal season tops out near 75 cm, around 16 February. Most winters land between 37 cm and 109 cm by mid-February. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and early March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 36 cm; a snowy December gave 64 cm, a dry one 10 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 5 cm: spring snow is mostly gone at this height.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 712 m: enough height for proper long runs. The lowest slopes are at only 400 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. Getting up the mountain: 2 gondolas, 8 chairlifts and 2 moving carpets, 12 lifts in all. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Mount Mansfield (1,340 m), 2.6 km to the north-west, about 228 m higher than the top lift.
