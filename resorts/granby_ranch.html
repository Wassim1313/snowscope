    About Granby Ranch

    Granby Ranch is a mid-sized ski area with about 24 km of pistes, number 89 of 175 in US West by length. Skiing here happens between 2,540 m and 2,795 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Colorado Adventure Park, 13 km south-east, and 5 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 43 runs and around 24 km to ski. The largest share is blue square, with 13 km (55%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Experts can count on some 3.6 km of difficult runs.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 255 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 2,540 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Its summit is modest for the area; the highest lifts nearby, at Loveland Basin, reach 3,866 m. Its 6 lifts break down into 4 chairlifts, 1 rope tow and 1 moving carpet. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. The highest named summit within 8 km is Ninemile Mountain at 2,955 m, standing 160 m above the top lift.

    When the snow is good

    At about 2,668 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 12 December and lasts to 9 May: about 21 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 42 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 71 cm in the best years. Year to year the snow is steady, with 52 cm to 104 cm in mid-February in most winters. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 82 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and early March. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 64 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring.
