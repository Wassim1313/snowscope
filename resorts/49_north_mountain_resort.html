    About 49° North

    49° North is one of the bigger ski areas of US West: about 52 km of pistes put it at number 36 of 175. Its lifts climb from 1,190 m to 1,756 m.

    What there is to ski at 49° North

    In all, 83 marked runs cover roughly 52 km. The largest share is blue square, with 19 km (37%). With 34% of the pistes graded difficult, it suits strong skiers looking for steep runs. The steepest pistes add up to about 18 km.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, 49° North gives 566 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Getting up the mountain: 6 chairlifts and 1 rope tow, 7 lifts in all. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Chewelah Mountain (1,759 m), Goddards Peak (1,687 m) and Little Chewelah Peak (1,585 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 1,473 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 29 November and lasts to 12 April: about 19 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-January and late January. For about 11 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 35 cm and 159 cm. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 69 cm, with 130 cm in the best years. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 155 cm.

    Questions about 49° North

    When does 49° North have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-January and late January; the cover is usually good from late November to mid-April.

    Is there snow at 49° North at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 69 cm deep.

    What altitude is 49° North at?

    The top is at 1,756 m and the base at 1,190 m.

    Is 49° North good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 18 km of its graded pistes (34%) are difficult.

    How big is 49° North?

    About 52 km of pistes and 7 lifts.
