    Hochzillertal-Hochfügen in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Hochzillertal-Hochfügen (1,464 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 31 cm; half of the winters sit between 21 cm and 42 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 53 cm (41 cm–62 cm).

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 57 cm.

    By 15 December 7 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 15 %.

    2018-19 was the big one (95 cm); 1989-90 the lean one (41 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to early May, about 160 days.

    Hochzillertal-Hochfügen: quick answers

    Can you ski Hochzillertal-Hochfügen at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 31 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 15 % of 46 winters. Right now: 45 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Hochzillertal-Hochfügen?

    February: the snow peaks at about 57 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to early May.

    How much snow does Hochzillertal-Hochfügen get?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 57 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 95 cm (2018-19), the thinnest 41 cm (1989-90).

    How high do the lifts go at Hochzillertal-Hochfügen?

    The top is at 2,374 m and the base at 553 m.

    How many km of pistes does Hochzillertal-Hochfügen have?

    About 82 km of pistes and 35 lifts.

    About Hochzillertal-Hochfügen

    Hochzillertal-Hochfügen is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 82 km of pistes put it at number 68 of 1,056. Its lifts climb from 553 m to 2,374 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Spieljoch, 7.6 km to the north.

    Lifts and terrain

    1,821 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. At 553 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Its top at 2,374 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km.
