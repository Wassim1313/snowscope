    About Storefjell Resort

    With roughly 7.0 km of runs, Storefjell Resort sits in the middle of the size table of Scandinavia. Skiing here happens between 946 m and 1,147 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Gol Skisenter, 12 km to the south.

    The pistes of Storefjell Resort

    In all, 10 marked runs cover roughly 7.0 km. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 2.4 km, or 34%. Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    How much snow Storefjell Resort usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 1,046 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-November to late May, about 28 weeks of good cover. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 98 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 67 cm, from 47 cm in a poor year to 101 cm in a big one. For the best snow, aim for mid-March and late March. Most winters land between 78 cm and 123 cm by mid-February. The snow is usually deepest around 16 March, with about 103 cm. For about 4 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more.

    How Storefjell Resort is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Storefjell Resort gives 201 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Its top at 1,147 m is the second highest of the 6 ski areas within 50 km. All 5 lifts here are drag lifts. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders.
