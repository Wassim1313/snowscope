    About Lee Canyon

    Lee Canyon is a mid-sized ski area with about 10 km of pistes, number 125 of 175 in US West by length. Skiing here happens between 2,617 m and 2,850 m above sea level.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Lee Canyon gives 233 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Even the lowest lift starts at 2,617 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Getting up the mountain: 4 chairlifts and 2 moving carpets, 6 lifts in all. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Charleston Peak at 3,632 m, standing 782 m above the top lift.

    Runs by difficulty

    In all, 28 marked runs cover roughly 9.9 km. The largest share is black diamond, with 4.2 km (42%). Steep ground is the speciality: 42% of the length is graded difficult. Experts can count on some 4.2 km of difficult runs.

    Snow at Lee Canyon through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 2,734 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. For the best snow, aim for early January and mid-January. Depth normally peaks in late December at roughly 4 cm. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 0 cm to 50 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 4 cm (snow lay here in 6 winters out of ten) (lean years 0 cm, snowy years 36 cm). Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow.
