    Ski Civetta in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Ski Civetta (1,531 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 29 cm; half of the winters sit between 17 cm and 43 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 46 cm, between 38 cm and 62 cm in a normal winter.

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 53 cm.

    11 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 22 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 2013-14, peaking at 155 cm. Worst: 2016-17, which never passed 29 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late November to late April (133 days).

    This winter so far: 4 cm on 19 November, against a usual 9 cm for the date.

    Ski Civetta: quick answers

    Does Ski Civetta have snow over Christmas?

    Not every year: about 29 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 22 % of 46 winters. Right now: 7 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Ski Civetta?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 53 cm). For a sure base, late November to late April is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Ski Civetta?

    About 53 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 29 cm in the leanest winter (2016-17) and 155 cm in the biggest (2013-14).

    How high do the lifts go at Ski Civetta?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,078 m; the lowest starts at 984 m (1,094 m of vertical).

    Can beginners ski at Ski Civetta?

    Yes: 43% of the graded pistes, about 23 km, are easy.

    How big is Ski Civetta?

    About 53 km of pistes and 27 lifts.

    About Ski Civetta

    With some 53 km of runs, Ski Civetta belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 984 m to 2,078 m. The closest other ski area is San Vito di Cadore (13 km, north-east).

    Pistes and who they suit

    The piste map lists 90 graded runs, about 53 km in total. The largest share is red, with 26 km (49%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Advanced skiers have about 4.2 km of black or expert runs.

    Snow this week

    Snow on the ground at Ski Civetta today: about 7 cm up high. 8 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 6 cm of new snow is expected by Sunday. Mid-mountain is about where a typical winter would be now (11 cm).

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 1,094 m: enough height for proper long runs. The base at 984 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. Among the 9 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Marmolada reaches 3,235 m. The lift system counts 27 lifts: 3 gondolas, 10 chairlifts, 11 button lifts and 3 moving carpets. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. First-timers get 3 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Above the ski area rises Monte Civetta (3,220 m), 3.5 km to the south-west, about 1,142 m higher than the top lift.

    Snow at Ski Civetta through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 1,531 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from late December to mid-April, about 16 weeks of good cover. Year to year the snow is steady, with 31 cm to 82 cm in mid-February in most winters. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 29 cm (snow lay here in nearly every winter) (lean years 5 cm, snowy years 55 cm). For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. By mid-April the typical depth is 30 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. The snow is usually deepest around 3 March, with about 53 cm.
