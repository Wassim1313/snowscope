    The snow at St. Anton/St. Christoph/Stuben, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of St. Anton/St. Christoph/Stuben (about 1,962 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 76 cm, with a normal range of 55 cm–95 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 146 cm (120 cm–178 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 164 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 61 % of winters, and by 1 January in 87 %.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 309 cm. Worst: 2024-25, which never passed 117 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June, about 209 days.

    So far this winter: 25 cm on 21 November (usual: 26 cm).

    About St. Anton/St. Christoph/Stuben

    St. Anton/St. Christoph/Stuben is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 99 km of pistes put it at number 50 of 1,056. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,282 m up to 2,642 m. Its nearest neighbour is Ski Arlberg, 7.4 km north-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 1,360 m of vertical, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 1 reaches higher. To get up there are 2 cable cars, 7 gondolas, 15 chairlifts, 4 T-bars, 11 button lifts and 1 moving carpet. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. Above the ski area rises Faselfadspitze (2,993 m), 7.5 km to the south, about 351 m higher than the top lift.

    How much snow St. Anton/St. Christoph/Stuben usually gets

    Count on about 27 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,962 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May or later. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 164 cm. For about 17 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Year to year the snow is steady, with 100 cm to 200 cm in mid-February in most winters. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 145 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 76 cm; a snowy December gave 108 cm, a dry one 39 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and late March.
