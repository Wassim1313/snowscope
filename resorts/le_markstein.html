    Le Markstein in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Le Markstein (about 1,146 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 6 cm; half of the winters sit between 0 cm and 22 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 16 cm, between 2 cm and 54 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 17 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early January to early March (51 days).

    Common questions

    Can you ski Le Markstein at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 6 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 11 % of 46 winters. Right now: no snow.

    When is the best month to ski Le Markstein?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 17 cm). For a sure base, early January to early March is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Le Markstein?

    About 17 cm at the peak of a normal winter (late January); between 23 cm in the leanest winter (2024-25) and 240 cm in the biggest (1980-81).

    When does Le Markstein have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-January and mid-February.

    How high do the lifts go at Le Markstein?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,261 m; the lowest starts at 1,032 m (229 m of vertical).

    About Le Markstein

    Neither huge nor tiny, Le Markstein offers about 9.0 km of pistes (number 520 of 1,056 in the Alps). The slopes start at 1,032 m and reach 1,261 m at the top. Le Frenz is the next resort, 5.8 km away to the west.

    The pistes of Le Markstein

    Le Markstein has 28 graded runs adding up to about 8.7 km. Most of the length is blue (41%, about 3.6 km). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 229 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lift system counts 9 lifts: 8 drag lifts and 1 rope tow. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. Above the ski area rises Grand Ballon (1,424 m), 6.1 km to the south-east, about 163 m higher than the top lift.

    When the snow is good

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 1,146 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. For the best snow, aim for mid-January and mid-February. Depth normally peaks in late January at roughly 17 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in 6 winters out of ten; the usual depth was 6 cm; a snowy December gave 63 cm, a dry one 0 cm. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 0 cm to 97 cm. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April.
