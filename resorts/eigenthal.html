    About Eigenthal

    Eigenthal has about 0.3 km of pistes. Skiing here happens between 964 m and 986 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Pilatus (3.7 km, east).

    Snow this week

    Snow on the ground at Eigenthal today: about 12 cm up high. The last seven days brought 29 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. Mid-mountain is about where a typical winter would be now (9 cm).

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Eigenthal gives 22 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Below about 1,164 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. The only lift is a T-bar. The skyline is dominated by Tomlishorn, 2,128 m high and 4.1 km south-east of the resort.

    Snow at Eigenthal through a normal winter

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 975 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 15 February and keeps it until about 20 February, roughly 1 weeks. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 15 cm (lean years 5 cm, snowy years 27 cm). Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 30 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 15 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. Year to year the snow is steady, with 10 cm to 45 cm in mid-February in most winters. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and early March.

    Questions about Eigenthal

    When is the best time to ski at Eigenthal?

    Aim for mid-February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-February to mid-February.

    Is there snow at Eigenthal at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 15 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high is Eigenthal?

    The top is at 986 m and the base at 964 m.

    How many km of pistes does Eigenthal have?

    About 0.3 km of pistes and 1 lift.
