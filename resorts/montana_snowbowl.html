    Montana Snowbowl in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Montana Snowbowl (about 6,263 ft (1,909 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 37 in (93 cm) of cover (28 in (71 cm) to 45 in (114 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 61 in (154 cm), between 52 in (132 cm) and 75 in (191 cm) in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 74 in (189 cm).

    83 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 98 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 2010-11, peaking at 239 in (606 cm). Worst: 2004-05, which never passed 37 in (93 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to mid-May (187 days).

    This winter so far: 12 in (30 cm) on 27 November, against a usual 15 in (38 cm) for the date.

    About Montana Snowbowl

    Montana Snowbowl is a mid-sized ski area with about 43 km of pistes, number 44 of 175 in US West by length. The slopes start at 1,522 m and reach 2,296 m at the top.

    The pistes of Montana Snowbowl

    Counting every graded piste, there are 64 runs and around 43 km to ski. Green circle runs make up the biggest part: 15 km, or 36%. Mixed groups do well here, with gentle slopes for learners and steeper pistes for the experts. Advanced skiers have about 12 km of black or expert runs.

    Snow this week

    The upper slopes of Montana Snowbowl hold around 50 cm of snow today. 3 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 6 cm of new snow is expected by Monday. An ordinary winter would have around 64 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 774 m: enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,522 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. The lift system counts 6 lifts: 4 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 1 rope tow. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The skyline is dominated by Murphy Peak, 2,488 m high and 4.6 km north-east of the resort.

    How much snow Montana Snowbowl usually gets

    At about 1,909 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 20 November and lasts to 20 May: about 26 weeks. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 19 weeks. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 162 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Most winters land between 109 cm and 214 cm by mid-February. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 93 cm, with 138 cm in the best years. The snow is usually deepest around 17 March, with about 189 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-March and late March.
