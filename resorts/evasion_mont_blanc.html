    Evasion Mont Blanc in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Evasion Mont Blanc (1,632 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 73 cm; half of the winters sit between 46 cm and 104 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 145 cm; the middle half of winters range from 120 cm to 170 cm.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 147 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 57 % of winters, and by 1 January in 83 %.

    The deepest winter was 1994-95 (257 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2010-11 (113 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late November to late May (186 days).

    This winter so far: 78 cm on 27 November, against a usual 24 cm for the date.

    About Evasion Mont Blanc

    About 293 km of pistes make Evasion Mont Blanc one of the ten biggest ski areas of the Alps (number 6). The ski area spans the mountain from 820 m up to 2,444 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Megève, 1.8 km to the north-east.

    Pistes and who they suit

    The piste map lists 456 graded runs, about 292 km in total. Most of the length is red (38%, about 111 km). The mix is balanced: easy slopes to learn on and enough steeper runs to keep good skiers busy. Experts can count on some 36 km of difficult runs. 7.8 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    The mountain at Evasion Mont Blanc

    From top to bottom that makes 1,624 m of vertical, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. The base at 820 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 1 reaches higher. Its 106 lifts break down into 2 cable cars, 13 gondolas, 35 chairlifts, 33 button lifts, 13 drag lifts, 2 rope tows and 8 moving carpets. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. First-timers get 8 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Mont Joly (2,524 m), Aiguille Croche (2,487 m) and Tête de la Combaz (2,445 m).

    How much snow Evasion Mont Blanc usually gets

    Count on about 25 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,632 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May. For about 15 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. The snow is usually deepest around 5 March, with about 147 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 73 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 122 cm, a dry one 29 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 127 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Most winters land between 90 cm and 193 cm by mid-February. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March.
