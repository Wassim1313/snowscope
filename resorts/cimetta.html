    About Cimetta

    Cimetta is a ski area. Skiing here happens between 202 m and 1,646 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Alpe di Neggia (10 km, south-east).

    The mountain at Cimetta

    1,444 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Because the lifts start as low as 202 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. Getting up the mountain: 1 cable car, 13 funiculars and 1 chairlift, 15 lifts in all. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. The skyline is dominated by Madom da Sgióf, 2,265 m high and 7.9 km north of the resort.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 924 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 4 cm to 29 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 7 cm, with 21 cm in the best years. Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 16 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March.

    Cimetta: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Cimetta?

    Aim for late February and early March.

    Is there snow at Cimetta at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in nearly every winter, typically 7 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    How high is Cimetta?

    The top is at 1,646 m and the base at 202 m.
