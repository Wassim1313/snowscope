    What 46 winters say about Les Pléiades

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Les Pléiades (1,278 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 56 cm, with a normal range of 29 cm–83 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 93 cm, between 63 cm and 112 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 95 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 39 % of winters, and by 1 January in 59 %.

    Best winter on record: 1981-82, peaking at 288 cm. Worst: 2016-17, which never passed 63 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to mid-May, about 164 days.

    Getting to Les Pléiades and what it costs

    Common questions

    Is there snow in Les Pléiades at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 56 cm, and only 59 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is no snow.

    What is the best time to ski Les Pléiades?

    March: the snow peaks at about 95 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to mid-May.

    How snowy is Les Pléiades?

    About 95 cm at the peak of a normal winter (late March); between 63 cm in the leanest winter (2016-17) and 288 cm in the biggest (1981-82).

    How high is Les Pléiades?

    Between 1,159 m and 1,396 m, so 237 m of vertical drop.

    Which bigger ski area is near Les Pléiades?

    About Les Pléiades

    With roughly 6.0 km of runs, Les Pléiades sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 1,159 m to 1,396 m. Its nearest neighbour is Les Pléiades, 1.6 km south, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How Les Pléiades compares nearby

    For more terrain, Les Paccots (3.5 km north-east) has about 9.0 km of pistes against 6.0 km here. The highest nearby is Rochers-de-Naye, topping out at 2,015 m.
