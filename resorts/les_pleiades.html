    What 46 winters say about Les Pléiades

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Les Pléiades (1,278 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 56 cm, with a normal range of 29 cm–83 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 93 cm, between 63 cm and 112 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 95 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 39 % of winters, and by 1 January in 59 %.

    Best winter on record: 1981-82, peaking at 288 cm. Worst: 2016-17, which never passed 63 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to mid-May, about 164 days.

    Today's 6 cm compares with a usual 20 cm for 28 November.

    With roughly 6.0 km of runs, Les Pléiades sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 1,159 m to 1,396 m. Its nearest neighbour is Les Pléiades, 1.6 km south, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How much snow Les Pléiades usually gets

    Halfway up the slopes, around 1,278 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 7 December and lasts to 13 May: about 23 weeks. By mid-April the typical depth is 90 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 56 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 105 cm, a dry one 15 cm. For the best snow, aim for late March and early April. The snow is usually deepest around 25 March, with about 95 cm. Most winters land between 44 cm and 139 cm by mid-February.

    Les Pléiades today and the next days

    Up high, Les Pléiades has roughly 23 cm of snow on the ground today. The last seven days brought 42 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. That is close to a normal winter for the date (about 28 cm at mid-mountain).

    The mountain at Les Pléiades

    Skied top to bottom, Les Pléiades gives 237 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Getting up the mountain: 1 chairlift, 3 button lifts and 2 rope tows, 6 lifts in all. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. The highest named summit within 8 km is Dent de Lys at 2,014 m, standing 618 m above the top lift.
