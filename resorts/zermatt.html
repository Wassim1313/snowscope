    Zermatt in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Zermatt (2,716 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 64 cm, with a normal range of 47 cm–80 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 97 cm, between 79 cm and 115 cm in a normal winter.

    April is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 123 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 54 % of winters, and by 1 January in 70 %.

    The deepest winter was 2008-09 (206 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2022-23 (89 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-November until mid-June in a normal winter, roughly 210 days.

    Common questions

    Can you ski Zermatt at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 64 cm at Christmas, and 70 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 121 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Zermatt?

    The deepest snow comes in April (about 123 cm). For a sure base, mid-November to mid-June is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How much snow does Zermatt get?

    About 123 cm at the peak of a normal winter (mid-April); between 89 cm in the leanest winter (2022-23) and 206 cm in the biggest (2008-09).

    How high do the lifts go at Zermatt?

    The top is at 3,872 m and the base at 1,559 m.

    How big is Zermatt?

    About 208 km of pistes and 63 lifts.

    About Zermatt

    With some 208 km of runs, Zermatt belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,559 m up to 3,872 m. Valtournenche is the next resort, 7.7 km away to the south-west.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 2,313 m of vertical, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Starting at 1,559 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Its top at 3,872 m is the highest of the 8 ski areas within 50 km.
