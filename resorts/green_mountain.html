    The snow at Green Mountain, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Green Mountain (9,206 ft (2,806 m)), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 33 in (84 cm), with a normal range of 24 in (61 cm)–41 in (105 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 52 in (132 cm); the middle half of winters range from 43 in (108 cm) to 64 in (163 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 59 in (150 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 76 % of winters, and by 1 January in 96 %.

    Best winter on record: 1983-84, peaking at 98 in (250 cm). Worst: 2025-26, which never passed 35 in (90 cm).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from mid-November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 191 days.

    Today's 7.1 in (18 cm) compares with a usual 17 in (42 cm) for 28 November.

    Green Mountain: quick answers

    Can you ski Green Mountain at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 33 in (84 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 96 % of 46 winters. Right now: 15 in (37 cm).

    What is the best time to ski Green Mountain?

    March: the snow peaks at about 59 in (150 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May.

    How deep does the snow get at Green Mountain?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 59 in (150 cm) in late March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 98 in (250 cm) (1983-84), the thinnest 35 in (90 cm) (2025-26).

    When does Green Mountain have the most snow?

    Aim for early January and mid-March; the cover is usually good from mid-November to late May.

    How high do the lifts go at Green Mountain?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,980 m; the lowest starts at 2,633 m (347 m of vertical).

    Is Green Mountain good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 2.5 km of its graded pistes (29%) are difficult.

    About Green Mountain

    Neither huge nor tiny, Green Mountain offers about 9.0 km of pistes (number 127 of 175 in US West). Its lifts climb from 2,633 m to 2,980 m. Its nearest neighbour is Three Forks Ranch, 21 km south-west, and 1 ski area lie within 40 km in all.

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 2,806 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 16 November and keeps it until about 21 May, roughly 27 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 84 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 128 cm in the best years. A normal season tops out near 150 cm, around 31 March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 95 cm to 181 cm in mid-February in most winters. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 124 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 16 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually early January and mid-March.

    The pistes of Green Mountain

    Counting every graded piste, there are 13 runs and around 8.7 km to ski. The largest share is blue square, with 4.7 km (54%). Intermediate runs make up 54% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. Advanced skiers have about 2.5 km of black or expert runs.

    Run colours follow the North American system: green circles are easiest, blue squares intermediate, black diamonds difficult.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    347 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Even the lowest lift starts at 2,633 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. The highest named summit within 8 km is Red Mountain at 3,200 m, standing 220 m above the top lift.
