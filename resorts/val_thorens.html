    About Val Thorens

    Val Thorens is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 141 km of pistes put it at number 31 of 1,056. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,726 m up to 3,221 m. Val Thorens is the next resort, 0.7 km away to the north.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Val Thorens has 213 graded runs adding up to about 141 km. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 69 km, or 49%. Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. 1.6 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 1,495 m of vertical, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. With its base at 1,726 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Its top at 3,221 m is the third highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 3 cable cars, 9 gondolas, 12 chairlifts, 3 button lifts and 6 moving carpets. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. 6 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Aiguille de Péclet (3,561 m), 3.7 km to the east, about 340 m higher than the top lift.

    This week's snow

    Snow on the ground at Val Thorens today: about 108 cm up high. 41 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 102 cm of new snow is expected by Friday. At mid-mountain that is more snow than in nine winters out of ten for the date (usually about 48 cm). The 2026/27 season at Val Thorens is announced to start on 21 November 2026 and to run until 2 May 2027.

    Snow at Val Thorens through a normal winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 2,474 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 12 November and lasts to the end of May: about 29 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 109 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 162 cm, a dry one 59 cm. Most winters land between 135 cm and 230 cm by mid-February. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 22 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and early April. The snow is usually deepest around 10 March, with about 212 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 192 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.

    Questions about Val Thorens

    When does Val Thorens have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and early April; the cover is usually good from mid-November to late May.

    Can you ski at Val Thorens at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 109 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    When does Val Thorens open for the 2026/27 season?

    The announced opening day is 21 November 2026; the last ski day is planned for 2 May 2027.

    How high do the lifts go at Val Thorens?

    Between 1,726 m and 3,221 m, so 1,495 m of vertical drop.

    Is Val Thorens suitable for families?

    Yes: 60% of the graded pistes, about 84 km, are easy.
