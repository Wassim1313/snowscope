    The snow at Wolf Creek, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Wolf Creek (about 11,010 ft (3,356 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 41 in (104 cm), with a normal range of 22 in (56 cm)–60 in (152 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 65 in (165 cm), between 43 in (108 cm) and 81 in (206 cm) in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 72 in (183 cm).

    By 15 December 72 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 85 %.

    The deepest winter was 1984-85 (140 in (356 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2025-26 (30 in (75 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May, about 205 days.

    So far this winter: 19 in (48 cm) on 28 November (usual: 19 in (48 cm)).

    About Wolf Creek

    With some 50 km of runs, Wolf Creek belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of US West. The slopes start at 3,128 m and reach 3,585 m at the top.

    When the snow is good

    At about 3,356 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 14 November and lasts to 30 May: about 28 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually early January and mid-March. A normal season tops out near 183 cm, around 17 March. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 49 cm and 240 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 148 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 104 cm, with 187 cm in the best years. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 19 weeks.

    Runs by difficulty

    Wolf Creek has 89 graded runs adding up to about 49 km. Blue square runs make up the biggest part: 28 km, or 58%. This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Advanced skiers have about 11 km of black or expert runs.

    Run colours follow the North American system: green circles are easiest, blue squares intermediate, black diamonds difficult.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 457 m: runs of a moderate length. Even the lowest lift starts at 3,128 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Getting up the mountain: 8 chairlifts, 1 button lift and 2 moving carpets, 11 lifts in all. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. First-timers get 2 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Treasure Mountain (3,613 m), Alberta Peak (3,613 m) and Fox Mountain (3,511 m).
