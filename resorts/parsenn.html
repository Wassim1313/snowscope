    About Parsenn

    Parsenn is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 81 km of pistes put it at number 70 of 1,056. Skiing here happens between 1,000 m and 2,811 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Selfranga, 4.9 km east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 1,811 m: one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. The base at 1,000 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 2,811 m. To get up there are 4 cable cars, 20 funiculars, 2 gondolas, 6 chairlifts, 4 T-bars, 1 rope tow and 2 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Weissflue (2,843 m), Schiahorn (2,709 m) and Zenjiflue (2,686 m).

    The pistes of Parsenn

    The piste map lists 101 graded runs, about 81 km in total. Most of the length is red (61%, about 50 km). Intermediate runs make up 61% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. Experts can count on some 11 km of difficult runs. 1.8 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    How much snow Parsenn usually gets

    Halfway up the slopes, around 1,906 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 29 November and lasts to the end of May: about 26 weeks. For the best snow, aim for early March and late March. Most winters land between 76 cm and 143 cm by mid-February. A normal season tops out near 119 cm, around 23 March. By mid-April the typical depth is 111 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 60 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 81 cm in the best years. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 12 weeks.
