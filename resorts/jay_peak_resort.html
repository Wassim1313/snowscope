    What 46 winters say about Jay Peak Resort

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Jay Peak Resort (2,831 ft (863 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 13 in (34 cm); half of the winters sit between 6.3 in (16 cm) and 19 in (47 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 27 in (68 cm) (19 in (48 cm)–33 in (83 cm)).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 29 in (74 cm).

    By 15 December 17 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 24 %.

    The deepest winter was 2018-19 (55 in (139 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2015-16 (12 in (31 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-December to early April, about 115 days.

    Common questions

    Is there snow in Jay Peak Resort at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 13 in (34 cm) at Christmas on average, with a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 24 % of 46 winters. Right now: 1.6 in (4 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Jay Peak Resort?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 29 in (74 cm)). For a sure base, mid-December to early April is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Jay Peak Resort?

    About 29 in (74 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (mid-March); between 12 in (31 cm) in the leanest winter (2015-16) and 55 in (139 cm) in the biggest (2018-19).

    About Jay Peak Resort

    With some 41 km of runs, Jay Peak Resort belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of US Northeast. The ski area spans the mountain from 560 m up to 1,166 m. Its nearest neighbour is Mont Sutton, 20 km north, and 2 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 606 m: runs of a moderate length. At 560 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 1,166 m.
