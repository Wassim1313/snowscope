    The snow at Mürren/Schilthorn, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Mürren/Schilthorn (about 1,906 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 44 cm; half of the winters sit between 31 cm and 54 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 79 cm; the middle half of winters range from 68 cm to 90 cm.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 91 cm.

    By 15 December 17 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 33 %.

    1981-82 was the big one (151 cm); 2024-25 the lean one (68 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to late May, about 165 days.

    Today's 22 cm compares with a usual 14 cm for 22 November.

    About Mürren/Schilthorn

    With some 39 km of runs, Mürren/Schilthorn belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 863 m and 2,949 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Grindelwald, 7.4 km north-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How Mürren/Schilthorn is laid out

    2,086 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. With the bottom at 863 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Its top at 2,949 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. Its 18 lifts break down into 4 cable cars, 5 funiculars, 7 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 1 button lift. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. The skyline is dominated by Jungfrau, 4,158 m high and 7.1 km south-east of the resort.

    What there is to ski at Mürren/Schilthorn

    Counting every graded piste, there are 56 runs and around 39 km to ski. The largest share is blue, with 17 km (42%). There is a bit of everything, from nursery slopes to proper steep pistes. The steepest pistes add up to about 7.7 km.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 1,906 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to late May, about 24 weeks of good cover. Most winters land between 50 cm and 106 cm by mid-February. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-March and late March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 44 cm; a snowy December gave 60 cm, a dry one 22 cm. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 91 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 87 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.
