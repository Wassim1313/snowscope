    About Biancoia

    With roughly 4.0 km of runs, Biancoia sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 1,099 m and 1,232 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Monte Valbella (Asiago), 6.3 km north-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Runs by difficulty

    Biancoia has 7 graded runs adding up to about 3.8 km. The largest share is blue, with 3.8 km (100%). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    The mountain at Biancoia

    From top to bottom that makes 133 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. All 3 lifts here are drag lifts. Above the ski area rises Monte Gusella (M. Mosca) (1,388 m), 6.1 km to the west, about 156 m higher than the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,166 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in 8 winters out of ten; the usual depth was 5 cm, from 0 cm in a poor year to 23 cm in a big one. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 1 cm to 45 cm. The snow is usually deepest around 1 March, with about 19 cm.

    Biancoia: common questions

    When does Biancoia have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March.

    How much snow does Biancoia have at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in 8 winters out of ten, typically 5 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    How high is Biancoia?

    Between 1,099 m and 1,232 m, so 133 m of vertical drop.

    Is Biancoia suitable for families?

    Yes: 100% of the graded pistes, about 3.8 km, are easy.

    Which bigger ski area is near Biancoia?

    Monte Valbella (Asiago), 6.3 km to the north-west, with about 12 km of pistes.
