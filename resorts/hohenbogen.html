    About Hohenbogen

    Around 6.0 km of marked runs place Hohenbogen among the larger resorts of Central Europe, number 43 by length. Skiing here happens between 655 m and 1,049 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Ski Eck, 8.6 km south, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    What there is to ski at Hohenbogen

    In all, 13 marked runs cover roughly 6.0 km. Red runs make up the biggest part: 3.3 km, or 54%. Intermediate runs make up 54% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Hohenbogen gives 394 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Because the lifts start as low as 655 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. To get up there are 1 chairlift and 1 drag lift.

    How much snow Hohenbogen usually gets

    Even in a typical winter the snow at about 852 m, the middle of the ski area rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 1 cm to 77 cm. The snow is usually deepest around 9 February, with about 26 cm. For the best snow, aim for late January and early February. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 13 cm (snow lay here in nearly every winter); a snowy December gave 46 cm, a dry one 1 cm. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April.

    Hohenbogen: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Hohenbogen?

    Aim for late January and early February.

    How much snow does Hohenbogen have at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in nearly every winter, typically 13 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    How high is Hohenbogen?

    Between 655 m and 1,049 m, so 394 m of vertical drop.

    What level of skier is Hohenbogen best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 54% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How many km of pistes does Hohenbogen have?

    About 6.0 km of pistes and 2 lifts.
