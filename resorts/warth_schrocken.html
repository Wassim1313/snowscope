    Warth-Schröcken in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Warth-Schröcken (1,782 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 79 cm; half of the winters sit between 55 cm and 98 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 166 cm, between 135 cm and 199 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 189 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 65 % of winters, and by 1 January in 87 %.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 360 cm. Worst: 2024-25, which never passed 135 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs mid-November to late May (193 days).

    So far this winter: 11 cm on 19 November (usual: 19 cm).

    About Warth-Schröcken

    Warth-Schröcken is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 52 km of pistes put it at number 117 of 1,056. Its lifts climb from 1,513 m to 2,051 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Lech/Zürs, 7.0 km to the south.

    Count on about 27 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 1,782 m in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May. By mid-April the typical depth is 160 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. A normal season tops out near 189 cm, around 24 March.     Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 538 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. With its base at 1,513 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Its 14 lifts break down into 1 gondola, 12 chairlifts and 1 button lift. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. The highest named summit within 8 km is Braunarlspitze at 2,649 m, standing 598 m above the top lift.
