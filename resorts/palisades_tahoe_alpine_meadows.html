    What 46 winters say about Palisades Tahoe Alpine Meadows

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Palisades Tahoe Alpine Meadows (7,359 ft (2,243 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 25 in (64 cm); half of the winters sit between 12 in (30 cm) and 41 in (104 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 48 in (123 cm), between 35 in (88 cm) and 65 in (166 cm) in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 58 in (148 cm).

    By 15 December 48 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 67 %.

    2022-23 was the big one (120 in (306 cm)); 2014-15 the lean one (24 in (61 cm)).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from late November until mid-May in a normal winter, roughly 165 days.

    So far this winter: 22 in (57 cm) on 27 November (usual: 5.9 in (15 cm)).

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Palisades Tahoe Alpine Meadows gives 692 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Even the lowest lift starts at 1,897 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Of the 8 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. The highest named summit within 8 km is Granite Chief at 2,749 m, standing 160 m above the top lift.
