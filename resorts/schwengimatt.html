    About Schwengimatt

    Compact and local, Schwengimatt has about 2.0 km of runs, small for the Alps. Its lifts climb from 997 m to 1,099 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Langenbruck, 8.7 km to the north-east.

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 4 graded runs, about 1.6 km in total. Most of the length is blue (100%, about 1.6 km). With 100% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    How Schwengimatt is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Schwengimatt gives 102 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The base at 997 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. Its top at 1,099 m is the third highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. The only lift is a T-bar. The highest named summit within 8 km is Chamben at 1,251 m, standing 152 m above the top lift.

    Snow at Schwengimatt through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at about 1,048 m, the middle of the ski area rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. A normal season tops out near 7 cm, around 26 January. At Christmas there was snow here in 6 winters out of ten, typically 3 cm (lean years 0 cm, snowy years 17 cm). Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 0 cm to 26 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. The best weeks for snow are usually late January and early February. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow.

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at Schwengimatt?

    Aim for late January and early February.

    Can you ski at Schwengimatt at Christmas?

    Sometimes. Christmas snow at mid-mountain came in 6 winters out of ten, usually about 3 cm, so check before you go.

    How high is Schwengimatt?

    The top is at 1,099 m and the base at 997 m.

    Can beginners ski at Schwengimatt?

    Yes: 100% of the graded pistes, about 1.6 km, are easy.

    How many km of pistes does Schwengimatt have?

    About 2.0 km of pistes and 1 lift.
