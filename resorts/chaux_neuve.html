    What 46 winters say about Chaux-Neuve

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Chaux-Neuve (about 1,071 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 10 cm, with a normal range of 1 cm–27 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 22 cm; the middle half of winters range from 3 cm to 50 cm.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 23 cm.

    11 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 11 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1980-81 (130 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2000-01 (17 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from early January until mid-March in a normal winter, roughly 61 days.

    Today's 1 cm compares with a usual 2 cm for 24 November.

    About Chaux-Neuve

    Chaux-Neuve is a small ski area by the standards of the Alps, with about 1.0 km of pistes. Skiing here happens between 1,010 m and 1,132 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Foncine le Haut (4.1 km, south-west).

    Nearby alternatives compared

    For more terrain, L'Abbaye (14 km east) has about 7.0 km of pistes against 1.0 km here. L'Abbaye has the longer snow season: around 5 weeks of solid cover, compared with 0 at Chaux-Neuve. The highest nearby is L'Abbaye, topping out at 1,435 m.

    Lifts and terrain

    122 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Its summit is modest for the area; the highest lifts nearby, at Dent de Vaulion, reach 1,465 m. To get up there are 1 chairlift and 1 button lift. The skyline is dominated by Gros Crêt, 1,408 m high and 8.0 km east of the resort.
