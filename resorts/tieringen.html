    About Tieringen

    About 2.0 km of pistes make Tieringen one of the smaller places to ski in the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 806 m up to 859 m. Deilingen is the next resort, 7.1 km away to the south-west.

    Snow at Tieringen through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 832 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 0 cm to 18 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. Depth normally peaks in mid-January at roughly 4 cm. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April. The best weeks for snow are usually late January and early February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in 6 winters out of ten; the usual depth was 2 cm, with 11 cm in the best years.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Tieringen gives 53 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 806 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. The only lift is a T-bar. Above the ski area rises Wandbühl (1,007 m), 7.6 km to the south-west, about 148 m higher than the top lift.

    Tieringen: common questions

    When does Tieringen have the most snow?

    Aim for late January and early February.

    Can you ski at Tieringen at Christmas?

    Sometimes. Christmas snow at mid-mountain came in 6 winters out of ten, usually about 2 cm, so check before you go.

    What altitude is Tieringen at?

    Between 806 m and 859 m, so 53 m of vertical drop.

    How big is Tieringen?

    About 2.0 km of pistes and 1 lift.
