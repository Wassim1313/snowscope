    About Heidialm

    Heidialm is a mid-sized ski area with about 7.0 km of pistes, number 575 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. Skiing here happens between 1,860 m and 2,124 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Bad Kleinkirchheim, 6.6 km to the south-west.

    Snow at Heidialm through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,992 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to early May, about 20 weeks of good cover. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 40 cm and 97 cm. A normal season tops out near 70 cm, around 25 February. By mid-April the typical depth is 51 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 36 cm (lean years 13 cm, snowy years 61 cm).

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 264 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. A high base at 1,860 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. Its top at 2,124 m is the third highest of the 8 ski areas within 50 km. Getting up the mountain: 1 T-bar, 1 button lift and 1 drag lift, 3 lifts in all. Above the ski area rises Rinsennock (2,334 m), 5.0 km to the north, about 210 m higher than the top lift.

    Heidialm: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Heidialm?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to early May.

    Is there snow at Heidialm at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 36 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high is Heidialm?

    Between 1,860 m and 2,124 m, so 264 m of vertical drop.

    Who is Heidialm best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 68% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    Which bigger ski area is near Heidialm?

    Bad Kleinkirchheim, 6.7 km to the south-west, with about 68 km of pistes.
