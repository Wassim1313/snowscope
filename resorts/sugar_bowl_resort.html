    The snow at Sugar Bowl Resort, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Sugar Bowl Resort (about 7,641 ft (2,329 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 33 in (84 cm), with a normal range of 16 in (41 cm)–56 in (143 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 68 in (172 cm) (47 in (119 cm)–92 in (233 cm)).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 83 in (211 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 61 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 78 %.

    Best winter on record: 1982-83, peaking at 176 in (448 cm). Worst: 2014-15, which never passed 33 in (85 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs late November to mid-May (179 days).

    This winter so far: 39 in (98 cm) on 19 November, against a usual 4.3 in (11 cm) for the date.

    Which month has the most snow at Sugar Bowl Resort?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 83 in (211 cm)). For a sure base, late November to mid-May is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How much snow does Sugar Bowl Resort get?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 83 in (211 cm) in mid-March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 176 in (448 cm) (1982-83), the thinnest 33 in (85 cm) (2014-15).

    When does Sugar Bowl Resort have the most snow?

    Aim for early January and early March; the cover is usually good from early December to mid-May.

    About Sugar Bowl Resort

    Neither huge nor tiny, Sugar Bowl Resort offers about 38 km of pistes (number 57 of 175 in US West). Its lifts climb from 2,108 m to 2,550 m. The closest other ski area is Christmas Tree Homesites (0.1 km, south-east).

    The pistes of Sugar Bowl Resort

    The piste map lists 92 graded runs, about 38 km in total. The largest share is blue square, with 21 km (56%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Experts can count on some 9.7 km of difficult runs.

    When the snow is good

    At mid-mountain (about 2,329 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 4 December and lasts to 16 May: about 24 weeks. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early January and early March. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 211 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 84 cm; a snowy December gave 177 cm, a dry one 11 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 76 cm to 304 cm in mid-February in most winters. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 16 weeks. By mid-April the typical depth is 132 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Sugar Bowl Resort gives 442 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. A high base at 2,108 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point. Its 13 lifts break down into 1 gondola, 9 chairlifts, 1 button lift and 2 moving carpets. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. For small children there are 2 conveyor-belt lifts. The skyline is dominated by Castle Peak, 2,773 m high and 7.4 km north of the resort.
