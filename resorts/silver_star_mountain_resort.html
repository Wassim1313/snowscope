    About Silver Star

    About 111 km of pistes make Silver Star one of the ten biggest ski areas of Canada West (number 3). Its lifts climb from 1,146 m to 1,886 m.

    Runs by difficulty

    Silver Star has 184 graded runs adding up to about 110 km. Most of the length is blue square (41%, about 45 km). No single level dominates; beginners and good skiers both find runs to suit them. Advanced skiers have about 33 km of black or expert runs.

    How Silver Star is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 740 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. To get up there are 1 gondola, 5 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 4 moving carpets. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. First-timers get 4 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes.

    How much snow Silver Star usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,516 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to late April, about 19 weeks of good cover. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 46 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 38 cm and 81 cm. For the best snow, aim for early February and late February. A normal season tops out near 67 cm, around 7 March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 42 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 22 cm in a poor year to 61 cm in a big one.

    Questions about Silver Star

    When is the best time to ski at Silver Star?

    Aim for early February and late February; the cover is usually good from mid-December to late April.

    How much snow does Silver Star have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 42 cm deep.

    When does Silver Star open for the 2026/27 season?

    The announced opening day is 27 November 2026.

    How high is Silver Star?

    The top is at 1,886 m and the base at 1,146 m.

    Is Silver Star good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 33 km of its graded pistes (30%) are difficult.
