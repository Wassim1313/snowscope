    About Aavasaksa

    Aavasaksa is a ski area. The slopes start at 85 m and reach 198 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Aavasaksanrinteet (0.1 km, south-east).

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 20 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 142 m) in an ordinary winter, from mid-December to early May. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 38 cm, with 51 cm in the best years. By mid-April the typical depth is 54 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Year to year the snow is steady, with 50 cm to 80 cm in mid-February in most winters. The snow is usually deepest around 20 March, with about 68 cm.

    How Aavasaksa is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 113 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Among the 4 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Svanstein reaches 334 m. Its 2 lifts break down into 1 T-bar and 1 moving carpet. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Aavasaksa (244 m) and Isovaara (174 m).

    Where to stay

    Near the lifts you will find Jokivarren Majatalo ja Mökit (guesthouse), Matarengi Lodge (hotel), Hotell Prästgården (hotel) and Norrsken Lodge (hotel).

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at Aavasaksa?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to early May.

    Can you ski at Aavasaksa at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 38 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    How high is Aavasaksa?

    Its highest lift reaches 198 m; the lowest starts at 85 m (113 m of vertical).
