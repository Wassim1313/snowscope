    About Mizuho Highland

    With some 14 km of runs, Mizuho Highland belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of Japan. The slopes start at 491 m and reach 1,213 m at the top. There are 6 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Ski Park Kanbiki, 6.1 km to the east.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Mizuho Highland has 13 graded runs adding up to about 14 km. Red runs make up the biggest part: 5.6 km, or 39%. The mix is balanced: easy slopes to learn on and enough steeper runs to keep good skiers busy. Advanced skiers have about 4.3 km of black or expert runs.

    How Mizuho Highland is laid out

    722 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. At 491 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. Its 5 lifts break down into 1 gondola and 4 chairlifts. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up.

    Questions about Mizuho Highland

    How high is Mizuho Highland?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,213 m; the lowest starts at 491 m (722 m of vertical).

    Is Mizuho Highland good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 4.3 km of its graded pistes (30%) are difficult.

    How big is Mizuho Highland?

    About 14 km of pistes and 5 lifts.
