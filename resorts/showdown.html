    The snow at Showdown, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Showdown (about 7,533 ft (2,296 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 24 in (62 cm); half of the winters sit between 19 in (48 cm) and 32 in (82 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 43 in (110 cm); the middle half of winters range from 37 in (94 cm) to 56 in (143 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 50 in (128 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 57 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 83 %.

    Best winter on record: 2010-11, peaking at 126 in (320 cm). Worst: 1980-81, which never passed 36 in (91 cm).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from mid-November until mid-May in a normal winter, roughly 188 days.

    This winter so far: 1.6 in (4 cm) on 19 November, against a usual 9.4 in (24 cm) for the date.

    About Showdown

    Neither huge nor tiny, Showdown offers about 26 km of pistes (number 86 of 175 in US West). The slopes start at 2,095 m and reach 2,496 m at the top.

    The pistes of Showdown

    In all, 42 marked runs cover roughly 26 km. Most of the length is blue square (46%, about 12 km). Intermediate runs make up 46% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. Advanced skiers have about 4.2 km of black or expert runs.

    When the snow is good

    At about 2,296 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 23 November and lasts to 16 May: about 25 weeks. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and early April. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 62 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 102 cm in the best years. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 128 cm. For about 13 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 64 cm and 161 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 119 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Showdown gives 401 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. At 2,095 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Its 4 lifts break down into 3 chairlifts and 1 moving carpet. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Porphyry Peak (2,498 m), Grendah Mountain (2,492 m) and Kings Hill (2,441 m).
