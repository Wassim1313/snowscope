    Klippitztörl in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Klippitztörl (1,644 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 23 cm of cover (13 cm to 40 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 44 cm; the middle half of winters range from 22 cm to 57 cm.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 48 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 2 % of winters, and by 1 January in 26 %.

    The deepest winter was 1985-86 (232 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2001-02 (22 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late December to early April (111 days).

    Today's 11 cm compares with a usual 6 cm for 27 November.

    About Klippitztörl

    Klippitztörl is a mid-sized ski area with about 13 km of pistes, number 413 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,470 m up to 1,817 m. Its nearest neighbour is Schilift Obdach, 12 km north, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Snow at Klippitztörl through a normal winter

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 1,644 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 8 January and keeps it until about 24 March, roughly 11 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 5 cm: spring snow is mostly gone at this height. Most winters land between 7 cm and 74 cm by mid-February. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 23 cm, from 5 cm in a poor year to 55 cm in a big one. The snow is usually deepest around 26 February, with about 48 cm.

    Runs by difficulty

    In all, 28 marked runs cover roughly 13 km. Most of the length is blue (85%, about 11 km). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    347 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Starting at 1,470 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Of the 8 ski areas within 50 km, only 1 reaches higher. Getting up the mountain: 2 chairlifts, 2 T-bars, 1 button lift and 1 rope tow, 6 lifts in all. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. Above the ski area rises Forstalpe (2,034 m), 6.6 km to the south, about 217 m higher than the top lift.
