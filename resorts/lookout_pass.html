    Lookout Pass in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Lookout Pass (5,328 ft (1,624 m)), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 38 in (97 cm), with a normal range of 29 in (73 cm)–47 in (120 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 68 in (172 cm) (56 in (142 cm)–79 in (201 cm)).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 75 in (191 cm).

    By 15 December 85 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 98 %.

    The deepest winter was 1996-97 (141 in (359 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2004-05 (42 in (106 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late November to mid-May, about 185 days.

    Questions about Lookout Pass

    Does Lookout Pass have snow over Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 38 in (97 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 98 % of 46 winters. Right now: 10 in (26 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Lookout Pass?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 75 in (191 cm)). For a sure base, late November to mid-May is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How much snow does Lookout Pass get?

    About 75 in (191 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (late February); between 42 in (106 cm) in the leanest winter (2004-05) and 141 in (359 cm) in the biggest (1996-97).

    How high do the lifts go at Lookout Pass?

    Between 1,374 m and 1,875 m, so 501 m of vertical drop.

    How big is Lookout Pass?

    About 37 km of pistes and 5 lifts.
