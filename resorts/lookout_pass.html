    About Lookout Pass

    With roughly 37 km of runs, Lookout Pass sits in the middle of the size table of US West. The slopes start at 1,374 m and reach 1,875 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Silver (32 km, west).

    The pistes of Lookout Pass

    Counting every graded piste, there are 55 runs and around 37 km to ski. Most of the length is blue square (51%, about 19 km). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Experts can count on some 13 km of difficult runs.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 26 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 1,624 m) in an ordinary winter, from late November to mid-May. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 97 cm; a snowy December gave 134 cm, a dry one 58 cm. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 191 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 18 weeks. Year to year the snow is steady, with 109 cm to 231 cm in mid-February in most winters. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 159 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring.

    How Lookout Pass is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 501 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. The bottom station at 1,374 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. All 5 lifts here are chairlifts. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Above the ski area rises Stevens Peak (2,084 m), 5.2 km to the south-west, about 209 m higher than the top lift.

    Questions about Lookout Pass

    When does Lookout Pass have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from late November to mid-May.

    Is there snow at Lookout Pass at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 97 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high do the lifts go at Lookout Pass?

    Between 1,374 m and 1,875 m, so 501 m of vertical drop.

    Is Lookout Pass good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 13 km of its graded pistes (34%) are difficult.

    How big is Lookout Pass?

    About 37 km of pistes and 5 lifts.
