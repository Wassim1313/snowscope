    Kappl in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Kappl (1,906 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 51 cm of cover (37 cm to 62 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 89 cm; the middle half of winters range from 75 cm to 109 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 100 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 35 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 57 %.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 185 cm. Worst: 2024-25, which never passed 69 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs mid-November to early June (202 days).

    This winter so far: 18 cm on 19 November, against a usual 20 cm for the date.

    Kappl: quick answers

    Does Kappl have snow over Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 51 cm, and only 57 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 25 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Kappl?

    March: the snow peaks at about 100 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June.

    How snowy is Kappl?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 100 cm in late March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 185 cm (1998-99), the thinnest 69 cm (2024-25).

    When does Kappl have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and late March; the cover is usually good from early December to late May.

    How high is Kappl?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,639 m; the lowest starts at 1,173 m (1,466 m of vertical).

    Who is Kappl best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 60% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    About Kappl

    Kappl is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 30 km of pistes put it at number 204 of 1,056. The slopes start at 1,173 m and reach 2,639 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Ski Arlberg (8.3 km, north).

    Lifts and terrain

    1,466 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Getting up the mountain: 1 gondola, 3 chairlifts, 2 T-bars and 6 moving carpets, 12 lifts in all. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 6 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Hoher Riffler (Nord) (3,167 m), 5.2 km to the north, about 528 m higher than the top lift.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Kappl has 74 graded runs adding up to about 29 km. The largest share is red, with 17 km (60%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Advanced skiers have about 4.3 km of black or expert runs.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 26 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,906 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from early December to late May or later. Year to year the snow is steady, with 61 cm to 121 cm in mid-February in most winters. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and late March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 51 cm; a snowy December gave 73 cm, a dry one 27 cm. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 100 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 90 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered.
