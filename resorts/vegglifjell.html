    About Vegglifjell

    Compact and local, Vegglifjell has about 2.0 km of runs, small for Scandinavia. The slopes start at 966 m and reach 1,112 m at the top. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Gaustablikk Skisenter, 24 km to the south-west.

    Snow at Vegglifjell through a normal winter

    Count on about 28 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 1,039 m in an ordinary winter, from mid-November to late May. Year to year the snow is steady, with 73 cm to 142 cm in mid-February in most winters. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 67 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 42 cm in a poor year to 101 cm in a big one. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 104 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 113 cm. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 12 weeks.

    How Vegglifjell is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Vegglifjell gives 146 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Among its 4 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. The only lift is a T-bar. The skyline is dominated by Eidsfjell, 1,333 m high and 7.2 km north of the resort.

    Vegglifjell: common questions

    When does Vegglifjell have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from mid-November to late May.

    Is there snow at Vegglifjell at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 67 cm deep.

    How high is Vegglifjell?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,112 m; the lowest starts at 966 m (146 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Vegglifjell have?

    About 2.0 km of pistes and 1 lift.
