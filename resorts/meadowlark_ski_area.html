    About Meadowlark

    Meadowlark is a small ski area by the standards of US West, with about 6.0 km of pistes. Its lifts climb from 2,583 m to 2,825 m.

    What there is to ski at Meadowlark

    In all, 11 marked runs cover roughly 5.9 km. The largest share is green circle, with 3.7 km (62%). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    How Meadowlark is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Meadowlark gives 242 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 2,583 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. All 2 lifts here are chairlifts.

    How much snow Meadowlark usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 2,704 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to early May, about 22 weeks of good cover. Year to year the snow is steady, with 32 cm to 91 cm in mid-February in most winters. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March. A normal season tops out near 77 cm, around 1 April. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 39 cm, with 70 cm in the best years. By mid-April the typical depth is 66 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring.

    Quick answers

    When does Meadowlark have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from early December to early May.

    How much snow does Meadowlark have at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 39 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    What altitude is Meadowlark at?

    Between 2,583 m and 2,825 m, so 242 m of vertical drop.

    Is Meadowlark good for beginners?

    Yes: 62% of the graded pistes, about 3.7 km, are easy.

    How big is Meadowlark?

    About 6.0 km of pistes and 2 lifts.
