    The snow at Winter Park Resort, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Winter Park Resort (10,538 ft (3,212 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 26 in (65 cm) of cover (15 in (38 cm) to 32 in (81 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 40 in (102 cm); the middle half of winters range from 33 in (84 cm) to 48 in (122 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 49 in (125 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    57 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 72 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1983-84 (88 in (224 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2025-26 (17 in (44 cm)).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from mid-November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 194 days.

    Today's 2 in (5 cm) compares with a usual 11 in (28 cm) for 21 November.

    Questions about Winter Park Resort

    Can you ski Winter Park Resort at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 26 in (65 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 72 % of 46 winters. Right now: 6.3 in (16 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Winter Park Resort?

    March: the snow peaks at about 49 in (125 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May.

    How deep does the snow get at Winter Park Resort?

    About 49 in (125 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (late March); between 17 in (44 cm) in the leanest winter (2025-26) and 88 in (224 cm) in the biggest (1983-84).

    How high is Winter Park Resort?

    Its highest lift reaches 3,654 m; the lowest starts at 2,771 m (883 m of vertical).

    Does Winter Park Resort have steep runs?

    Yes: about 40 km of its graded pistes (36%) are difficult.

    About Winter Park Resort

    Winter Park Resort is one of the bigger ski areas of US West: about 112 km of pistes put it at number 12 of 175. The ski area spans the mountain from 2,771 m up to 3,654 m. The closest other ski area is Colorado Adventure Park (8.6 km, north).

    The pistes of Winter Park Resort

    Winter Park Resort has 149 graded runs adding up to about 110 km. The largest share is blue square, with 41 km (37%). With 36% of the pistes graded difficult, it suits strong skiers looking for steep runs. Advanced skiers have about 40 km of black or expert runs.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    883 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. A high base at 2,771 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. To get up there are 2 gondolas, 18 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 1 rope tow and 3 moving carpets. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. For small children there are 3 conveyor-belt lifts. The skyline is dominated by Parry Peak, 4,076 m high and 6.5 km south-east of the resort.

    How much snow Winter Park Resort usually gets

    Count on about 27 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 3,212 m) in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 65 cm; a snowy December gave 98 cm, a dry one 28 cm. For about 11 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Most winters land between 70 cm and 142 cm by mid-February. The snow is usually deepest around 26 March, with about 125 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late March and early April. By mid-April the typical depth is 110 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered.
