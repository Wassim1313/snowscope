    About Alpe d'Huez Grand Domaine

    Alpe d'Huez Grand Domaine is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 163 km of pistes put it at number 23 of 1,056. Skiing here happens between 1,124 m and 3,306 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Les Deux Alpes, 13 km south-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 2,182 m of vertical, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. Getting up the mountain: 4 cable cars, 2 funiculars, 22 gondolas, 5 chair-gondolas, 15 chairlifts, 17 button lifts, 1 drag lift, 1 rope tow and 15 moving carpets, 82 lifts in all. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. For small children there are 15 conveyor-belt lifts. The highest named summit within 8 km is Pic Bayle at 3,465 m, standing 159 m above the top lift.

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 311 marked runs cover roughly 160 km. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 49 km, or 30%. With 56% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 2,215 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to late May, about 26 weeks of good cover. Year to year the snow is steady, with 103 cm to 188 cm in mid-February in most winters. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 84 cm (lean years 43 cm, snowy years 117 cm). A normal season tops out near 159 cm, around 5 March. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. By mid-April the typical depth is 136 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 17 weeks.
