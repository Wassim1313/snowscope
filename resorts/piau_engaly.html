    What 46 winters say about Piau-Engaly

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Piau-Engaly (about 2,144 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 40 cm of cover (18 cm to 58 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 90 cm; the middle half of winters range from 53 cm to 115 cm.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 98 cm.

    28 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 43 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 2013-14, peaking at 207 cm. Worst: 1992-93, which never passed 44 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to late April, about 140 days.

    Common questions

    Does Piau-Engaly have snow over Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 40 cm, and only 43 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 35 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Piau-Engaly?

    February: the snow peaks at about 98 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to late April.

    How snowy is Piau-Engaly?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 98 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 207 cm (2013-14), the thinnest 44 cm (1992-93).

    What altitude is Piau-Engaly at?

    The top is at 2,527 m and the base at 1,762 m.

    How big is Piau-Engaly?

    About 32 km of pistes and 8 lifts.

    About Piau-Engaly

    With roughly 32 km of runs, Piau-Engaly sits in the middle of the size table of the Pyrenees. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,762 m up to 2,527 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Saint-Lary, 9.4 km to the north-east.

    How Piau-Engaly is laid out

    765 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,762 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Among its 6 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point.
