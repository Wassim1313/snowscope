    The snow at Titus Mountain, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Titus Mountain (about 1,522 ft (464 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 9.4 in (24 cm); half of the winters sit between 3.9 in (10 cm) and 12 in (31 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 17 in (44 cm), between 11 in (29 cm) and 22 in (57 cm) in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 18 in (46 cm).

    4 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 7 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1992-93 (41 in (104 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2015-16 (10 in (26 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late December to late March, about 84 days.

    About Titus Mountain

    Titus Mountain is a mid-sized ski area with about 22 km of pistes, number 32 of 93 in US Northeast by length. Skiing here happens between 320 m and 609 m above sea level.

    How Titus Mountain is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Titus Mountain gives 289 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 320 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Getting up the mountain: 8 chairlifts and 1 moving carpet, 9 lifts in all. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Mount Immortelle (702 m), Owls Head (679 m) and Little Humbug Mountain (674 m).

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 9 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 464 m in an ordinary winter, from late January to late March. A normal season tops out near 46 cm, around 18 February. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and late February. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 24 cm, from 4 cm in a poor year to 40 cm in a big one. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 21 cm and 70 cm. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any.
