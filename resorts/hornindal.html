    The snow at Hornindal, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Hornindal (about 565 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 122 cm, with a normal range of 92 cm–146 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 188 cm; the middle half of winters range from 150 cm to 240 cm.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 232 cm.

    93 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 100 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1988-89 (365 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2000-01 (153 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from early November until mid-June in a normal winter, roughly 223 days.

    This winter so far: 24 cm on 26 November, against a usual 64 cm for the date.

    About Hornindal

    About 1.0 km of pistes make Hornindal one of the smaller places to ski in Scandinavia. The ski area spans the mountain from 390 m up to 740 m. Its nearest neighbour is Hornindal, 0.3 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How much snow Hornindal usually gets

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 565 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 8 November and keeps it until about the end of May, roughly 29 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually late March and early April. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 220 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. The snow is usually deepest around 22 March, with about 232 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 122 cm (lean years 75 cm, snowy years 166 cm). For about 24 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Most winters land between 123 cm and 273 cm by mid-February.

    The mountain at Hornindal

    The vertical drop is 350 m: runs of a moderate length. The only lift is a drag lift. The skyline is dominated by Storhornet, 1,600 m high and 7.2 km north of the resort.
