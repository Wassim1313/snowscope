    About Ridin-Hy

    Ridin-Hy has about 0.5 km of pistes. The slopes start at 288 m and reach 332 m at the top. There are 6 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Dynamite Hill, 5.4 km to the north-west.

    How Ridin-Hy compares nearby

    Gore Mountain has the longer snow season: around 12 weeks of solid cover, compared with 8 at Ridin-Hy. The highest nearby is Gore Mountain, topping out at 1,092 m.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 310 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from late January to late March, about 8 weeks of good cover. Most winters land between 17 cm and 70 cm by mid-February. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 40 cm. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 17 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 38 cm in the best years.

    The mountain at Ridin-Hy

    44 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lowest slopes are at only 288 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. The only lift is a T-bar. Above the ski area rises Thomas Mountain (619 m), 4.7 km to the south-east, about 287 m higher than the top lift.

    Staying nearby

    Near the lifts you will find 2 guesthouses: The Chester Inn and Landon Hill Bed & Breakfast.

    Questions about Ridin-Hy

    When is the best time to ski at Ridin-Hy?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from late January to late March.

    How much snow does Ridin-Hy have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 17 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Ridin-Hy?

    Its highest lift reaches 332 m; the lowest starts at 288 m (44 m of vertical).

    How big is Ridin-Hy?

    About 0.5 km of pistes and 1 lift.
