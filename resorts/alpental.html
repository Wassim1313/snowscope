    Alpental in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Alpental (4,291 ft (1,308 m)), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 49 in (125 cm), with a normal range of 37 in (93 cm)–60 in (152 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 99 in (251 cm), between 79 in (201 cm) and 114 in (290 cm) in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 113 in (287 cm).

    87 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 96 % by 1 January.

    1998-99 was the big one (206 in (523 cm)); 2014-15 the lean one (45 in (114 cm)).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from mid-November until mid-June in a normal winter, roughly 213 days.

    So far this winter: 0.4 in (1 cm) on 21 November (usual: 11 in (29 cm)).

    Alpental: quick answers

    Can you ski Alpental at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 49 in (125 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 96 % of 46 winters. Right now: 3.5 in (9 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Alpental?

    March: the snow peaks at about 113 in (287 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to mid-June.

    How much snow does Alpental get?

    About 113 in (287 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (late March); between 45 in (114 cm) in the leanest winter (2014-15) and 206 in (523 cm) in the biggest (1998-99).

    When does Alpental have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from mid-November to late May.

    What altitude is Alpental at?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,659 m; the lowest starts at 958 m (701 m of vertical).

    Is Alpental good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 9.2 km of its graded pistes (69%) are difficult.

    About Alpental

    Neither huge nor tiny, Alpental offers about 14 km of pistes (number 115 of 175 in US West). The slopes start at 958 m and reach 1,659 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Sahalie Ski Club (1.8 km, south-east).

    When the snow is good

    At about 1,308 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 20 November and lasts to the end of May: about 28 weeks. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 287 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and late February. Year to year the snow is steady, with 154 cm to 342 cm in mid-February in most winters. By mid-April the typical depth is 262 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 125 cm, with 183 cm in the best years. For about 24 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 32 runs and around 13 km to ski. Most of the length is black diamond (46%, about 6.1 km). Steep ground is the speciality: 69% of the length is graded difficult. The steepest pistes add up to about 9.2 km.

    The mountain at Alpental

    From top to bottom that makes 701 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. Below about 1,158 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. Its top at 1,659 m is the second highest of the 6 ski areas within 50 km. Getting up the mountain: 5 chairlifts and 1 moving carpet, 6 lifts in all. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. The highest named summit within 8 km is Mount Thomson at 1,998 m, standing 339 m above the top lift.
