    The snow at Alta Badia, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Alta Badia (about 1,934 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 33 cm, with a normal range of 22 cm–48 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 56 cm; the middle half of winters range from 44 cm to 70 cm.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 59 cm.

    By 15 December 15 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 30 %.

    Best winter on record: 2013-14, peaking at 129 cm. Worst: 2016-17, which never passed 37 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from early December until early May in a normal winter, roughly 158 days.

    This winter so far: 27 cm on 27 November, against a usual 17 cm for the date.

    About Alta Badia

    Alta Badia is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 127 km of pistes put it at number 34 of 1,056. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,341 m up to 2,526 m. Its nearest neighbour is Dolomiti Superski, 2.4 km south, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Snow at Alta Badia through a normal winter

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 1,934 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 16 December and keeps it until about 5 May, roughly 20 weeks. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 59 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 53 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 33 cm, from 12 cm in a poor year to 64 cm in a big one. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 38 cm and 85 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Alta Badia gives 1,185 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,341 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. Its 59 lifts break down into 1 funicular, 11 gondolas, 29 chairlifts, 9 button lifts, 1 drag lift and 8 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. For small children there are 8 conveyor-belt lifts. The highest named summit within 8 km is Piz Boè at 3,152 m, standing 626 m above the top lift.
