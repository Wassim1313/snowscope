    What 46 winters say about Whitewater

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Whitewater (about 1,725 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 91 cm of cover (79 cm to 107 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 128 cm (112 cm–141 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 148 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    89 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 100 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 233 cm. Worst: 2014-15, which never passed 117 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs mid-November to early June (203 days).

    So far this winter: 19 cm on 25 November (usual: 53 cm).

    About Whitewater

    Neither huge nor tiny, Whitewater offers about 55 km of pistes (number 14 of 53 in Canada West). The slopes start at 1,390 m and reach 2,060 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Salmo, 30 km south, and 1 ski area lie within 40 km in all.

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 86 marked runs cover roughly 54 km. Most of the length is blue square (45%, about 24 km). Steep ground is the speciality: 43% of the length is graded difficult. Advanced skiers have about 23 km of black or expert runs.

    Run colours follow the North American system: green circles are easiest, blue squares intermediate, black diamonds difficult.

    How Whitewater is laid out

    670 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. Starting at 1,390 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Getting up the mountain: 4 chairlifts and 1 rope tow, 5 lifts in all. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. The skyline is dominated by Ymir Mountain, 2,398 m high and 2.7 km east of the resort.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,725 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-November to late May or later, about 28 weeks of good cover. For about 19 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 91 cm, with 125 cm in the best years. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 141 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-March and late March. A normal season tops out near 148 cm, around 27 March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 101 cm to 156 cm in mid-February in most winters.
