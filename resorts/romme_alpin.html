    About Romme Alpin

    With some 28 km of runs, Romme Alpin belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of Scandinavia. The slopes start at 141 m and reach 406 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Nybroberget, 4.5 km north-east, and 2 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 57 runs and around 28 km to ski. The largest share is blue, with 9.3 km (33%). With 60% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Romme Alpin gives 265 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Its top at 406 m is the highest of the 4 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 6 chairlifts, 2 T-bars, 6 button lifts and 3 moving carpets. First-timers get 3 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Klackberget (410 m), Gethällsklack (395 m) and Solklinten (390 m).

    Snow this week

    Up high, Romme Alpin has roughly 11 cm of snow on the ground today. The last seven days brought 7 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. An ordinary winter would have around 6 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track.

    How much snow Romme Alpin usually gets

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 274 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. The snow is usually deepest around 10 February, with about 28 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 7 cm to 48 cm in mid-February in most winters. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 14 cm (snow lay here in nearly every winter), with 32 cm in the best years. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early February and late February. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any.
