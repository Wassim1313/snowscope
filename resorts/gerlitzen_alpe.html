    Gerlitzen Alpe in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Gerlitzen Alpe (about 1,455 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 19 cm of cover (11 cm to 32 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 31 cm (22 cm–37 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 35 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 2 % of winters, and by 1 January in 7 %.

    The deepest winter was 2008-09 (141 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2001-02 (18 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from late December until late April in a normal winter, roughly 113 days.

    Today's 15 cm compares with a usual 6 cm for 27 November.

    Around 37 km of marked runs place Gerlitzen Alpe among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 166 by length. The slopes start at 1,002 m and reach 1,908 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Bad Kleinkirchheim, 16 km north-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How Gerlitzen Alpe is laid out

    906 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. To get up there are 2 gondolas, 7 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 2 button lifts, 2 drag lifts, 1 rope tow and 5 moving carpets. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. For small children there are 5 conveyor-belt lifts. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Gerlitzen (1,909 m), Buchskopf (1,865 m) and Dürrer Baum (1,771 m).

    When the snow is good

    At about 1,455 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 12 February and lasts to 4 April: about 8 weeks. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 19 cm, with 42 cm in the best years. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 35 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 24 cm, and the lower runs are often thin by then. Year to year the snow is steady, with 13 cm to 53 cm in mid-February in most winters. For the best snow, aim for late March and early April.
