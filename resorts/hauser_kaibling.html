    About Hauser Kaibling

    With some 30 km of runs, Hauser Kaibling belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 724 m up to 2,004 m. Its nearest neighbour is Horsefeathers Superpark Planai, 4.5 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Runs by difficulty

    Hauser Kaibling has 48 graded runs adding up to about 30 km. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 19 km, or 63%. With 63% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 1,280 m of vertical, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. With the bottom at 724 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Of the 8 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. Getting up the mountain: 1 cable car, 2 gondolas, 6 chairlifts and 2 button lifts, 11 lifts in all. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. The highest named summit within 8 km is Hochwildstelle at 2,747 m, standing 743 m above the top lift.

    Snow at Hauser Kaibling through a normal winter

    At mid-mountain (about 1,364 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 23 December and lasts to 29 April: about 18 weeks. Most winters land between 32 cm and 74 cm by mid-February. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. The snow is usually deepest around 29 February, with about 63 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 31 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 52 cm in the best years. By mid-April the typical depth is 37 cm; by then it pays to ski early, before the sun softens it.

    Quick answers

    When does Hauser Kaibling have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from late December to late April.

    Can you ski at Hauser Kaibling at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 31 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    What altitude is Hauser Kaibling at?

    Between 724 m and 2,004 m, so 1,280 m of vertical drop.

    Can beginners ski at Hauser Kaibling?

    Yes: 63% of the graded pistes, about 19 km, are easy.

    How many km of pistes does Hauser Kaibling have?

    About 30 km of pistes and 11 lifts.
