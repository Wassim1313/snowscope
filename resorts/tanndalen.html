    What 46 winters say about Tänndalen

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Tänndalen (about 888 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 49 cm of cover (41 cm to 58 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 79 cm; the middle half of winters range from 68 cm to 87 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 87 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 35 % of winters, and by 1 January in 63 %.

    The deepest winter was 1987-88 (129 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1995-96 (60 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until mid-May in a normal winter, roughly 179 days.

    Today's 9 cm compares with a usual 24 cm for 19 November.

    About Tänndalen

    With some 26 km of runs, Tänndalen belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of Scandinavia. The slopes start at 757 m and reach 1,020 m at the top. Funäsdalen is the next resort, 16 km away to the east.

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 47 marked runs cover roughly 25 km. The largest share is red, with 13 km (50%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. The steepest pistes add up to about 2.2 km.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 25 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 888 m in an ordinary winter, from late November to mid-May. By mid-April the typical depth is 83 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late March and early April. The snow is usually deepest around 29 March, with about 87 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 61 cm to 94 cm in mid-February in most winters. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 49 cm, from 34 cm in a poor year to 67 cm in a big one.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Tänndalen gives 263 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 1,020 m. The lift system counts 17 lifts: 2 chairlifts, 7 T-bars, 5 button lifts and 3 moving carpets. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. 3 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Stor-Skarven at 1,260 m, standing 240 m above the top lift.
