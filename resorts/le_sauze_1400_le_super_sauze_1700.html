    About Le Sauze 1400 / Le Super-Sauze 1

    Around 53 km of marked runs place Le Sauze 1400 / Le Super-Sauze 1 among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 112 by length. The slopes start at 1,374 m and reach 2,388 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Pra-Loup 1600 (9.0 km, west).

    The pistes of Le Sauze 1400 / Le Super-Sauze 1

    The piste map lists 126 graded runs, about 53 km in total. Red runs make up the biggest part: 18 km, or 34%. Mixed groups do well here, with gentle slopes for learners and steeper pistes for the experts. Advanced skiers have about 2.7 km of black or expert runs. 9.5 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 1,014 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,374 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Les Orres, for one, climbs to 2,700 m. Its 19 lifts break down into 3 chairlifts, 15 button lifts and 1 moving carpet. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. The highest named summit within 8 km is Le Cimet at 3,020 m, standing 632 m above the top lift.

    Snow at Le Sauze 1400 / Le Super-Sauze 1 through a normal winter

    At mid-mountain (about 1,881 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 26 December and lasts to 18 April: about 16 weeks. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 34 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 29 cm, from 14 cm in a poor year to 64 cm in a big one. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 35 cm to 87 cm in mid-February in most winters. A normal season tops out near 66 cm, around 7 March.
