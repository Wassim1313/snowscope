    What 46 winters say about Ski Arlberg

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Ski Arlberg (1,962 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 78 cm, with a normal range of 54 cm–98 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 150 cm, between 124 cm and 186 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 169 cm.

    By 15 December 63 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 89 %.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 313 cm. Worst: 2024-25, which never passed 127 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June, about 203 days.

    Today's 23 cm compares with a usual 24 cm for 19 November.

    Ski Arlberg: quick answers

    Can you ski Ski Arlberg at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 78 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 89 % of 46 winters. Right now: 37 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Ski Arlberg?

    March: the snow peaks at about 169 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June.

    How snowy is Ski Arlberg?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 169 cm in late March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 313 cm (1998-99), the thinnest 127 cm (2024-25).

    When does Ski Arlberg have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and late March; the cover is usually good from late November to late May.

    When does Ski Arlberg open for the 2026/27 season?

    About Ski Arlberg

    Ski Arlberg ranks number 7 of 1,056 ski areas in the Alps by piste length, with about 272 km of runs. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,282 m up to 2,642 m. Its nearest neighbour is Lech/Zürs, 3.8 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    The mountain at Ski Arlberg

    Skied top to bottom, Ski Arlberg gives 1,360 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 1 reaches higher. Getting up the mountain: 5 cable cars, 12 gondolas, 1 chair-gondola, 42 chairlifts, 6 T-bars, 16 button lifts, 3 rope tows and 7 moving carpets, 92 lifts in all. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. First-timers get 7 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The skyline is dominated by Valluga, 2,809 m high and 4.4 km south-east of the resort.

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 510 runs and around 272 km to ski. Most of the length is blue (46%, about 125 km). Mixed groups do well here, with gentle slopes for learners and steeper pistes for the experts. Experts can count on some 44 km of difficult runs.

    Snow at Ski Arlberg through a normal winter

    Count on about 27 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 1,962 m in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May or later. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 16 weeks. For the best snow, aim for early March and late March. Most winters land between 104 cm and 206 cm by mid-February. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 169 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 151 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 78 cm, from 36 cm in a poor year to 110 cm in a big one.
