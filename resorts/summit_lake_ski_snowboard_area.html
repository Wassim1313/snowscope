    About Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area

    Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area is a small ski area by the standards of Canada West, with about 4.0 km of pistes. Skiing here happens between 770 m and 911 m above sea level.

    The pistes of Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area

    Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area has 10 graded runs adding up to about 3.6 km. The largest share is blue square, with 1.7 km (48%). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety.

    How much snow Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area usually gets

    In a typical winter the snow at about 840 m, the middle of the ski area reaches a solid 30 cm base around 19 November and keeps it until about 9 May, roughly 25 weeks. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 77 cm (lean years 56 cm, snowy years 99 cm). For about 9 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Most winters land between 87 cm and 141 cm by mid-February. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 86 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. The snow is usually deepest around 14 March, with about 111 cm.

    The mountain at Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area

    From top to bottom that makes 141 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The base at 770 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. The lift system counts 2 lifts: 1 T-bar and 1 rope tow. The skyline is dominated by Big Sister Mountain, 2,465 m high and 4.9 km south-east of the resort.

    Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from mid-November to early May.

    Is there snow at Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 77 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high is Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area?

    Its highest lift reaches 911 m; the lowest starts at 770 m (141 m of vertical).

    Who is Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 48% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How many km of pistes does Summit Lake Ski & Snowboard Area have?

    About 4.0 km of pistes and 2 lifts.
