    About Jeizinen

    With roughly 9.0 km of runs, Jeizinen sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 1,554 m and 2,189 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Leukerbad (Torrent), 7.6 km to the north-west.

    The pistes of Jeizinen

    The piste map lists 14 graded runs, about 8.9 km in total. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 7.5 km, or 85%. Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    Nearby alternatives compared

    The big name nearby is Saint-Luc: about 56 km of pistes, 14 km to the south-west. Saint-Luc has the longer snow season: around 27 weeks of solid cover, compared with 24 at Jeizinen. The highest nearby is Lauchernalp, topping out at 3,084 m.

    Snow this week

    Up high, Jeizinen has roughly 30 cm of snow on the ground today. 24 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 52 cm of new snow is expected by Sunday. An ordinary winter would have around 21 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track.

    Snow at Jeizinen through a normal winter

    At about 1,872 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 9 December and lasts to 25 May: about 24 weeks. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 80 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 54 cm and 103 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 46 cm (lean years 26 cm, snowy years 66 cm). Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 87 cm.

    The mountain at Jeizinen

    Skied top to bottom, Jeizinen gives 635 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Starting at 1,554 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Among the 9 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Lauchernalp reaches 3,084 m. Its 6 lifts break down into 1 cable car, 1 chairlift, 1 T-bar, 1 button lift and 2 rope tows. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. The skyline is dominated by Hohgleifa, 3,279 m high and 6.5 km north-east of the resort.

    Where to stay

    Near the lifts you will find Leuca (chalet), Burgerhaus (chalet), Pension Park (guesthouse) and Burgerhaus Jeizinen (hostel).

    Jeizinen: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Jeizinen?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from early December to late May.

    Can you ski at Jeizinen at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 46 cm deep.

    What altitude is Jeizinen at?

    The top is at 2,189 m and the base at 1,554 m.

    Is Jeizinen suitable for families?

    Yes: 85% of the graded pistes, about 7.5 km, are easy.

    Can Jeizinen make its own snow?

    Yes, on about 1.5 km of its pistes (16%).
