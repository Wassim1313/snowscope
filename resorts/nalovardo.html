    About Nalovardo

    With roughly 4.0 km of runs, Nalovardo sits in the middle of the size table of Scandinavia. The slopes start at 422 m and reach 718 m at the top.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 2 runs and around 4.0 km to ski. The largest share is red, with 4.0 km (100%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns.

    The mountain at Nalovardo

    The vertical drop is 296 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Its 3 lifts break down into 2 T-bars and 1 rope tow.

    The snow right now

    Today there are about 20 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Nalovardo. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. That is less than usual: mid-mountain normally has about 36 cm by now.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 24 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 570 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from late November to mid-May. Most winters land between 58 cm and 100 cm by mid-February. Depth normally peaks in early February at roughly 80 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 74 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 50 cm (lean years 34 cm, snowy years 72 cm). For the best snow, aim for mid-February and mid-March.

    Nalovardo: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Nalovardo?

    Aim for mid-February and mid-March; the cover is usually good from late November to mid-May.

    Can you ski at Nalovardo at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 50 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high is Nalovardo?

    The top is at 718 m and the base at 422 m.

    What level of skier is Nalovardo best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 100% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How many km of pistes does Nalovardo have?

    About 4.0 km of pistes and 3 lifts.
