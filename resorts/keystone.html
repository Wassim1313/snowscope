    Keystone in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Keystone (about 10,781 ft (3,286 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 27 in (68 cm); half of the winters sit between 16 in (41 cm) and 33 in (84 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 42 in (106 cm) (33 in (85 cm)–49 in (124 cm)).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 55 in (140 cm).

    52 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 72 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1983-84 (89 in (227 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2025-26 (26 in (67 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June, about 201 days.

    This winter so far: 2.8 in (7 cm) on 22 November, against a usual 12 in (31 cm) for the date.

    Common questions

    Can you ski Keystone at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 27 in (68 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 72 % of 46 winters. Right now: 8.7 in (22 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Keystone?

    March: the snow peaks at about 55 in (140 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June.

    How deep does the snow get at Keystone?

    About 55 in (140 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (early April); between 26 in (67 cm) in the leanest winter (2025-26) and 89 in (227 cm) in the biggest (1983-84).

    When does Keystone have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-March and late March; the cover is usually good from mid-November to late May.

    How high is Keystone?

    Between 2,829 m and 3,742 m, so 913 m of vertical drop.

    Is Keystone good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 54 km of its graded pistes (46%) are difficult.

    About Keystone

    About 119 km of pistes make Keystone one of the ten biggest ski areas of US West (number 8). The ski area spans the mountain from 2,829 m up to 3,742 m. The closest other ski area is Arapahoe Basin (7.2 km, north-east).

    Runs by difficulty

    Keystone has 179 graded runs adding up to about 118 km. Black diamond runs make up the biggest part: 54 km, or 46%. Good skiers will like it here; 46% of the runs are steep. Experts can count on some 54 km of difficult runs.

    About 48 km of pistes (41%) have snowmaking, so the main runs can open even when natural snow is late.

    Run colours follow the North American system: green circles are easiest, blue squares intermediate, black diamonds difficult.

    The mountain at Keystone

    The vertical drop is 913 m: enough height for proper long runs. Even the lowest lift starts at 2,829 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Getting up the mountain: 2 gondolas, 11 chairlifts and 7 moving carpets, 20 lifts in all. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 7 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Bear Mountain (3,835 m), Independence Mountain (3,828 m) and Morgan Peak (3,791 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 28 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 3,286 m) in an ordinary winter, from mid-November to late May or later. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 68 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 107 cm, a dry one 31 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 66 cm to 148 cm in mid-February in most winters. For the best snow, aim for mid-March and late March. A normal season tops out near 140 cm, around 4 April. By mid-April the typical depth is 122 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 11 weeks.
