    About Belalp

    Around 40 km of marked runs place Belalp among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 145 by length. Its lifts climb from 1,332 m to 3,101 m. Aletsch Arena is the next resort, 8.0 km away to the east.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 1,769 m: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Starting at 1,332 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Its top at 3,101 m is the highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 2 cable cars, 1 gondola, 3 chairlifts, 3 T-bars, 1 button lift and 3 moving carpets. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 3 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Nesthorn, 3,822 m high and 5.5 km north-west of the resort.

    When the snow is good

    At mid-mountain (about 2,216 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 24 November and lasts to the end of May: about 27 weeks. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 106 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 63 cm, with 92 cm in the best years. Year to year the snow is steady, with 71 cm to 132 cm in mid-February in most winters. For the best snow, aim for early March and late March. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 10 weeks. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 111 cm.

    Questions about Belalp

    What is the best month to ski at Belalp?

    Aim for early March and late March; the cover is usually good from late November to late May.

    Can you ski at Belalp at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 63 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high is Belalp?

    The top is at 3,101 m and the base at 1,332 m.

    Is Belalp good for beginners?

    Yes: 50% of the graded pistes, about 20 km, are easy.

    How many km of pistes does Belalp have?

    About 40 km of pistes and 13 lifts.
