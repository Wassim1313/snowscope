    Stratton in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Stratton (about 2,936 ft (895 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 7.9 in (20 cm) of cover (3.9 in (10 cm) to 11 in (28 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 16 in (41 cm) (11 in (29 cm)–23 in (59 cm)).

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 18 in (45 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 2 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 7 %.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (44 in (113 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2015-16 (5.1 in (13 cm)).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from late December until late March in a normal winter, roughly 95 days.

    Stratton: quick answers

    Can you ski Stratton at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 7.9 in (20 cm), and only 7 % of the past 46 winters had 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 1.6 in (4 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Stratton?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 18 in (45 cm)). For a sure base, late December to late March is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How snowy is Stratton?

    About 18 in (45 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 5.1 in (13 cm) in the leanest winter (2015-16) and 44 in (113 cm) in the biggest (2000-01).

    How Stratton is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 576 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. The lowest slopes are at only 607 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. Its 17 lifts break down into 1 gondola, 9 chairlifts, 1 drag lift and 6 moving carpets. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. For small children there are 6 conveyor-belt lifts. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Stratton Mountain (1,194 m), The Pinnacle (769 m) and Mundal Hill (647 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 9 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 895 m) in an ordinary winter, from late January to late March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 14 cm to 78 cm in mid-February in most winters. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 20 cm (lean years 3 cm, snowy years 41 cm). In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. The snow is usually deepest around 4 March, with about 45 cm.
