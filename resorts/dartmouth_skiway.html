    What 46 winters say about Dartmouth Skiway

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Dartmouth Skiway (1,411 ft (430 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 7.5 in (19 cm) of cover (3.5 in (9 cm) to 10 in (26 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 15 in (39 cm); the middle half of winters range from 10 in (26 cm) to 20 in (52 cm).

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 17 in (42 cm).

    0 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 4 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (42 in (107 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2015-16 (6.3 in (16 cm)).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from early January until late March in a normal winter, roughly 82 days.

    Questions about Dartmouth Skiway

    Is there snow in Dartmouth Skiway at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 7.5 in (19 cm) at Christmas on average, with a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 4 % of 46 winters. Right now: no snow.

    What is the best time to ski Dartmouth Skiway?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 17 in (42 cm)). For a sure base, early January to late March is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How snowy is Dartmouth Skiway?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 17 in (42 cm) in mid-February. The deepest of 46 winters reached 42 in (107 cm) (2000-01), the thinnest 6.3 in (16 cm) (2015-16).

    About Dartmouth Skiway

    Neither huge nor tiny, Dartmouth Skiway offers about 16 km of pistes (number 40 of 93 in US Northeast). Its lifts climb from 280 m to 580 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Grill Hill, 16 km to the south-west.

    How Dartmouth Skiway is laid out

    The vertical drop is 300 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Because the lifts start as low as 280 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point.
