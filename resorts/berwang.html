    About Berwang

    With some 28 km of runs, Berwang belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 1,046 m and 1,739 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Waldrastlift, 6.2 km to the north.

    Snow at Berwang through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 1,392 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to late April, about 19 weeks of good cover. The snow is usually deepest around 26 February, with about 74 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and late February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 38 cm; a snowy December gave 59 cm, a dry one 17 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 45 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. Year to year the snow is steady, with 45 cm to 102 cm in mid-February in most winters.

    How Berwang is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Berwang gives 693 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The lift system counts 15 lifts: 2 gondolas, 1 chair-gondola, 4 chairlifts, 5 T-bars, 1 button lift and 2 moving carpets. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Loreakopf, 2,471 m high and 7.4 km south of the resort.

    Berwang: common questions

    When does Berwang have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from mid-December to late April.

    Is there snow at Berwang at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 38 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Berwang?

    The top is at 1,739 m and the base at 1,046 m.

    Is Berwang good for beginners?

    Yes: 59% of the graded pistes, about 16 km, are easy.

    How many km of pistes does Berwang have?

    About 28 km of pistes and 15 lifts.
