    Cortina d'Ampezzo in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Cortina d'Ampezzo (1,799 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 36 cm; half of the winters sit between 21 cm and 50 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 52 cm, between 43 cm and 69 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 58 cm.

    15 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 28 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 2013-14, peaking at 152 cm. Worst: 2001-02, which never passed 35 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early May, about 175 days.

    This winter so far: 4 cm on 24 November, against a usual 15 cm for the date.

    About Cortina d'Ampezzo

    With some 113 km of runs, Cortina d'Ampezzo belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 859 m to 2,739 m. Cortina Tofane is the next resort, 2.5 km away to the west.

    What there is to ski at Cortina d'Ampezzo

    Counting every graded piste, there are 159 runs and around 107 km to ski. The largest share is red, with 53 km (49%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Experts can count on some 11 km of difficult runs.

    The mountain at Cortina d'Ampezzo

    1,880 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. With the bottom at 859 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point. The lift system counts 39 lifts: 4 cable cars, 5 gondolas, 22 chairlifts, 4 button lifts, 1 drag lift and 3 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. First-timers get 3 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The skyline is dominated by Tofana di Mezzo, 3,244 m high and 5.4 km west of the resort.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    At mid-mountain (about 1,799 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 14 December and lasts to 3 May: about 20 weeks. By mid-April the typical depth is 52 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Most winters land between 36 cm and 88 cm by mid-February. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 36 cm, from 12 cm in a poor year to 66 cm in a big one. A normal season tops out near 58 cm, around 3 March.
