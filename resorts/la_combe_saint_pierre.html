    About La Combe Saint-Pierre

    With roughly 4.0 km of runs, La Combe Saint-Pierre sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 867 m to 1,044 m. The closest other ski area is Goule (7.5 km, north).

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 4 runs and around 3.4 km to ski. Most of the length is blue (100%, about 3.4 km). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    Snow at La Combe Saint-Pierre through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at about 956 m, the middle of the ski area rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. At Christmas there was snow here in 8 winters out of ten, typically 6 cm, from 0 cm in a poor year to 32 cm in a big one. Depth normally peaks in late January at roughly 11 cm. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early January and early February. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 0 cm to 42 cm.

    How La Combe Saint-Pierre is laid out

    177 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The base at 867 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. All 4 lifts here are button lifts. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Le Grand Mont (1,049 m), Le Peu (1,046 m) and Le Faux Verger (985 m).

    Staying nearby

    Among the closest beds to the pistes are Au Bois de la Biche (hotel), Le Grand Frêne (chalet), Auberge de la Bouège (guesthouse) and La Couronne (hotel).

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at La Combe Saint-Pierre?

    Aim for early January and early February.

    Is there snow at La Combe Saint-Pierre at Christmas?

    It is a gamble: only 8 winters out of ten had snow at mid-mountain on Christmas Day (typically 6 cm).

    What altitude is La Combe Saint-Pierre at?

    Between 867 m and 1,044 m, so 177 m of vertical drop.

    Is La Combe Saint-Pierre suitable for families?

    Yes: 100% of the graded pistes, about 3.4 km, are easy.

    Which bigger ski area is near La Combe Saint-Pierre?

    Bugnenets-Savagnières, 15 km to the south-east, with about 16 km of pistes.
