    About Ponte di Legno

    With some 56 km of runs, Ponte di Legno belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,120 m and reach 2,993 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Pejo 3000, 15 km north-east, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    What there is to ski at Ponte di Legno

    Counting every graded piste, there are 95 runs and around 56 km to ski. Red runs make up the biggest part: 31 km, or 56%. This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Advanced skiers have about 10 km of black or expert runs.

    How Ponte di Legno is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 1,873 m of vertical, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Its 29 lifts break down into 5 gondolas, 19 chairlifts, 3 button lifts and 2 moving carpets. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Cima Busazza, 3,325 m high and 5.8 km south-east of the resort.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 2,056 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to mid-May, about 23 weeks of good cover. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 49 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 18 cm, snowy years 84 cm). By mid-April the typical depth is 71 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. A normal season tops out near 82 cm, around 3 March. Most winters land between 50 cm and 118 cm by mid-February.
