    About Burke

    Burke is a mid-sized ski area with about 30 km of pistes, number 24 of 93 in US Northeast by length. Its lifts climb from 371 m to 977 m. Lyndon Outing Club is the next resort, 8.3 km away to the south-west.

    Runs by difficulty

    In all, 59 marked runs cover roughly 30 km. The largest share is blue square, with 16 km (54%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. The steepest pistes add up to about 7.2 km.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Burke gives 606 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The lowest slopes are at only 371 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Its top at 977 m is the second highest of the 5 ski areas within 50 km. Getting up the mountain: 2 chairlifts, 2 T-bars and 1 moving carpet, 5 lifts in all. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Burke Mountain (976 m), West Peak Burke Mountain (956 m) and East Haven Mountain (924 m).

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 674 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 25 December and keeps it until about 9 April, roughly 15 weeks. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. The snow is usually deepest around 18 March, with about 63 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 30 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 8 cm in a poor year to 55 cm in a big one. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 33 cm and 91 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 15 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy.

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from late December to early April.
