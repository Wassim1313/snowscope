    About Lakis

    With roughly 2.0 km of runs, Lakis sits in the middle of the size table of Scandinavia. The slopes start at 92 m and reach 168 m at the top.

    Snow at Lakis through a normal winter

    At mid-mountain (about 130 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 9 January and lasts to 12 April: about 14 weeks. A normal season tops out near 44 cm, around 24 February. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early February and early March. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 28 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 22 cm, with 38 cm in the best years. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 25 cm and 54 cm.

    How Lakis is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 76 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. All 2 lifts here are drag lifts.

    Lakis today and the next days

    The upper slopes of Lakis hold around 7 cm of snow today. The last seven days brought 9 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. The season is behind a normal winter, which would have about 11 cm at mid-mountain by this date.

    Beds near the slopes

    Near the lifts you will find 2 chalets: Villa Jokiunelma and Kajo.

    Questions about Lakis

    What is the best month to ski at Lakis?

    Aim for early February and early March; the cover is usually good from early January to mid-April.

    Is there snow at Lakis at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 22 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Lakis?

    Between 92 m and 168 m, so 76 m of vertical drop.

    How many km of pistes does Lakis have?

    About 2.0 km of pistes and 2 lifts.
