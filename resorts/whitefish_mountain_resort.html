    What 46 winters say about Whitefish

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Whitefish (about 5,623 ft (1,714 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 45 in (115 cm), with a normal range of 36 in (92 cm)–52 in (133 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 70 in (177 cm), between 57 in (146 cm) and 82 in (208 cm) in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 84 in (214 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 98 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 100 %.

    Best winter on record: 2010-11, peaking at 156 in (397 cm). Worst: 2014-15, which never passed 70 in (179 cm).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May, about 192 days.

    So far this winter: 9.1 in (23 cm) on 23 November (usual: 22 in (56 cm)).

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 1,714 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-November to late May, about 27 weeks of good cover. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 196 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. For about 21 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. A normal season tops out near 214 cm, around 31 March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 115 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 149 cm in the best years. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 135 cm and 243 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-March and late March.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Whitefish gives 699 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. With its base at 1,364 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. The lift system counts 16 lifts: 12 chairlifts, 2 T-bars and 2 moving carpets. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. For small children there are 2 conveyor-belt lifts.
