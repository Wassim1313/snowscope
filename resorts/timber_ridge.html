    About Timber Ridge

    About 2.0 km of pistes make Timber Ridge one of the smaller places to ski in US Northeast. Skiing here happens between 617 m and 849 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Magic Mountain (2.5 km, west).

    What there is to ski at Timber Ridge

    The piste map lists 5 graded runs, about 1.9 km in total. Most of the length is green circle (62%, about 1.2 km). With 62% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    Snow at Timber Ridge through a normal winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 733 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 22 January and lasts to 27 March: about 9 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. Most winters land between 15 cm and 79 cm by mid-February. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 46 cm. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 20 cm, from 4 cm in a poor year to 44 cm in a big one.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Timber Ridge gives 232 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lowest slopes are at only 617 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Stratton, for one, climbs to 1,183 m. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Glebe Mountain (891 m), Roundy Mountain (703 m) and Turkey Mountain (675 m).

    Timber Ridge: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Timber Ridge?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from late January to late March.

    Can you ski at Timber Ridge at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 20 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    What altitude is Timber Ridge at?

    Its highest lift reaches 849 m; the lowest starts at 617 m (232 m of vertical).

    Can beginners ski at Timber Ridge?

    Yes: 62% of the graded pistes, about 1.2 km, are easy.

    Is there a larger resort near Timber Ridge?

    Stratton, 17 km to the south-west, with about 72 km of pistes.
