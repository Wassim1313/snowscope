    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 33 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Below about 1,101 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. Its summit is modest for the area; the highest lifts nearby, at Stevens Pass, reach 1,727 m. The only lift is a rope tow. The skyline is dominated by Mount Thomson, 1,998 m high and 6.2 km north-east of the resort.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 918 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to mid-April, about 18 weeks of good cover. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 39 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 9 cm to 117 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 36 cm (lean years 10 cm, snowy years 62 cm). A normal season tops out near 74 cm, around 24 February. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and late February.

    Staying nearby

    Near the lifts you will find Guye Cabin (mountain hut), Summit House (hotel), Slide-In Lodge (mountain hut) and Alpenhaus Lodge (mountain hut). Several of them are mountain huts, reached on skis or on foot.

    Questions about Sahalie Ski Club

    When is the best time to ski at Sahalie Ski Club?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from mid-December to mid-April.

    Can you ski at Sahalie Ski Club at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 36 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    What altitude is Sahalie Ski Club at?

    Between 901 m and 934 m, so 33 m of vertical drop.
