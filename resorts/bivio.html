    About Bivio

    With roughly 15 km of runs, Bivio sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,767 m up to 2,542 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Avers, 7.6 km to the west.

    Snow at Bivio through a normal winter

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 2,154 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 21 November and keeps it until about the end of May, roughly 28 weeks. A normal season tops out near 117 cm, around 28 March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 64 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 35 cm, snowy years 102 cm). Most winters land between 73 cm and 144 cm by mid-February. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and late March. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 11 weeks. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 114 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.

    How Bivio is laid out

    775 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,767 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Its 4 lifts break down into 3 T-bars and 1 moving carpet. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. The highest named summit within 8 km is Piz Platta at 3,392 m, standing 850 m above the top lift.

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Bivio?

    Aim for early March and late March; the cover is usually good from late November to late May.

    Is there snow at Bivio at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 64 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high do the lifts go at Bivio?

    The top is at 2,542 m and the base at 1,767 m.

    What level of skier is Bivio best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 54% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How big is Bivio?

    About 15 km of pistes and 4 lifts.
