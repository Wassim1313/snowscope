    About Ski Sunne

    With roughly 7.0 km of runs, Ski Sunne sits in the middle of the size table of Scandinavia. The ski area spans the mountain from 114 m up to 374 m.

    The pistes of Ski Sunne

    The piste map lists 11 graded runs, about 6.8 km in total. The largest share is red, with 3.1 km (46%). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 260 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lift system counts 3 lifts: 1 chairlift and 2 drag lifts. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Bergsätterskullen (403 m) and Frykdalshöjden (310 m).

    How much snow Ski Sunne usually gets

    Count on about 1 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 244 m in an ordinary winter, from early February to mid-February. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. A normal season tops out near 30 cm, around 6 February. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 10 cm; a snowy December gave 29 cm, a dry one 1 cm. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 4 cm and 49 cm. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow.

    Ski Sunne: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Ski Sunne?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from early February to mid-February.

    Can you ski at Ski Sunne at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in nearly every winter, typically 10 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    What altitude is Ski Sunne at?

    The top is at 374 m and the base at 114 m.

    Can beginners ski at Ski Sunne?

    Yes: 45% of the graded pistes, about 3.0 km, are easy.

    How big is Ski Sunne?

    About 7.0 km of pistes and 3 lifts.
