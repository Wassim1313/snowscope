    The snow at Champéry, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Champéry (1,602 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 60 cm, with a normal range of 38 cm–85 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 124 cm, between 96 cm and 146 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 131 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 43 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 74 %.

    1981-82 was the big one (230 cm); 2022-23 the lean one (84 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until mid-May in a normal winter, roughly 169 days.

    Today's 54 cm compares with a usual 21 cm for 25 November.

    Questions about Champéry

    Is there snow in Champéry at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 60 cm at Christmas, and 74 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 50 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Champéry?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 131 cm). For a sure base, late November to mid-May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Champéry?

    About 131 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 84 cm in the leanest winter (2022-23) and 230 cm in the biggest (1981-82).

    When does Champéry have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from early December to mid-May.

    When does Champéry open for the 2026/27 season?

    The announced opening day is 28 November 2026; the last ski day is planned for 11 April 2027.

    How high do the lifts go at Champéry?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,145 m; the lowest starts at 1,060 m (1,085 m of vertical).

    About Champéry

    With some 82 km of runs, Champéry belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,060 m and reach 2,145 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Avoriaz, 3.2 km south-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    What there is to ski at Champéry

    The piste map lists 133 graded runs, about 82 km in total. Most of the length is blue (64%, about 53 km). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy.

    How Champéry is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Champéry gives 1,085 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. Getting up the mountain: 1 cable car, 1 gondola, 12 chairlifts, 4 T-bars, 10 button lifts, 1 drag lift and 3 rope tows, 32 lifts in all. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. Above the ski area rises Dent de la Chaux (2,767 m), 8.0 km to the south-east, about 622 m higher than the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,602 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to mid-May, about 24 weeks of good cover. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 60 cm (lean years 19 cm, snowy years 100 cm). For about 12 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. A normal season tops out near 131 cm, around 20 February. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 106 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Year to year the snow is steady, with 79 cm to 167 cm in mid-February in most winters. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March.
