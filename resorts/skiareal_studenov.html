    How much snow Skiareál Studenov usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 653 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from late January to early March, about 5 weeks of good cover. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 5 cm to 61 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. A normal season tops out near 35 cm, around 21 February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 14 cm, with 51 cm in the best years. The best weeks for snow are usually early February and late February.

    The snow right now

    Snow on the ground at Skiareál Studenov today: about 4 cm up high. The last seven days brought 7 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. So far the season is running close to normal, which is about 9 cm at mid-mountain.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Skiareál Studenov gives 44 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Because the lifts start as low as 631 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. Among the 9 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Skiareál Horní Domky reaches 1,308 m. The only lift is a button lift.

    Questions about Skiareál Studenov

    When does Skiareál Studenov have the most snow?

    Aim for early February and late February; the cover is usually good from late January to early March.

    Can you ski at Skiareál Studenov at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in nearly every winter, typically 14 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    What altitude is Skiareál Studenov at?

    Its highest lift reaches 675 m; the lowest starts at 631 m (44 m of vertical).

    How big is Skiareál Studenov?

    About 0.3 km of pistes and 1 lift.
