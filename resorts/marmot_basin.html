    Marmot Basin in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Marmot Basin (about 2,114 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 140 cm of cover (123 cm to 152 cm in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 165 cm, between 146 cm and 191 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in April: about 186 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 100 % of winters, and by 1 January in 100 %.

    Best winter on record: 2025-26, peaking at 286 cm. Worst: 1992-93, which never passed 142 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-October to late June, about 249 days.

    Marmot Basin: quick answers

    Is there snow in Marmot Basin at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 140 cm at Christmas, and 100 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 62 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Marmot Basin?

    April: the snow peaks at about 186 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-October to late June.

    How deep does the snow get at Marmot Basin?

    About 186 cm at the peak of a normal winter (mid-April); between 142 cm in the leanest winter (1992-93) and 286 cm in the biggest (2025-26).

    How high do the lifts go at Marmot Basin?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,504 m; the lowest starts at 1,724 m (780 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Marmot Basin have?

    About 37 km of pistes and 6 lifts.
