    About Marmot Basin

    With roughly 37 km of runs, Marmot Basin sits in the middle of the size table of Canada West. The slopes start at 1,724 m and reach 2,504 m at the top.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 2,114 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from late October to late May or later, about 32 weeks of good cover. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 185 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. For about 27 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 126 cm and 207 cm. The snow is usually deepest around 13 April, with about 186 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually early April and mid-April. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 140 cm, from 101 cm in a poor year to 171 cm in a big one.

    The mountain at Marmot Basin

    The vertical drop is 780 m: enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,724 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. All 6 lifts here are chairlifts. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The skyline is dominated by Mount Estella, 3,069 m high and 7.9 km south-west of the resort.

    What there is to ski at Marmot Basin

    Marmot Basin has 75 graded runs adding up to about 36 km. Most of the length is black diamond (51%, about 18 km). Steep ground is the speciality: 51% of the length is graded difficult. Experts can count on some 18 km of difficult runs.

    Questions about Marmot Basin

    What is the best month to ski at Marmot Basin?

    Aim for early April and mid-April; the cover is usually good from late October to late May.

    Can you ski at Marmot Basin at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 140 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    When does the ski season start at Marmot Basin?

    It opened on 13 November 2026.

    How high do the lifts go at Marmot Basin?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,504 m; the lowest starts at 1,724 m (780 m of vertical).

    Is Marmot Basin good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 18 km of its graded pistes (51%) are difficult.
