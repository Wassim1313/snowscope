    About Sasquatch

    With roughly 18 km of runs, Sasquatch sits in the middle of the size table of Canada West. Its lifts climb from 975 m to 1,316 m.

    How much snow Sasquatch usually gets

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 1,146 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 15 November and keeps it until about the end of May, roughly 28 weeks. The snow is usually deepest around 28 March, with about 339 cm. For the best snow, aim for late March and mid-April. By mid-April the typical depth is 323 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. For about 26 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 184 cm, with 248 cm in the best years. Most winters land between 159 cm and 344 cm by mid-February.

    Lifts and terrain

    341 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 975 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. The lift system counts 5 lifts: 3 chairlifts and 2 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. For small children there are 2 conveyor-belt lifts. The skyline is dominated by Mount Downing, 1,504 m high and 7.5 km north of the resort.
