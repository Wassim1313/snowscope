    Tusten Skiheiser in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Tusten Skiheiser (about 390 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 26 cm; half of the winters sit between 15 cm and 46 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 53 cm, between 32 cm and 71 cm in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 60 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 9 % of winters, and by 1 January in 24 %.

    1980-81 was the big one (144 cm); 2013-14 the lean one (35 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-December until mid-April in a normal winter, roughly 124 days.

    Today's 14 cm compares with a usual 8 cm for 27 November.

    Questions about Tusten Skiheiser

    Is there snow in Tusten Skiheiser at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 26 cm, and only 24 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 23 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Tusten Skiheiser?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 60 cm). For a sure base, mid-December to mid-April is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Tusten Skiheiser?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 60 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 144 cm (1980-81), the thinnest 35 cm (2013-14).

    How high is Tusten Skiheiser?

    The top is at 661 m and the base at 119 m.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 542 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. Among its 5 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point. To get up there are 3 T-bars and 1 button lift. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. Above the ski area rises Urdfjellet (979 m), 6.2 km to the north, about 318 m higher than the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 16 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 390 m) in an ordinary winter, from late December to mid-April. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-February and early March. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 60 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 34 cm; by then it pays to ski early, before the sun softens it. Most winters land between 16 cm and 85 cm by mid-February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 26 cm; a snowy December gave 60 cm, a dry one 4 cm.
