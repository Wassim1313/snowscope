    What 46 winters say about Manning Park Resort

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Manning Park Resort (1,574 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 115 cm, with a normal range of 93 cm–138 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 162 cm; the middle half of winters range from 144 cm to 191 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 185 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 98 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 100 %.

    1998-99 was the big one (309 cm); 2000-01 the lean one (134 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from early November until mid-June in a normal winter, roughly 220 days.

    This winter so far: 10 cm on 22 November, against a usual 53 cm for the date.

    About Manning Park Resort

    Manning Park Resort is a mid-sized ski area with about 20 km of pistes, number 25 of 53 in Canada West by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,359 m up to 1,789 m.

    The pistes of Manning Park Resort

    In all, 57 marked runs cover roughly 20 km. The largest share is black diamond, with 7.5 km (37%). Steep ground is the speciality: 37% of the length is graded difficult. The steepest pistes add up to about 7.5 km.

    How much snow Manning Park Resort usually gets

    At mid-mountain (about 1,574 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 11 November and lasts to the end of May: about 29 weeks. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late March and early April. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 115 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 78 cm, snowy years 166 cm). By mid-April the typical depth is 178 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 185 cm. For about 24 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Most winters land between 110 cm and 220 cm by mid-February.

    How Manning Park Resort is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Manning Park Resort gives 430 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The bottom station at 1,359 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. The lift system counts 5 lifts: 2 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 1 rope tow and 1 moving carpet. 1 moving carpet serves the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Frosty Mountain (2,426 m), 7.1 km to the south, about 637 m higher than the top lift.
