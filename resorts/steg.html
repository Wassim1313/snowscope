    What 46 winters say about Steg

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Steg (about 855 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 16 cm; half of the winters sit between 9 cm and 25 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 31 cm; the middle half of winters range from 20 cm to 43 cm.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 31 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 2 % of winters, and by 1 January in 2 %.

    Best winter on record: 1981-82, peaking at 96 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 15 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-January to late April, about 99 days.

    Getting to Steg and what it costs

    The pistes run from 711 m to 999 m (288 m of vertical), with 3 km of marked runs and 4 lifts.

    Common questions

    Does Steg have snow over Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 16 cm, and only 2 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 12 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Steg?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 31 cm). For a sure base, mid-January to late April is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How much snow does Steg get?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 31 cm in mid-February. The deepest of 46 winters reached 96 cm (1981-82), the thinnest 15 cm (2022-23).

    What altitude is Steg at?

    The top is at 999 m and the base at 711 m.

    How many km of pistes does Steg have?

    About Steg

    Compact and local, Steg has about 3.0 km of runs, small for the Alps. Its lifts climb from 711 m to 999 m. Fischenthal is the next resort, 2.2 km away to the south.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    288 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Below about 911 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet.
