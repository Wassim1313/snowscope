    About Le Grand Puy

    Le Grand Puy has about 0.2 km of pistes. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,349 m up to 1,602 m. Chabanon is the next resort, 7.7 km away to the west.

    Nearby alternatives compared

    How Le Grand Puy is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Le Grand Puy gives 253 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With its base at 1,349 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Among the 9 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Les Orres reaches 2,700 m. Getting up the mountain: 2 button lifts, 1 drag lift and 1 rope tow, 4 lifts in all. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. The highest named summit within 8 km is Roche Close at 2,739 m, standing 1,137 m above the top lift.

    Snow at Le Grand Puy through a normal winter

    Count on about 5 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 1,476 m in an ordinary winter, from early February to early March. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 12 cm, with 40 cm in the best years. Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 35 cm. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 4 cm to 64 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and late February. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any.

    Where to stay

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Le Grand Puy?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from early February to early March.

    How much snow does Le Grand Puy have at Christmas?

    It is a gamble: only nearly every winter had snow at mid-mountain on Christmas Day (typically 12 cm).

    How high is Le Grand Puy?

    Between 1,349 m and 1,602 m, so 253 m of vertical drop.

    How big is Le Grand Puy?

    About 0.2 km of pistes and 4 lifts.
