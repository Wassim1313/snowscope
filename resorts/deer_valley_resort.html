    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 948 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. A high base at 1,964 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. Its 37 lifts break down into 3 funiculars, 3 gondolas, 30 chairlifts and 1 button lift. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. The highest named summit within 8 km is Clayton Peak at 3,258 m, standing 346 m above the top lift.

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 2,438 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 23 November and keeps it until about 11 May, roughly 24 weeks. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 92 cm and 203 cm. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 15 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 82 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 156 cm in the best years. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 113 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. A normal season tops out near 156 cm, around 13 March.
