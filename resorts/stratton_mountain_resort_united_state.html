    The snow at Stratton, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Stratton (about 2,936 ft (895 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 7.9 in (20 cm); half of the winters sit between 3.9 in (10 cm) and 11 in (28 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 16 in (41 cm) (11 in (29 cm)–23 in (59 cm)).

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 18 in (45 cm).

    2 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 7 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 2000-01, peaking at 44 in (113 cm). Worst: 2015-16, which never passed 5.1 in (13 cm).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late December to late March, about 95 days.

    So far this winter: 0 in (0 cm) on 27 November (usual: 1.2 in (3 cm)).

    Stratton: quick answers

    Can you ski Stratton at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 7.9 in (20 cm) at Christmas on average, with a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 7 % of 46 winters. Right now: no snow.

    What is the best time to ski Stratton?

    February: the snow peaks at about 18 in (45 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late December to late March.

    How snowy is Stratton?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 18 in (45 cm) in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 44 in (113 cm) (2000-01), the thinnest 5.1 in (13 cm) (2015-16).

    How high is Stratton?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,183 m; the lowest starts at 607 m (576 m of vertical).

    Is Stratton good for beginners?

    Yes: 42% of the graded pistes, about 30 km, are easy.

    How big is Stratton?

    About 72 km of pistes and 17 lifts.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    576 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. Because the lifts start as low as 607 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. Its 17 lifts break down into 1 gondola, 9 chairlifts, 1 drag lift and 6 moving carpets. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. First-timers get 6 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Stratton Mountain (1,194 m), The Pinnacle (769 m) and Mundal Hill (647 m).

    When the snow is good

    Halfway up the slopes, around 895 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 24 January and lasts to 27 March: about 9 weeks. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 45 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 20 cm, with 41 cm in the best years. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. Year to year the snow is steady, with 14 cm to 78 cm in mid-February in most winters.
