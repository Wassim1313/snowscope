    About Gstaad

    Around 39 km of marked runs place Gstaad among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 155 by length. Skiing here happens between 968 m and 2,152 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Wispile (4.8 km, south-east).

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 35 graded runs, about 39 km in total. The largest share is blue, with 24 km (62%). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy.

    Snow at Gstaad through a normal winter

    Count on about 22 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,560 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from mid-December to mid-May. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 83 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. A normal season tops out near 97 cm, around 19 February. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and early March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 44 cm, from 16 cm in a poor year to 68 cm in a big one. Year to year the snow is steady, with 59 cm to 130 cm in mid-February in most winters.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 1,184 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. With the bottom at 968 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 2,152 m. Getting up the mountain: 4 gondolas, 3 chairlifts, 3 drag lifts and 1 rope tow, 11 lifts in all. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. The skyline is dominated by Gummfluh, 2,458 m high and 4.5 km south-west of the resort.

    Quick answers

    When does Gstaad have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to mid-May.

    Can you ski at Gstaad at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 44 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    How high is Gstaad?

    The top is at 2,152 m and the base at 968 m.

    Is Gstaad good for beginners?

    Yes: 62% of the graded pistes, about 24 km, are easy.

    How big is Gstaad?

    About 39 km of pistes and 11 lifts.
