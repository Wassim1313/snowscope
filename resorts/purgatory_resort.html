    The snow at Purgatory Resort, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Purgatory Resort (about 9,823 ft (2,994 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 31 in (80 cm), with a normal range of 19 in (47 cm)–47 in (119 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 53 in (135 cm), between 36 in (92 cm) and 69 in (174 cm) in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 58 in (148 cm).

    63 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 76 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1984-85, peaking at 114 in (289 cm). Worst: 2025-26, which never passed 25 in (64 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to early May (175 days).

    So far this winter: 7.5 in (19 cm) on 28 November (usual: 15 in (38 cm)).

    About Purgatory Resort

    Purgatory Resort is one of the bigger ski areas of US West: about 68 km of pistes put it at number 27 of 175. The ski area spans the mountain from 2,696 m up to 3,291 m. Its nearest neighbour is Kendall Mountain, 25 km north-east, and 4 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 90 marked runs cover roughly 67 km. The largest share is blue square, with 35 km (51%). Intermediate runs make up 51% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. Experts can count on some 17 km of difficult runs.

    How much snow Purgatory Resort usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 2,994 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to early May, about 24 weeks of good cover. For about 14 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 80 cm, from 10 cm in a poor year to 144 cm in a big one. The best weeks for snow are usually early January and mid-March. The snow is usually deepest around 8 March, with about 148 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 104 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 61 cm and 208 cm.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 595 m: runs of a moderate length. A high base at 2,696 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. Of the 7 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. Its 14 lifts break down into 10 chairlifts, 2 drag lifts and 2 moving carpets. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. First-timers get 2 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The highest named summit within 8 km is Grayrock Peak at 3,811 m, standing 520 m above the top lift.
