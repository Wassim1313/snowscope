    The snow at Nakiska, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Nakiska (about 1,883 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 41 cm of cover (34 cm to 57 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 60 cm (50 cm–70 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 69 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 20 % of winters, and by 1 January in 41 %.

    The deepest winter was 2019-20 (120 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1991-92 (54 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 179 days.

    Today's 11 cm compares with a usual 23 cm for 22 November.

    About Nakiska

    With roughly 27 km of runs, Nakiska sits in the middle of the size table of Canada West. The slopes start at 1,500 m and reach 2,266 m at the top. There are 1 other ski area within 40 km, the closest being Fortress, 15 km to the south.

    The pistes of Nakiska

    In all, 54 marked runs cover roughly 27 km. Blue square runs make up the biggest part: 14 km, or 50%. This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. The steepest pistes add up to about 8.6 km.

    How Nakiska is laid out

    The vertical drop is 766 m: enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,500 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. Of the 4 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. The lift system counts 7 lifts: 4 chairlifts, 1 button lift and 2 moving carpets. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Wind Mountain at 3,153 m, standing 887 m above the top lift.

    Snow at Nakiska through a normal winter

    At mid-mountain (about 1,883 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 26 November and lasts to 20 May: about 25 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 41 cm, with 75 cm in the best years. Most winters land between 42 cm and 81 cm by mid-February. By mid-April the typical depth is 67 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. The best weeks for snow are usually late March and early April. The snow is usually deepest around 10 April, with about 69 cm.
