    Pico in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Pico (about 2,966 ft (904 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 9.4 in (24 cm) of cover (5.1 in (13 cm) to 13 in (34 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 20 in (52 cm); the middle half of winters range from 14 in (35 cm) to 26 in (66 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 22 in (56 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 2 % of winters, and by 1 January in 9 %.

    Best winter on record: 2000-01, peaking at 50 in (127 cm). Worst: 2015-16, which never passed 7.5 in (19 cm).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from late December until early April in a normal winter, roughly 99 days.

    So far this winter: 0 in (0 cm) on 28 November (usual: 1.6 in (4 cm)).

    Common questions

    Can you ski Pico at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 9.4 in (24 cm) at Christmas on average, with a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 9 % of 46 winters. Right now: no snow.

    Which month has the most snow at Pico?

    March: the snow peaks at about 22 in (56 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late December to early April.

    How snowy is Pico?

    About 22 in (56 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 7.5 in (19 cm) in the leanest winter (2015-16) and 50 in (127 cm) in the biggest (2000-01).

    How high do the lifts go at Pico?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,199 m; the lowest starts at 610 m (589 m of vertical).

    Does Pico have steep runs?

    Yes: about 8.3 km of its graded pistes (27%) are difficult.

    About Pico

    Pico is one of the bigger ski areas of US Northeast: about 31 km of pistes put it at number 22 of 93. Skiing here happens between 610 m and 1,199 m above sea level. Killington Resort is the next resort, 6.0 km away to the south-east.

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 66 marked runs cover roughly 31 km. Most of the length is blue square (57%, about 17 km). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. The steepest pistes add up to about 8.3 km.

    Run colours follow the North American system: green circles are easiest, blue squares intermediate, black diamonds difficult.

    How Pico is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Pico gives 589 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. At 610 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Of the 8 ski areas within 50 km, only 1 reaches higher. The lift system counts 7 lifts: 5 chairlifts and 2 rope tows. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Killington Peak (1,293 m), Pico Peak (1,206 m) and Little Killington (1,194 m).

    When the snow is good

    Halfway up the slopes, around 904 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 9 January and lasts to 31 March: about 12 weeks. The snow is usually deepest around 2 March, with about 56 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and early March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 24 cm, from 6 cm in a poor year to 47 cm in a big one. Most winters land between 25 cm and 85 cm by mid-February. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow.
