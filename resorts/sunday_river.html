    About Sunday River

    Sunday River ranks number 2 of 93 ski areas in US Northeast by piste length, with about 86 km of runs. The ski area spans the mountain from 249 m up to 955 m. Mount Abram is the next resort, 17 km away to the south-east.

    The pistes of Sunday River

    Counting every graded piste, there are 145 runs and around 85 km to ski. Most of the length is green circle (37%, about 32 km). Steep ground is the speciality: 32% of the length is graded difficult. Experts can count on some 26 km of difficult runs. 1.5 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 706 m: enough height for proper long runs. The lowest slopes are at only 249 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Of the 7 ski areas within 50 km, only 1 reaches higher. To get up there are 1 chair-gondola, 15 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 3 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. First-timers get 3 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Wheeler Mountain (969 m), Black Mountain (964 m) and Oz (958 m).

    How much snow Sunday River usually gets

    Count on about 16 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 602 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from late December to mid-April. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 32 cm; by then it pays to ski early, before the sun softens it. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 29 cm (lean years 8 cm, snowy years 53 cm). The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 35 cm and 99 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. The snow is usually deepest around 7 March, with about 68 cm.
