    The snow at Schweitzer, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Schweitzer (about 5,200 ft (1,585 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 43 in (110 cm); half of the winters sit between 33 in (84 cm) and 57 in (146 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 72 in (183 cm) (57 in (144 cm)–88 in (224 cm)).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 83 in (210 cm).

    By 15 December 85 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 98 %.

    The deepest winter was 2010-11 (213 in (541 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2025-26 (37 in (94 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to mid-May (182 days).

    So far this winter: 3.1 in (8 cm) on 26 November (usual: 18 in (45 cm)).

    Is there snow in Schweitzer at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 43 in (110 cm) at Christmas, and 98 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 15 in (37 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Schweitzer?

    March: the snow peaks at about 83 in (210 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to mid-May.

    How much snow does Schweitzer get?

    About 83 in (210 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (mid-March); between 37 in (94 cm) in the leanest winter (2025-26) and 213 in (541 cm) in the biggest (2010-11).

    About Schweitzer

    Schweitzer is one of the bigger ski areas of US West: about 74 km of pistes put it at number 25 of 175. The slopes start at 1,221 m and reach 1,949 m at the top.

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 97 graded runs, about 71 km in total. Most of the length is blue square (55%, about 39 km). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Experts can count on some 31 km of difficult runs.

    When the snow is good

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,585 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 21 November and keeps it until about 17 May, roughly 26 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 110 cm (lean years 56 cm, snowy years 171 cm). In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 177 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. The snow is usually deepest around 14 March, with about 210 cm. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 21 weeks. Year to year the snow is steady, with 116 cm to 243 cm in mid-February in most winters. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early February and late February.

    The mountain at Schweitzer

    728 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. To get up there are 8 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 1 moving carpet. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Mount Casey (2,027 m), Blue Mountain (1,997 m) and Keokee Mountain (1,967 m).
