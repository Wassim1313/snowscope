    About Whistler Blackcomb

    With about 299 km of pistes, Whistler Blackcomb is the largest ski area of Canada West. Its lifts climb from 653 m to 2,259 m. Ski Callaghan is the next resort, 18 km away to the north-west.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 1,606 m: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. At 653 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Getting up the mountain: 9 gondolas, 17 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 9 moving carpets, 36 lifts in all. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 9 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Mount Trorey, 2,461 m high and 8.0 km south-east of the resort.

    The pistes of Whistler Blackcomb

    Whistler Blackcomb has 607 graded runs adding up to about 298 km. The largest share is blue square, with 123 km (41%). Good skiers will like it here; 33% of the runs are steep. Experts can count on some 99 km of difficult runs.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 1,456 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 26 October and keeps it until about the end of May, roughly 31 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 223 cm; a snowy December gave 290 cm, a dry one 148 cm. For about 28 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Year to year the snow is steady, with 212 cm to 366 cm in mid-February in most winters. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-March and late March. By mid-April the typical depth is 334 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 339 cm.
