    The snow at Torgnon, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Torgnon (about 1,900 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 40 cm; half of the winters sit between 31 cm and 53 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 67 cm, between 55 cm and 82 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 74 cm.

    By 15 December 24 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 35 %.

    Best winter on record: 2023-24, peaking at 134 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 58 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to mid-May, about 164 days.

    This winter so far: 20 cm on 22 November, against a usual 16 cm for the date.

    Skiing Torgnon: the practical side

    The pistes run from 1,542 m to 2,258 m (716 m of vertical), with 13 km of marked runs and 7 lifts.

    Nearest airports: Turin (TRN, 68 km), Milan Malpensa (MXP, 93 km), Geneva (GVA, 122 km).

    Questions about Torgnon

    Can you ski Torgnon at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 40 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 35 % of 46 winters. Right now: 28 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Torgnon?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 74 cm). For a sure base, early December to mid-May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Torgnon?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 74 cm in mid-March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 134 cm (2023-24), the thinnest 58 cm (2022-23).

    When does Torgnon have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and mid-March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to mid-May.

    How high do the lifts go at Torgnon?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,258 m; the lowest starts at 1,542 m (716 m of vertical).

    Who is Torgnon best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 65% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    About Torgnon

    With roughly 13 km of runs, Torgnon sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 1,542 m and 2,258 m above sea level. La Magdeleine is the next resort, 5.2 km away to the east.

    How much snow Torgnon usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 1,900 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to mid-May, about 23 weeks of good cover. By mid-April the typical depth is 68 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 49 cm and 88 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 40 cm, from 22 cm in a poor year to 62 cm in a big one. A normal season tops out near 74 cm, around 8 March. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and mid-March.

    The snow right now

    Up high, Torgnon has roughly 28 cm of snow on the ground today. 20 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 20 cm of new snow is expected by Wednesday. An ordinary winter would have around 21 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track.

    How Torgnon is laid out

    The vertical drop is 716 m: enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,542 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Getting up the mountain: 1 gondola, 3 chairlifts and 3 moving carpets, 7 lifts in all. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. 3 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Dome du Tsan - Nord (3,349 m), 7.9 km to the north, about 1,091 m higher than the top lift.
