    About Loveland Basin

    With some 48 km of runs, Loveland Basin belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of US West. The slopes start at 3,318 m and reach 3,866 m at the top. Loveland Valley is the next resort, 2.6 km away to the east.

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 96 marked runs cover roughly 47 km. Most of the length is blue square (56%, about 27 km). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns. Advanced skiers have about 14 km of black or expert runs.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 548 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. Even the lowest lift starts at 3,318 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Its top at 3,866 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. All 7 lifts here are chairlifts. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Above the ski area rises Pettingell Peak (4,104 m), 5.7 km to the north, about 238 m higher than the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 3,592 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 12 November and lasts to the end of May: about 29 weeks. For about 20 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. By mid-April the typical depth is 191 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. A normal season tops out near 197 cm, around 22 April. Most winters land between 95 cm and 197 cm by mid-February. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 91 cm; a snowy December gave 140 cm, a dry one 39 cm.

    Loveland Basin: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Loveland Basin?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from mid-November to late May.

    Is there snow at Loveland Basin at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 91 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    What altitude is Loveland Basin at?

    Between 3,318 m and 3,866 m, so 548 m of vertical drop.

    Is Loveland Basin good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 14 km of its graded pistes (29%) are difficult.

    Is there a larger resort near Loveland Basin?

    Keystone, 11 km to the south, with about 119 km of pistes.
