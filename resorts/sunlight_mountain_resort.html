    The snow at Sunlight, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Sunlight (about 8,993 ft (2,741 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 31 in (80 cm); half of the winters sit between 24 in (61 cm) and 41 in (105 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 51 in (129 cm) (43 in (108 cm)–61 in (156 cm)).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 59 in (149 cm).

    72 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 91 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1984-85, peaking at 99 in (252 cm). Worst: 1980-81, which never passed 40 in (101 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to late May (186 days).

    Today's 4.3 in (11 cm) compares with a usual 11 in (29 cm) for 22 November.

    About Sunlight

    Neither huge nor tiny, Sunlight offers about 37 km of pistes (number 62 of 175 in US West). Its lifts climb from 2,480 m to 3,002 m. Its nearest neighbour is Ski Stout, 16 km north-west, and 2 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Halfway up the slopes, around 2,741 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 20 November and lasts to 22 May: about 26 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 80 cm; a snowy December gave 127 cm, a dry one 41 cm. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 149 cm.
