    The snow at Vail, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Vail (9,806 ft (2,989 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 30 in (75 cm); half of the winters sit between 20 in (52 cm) and 37 in (95 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 48 in (122 cm); the middle half of winters range from 40 in (101 cm) to 58 in (147 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 56 in (143 cm).

    70 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 83 % by 1 January.

    1983-84 was the big one (96 in (245 cm)); 1980-81 the lean one (37 in (94 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to late May, about 184 days.

    Questions about Vail

    Can you ski Vail at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 30 in (75 cm) at Christmas, and 83 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 11 in (29 cm).

    Which month has the most snow at Vail?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 56 in (143 cm)). For a sure base, mid-November to late May is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How snowy is Vail?

    About 56 in (143 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (late March); between 37 in (94 cm) in the leanest winter (1980-81) and 96 in (245 cm) in the biggest (1983-84).

    What altitude is Vail at?

    Between 2,452 m and 3,526 m, so 1,074 m of vertical drop.

    How big is Vail?

    About 215 km of pistes and 32 lifts.
