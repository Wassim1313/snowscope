    About Easton

    Easton is a ski area. The slopes start at 101 m and reach 235 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Berkshire East, 24 km west, and 2 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Lifts and terrain

    134 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 101 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Among its 3 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point. The only lift is a chairlift. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Stoddard Hill (335 m), Great Hill (301 m) and Arthurs Seat (293 m).

    Snow at Easton through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 168 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 5 cm (snow lay here in nearly every winter), with 21 cm in the best years. A normal season tops out near 16 cm, around 16 February. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 2 cm to 49 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and late February.

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at Easton?

    Aim for mid-February and late February.

    How much snow does Easton have at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in nearly every winter, typically 5 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    What altitude is Easton at?

    Its highest lift reaches 235 m; the lowest starts at 101 m (134 m of vertical).
