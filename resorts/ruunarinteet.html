    About Ruunarinteet

    With roughly 3.0 km of runs, Ruunarinteet sits in the middle of the size table of Scandinavia. The slopes start at 88 m and reach 162 m at the top. Ruunarinteet is the next resort, 0.0 km away to the south-west.

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 9 runs and around 2.9 km to ski. The largest share is blue, with 1.6 km (54%). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    Snow at Ruunarinteet through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 125 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Most winters land between 10 cm and 19 cm by mid-February. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 12 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. A normal season tops out near 16 cm, around 23 February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 9 cm (lean years 3 cm, snowy years 12 cm).

    Questions about Ruunarinteet

    When does Ruunarinteet have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and early March.

    Is there snow at Ruunarinteet at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in every winter, typically 9 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    How high do the lifts go at Ruunarinteet?

    Its highest lift reaches 162 m; the lowest starts at 88 m (74 m of vertical).

    Is Ruunarinteet good for beginners?

    Yes: 66% of the graded pistes, about 1.9 km, are easy.

    How big is Ruunarinteet?

    About 3.0 km of pistes and 2 lifts.
