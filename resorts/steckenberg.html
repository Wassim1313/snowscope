    About Steckenberg

    Compact and local, Steckenberg has about 2.0 km of runs, small for the Alps. The slopes start at 858 m and reach 1,000 m at the top. Kolbensattel (Oberammergau) is the next resort, 2.5 km away to the south-east.

    In all, 12 marked runs cover roughly 2.0 km. The largest share is red, with 1.0 km (49%). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 142 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 858 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Getting up the mountain: 1 T-bar, 3 button lifts and 2 rope tows, 6 lifts in all. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. The skyline is dominated by Kienjoch, 1,953 m high and 7.7 km south of the resort.

    Snow at Steckenberg through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 929 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 4 cm to 46 cm. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. For the best snow, aim for early February and mid-February. Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 23 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in 8 winters out of ten; the usual depth was 13 cm (lean years 0 cm, snowy years 30 cm).
