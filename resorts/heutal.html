    What 46 winters say about Heutal

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Heutal (1,246 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 40 cm, with a normal range of 24 cm–59 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 76 cm, between 59 cm and 107 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 88 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 24 % of winters, and by 1 January in 41 %.

    1981-82 was the big one (276 cm); 2006-07 the lean one (47 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to mid-May, about 162 days.

    This winter so far: 6 cm on 22 November, against a usual 8 cm for the date.

    About Heutal

    Neither huge nor tiny, Heutal offers about 9.0 km of pistes (number 513 of 1,056 in the Alps). The ski area spans the mountain from 961 m up to 1,531 m. Its nearest neighbour is Steinplatte Winklmoosalm, 6.1 km south-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    When the snow is good

    Halfway up the slopes, around 1,246 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 11 December and lasts to 11 May: about 22 weeks. Most winters land between 42 cm and 125 cm by mid-February. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 88 cm. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 40 cm (lean years 8 cm, snowy years 79 cm). In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 68 cm, plenty for sunny spring days.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Heutal gives 570 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The base at 961 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. To get up there are 2 T-bars, 1 button lift and 1 rope tow. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. The skyline is dominated by Sonntagshorn, 1,961 m high and 5.2 km north-east of the resort.
