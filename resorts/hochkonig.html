    What 46 winters say about Hochkönig

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Hochkönig (about 1,346 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 33 cm; half of the winters sit between 21 cm and 45 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 62 cm; the middle half of winters range from 50 cm to 78 cm.

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 75 cm.

    15 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 28 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 114 cm. Worst: 2006-07, which never passed 49 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to early May, about 159 days.

    So far this winter: 2 cm on 19 November (usual: 11 cm).

    Questions about Hochkönig

    Can you ski Hochkönig at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 33 cm, and only 28 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 20 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Hochkönig?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 75 cm). For a sure base, early December to early May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Hochkönig?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 75 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 114 cm (1998-99), the thinnest 49 cm (2006-07).

    When does the ski season start at Hochkönig?

    The announced opening day is 28 November 2026; the last ski day is planned for 4 April 2027.

    What altitude is Hochkönig at?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,890 m; the lowest starts at 801 m (1,089 m of vertical).

    Is Hochkönig suitable for families?

    Yes: 51% of the graded pistes, about 42 km, are easy.

    About Hochkönig

    Around 82 km of marked runs place Hochkönig among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 67 by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 801 m up to 1,890 m. The closest other ski area is Embach (11 km, south).

    Snow at Hochkönig through a normal winter

    Count on about 19 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 1,346 m) in an ordinary winter, from late December to early May. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and late February. Most winters land between 40 cm and 87 cm by mid-February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 33 cm, from 12 cm in a poor year to 57 cm in a big one. A normal season tops out near 75 cm, around 29 February. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 44 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.

    How Hochkönig is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Hochkönig gives 1,089 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. Below about 1,001 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. To get up there are 7 gondolas, 8 chairlifts, 8 T-bars, 3 button lifts, 1 rope tow and 1 moving carpet. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. Above the ski area rises Hochkönig (2,941 m), 7.8 km to the north-east, about 1,051 m higher than the top lift.
