    Mt. Shasta Ski Park in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Mt. Shasta Ski Park (about 6,503 ft (1,982 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 42 in (106 cm); half of the winters sit between 20 in (51 cm) and 69 in (175 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 82 in (209 cm) (56 in (143 cm)–120 in (306 cm)).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 114 in (289 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    70 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 83 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1982-83 (483 in (1228 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2013-14 (30 in (75 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to late May (181 days).

    Mt. Shasta Ski Park: quick answers

    Does Mt. Shasta Ski Park have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 42 in (106 cm) at Christmas, and 83 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 32 in (82 cm).

    What is the best time to ski Mt. Shasta Ski Park?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 114 in (289 cm)). For a sure base, mid-November to late May is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Mt. Shasta Ski Park?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 114 in (289 cm) in mid-March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 483 in (1228 cm) (1982-83), the thinnest 30 in (75 cm) (2013-14).

    How high do the lifts go at Mt. Shasta Ski Park?

    Between 1,666 m and 2,298 m, so 632 m of vertical drop.

    How many km of pistes does Mt. Shasta Ski Park have?

    About 29 km of pistes and 6 lifts.
