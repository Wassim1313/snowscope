    About Mt. Shasta Ski Park

    Neither huge nor tiny, Mt. Shasta Ski Park offers about 29 km of pistes (number 80 of 175 in US West). Skiing here happens between 1,666 m and 2,298 m above sea level.

    How much snow Mt. Shasta Ski Park usually gets

    At mid-mountain (about 1,982 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 29 November and lasts to 23 May: about 25 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually early January and early March. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 21 weeks. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 61 cm and 377 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 239 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Depth normally peaks in mid-March at roughly 289 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 106 cm; a snowy December gave 228 cm, a dry one 11 cm.

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 36 runs and around 29 km to ski. Most of the length is black diamond (36%, about 10 km). Steep ground is the speciality: 43% of the length is graded difficult. Experts can count on some 10 km of difficult runs. 2.2 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    The mountain at Mt. Shasta Ski Park

    632 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. The bottom station at 1,666 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. Getting up the mountain: 4 chairlifts and 2 moving carpets, 6 lifts in all. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. First-timers get 2 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Above the ski area rises Thumb Rock (3,939 m), 7.9 km to the north, about 1,641 m higher than the top lift.
