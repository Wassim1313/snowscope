    The snow at Espace Lumière, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Espace Lumière (about 2,044 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 46 cm; half of the winters sit between 31 cm and 66 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 91 cm (66 cm–113 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 96 cm.

    By 15 December 30 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 48 %.

    Best winter on record: 2013-14, peaking at 187 cm. Worst: 1992-93, which never passed 35 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to early May, about 152 days.

    So far this winter: 5 cm on 21 November (usual: 6 cm).

    Questions about Espace Lumière

    Does Espace Lumière have snow over Christmas?

    Not every year: about 46 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 48 % of 46 winters. Right now: 22 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Espace Lumière?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 96 cm). For a sure base, early December to early May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Espace Lumière?

    About 96 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 35 cm in the leanest winter (1992-93) and 187 cm in the biggest (2013-14).

    When does Espace Lumière have the most snow?

    About Espace Lumière

    Around 185 km of marked runs place Espace Lumière among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 16 by length. The slopes start at 1,495 m and reach 2,592 m at the top. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Pra-Loup 1600, 2.4 km to the north.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 21 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 2,044 m) in an ordinary winter, from early December to early May. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 46 cm and 134 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 57 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and early March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 46 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 14 cm in a poor year to 99 cm in a big one. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 96 cm.

    How Espace Lumière is laid out

    1,097 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,495 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point. Getting up the mountain: 2 gondolas, 1 chair-gondola, 12 chairlifts, 12 button lifts, 4 drag lifts, 2 rope tows and 6 moving carpets, 39 lifts in all. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. For small children there are 6 conveyor-belt lifts. The highest named summit within 8 km is Tête de l'Estrop at 2,961 m, standing 369 m above the top lift.
