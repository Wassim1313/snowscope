    Andermatt-Sedrun in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Andermatt-Sedrun (2,016 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 38 cm; half of the winters sit between 25 cm and 46 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 68 cm; the middle half of winters range from 57 cm to 80 cm.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 79 cm.

    9 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 7 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1981-82 (121 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2022-23 (62 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-December until late May in a normal winter, roughly 160 days.

    So far this winter: 26 cm on 21 November (usual: 14 cm).

    Questions about Andermatt-Sedrun

    Does Andermatt-Sedrun have snow over Christmas?

    Not every year: about 38 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 7 % of 46 winters. Right now: 32 cm.

    March: the snow peaks at about 79 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to late May.

    How deep does the snow get at Andermatt-Sedrun?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 79 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 121 cm (1981-82), the thinnest 62 cm (2022-23).

    About Andermatt-Sedrun

    Andermatt-Sedrun is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 45 km of pistes put it at number 133 of 1,056. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,437 m up to 2,595 m. The closest other ski area is Gemsstock (4.5 km, south-west).

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 113 marked runs cover roughly 45 km. Most of the length is blue (53%, about 24 km). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. 0.4 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 1,158 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,437 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Its top at 2,595 m is the third highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. Getting up the mountain: 1 cable car, 4 gondolas, 8 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 4 moving carpets, 18 lifts in all. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. For small children there are 4 conveyor-belt lifts. The highest named summit within 8 km is Piz Giuv at 3,096 m, standing 501 m above the top lift.

    How much snow Andermatt-Sedrun usually gets

    Count on about 23 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 2,016 m in an ordinary winter, from mid-December to late May. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 38 cm, with 50 cm in the best years. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 47 cm and 88 cm. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 79 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 73 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.
