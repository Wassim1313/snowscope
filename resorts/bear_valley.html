    Bear Valley in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Bear Valley (7,562 ft (2,305 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 27 in (69 cm) of cover (12 in (31 cm) to 46 in (116 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 53 in (134 cm); the middle half of winters range from 35 in (89 cm) to 70 in (178 cm).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 63 in (159 cm).

    46 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 74 % by 1 January.

    2010-11 was the big one (141 in (358 cm)); 2014-15 the lean one (29 in (74 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late November to early May, about 166 days.

    Questions about Bear Valley

    Can you ski Bear Valley at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 27 in (69 cm) on 25 December, and a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 74 % of 46 winters. Right now: 46 in (118 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Bear Valley?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 63 in (159 cm)). For a sure base, late November to early May is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How much snow does Bear Valley get?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 63 in (159 cm) in mid-March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 141 in (358 cm) (2010-11), the thinnest 29 in (74 cm) (2014-15).

    How high do the lifts go at Bear Valley?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,586 m; the lowest starts at 2,024 m (562 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Bear Valley have?

    About 36 km of pistes and 9 lifts.

    Bear Valley is a mid-sized ski area with about 36 km of pistes, number 63 of 175 in US West by length. Skiing here happens between 2,024 m and 2,586 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Kirkwood (22 km, north).

    Lifts and terrain

    562 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. At 2,024 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Its top at 2,586 m is the third highest of the 5 ski areas within 50 km.
