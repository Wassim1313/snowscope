    What 46 winters say about Lindvallen

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Lindvallen (711 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 33 cm, with a normal range of 22 cm–45 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 60 cm (47 cm–70 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 62 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 13 % of winters, and by 1 January in 28 %.

    The deepest winter was 1987-88 (118 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1995-96 (36 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-December until late April in a normal winter, roughly 133 days.

    So far this winter: 23 cm on 22 November (usual: 9 cm).

    About Lindvallen

    With some 35 km of runs, Lindvallen belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of Scandinavia. Skiing here happens between 568 m and 854 m above sea level. Högfjället is the next resort, 3.2 km away to the west.

    What there is to ski at Lindvallen

    Lindvallen has 56 graded runs adding up to about 35 km. Most of the length is green (beginner) (40%, about 14 km). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    286 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. To get up there are 2 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 19 button lifts, 13 drag lifts and 4 moving carpets. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. First-timers get 4 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Storfjället (924 m), Mellanfjället (910 m) and Källfjället (903 m).

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 18 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 711 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from mid-December to late April. A normal season tops out near 62 cm, around 5 March. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 43 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. Year to year the snow is steady, with 40 cm to 81 cm in mid-February in most winters. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 33 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 12 cm, snowy years 57 cm). For the best snow, aim for late February and early March.
