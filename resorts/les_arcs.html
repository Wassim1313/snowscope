    About Les Arcs

    With some 168 km of runs, Les Arcs belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 1,222 m and 3,205 m above sea level. Paradiski is the next resort, 6.2 km away to the south-west.

    Runs by difficulty

    Les Arcs has 318 graded runs adding up to about 168 km. Most of the length is blue (55%, about 92 km). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. 0.4 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Around 1% of the pistes, some 1.5 km, can be covered with machine-made snow, mostly the busiest runs and the way back to the bottom.

    The mountain at Les Arcs

    The vertical drop is 1,983 m: one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Its top at 3,205 m is the third highest of the 8 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 4 cable cars, 6 gondolas, 23 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 4 button lifts, 4 drag lifts, 4 rope tows and 2 moving carpets. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Mont Pourri, 3,779 m high and 6.3 km south-east of the resort.

    Snow at Les Arcs through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 2,214 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to late May or later, about 28 weeks of good cover. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 19 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 84 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 45 cm, snowy years 132 cm). In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 156 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 104 cm to 196 cm in mid-February in most winters. The snow is usually deepest around 10 March, with about 170 cm.
