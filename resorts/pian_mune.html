    What 46 winters say about Pian Munè

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Pian Munè (1,809 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 29 cm; half of the winters sit between 12 cm and 45 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 52 cm, between 30 cm and 74 cm in a normal winter.

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 55 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 13 % of winters, and by 1 January in 22 %.

    Best winter on record: 2017-18, peaking at 149 cm. Worst: 2006-07, which never passed 21 cm.

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs late December to mid-April (120 days).

    Today's 9 cm compares with a usual 8 cm for 26 November.

    About Pian Munè

    With roughly 6.0 km of runs, Pian Munè sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,531 m and reach 2,087 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Sampeyre (8.9 km, south-west).

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 6 runs and around 6.4 km to ski. The largest share is red, with 3.5 km (55%). This is intermediate country: long, flowing runs for skiers who can link their turns.

    The snow right now

    Up high, Pian Munè has roughly 20 cm of snow on the ground today. 2 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. That is close to a normal winter for the date (about 16 cm at mid-mountain).

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Pian Munè gives 556 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The bottom station at 1,531 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. To get up there are 1 chairlift and 1 button lift. The highest named summit within 8 km is Punta Rasciassa at 2,664 m, standing 577 m above the top lift.

    How much snow Pian Munè usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 1,809 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from late December to early April, about 15 weeks of good cover. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 9 cm and 86 cm. The snow is usually deepest around 4 March, with about 55 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 29 cm, with 58 cm in the best years. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 21 cm, and the lower runs are often thin by then.
