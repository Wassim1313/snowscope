    The snow at Kasurila, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Kasurila (about 146 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 24 cm of cover (18 cm to 32 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 44 cm; the middle half of winters range from 38 cm to 50 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 46 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    2 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 7 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1980-81, peaking at 82 cm. Worst: 2013-14, which never passed 21 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from early January until mid-April in a normal winter, roughly 109 days.

    Today's 9 cm compares with a usual 8 cm for 27 November.

    About Kasurila

    With roughly 3.0 km of runs, Kasurila sits in the middle of the size table of Scandinavia. Its lifts climb from 96 m to 197 m. The closest other ski area is Tahko (32 km, north-east).

    How Kasurila is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Kasurila gives 101 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. To get up there are 5 drag lifts and 1 moving carpet. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. 1 moving carpet serves the beginner and children's areas. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Soidinmäki (199 m), Kukkuri (197 m) and Kipsivuori (192 m).

    Snow this week

    Up high, Kasurila has roughly 10 cm of snow on the ground today. The last seven days brought 7 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. That is close to a normal winter for the date (about 11 cm at mid-mountain).

    Snow at Kasurila through a normal winter

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 146 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 6 January and keeps it until about 19 April, roughly 15 weeks. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 24 cm (lean years 8 cm, snowy years 36 cm). Most winters land between 28 cm and 59 cm by mid-February. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 34 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March. A normal season tops out near 46 cm, around 21 February.
