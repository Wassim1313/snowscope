    About Aramón Cerler

    About 62 km of pistes make Aramón Cerler one of the ten biggest ski areas of the Pyrenees (number 5). The ski area spans the mountain from 1,503 m up to 2,618 m. The closest other ski area is Superbagnères (23 km, north).

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 94 runs and around 62 km to ski. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 25 km, or 41%. Mixed groups do well here, with gentle slopes for learners and steeper pistes for the experts. The steepest pistes add up to about 9.5 km.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 1,115 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,503 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. The lift system counts 21 lifts: 1 gondola, 10 chairlifts, 2 button lifts, 2 drag lifts and 6 moving carpets. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 6 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Pico de Castanesa at 2,858 m, standing 240 m above the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    At about 2,060 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 29 December and lasts to 31 March: about 13 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 27 cm, from 4 cm in a poor year to 64 cm in a big one. The best weeks for snow are usually early February and mid-February. Most winters land between 8 cm and 111 cm by mid-February. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 9 cm, so the season is over at mid-mountain by then. The snow is usually deepest around 5 February, with about 66 cm.
