    About Iberg

    Neither huge nor tiny, Iberg offers about 3.0 km of pistes (number 760 of 1,056 in the Alps). Its lifts climb from 743 m to 951 m. Isny / Felderhalde is the next resort, 5.5 km away to the north.

    What there is to ski at Iberg

    In all, 6 marked runs cover roughly 2.6 km. The largest share is red, with 1.8 km (70%). Intermediate runs make up 70% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers.

    How Iberg is laid out

    The vertical drop is 208 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 743 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Getting up the mountain: 1 T-bar and 1 drag lift, 2 lifts in all. The highest named summit within 8 km is Salmaser Höhe at 1,254 m, standing 303 m above the top lift.

    How much snow Iberg usually gets

    Even in a typical winter the snow at about 847 m, the middle of the ski area rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. For the best snow, aim for early February and early March. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 12 cm; a snowy December gave 28 cm, a dry one 1 cm. Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 18 cm. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 3 cm to 37 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking.

    Questions about Iberg

    When is the best time to ski at Iberg?

    Aim for early February and early March.

    How much snow does Iberg have at Christmas?

    Sometimes. Christmas snow at mid-mountain came in nearly every winter, usually about 12 cm, so check before you go.

    How high is Iberg?

    The top is at 951 m and the base at 743 m.

    Who is Iberg best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 70% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How many km of pistes does Iberg have?
