    About Zauchensee-Flachauwinkl

    With some 38 km of runs, Zauchensee-Flachauwinkl belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,011 m up to 2,109 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Ski amadé, 6.7 km to the north-west.

    What there is to ski at Zauchensee-Flachauwinkl

    Zauchensee-Flachauwinkl has 74 graded runs adding up to about 38 km. The largest share is blue, with 24 km (64%). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children.

    How much snow Zauchensee-Flachauwinkl usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,560 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to early May, about 22 weeks of good cover. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 43 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 72 cm, a dry one 14 cm. The snow is usually deepest around 2 March, with about 103 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 51 cm to 117 cm in mid-February in most winters. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 61 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Zauchensee-Flachauwinkl gives 1,098 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. The lift system counts 14 lifts: 3 gondolas, 6 chairlifts, 3 T-bars and 2 button lifts. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. The skyline is dominated by Großer Pleißlingkeil, 2,501 m high and 7.9 km south-east of the resort.

    When is the best time to ski at Zauchensee-Flachauwinkl?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from early December to early May.

    What altitude is Zauchensee-Flachauwinkl at?
