    What 46 winters say about Pizol

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Pizol (about 1,382 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 43 cm, with a normal range of 32 cm–54 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 80 cm, between 66 cm and 100 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 87 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    20 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 37 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 176 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 57 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from early December until mid-May in a normal winter, roughly 159 days.

    Today's 28 cm compares with a usual 16 cm for 24 November.

    About Pizol

    Around 40 km of marked runs place Pizol among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 150 by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 513 m up to 2,250 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being St. Margrethenberg, 5.1 km to the east.

    How Pizol is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Pizol gives 1,737 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. At 513 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. Getting up the mountain: 2 gondolas, 4 chairlifts, 3 T-bars, 2 button lifts, 1 rope tow and 2 moving carpets, 14 lifts in all. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. For small children there are 2 conveyor-belt lifts. The skyline is dominated by Pizol, 2,844 m high and 5.2 km south-west of the resort.

    What there is to ski at Pizol

    The piste map lists 85 graded runs, about 40 km in total. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 17 km, or 41%. Mixed groups do well here, with gentle slopes for learners and steeper pistes for the experts. The steepest pistes add up to about 5.9 km. 3.7 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    When the snow is good

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,382 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to early May, about 21 weeks of good cover. A normal season tops out near 87 cm, around 27 February. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 43 cm, with 61 cm in the best years. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 53 cm and 116 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 66 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.
