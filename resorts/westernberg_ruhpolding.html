    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 61 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Because the lifts start as low as 666 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Steinplatte Winklmoosalm, for one, climbs to 1,853 m. Its 4 lifts break down into 1 T-bar, 1 drag lift and 2 rope tows. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. The highest named summit within 8 km is Gurnwandkopf at 1,691 m, standing 964 m above the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 696 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-January and late February. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 7 cm (snow lay here in 8 winters out of ten); a snowy December gave 21 cm, a dry one 0 cm. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 0 cm to 25 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 10 cm.

    Westernberg: common questions

    When does Westernberg have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-January and late February.

    Is there snow at Westernberg at Christmas?

    It is a gamble: only 8 winters out of ten had snow at mid-mountain on Christmas Day (typically 7 cm).

    How high do the lifts go at Westernberg?

    The top is at 727 m and the base at 666 m.

    How many km of pistes does Westernberg have?

    About 0.3 km of pistes and 4 lifts.
