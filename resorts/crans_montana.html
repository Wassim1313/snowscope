    Crans-Montana in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Crans-Montana (2,150 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 56 cm, with a normal range of 43 cm–71 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 98 cm, between 83 cm and 112 cm in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 105 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 43 % of winters, and by 1 January in 70 %.

    Best winter on record: 1994-95, peaking at 164 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 83 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until early June in a normal winter, roughly 188 days.

    Today's 12 cm compares with a usual 22 cm for 19 November.

    Crans-Montana: quick answers

    Can you ski Crans-Montana at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 56 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 70 % of 46 winters. Right now: 40 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Crans-Montana?

    March: the snow peaks at about 105 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early June.

    How deep does the snow get at Crans-Montana?

    About 105 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 83 cm in the leanest winter (2022-23) and 164 cm in the biggest (1994-95).

    When does the ski season start at Crans-Montana?

    The announced opening day is 28 November 2026; the last ski day is planned for 4 April 2027.

    How high do the lifts go at Crans-Montana?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,872 m; the lowest starts at 1,427 m (1,445 m of vertical).

    What level of skier is Crans-Montana best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 60% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    About Crans-Montana

    Around 74 km of marked runs place Crans-Montana among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 74 by length. Its lifts climb from 1,427 m to 2,872 m. Its nearest neighbour is Anzère, 7.3 km west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Pistes and who they suit

    The piste map lists 113 graded runs, about 74 km in total. The largest share is red, with 44 km (60%). Intermediate runs make up 60% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. Experts can count on some 12 km of difficult runs. 3.8 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 1,445 m of vertical, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. Starting at 1,427 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Its top at 2,872 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. The lift system counts 24 lifts: 5 gondolas, 6 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 8 button lifts and 4 moving carpets. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 4 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Schneehoren at 3,180 m, standing 308 m above the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 26 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 2,150 m in an ordinary winter, from early December to late May or later. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 97 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. The snow is usually deepest around 3 March, with about 105 cm. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 7 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 56 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 81 cm, a dry one 32 cm. For the best snow, aim for early March and early April. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 64 cm and 123 cm.
