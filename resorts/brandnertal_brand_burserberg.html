    What 46 winters say about Brandnertal

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Brandnertal (about 1,430 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    Christmas week typically brings 30 cm of cover (20 cm to 36 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 55 cm; the middle half of winters range from 43 cm to 68 cm.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 62 cm.

    By 15 December 2 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 7 %.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 120 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 40 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from late December until early May in a normal winter, roughly 141 days.

    This winter so far: 32 cm on 22 November, against a usual 11 cm for the date.

    About Brandnertal

    Brandnertal is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 45 km of pistes put it at number 131 of 1,056. The ski area spans the mountain from 889 m up to 1,971 m. The closest other ski area is Tschardund (7.9 km, north-west).

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 70 runs and around 45 km to ski. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 25 km, or 56%. Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Brandnertal gives 1,082 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. The base at 889 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point. To get up there are 1 cable car, 3 gondolas, 4 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 2 button lifts and 1 drag lift. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. The skyline is dominated by Schesaplana, 2,965 m high and 7.6 km south of the resort.

    Snow at Brandnertal through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,430 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from late December to early May, about 18 weeks of good cover. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 36 cm and 81 cm. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 46 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 30 cm, from 12 cm in a poor year to 44 cm in a big one. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 62 cm.
