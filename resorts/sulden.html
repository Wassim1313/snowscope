    About Sulden

    With some 33 km of runs, Sulden belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 1,843 m to 3,212 m. Trafoi is the next resort, 9.8 km away to the north-west.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 1,369 m of vertical, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. A high base at 1,843 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. Getting up the mountain: 2 cable cars, 2 gondolas, 5 chairlifts, 1 drag lift and 1 moving carpet, 11 lifts in all. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. The skyline is dominated by Ortler - Ortles, 3,905 m high and 4.2 km west of the resort.

    Runs by difficulty

    Counting every graded piste, there are 42 runs and around 33 km to ski. Most of the length is blue (40%, about 13 km). Mixed groups do well here, with gentle slopes for learners and steeper pistes for the experts. Advanced skiers have about 7.1 km of black or expert runs. 1.1 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    Snow this week

    Up high, Sulden has roughly 57 cm of snow on the ground today. 33 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days. Mid-mountain is about where a typical winter would be now (40 cm).

    How much snow Sulden usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 2,528 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to late May, about 27 weeks of good cover. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 106 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 58 cm; a snowy December gave 101 cm, a dry one 30 cm. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 8 weeks. The snow is usually deepest around 7 March, with about 106 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 62 cm to 141 cm in mid-February in most winters. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March.

    Sulden: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Sulden?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from late November to late May.

    Is there snow at Sulden at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 58 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    How high do the lifts go at Sulden?

    Its highest lift reaches 3,212 m; the lowest starts at 1,843 m (1,369 m of vertical).

    Can beginners ski at Sulden?

    Yes: 42% of the graded pistes, about 14 km, are easy.

    How big is Sulden?

    About 33 km of pistes and 11 lifts.
