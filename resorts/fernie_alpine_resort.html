    The snow at Fernie Alpine Resort, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Fernie Alpine Resort (about 1,578 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 72 cm; half of the winters sit between 62 cm and 86 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 101 cm (88 cm–113 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 112 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    85 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 98 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1996-97, peaking at 188 cm. Worst: 2009-10, which never passed 97 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to mid-May, about 183 days.

    This winter so far: 6 cm on 19 November, against a usual 32 cm for the date.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 1,578 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-November to mid-May, about 26 weeks of good cover. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 81 cm and 135 cm. For about 9 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 100 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 72 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 51 cm in a poor year to 102 cm in a big one. A normal season tops out near 112 cm, around 1 March.

    How Fernie Alpine Resort is laid out

    The vertical drop is 1,038 m: enough height for proper long runs. The lift system counts 10 lifts: 7 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 1 button lift and 1 moving carpet. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. First-timers get 1 moving carpet on the gentle nursery slopes. The highest named summit within 8 km is Mount Fernie at 2,210 m, standing 113 m above the top lift.
