    What 46 winters say about Mount Ashland

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Mount Ashland (6,896 ft (2,102 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 42 in (106 cm) of cover (11 in (29 cm) to 64 in (162 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 59 in (149 cm), between 26 in (66 cm) and 112 in (284 cm) in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 74 in (189 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 52 % of winters, and by 1 January in 65 %.

    Best winter on record: 1982-83, peaking at 381 in (967 cm). Worst: 2013-14, which never passed 19 in (47 cm).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late November to mid-April, about 152 days.

    Mount Ashland: quick answers

    Can you ski Mount Ashland at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 42 in (106 cm) at Christmas, and 65 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 11 in (27 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Mount Ashland?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 74 in (189 cm)). For a sure base, late November to mid-April is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How snowy is Mount Ashland?

    About 74 in (189 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 19 in (47 cm) in the leanest winter (2013-14) and 381 in (967 cm) in the biggest (1982-83).

    How high do the lifts go at Mount Ashland?

    The top is at 2,279 m and the base at 1,925 m.

    How many km of pistes does Mount Ashland have?

    About 17 km of pistes and 6 lifts.

    About Mount Ashland

    Neither huge nor tiny, Mount Ashland offers about 17 km of pistes (number 109 of 175 in US West). The ski area spans the mountain from 1,925 m up to 2,279 m.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    354 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. A high base at 1,925 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter.

    This week's snow

    Today there are about 27 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Mount Ashland. The last seven days brought 32 cm of new snow. The next days look mild and dry; the cover is expected to settle to about 21 cm by Friday.
