    About Paseky nad Jizerou

    With some 7.0 km of runs, Paseky nad Jizerou belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of Central Europe. The slopes start at 616 m and reach 859 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Skiareál Rejdice, 2.3 km north-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    Runs by difficulty

    In all, 10 marked runs cover roughly 7.4 km. The largest share is blue, with 4.4 km (59%). It is a resort for beginners, families and relaxed cruising: more than half the pistes are easy. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    How much snow Paseky nad Jizerou usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 738 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from late January to early March, about 7 weeks of good cover. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 15 cm, from 2 cm in a poor year to 56 cm in a big one. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. The snow is usually deepest around 21 February, with about 40 cm. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 5 cm to 68 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early February and late February.

    How Paseky nad Jizerou is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 243 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 616 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. Its 5 lifts break down into 2 chairlifts, 2 button lifts and 1 drag lift. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work.
