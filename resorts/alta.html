    What 46 winters say about Alta

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Alta (9,547 ft (2,910 m)), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 50 in (128 cm), with a normal range of 37 in (94 cm)–70 in (177 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 87 in (220 cm) (65 in (165 cm)–104 in (264 cm)).

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 96 in (243 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 89 % of winters, and by 1 January in 98 %.

    The deepest winter was 2022-23 (176 in (447 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2025-26 (62 in (158 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from early November to late May, about 205 days.

    Today's 23 in (59 cm) compares with a usual 24 in (62 cm) for 28 November.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Alta gives 615 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Even the lowest lift starts at 2,602 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Its top at 3,217 m is the second highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. Getting up the mountain: 7 chairlifts, 2 rope tows and 2 moving carpets, 11 lifts in all. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Twin Peaks (west), 3,502 m high and 3.0 km south-west of the resort.
