    About Fischenthal

    Fischenthal is a mid-sized ski area with about 3.0 km of pistes, number 743 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 824 m up to 1,049 m. Its nearest neighbour is Steg, 2.2 km north, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How much snow Fischenthal usually gets

    At mid-mountain (about 936 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 13 January and lasts to 25 April: about 15 weeks. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 17 cm and 72 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 21 cm, from 6 cm in a poor year to 38 cm in a big one. The snow is usually deepest around 6 April, with about 43 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 41 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-March and mid-April.

    Lifts and terrain

    The vertical drop is 225 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Below about 1,024 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. The lift system counts 4 lifts: 3 T-bars and 1 rope tow. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. Above the ski area rises Tweralpspitz (1,332 m), 8.0 km to the south-east, about 283 m higher than the top lift.

    Quick answers

    What is the best month to ski at Fischenthal?

    Aim for mid-March and mid-April; the cover is usually good from mid-January to late April.

    How much snow does Fischenthal have at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 21 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    What altitude is Fischenthal at?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,049 m; the lowest starts at 824 m (225 m of vertical).

    Who is Fischenthal best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 47% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    Which bigger ski area is near Fischenthal?

    Atzmännig, 6.9 km to the south-east, with about 8.0 km of pistes.
