    Reiteralm in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Reiteralm (1,314 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 33 cm, with a normal range of 21 cm–42 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 60 cm, between 48 cm and 76 cm in a normal winter.

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 70 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 11 % of winters, and by 1 January in 26 %.

    1986-87 was the big one (109 cm); 2025-26 the lean one (46 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to early May, about 154 days.

    This winter so far: 6 cm on 23 November, against a usual 11 cm for the date.

    About Reiteralm

    With some 28 km of runs, Reiteralm belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Its lifts climb from 771 m to 1,858 m. Fageralm is the next resort, 4.8 km away to the west.

    Runs by difficulty

    The mountain at Reiteralm

    1,087 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. With the bottom at 771 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. To get up there are 2 gondolas, 4 chairlifts, 2 T-bars, 4 button lifts and 1 moving carpet. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. 1 moving carpet serves the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Schiedeck (2,339 m), 7.8 km to the south-east, about 481 m higher than the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 19 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,314 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from late December to late April. Most winters land between 37 cm and 82 cm by mid-February. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 33 cm; a snowy December gave 58 cm, a dry one 10 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and late February. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 70 cm. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 41 cm, plenty for sunny spring days.
