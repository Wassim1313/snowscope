    About Chapman Hill

    Compact and local, Chapman Hill has about 1.0 km of runs, small for US West. Skiing here happens between 2,000 m and 2,095 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Hesperus, 17 km west, and 2 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How Chapman Hill is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Chapman Hill gives 95 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. A high base at 2,000 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. Its 2 lifts break down into 1 button lift and 1 rope tow. Above the ski area rises Perins Peak (2,541 m), 4.4 km to the west, about 446 m higher than the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    At mid-mountain (about 2,048 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 8 January and lasts to 12 March: about 9 weeks. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 0 cm to 86 cm. The snow is usually deepest around 10 February, with about 42 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in 8 winters out of ten, typically 18 cm, with 57 cm in the best years. The best weeks for snow are usually late January and early February. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow.

    Chapman Hill: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Chapman Hill?

    Aim for late January and early February; the cover is usually good from early January to mid-March.

    How much snow does Chapman Hill have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in 8 winters out of ten, about 18 cm deep.

    What altitude is Chapman Hill at?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,095 m; the lowest starts at 2,000 m (95 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Chapman Hill have?

    About 1.0 km of pistes and 2 lifts.
