    About Pilatus

    Pilatus is a ski area. Skiing here happens between 513 m and 2,063 m above sea level. Hergiswil is the next resort, 2.9 km away to the south-east.

    How Pilatus is laid out

    1,550 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. The lowest slopes are at only 513 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Its top at 2,063 m is the highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. Getting up the mountain: 1 cable car, 1 gondola and 1 drag lift, 3 lifts in all. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Tomlishorn (2,128 m), Esel (2,119 m) and Oberhaupt (2,106 m).

    Snow at Pilatus through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,288 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to mid-May, about 21 weeks of good cover. Year to year the snow is steady, with 43 cm to 99 cm in mid-February in most winters. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 56 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 38 cm (lean years 18 cm, snowy years 52 cm). The snow is usually deepest around 26 February, with about 74 cm.

    Questions about Pilatus

    What is the best month to ski at Pilatus?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to mid-May.

    Can you ski at Pilatus at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 38 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    What altitude is Pilatus at?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,063 m; the lowest starts at 513 m (1,550 m of vertical).
