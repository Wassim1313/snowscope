    What 46 winters say about Engelberg Titlis

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Engelberg Titlis (2,018 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 81 cm; half of the winters sit between 55 cm and 98 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 150 cm (131 cm–171 cm).

    The snowiest month is April, when the pack peaks at about 186 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 59 % of winters, and by 1 January in 89 %.

    1998-99 was the big one (306 cm); 2016-17 the lean one (144 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June, about 198 days.

    So far this winter: 7 cm on 19 November (usual: 27 cm).

    About Engelberg Titlis

    Around 42 km of marked runs place Engelberg Titlis among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 139 by length. Skiing here happens between 997 m and 3,040 m above sea level. Engelberg is the next resort, 5.0 km away to the north.

    The mountain at Engelberg Titlis

    2,043 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Below about 1,197 m the snow can be patchy in a mild spell; the higher lifts are the safer bet. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 3,040 m. To get up there are 5 cable cars, 8 funiculars, 3 gondolas, 8 chairlifts, 3 T-bars, 1 button lift, 1 rope tow and 1 moving carpet. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 1 moving carpet serves the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Titlis at 3,238 m, standing 198 m above the top lift.

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 88 graded runs, about 41 km in total. Red runs make up the biggest part: 20 km, or 50%. Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Experts can count on some 3.7 km of difficult runs.

    How much snow Engelberg Titlis usually gets

    Count on about 28 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 2,018 m in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May or later. Most winters land between 107 cm and 198 cm by mid-February. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 18 weeks. Depth normally peaks in mid-April at roughly 186 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 81 cm, with 107 cm in the best years. By mid-April the typical depth is 186 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. For the best snow, aim for early April and mid-April.
