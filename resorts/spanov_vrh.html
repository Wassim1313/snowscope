    About Španov vrh

    About 2.0 km of pistes make Španov vrh one of the smaller places to ski in the Alps. Skiing here happens between 948 m and 1,328 m above sea level. Mojstrana is the next resort, 8.8 km away to the west.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 380 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. The base at 948 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. The skyline is dominated by Weinasch / Vajnež, 2,104 m high and 7.2 km east of the resort.

    Pistes and who they suit

    The piste map lists 1 graded run, about 1.6 km in total. The largest share is freeride, with 1.6 km (100%). Steep ground is the speciality: 100% of the length is graded difficult. 1.6 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    How much snow Španov vrh usually gets

    Even in a typical winter the snow at about 1,138 m, the middle of the ski area rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 13 cm, with 43 cm in the best years. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 1 cm to 48 cm. For the best snow, aim for late March and early April. A normal season tops out near 23 cm, around 20 February. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any.

    Španov vrh: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Španov vrh?

    Aim for late March and early April.

    Is there snow at Španov vrh at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in nearly every winter, typically 13 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    What altitude is Španov vrh at?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,328 m; the lowest starts at 948 m (380 m of vertical).

    Is Španov vrh good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 1.6 km of its graded pistes (100%) are difficult.
