    About Glacier 3000

    Neither huge nor tiny, Glacier 3000 offers about 22 km of pistes (number 284 of 1,056 in the Alps). The ski area spans the mountain from 1,545 m up to 2,931 m. The closest other ski area is Heiti (5.5 km, north-east).

    The mountain at Glacier 3000

    Skied top to bottom, Glacier 3000 gives 1,386 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. With its base at 1,545 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 2,931 m. The lift system counts 17 lifts: 3 cable cars, 4 chairlifts, 3 T-bars, 6 drag lifts and 1 rope tow. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. The skyline is dominated by Sommet des Diablerets, 3,210 m high and 4.6 km south-west of the resort.

    Snow at Glacier 3000 through a normal winter

    Count on about 27 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 2,238 m in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May or later. The snow is usually deepest around 30 March, with about 164 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 80 cm, with 121 cm in the best years. By mid-April the typical depth is 161 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. For the best snow, aim for late March and early April. For about 18 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Most winters land between 99 cm and 197 cm by mid-February.
