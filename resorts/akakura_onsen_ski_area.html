    About Akakura Onsen

    With some 13 km of runs, Akakura Onsen belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of Japan. Skiing here happens between 685 m and 1,149 m above sea level. Akakura Kanko is the next resort, 1.2 km away to the south-west.

    What there is to ski at Akakura Onsen

    Akakura Onsen has 27 graded runs adding up to about 13 km. Green runs make up the biggest part: 8.8 km, or 68%. Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 464 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. At 685 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Among the 9 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Myoko Suginohara reaches 1,857 m. All 14 lifts here are chairlifts. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level.

    Akakura Onsen: common questions

    What altitude is Akakura Onsen at?

    Between 685 m and 1,149 m, so 464 m of vertical drop.

    Is Akakura Onsen good for beginners?

    Yes: 68% of the graded pistes, about 8.8 km, are easy.

    How many km of pistes does Akakura Onsen have?

    About 13 km of pistes and 14 lifts.
