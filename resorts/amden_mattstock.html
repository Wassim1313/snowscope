    About Amden Mattstock

    Amden Mattstock is a mid-sized ski area with about 6.0 km of pistes, number 604 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 931 m up to 1,636 m. Amden Arvenbüel is the next resort, 2.9 km away to the south-east.

    How much snow Amden Mattstock usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 1,284 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-December to mid-May, about 22 weeks of good cover. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 67 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. Most winters land between 42 cm and 118 cm by mid-February. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 42 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 15 cm in a poor year to 66 cm in a big one. The snow is usually deepest around 3 March, with about 83 cm.

    What there is to ski at Amden Mattstock

    In all, 7 marked runs cover roughly 5.9 km. Red runs make up the biggest part: 4.5 km, or 76%. Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety.

    The mountain at Amden Mattstock

    Skied top to bottom, Amden Mattstock gives 705 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. With the bottom at 931 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. Getting up the mountain: 1 chairlift and 2 drag lifts, 3 lifts in all. Above the ski area rises Schären (2,193 m), 7.1 km to the east, about 557 m higher than the top lift.

    Amden Mattstock: common questions

    When does Amden Mattstock have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from mid-December to mid-May.

    How much snow does Amden Mattstock have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 42 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Amden Mattstock?

    The top is at 1,636 m and the base at 931 m.

    Who is Amden Mattstock best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 76% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How many km of pistes does Amden Mattstock have?

    About 6.0 km of pistes and 3 lifts.
