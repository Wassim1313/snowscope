    About Nods

    Nods is a ski area. The ski area spans the mountain from 946 m up to 1,008 m. The closest other ski area is Bugnenets-Savagnières (4.7 km, west).

    Nods and the resorts around it

    When the snow is good

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 977 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 0 cm to 35 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. At Christmas there was snow here in 7 winters out of ten, typically 4 cm (lean years 0 cm, snowy years 21 cm). The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 10 cm.

    How Nods is laid out

    62 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 946 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Among the 9 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Bugnenets-Savagnières reaches 1,448 m. The only lift is a T-bar. The highest named summit within 8 km is Chasseral at 1,607 m, standing 599 m above the top lift.

    Where to stay

    Places to stay close to the slopes of Nods include Hôtel de Chasseral (hotel), Métairie de Neuf Chalet (chalet), Métairie Meuser (chalet) and Métairie de la Neuveville (chalet).

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at Nods?

    Aim for early March and mid-March.

    Can you ski at Nods at Christmas?

    It is a gamble: only 7 winters out of ten had snow at mid-mountain on Christmas Day (typically 4 cm).

    How high do the lifts go at Nods?

    Its highest lift reaches 1,008 m; the lowest starts at 946 m (62 m of vertical).
