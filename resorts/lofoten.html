    About Lofoten

    Lofoten is a mid-sized ski area with about 2.0 km of pistes, number 196 of 262 in Scandinavia by length. The ski area spans the mountain from 26 m up to 324 m.

    Lofoten today and the next days

    Up high, Lofoten has roughly 5 cm of snow on the ground today. The last seven days brought 6 cm of new snow. Little new snow is expected over the next 3 days.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Lofoten gives 298 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The highest named summit within 8 km is Justadtinden at 738 m, standing 414 m above the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 175 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. At Christmas there was snow here in 8 winters out of ten, typically 5 cm; a snowy December gave 20 cm, a dry one 0 cm. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 1 cm to 25 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. The best weeks for snow are usually mid-January and late January. The snow is usually deepest around 9 February, with about 8 cm.

    Where to stay

    Among the closest beds to the pistes are Live Lofoten Hotel (hotel), Stamsund Vandrerhjem (hostel), Steine Rorbu (hostel) and Hagstua (hotel).

    Lofoten: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Lofoten?

    Aim for mid-January and late January.

    Can you ski at Lofoten at Christmas?

    Sometimes. Christmas snow at mid-mountain came in 8 winters out of ten, usually about 5 cm, so check before you go.

    What altitude is Lofoten at?

    Between 26 m and 324 m, so 298 m of vertical drop.
