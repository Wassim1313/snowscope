    Boreal in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Boreal (about 7,434 ft (2,266 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 32 in (82 cm), with a normal range of 15 in (39 cm)–54 in (138 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 64 in (162 cm), between 44 in (111 cm) and 86 in (218 cm) in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 78 in (199 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    61 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 78 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1982-83 (168 in (427 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2014-15 (33 in (83 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late November to mid-May, about 174 days.

    This winter so far: 30 in (76 cm) on 22 November, against a usual 4.3 in (11 cm) for the date.

    About Boreal

    Neither huge nor tiny, Boreal offers about 14 km of pistes (number 116 of 175 in US West). Skiing here happens between 2,186 m and 2,347 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Donner Ski Ranch (1.5 km, south-east).

    Snow this week

    Snow on the ground at Boreal today: about 94 cm up high. 3 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 16 cm of new snow is expected by Wednesday. An ordinary winter would have around 22 cm at mid-mountain by now, so this one is on track.

    The pistes of Boreal

    The piste map lists 26 graded runs, about 13 km in total. Blue square runs make up the biggest part: 5.5 km, or 43%. The mix is balanced: easy slopes to learn on and enough steeper runs to keep good skiers busy. Experts can count on some 2.7 km of difficult runs.

    How much snow Boreal usually gets

    In a typical winter the snow at about 2,266 m, the middle of the ski area reaches a solid 30 cm base around 4 December and keeps it until about 16 May, roughly 24 weeks. By mid-April the typical depth is 121 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 82 cm, from 10 cm in a poor year to 175 cm in a big one. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 16 weeks. A normal season tops out near 199 cm, around 16 March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 70 cm to 290 cm in mid-February in most winters. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early January and early March.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 161 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 2,186 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Getting up the mountain: 6 chairlifts and 1 moving carpet, 7 lifts in all. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. The highest named summit within 8 km is Castle Peak at 2,773 m, standing 426 m above the top lift.
