    What 46 winters say about Steibis

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Steibis (1,112 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 21 cm, with a normal range of 9 cm–33 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 36 cm, between 22 cm and 46 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 37 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    2 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 4 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1981-82 (111 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1989-90 (18 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from early January until late April in a normal winter, roughly 104 days.

    Questions about Steibis

    Can you ski Steibis at Christmas?

    Not every year: about 21 cm at Christmas on average, with a 50 cm base by New Year in 4 % of 46 winters. Right now: 16 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Steibis?

    February: the snow peaks at about 37 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early January to late April.

    How snowy is Steibis?

    About 37 cm at the peak of a normal winter (late February); between 18 cm in the leanest winter (1989-90) and 111 cm in the biggest (1981-82).

    How high do the lifts go at Steibis?

    The top is at 1,354 m and the base at 870 m.

    How many km of pistes does Steibis have?

    About 16 km of pistes and 14 lifts.

    About Steibis

    Steibis is a mid-sized ski area with about 16 km of pistes, number 373 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. The slopes start at 870 m and reach 1,354 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Hochlitten-Riefensberg (2.4 km, west).

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Steibis gives 484 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The base at 870 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. Its top at 1,354 m is the third highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km.
