    Big Sky Resort in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Big Sky Resort (8,990 ft (2,740 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 39 in (98 cm) of cover (31 in (79 cm) to 48 in (123 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 56 in (142 cm); the middle half of winters range from 48 in (122 cm) to 65 in (164 cm).

    April is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 68 in (173 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 96 % of winters, and by 1 January in 96 %.

    Best winter on record: 2010-11, peaking at 145 in (368 cm). Worst: 2025-26, which never passed 57 in (146 cm).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from late October until early June in a normal winter, roughly 236 days.

    Common questions

    Is there snow in Big Sky Resort at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 39 in (98 cm) at Christmas, and 96 % of the past 46 winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 27 in (69 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Big Sky Resort?

    The deepest snow comes in April (about 68 in (173 cm)). For a sure base, late October to early June is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Big Sky Resort?

    About 68 in (173 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (late April); between 57 in (146 cm) in the leanest winter (2025-26) and 145 in (368 cm) in the biggest (2010-11).

    What altitude is Big Sky Resort at?

    Between 2,078 m and 3,402 m, so 1,324 m of vertical drop.

    How many km of pistes does Big Sky Resort have?

    About 236 km of pistes and 45 lifts.

    About Big Sky Resort

    About 236 km of pistes make Big Sky Resort one of the ten biggest ski areas of US West (number 2). Its lifts climb from 2,078 m to 3,402 m. Its nearest neighbour is Yellowstone Club, 3.9 km south, and 1 ski area lie within 40 km in all.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Big Sky Resort gives 1,324 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. A high base at 2,078 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter.
