    Big Sky Resort in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Big Sky Resort (8,990 ft (2,740 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 39 in (98 cm) of cover (31 in (79 cm) to 48 in (123 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 56 in (142 cm); the middle half of winters range from 48 in (122 cm) to 65 in (164 cm).

    April is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 68 in (173 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 96 % of winters, and by 1 January in 96 %.

    Best winter on record: 2010-11, peaking at 145 in (368 cm). Worst: 2025-26, which never passed 57 in (146 cm).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from late October until early June in a normal winter, roughly 236 days.

    Today's 13 in (33 cm) compares with a usual 24 in (62 cm) for 23 November.

    About Big Sky Resort

    About 236 km of pistes make Big Sky Resort one of the ten biggest ski areas of US West (number 2). Its lifts climb from 2,078 m to 3,402 m. Its nearest neighbour is Yellowstone Club, 3.9 km south, and 1 ski area lie within 40 km in all.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Big Sky Resort gives 1,324 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. A high base at 2,078 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. To get up there are 1 cable car, 3 gondolas, 25 chairlifts, 8 button lifts and 8 moving carpets. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. For small children there are 8 conveyor-belt lifts. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Lone Mountain (3,397 m), Castle Rock (3,143 m) and Mount Chipperfield (3,029 m).

    Runs by difficulty

    In all, 433 marked runs cover roughly 230 km. Blue square runs make up the biggest part: 90 km, or 39%. Good skiers will like it here; 33% of the runs are steep. Experts can count on some 75 km of difficult runs.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 29 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 2,740 m) in an ordinary winter, from early November to late May or later. The snow is usually deepest around 22 April, with about 173 cm. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 22 weeks. By mid-April the typical depth is 165 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 108 cm and 181 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 98 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 68 cm in a poor year to 140 cm in a big one. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-April and late April.
