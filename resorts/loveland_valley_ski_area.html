    About Loveland Valley

    About 5.0 km of pistes make Loveland Valley one of the smaller places to ski in US West. Skiing here happens between 3,243 m and 3,492 m above sea level. Loveland Basin is the next resort, 2.6 km away to the west.

    How much snow Loveland Valley usually gets

    Count on about 29 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 3,368 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from mid-November to late May or later. By mid-April the typical depth is 168 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Depth normally peaks in early April at roughly 173 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 82 cm, with 127 cm in the best years. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 87 cm and 180 cm. For about 17 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-March and late March.

    Runs by difficulty

    In all, 7 marked runs cover roughly 5.1 km. Green circle runs make up the biggest part: 3.1 km, or 60%. With 60% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served. Experts may find it tame: there are hardly any steep pistes.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Loveland Valley gives 249 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 3,243 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. Getting up the mountain: 2 chairlifts and 2 moving carpets, 4 lifts in all. For small children there are 2 conveyor-belt lifts. The skyline is dominated by Grays Peak, 4,352 m high and 7.6 km south-east of the resort.

    Questions about Loveland Valley

    When does Loveland Valley have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-March and late March; the cover is usually good from mid-November to late May.

    Is there snow at Loveland Valley at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 82 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Loveland Valley?

    Between 3,243 m and 3,492 m, so 249 m of vertical drop.

    Can beginners ski at Loveland Valley?

    Yes: 60% of the graded pistes, about 3.1 km, are easy.

    Which bigger ski area is near Loveland Valley?

    Keystone, 12 km to the south-west, with about 119 km of pistes.
