    About Turner Mountain

    Neither huge nor tiny, Turner Mountain offers about 16 km of pistes (number 111 of 175 in US West). The ski area spans the mountain from 1,178 m up to 1,812 m.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Counting every graded piste, there are 26 runs and around 16 km to ski. Most of the length is blue square (42%, about 6.6 km). The grades are spread fairly evenly, so most levels find something to ski. The steepest pistes add up to about 4.7 km.

    When the snow is good

    Halfway up the slopes, around 1,495 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 21 November and lasts to 14 May: about 25 weeks. The snow is usually deepest around 6 March, with about 148 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 106 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. For about 15 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 83 cm (lean years 51 cm, snowy years 118 cm). The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 92 cm and 181 cm.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    Skied top to bottom, Turner Mountain gives 634 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. The only lift is a chairlift. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Turner Mountain (1,808 m), Mount Tom (1,777 m) and Flatiron Mountain (1,764 m).

    Turner Mountain: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Turner Mountain?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from late November to mid-May.

    Is there snow at Turner Mountain at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 83 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Turner Mountain?

    Between 1,178 m and 1,812 m, so 634 m of vertical drop.

    Does Turner Mountain have steep runs?

    Yes: about 4.7 km of its graded pistes (30%) are difficult.

    How big is Turner Mountain?

    About 16 km of pistes and 1 lift.
