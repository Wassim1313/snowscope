    How Oberau is laid out

    81 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lowest slopes are at only 682 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Garmisch-Classic, for one, climbs to 2,047 m. The only lift is a T-bar. The highest named summit within 8 km is Krottenkopf at 2,086 m, standing 1,323 m above the top lift.

    Snow at Oberau through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 722 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. At Christmas there was snow here in 8 winters out of ten, typically 9 cm (lean years 0 cm, snowy years 25 cm). Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 0 cm to 37 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-January and late January. A normal season tops out near 14 cm, around 21 January. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any.

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at Oberau?

    Aim for mid-January and late January.

    Is there snow at Oberau at Christmas?

    It is a gamble: only 8 winters out of ten had snow at mid-mountain on Christmas Day (typically 9 cm).

    How high do the lifts go at Oberau?

    Between 682 m and 763 m, so 81 m of vertical drop.

    How many km of pistes does Oberau have?

    About 0.5 km of pistes and 1 lift.
