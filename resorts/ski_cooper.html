    Ski Cooper in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Ski Cooper (11,161 ft (3,402 m)), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 31 in (78 cm), with a normal range of 21 in (53 cm)–37 in (95 cm).

    On 15 February expect around 48 in (123 cm); the middle half of winters range from 41 in (105 cm) to 59 in (149 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 59 in (151 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 70 % of winters had reached 20 in (50 cm); by New Year it was 89 %.

    Best winter on record: 1983-84, peaking at 92 in (234 cm). Worst: 1980-81, which never passed 37 in (94 cm).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-November to early June, about 202 days.

    Today's 8.3 in (21 cm) compares with a usual 15 in (39 cm) for 23 November.

    About Ski Cooper

    Neither huge nor tiny, Ski Cooper offers about 32 km of pistes (number 74 of 175 in US West). Skiing here happens between 3,226 m and 3,579 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Dutch Henry Ski Hill (13 km, south).

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 46 graded runs, about 32 km in total. Blue square runs make up the biggest part: 12 km, or 38%. With 34% of the pistes graded difficult, it suits strong skiers looking for steep runs. Experts can count on some 11 km of difficult runs.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 3,402 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-November to late May or later, about 28 weeks of good cover. Most winters land between 93 cm and 167 cm by mid-February. For about 17 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. For the best snow, aim for late March and early April. A normal season tops out near 151 cm, around 31 March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 78 cm (lean years 40 cm, snowy years 112 cm). By mid-April the typical depth is 136 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring.

    How Ski Cooper is laid out

    The vertical drop is 353 m: runs of a moderate length. A high base at 3,226 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. The lift system counts 5 lifts: 2 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 1 button lift and 1 moving carpet. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. The highest named summit within 8 km is Buckeye Peak at 3,923 m, standing 344 m above the top lift.
