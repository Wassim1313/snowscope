    Steamboat in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Steamboat (about 8,720 ft (2,658 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 32 in (82 cm), with a normal range of 24 in (61 cm)–38 in (97 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 49 in (125 cm), between 42 in (107 cm) and 58 in (148 cm) in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 52 in (133 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 78 % of winters, and by 1 January in 96 %.

    Best winter on record: 1983-84, peaking at 92 in (233 cm). Worst: 2025-26, which never passed 35 in (89 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to mid-May (177 days).

    This winter so far: 0.8 in (2 cm) on 26 November, against a usual 16 in (40 cm) for the date.

    About Steamboat

    About 118 km of pistes make Steamboat one of the ten biggest ski areas of US West (number 9). Skiing here happens between 2,102 m and 3,213 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Howelson Hill (5.9 km, north-west).

    The pistes of Steamboat

    In all, 189 marked runs cover roughly 118 km. Black diamond runs make up the biggest part: 48 km, or 41%. Steep ground is the speciality: 44% of the length is graded difficult. The steepest pistes add up to about 52 km.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 1,111 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. A high base at 2,102 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. The lift system counts 24 lifts: 4 gondolas, 15 chairlifts and 5 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. First-timers get 5 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Mount Werner (3,220 m), Sunshine Peak (3,165 m) and Buffalo Mountain (3,165 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at about 2,658 m, the middle of the ski area: a 30 cm base is usually there from mid-November to mid-May, about 25 weeks of good cover. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 82 cm (lean years 49 cm, snowy years 109 cm). A normal season tops out near 133 cm, around 9 March. For about 14 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Most winters land between 94 cm and 161 cm by mid-February. By mid-April the typical depth is 112 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well.
