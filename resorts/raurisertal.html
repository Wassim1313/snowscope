    What 46 winters say about Raurisertal

    Christmas week typically brings 44 cm of cover (34 cm to 60 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 82 cm (62 cm–100 cm).

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 96 cm.

    30 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 54 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 2018-19 (131 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2025-26 (65 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early November to mid-May (184 days).

    About Raurisertal

    With roughly 19 km of runs, Raurisertal sits in the middle of the size table of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 927 m and 2,171 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Embach, 7.0 km north, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 22 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,549 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from early December to early May. Year to year the snow is steady, with 53 cm to 109 cm in mid-February in most winters. The best weeks for snow are usually late February and early March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 44 cm (snow lay here in every winter) (lean years 19 cm, snowy years 74 cm). Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 96 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 63 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring.

    How Raurisertal is laid out

    The vertical drop is 1,244 m: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. With the bottom at 927 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. To get up there are 3 gondolas, 1 chairlift, 3 T-bars, 1 button lift, 2 rope tows and 2 moving carpets. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Kramkogel, 2,454 m high and 6.5 km south-east of the resort.

    Pistes and who they suit

    In all, 31 marked runs cover roughly 19 km. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 9.9 km, or 51%. With 54% of the length graded easy, learners and families are well served.
