    The snow at Trysil, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Trysil (758 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 52 cm, with a normal range of 38 cm–61 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 81 cm; the middle half of winters range from 71 cm to 95 cm.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 87 cm.

    By 15 December 28 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 67 %.

    Best winter on record: 1987-88, peaking at 146 cm. Worst: 1995-96, which never passed 54 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early May, about 172 days.

    This winter so far: 5 cm on 27 November, against a usual 28 cm for the date.

    How much snow Trysil usually gets

    Count on about 23 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 758 m in an ordinary winter, from late November to early May. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 52 cm (snow lay here in every winter), from 28 cm in a poor year to 74 cm in a big one. Most winters land between 63 cm and 108 cm by mid-February. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 82 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. Depth normally peaks in late March at roughly 87 cm.

    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 702 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. Among its 6 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. To get up there are 1 gondola, 5 chairlifts, 1 T-bar, 15 button lifts, 9 drag lifts and 8 moving carpets. Most of them are surface lifts that pull you up on your skis, which beginner snowboarders find hard work. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. 8 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Trysilfjellet (1,132 m), Stensæterkanken (1,078 m) and Skurufjellet (1,055 m).
