    Orcières Merlette in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Orcières Merlette (2,278 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 84 cm of cover (52 cm to 99 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 144 cm; the middle half of winters range from 114 cm to 164 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 152 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 54 % of winters, and by 1 January in 85 %.

    Best winter on record: 2019-20, peaking at 244 cm. Worst: 2004-05, which never passed 95 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 182 days.

    So far this winter: 36 cm on 23 November (usual: 30 cm).

    About Orcières Merlette

    With some 49 km of runs, Orcières Merlette belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. Skiing here happens between 1,826 m and 2,731 m above sea level. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Serre Eyraud, 5.5 km to the south-west.

    What there is to ski at Orcières Merlette

    In all, 67 marked runs cover roughly 49 km. Blue runs make up the biggest part: 22 km, or 45%. Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children.

    The mountain at Orcières Merlette

    905 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. At 1,826 m, the base is high: snow down to the bottom station is the norm in midwinter. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 2,731 m. To get up there are 3 chair-gondolas, 5 chairlifts, 13 drag lifts, 1 rope tow and 4 moving carpets. First-timers get 4 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The highest named summit within 8 km is Pointes de Rougnoux at 3,179 m, standing 448 m above the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 2,278 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to late May, about 26 weeks of good cover. Most winters land between 91 cm and 175 cm by mid-February. The snow is usually deepest around 6 March, with about 152 cm. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 132 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. For about 16 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 84 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 123 cm, a dry one 40 cm.
