    Bydalsfjällen in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Bydalsfjällen (about 792 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 44 cm; half of the winters sit between 35 cm and 55 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 71 cm (59 cm–85 cm).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 76 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 28 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 48 %.

    Best winter on record: 1987-88, peaking at 135 cm. Worst: 1995-96, which never passed 50 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until early May in a normal winter, roughly 166 days.

    Common questions

    Is there snow in Bydalsfjällen at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 44 cm, and only 48 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 33 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Bydalsfjällen?

    March: the snow peaks at about 76 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early May.

    How much snow does Bydalsfjällen get?

    About 76 cm at the peak of a normal winter (late March); between 50 cm in the leanest winter (1995-96) and 135 cm in the biggest (1987-88).

    What altitude is Bydalsfjällen at?

    Between 583 m and 1,002 m, so 419 m of vertical drop.

    How big is Bydalsfjällen?

    About 48 km of pistes and 13 lifts.

    About Bydalsfjällen

    About 48 km of pistes make Bydalsfjällen one of the ten biggest ski areas of Scandinavia (number 5). Its lifts climb from 583 m to 1,002 m. The closest other ski area is Gräftåvallen (13 km, south-east).
