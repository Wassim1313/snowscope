    How Dynamite Hill is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Dynamite Hill gives 22 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lowest slopes are at only 250 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Gore Mountain, for one, climbs to 1,092 m. The only lift is a rope tow. The skyline is dominated by Round Top Mountain, 502 m high and 7.6 km north-west of the resort.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 261 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 31 January and lasts to 13 March: about 6 weeks. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 14 cm and 68 cm. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 16 cm, with 37 cm in the best years. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March. Depth normally peaks in mid-February at roughly 37 cm.

    Quick answers

    When does Dynamite Hill have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from late January to mid-March.

    How much snow does Dynamite Hill have at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in nearly every winter, about 16 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Dynamite Hill?

    Its highest lift reaches 272 m; the lowest starts at 250 m (22 m of vertical).

    How big is Dynamite Hill?

    About 0.2 km of pistes and 1 lift.
