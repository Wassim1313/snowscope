    About Arizona Snowbowl

    Neither huge nor tiny, Arizona Snowbowl offers about 22 km of pistes (number 94 of 175 in US West). Skiing here happens between 2,791 m and 3,510 m above sea level.

    The pistes of Arizona Snowbowl

    Counting every graded piste, there are 53 runs and around 22 km to ski. Blue square runs make up the biggest part: 11 km, or 52%. Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. The steepest pistes add up to about 6.0 km.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 719 m: enough height for proper long runs. A high base at 2,791 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. Getting up the mountain: 1 chair-gondola, 5 chairlifts and 2 moving carpets, 8 lifts in all. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Humphreys Peak at 3,851 m, standing 341 m above the top lift.

    How much snow Arizona Snowbowl usually gets

    At mid-mountain (about 3,150 m), a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 19 December and lasts to 19 March: about 13 weeks. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 32 cm (snow lay here in nearly every winter), with 175 cm in the best years. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. Expect big differences between winters; the usual mid-February range runs from 0 cm to 300 cm. Depth normally peaks in mid-January at roughly 73 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually early January and mid-January.

    Quick answers

    When is the best time to ski at Arizona Snowbowl?

    Aim for early January and mid-January; the cover is usually good from mid-December to mid-March.

    How much snow does Arizona Snowbowl have at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 32 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in nearly every winter.

    What altitude is Arizona Snowbowl at?

    Between 2,791 m and 3,510 m, so 719 m of vertical drop.

    Does Arizona Snowbowl have steep runs?

    Yes: about 6.1 km of its graded pistes (27%) are difficult.

    How big is Arizona Snowbowl?

    About 22 km of pistes and 8 lifts.
