    Mount Pisgah in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Mount Pisgah (about 1,909 ft (582 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 12 in (31 cm); half of the winters sit between 6.3 in (16 cm) and 15 in (39 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 23 in (59 cm) (18 in (46 cm)–30 in (76 cm)).

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 24 in (61 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    4 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 15 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (47 in (120 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2015-16 (11 in (29 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-December to mid-April (112 days).

    What is the best time to ski Mount Pisgah?

    The deepest snow comes in February (about 24 in (61 cm)). For a sure base, mid-December to mid-April is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    About 24 in (61 cm) at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 11 in (29 cm) in the leanest winter (2015-16) and 47 in (120 cm) in the biggest (2000-01).
