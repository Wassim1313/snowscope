    Les 7 Laux in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Les 7 Laux (about 1,852 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 58 cm; half of the winters sit between 33 cm and 74 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 111 cm; the middle half of winters range from 88 cm to 128 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 116 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 35 % of winters, and by 1 January in 67 %.

    1994-95 was the big one (201 cm); 2022-23 the lean one (72 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from early December until late May in a normal winter, roughly 173 days.

    Les 7 Laux: quick answers

    Is there snow in Les 7 Laux at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 58 cm at Christmas, and 67 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 67 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Les 7 Laux?

    March: the snow peaks at about 116 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to late May.

    How deep does the snow get at Les 7 Laux?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 116 cm in late March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 201 cm (1994-95), the thinnest 72 cm (2022-23).

    What altitude is Les 7 Laux at?

    Between 1,333 m and 2,372 m, so 1,039 m of vertical drop.

    How many km of pistes does Les 7 Laux have?

    About 55 km of pistes and 23 lifts.

    About Les 7 Laux

    Les 7 Laux is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 55 km of pistes put it at number 108 of 1,056. The slopes start at 1,333 m and reach 2,372 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Stade des Neiges du Barioz, 8.2 km north, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    This week's snow

    Today there are about 67 cm of snow on the upper slopes of Les 7 Laux. 30 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. The coming days should add about 90 cm by Monday.

    How Les 7 Laux is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Les 7 Laux gives 1,039 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. Starting at 1,333 m, the resort is high enough for skiing to the bottom most of the winter. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point.
