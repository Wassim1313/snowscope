    Mountain Green in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Mountain Green (5,584 ft (1,702 m)), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 11 in (29 cm); half of the winters sit between 5.9 in (15 cm) and 18 in (45 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 24 in (62 cm), between 15 in (38 cm) and 33 in (83 cm) in a normal winter.

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 25 in (63 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 13 % of winters, and by 1 January in 30 %.

    The deepest winter was 1983-84 (61 in (156 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2017-18 (6.7 in (17 cm)).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs late December to late March (101 days).

    Questions about Mountain Green

    Does Mountain Green have snow over Christmas?

    Not every year: about 11 in (29 cm) at Christmas on average, with a 20 in (50 cm) base by New Year in 30 % of 46 winters. Right now: 4.7 in (12 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Mountain Green?

    February: the snow peaks at about 25 in (63 cm) then. A base of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from late December to late March.

    How deep does the snow get at Mountain Green?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 25 in (63 cm) in mid-February. The deepest of 46 winters reached 61 in (156 cm) (1983-84), the thinnest 6.7 in (17 cm) (2017-18).

    Between 1,628 m and 1,777 m, so 149 m of vertical drop.

    How Mountain Green is laid out

    The vertical drop is 149 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The bottom station at 1,628 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Wasatch Peaks Ranch, for one, climbs to 2,843 m.
