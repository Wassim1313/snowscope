    Les Orres in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Les Orres (about 2,127 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 66 cm; half of the winters sit between 50 cm and 99 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 128 cm (99 cm–155 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 139 cm.

    By 15 December 52 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 83 %.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (258 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2004-05 (84 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from late November until mid-May in a normal winter, roughly 178 days.

    Today's 42 cm compares with a usual 20 cm for 22 November.

    Questions about Les Orres

    Is there snow in Les Orres at Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 66 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 83 % of 46 winters. Right now: 44 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Les Orres?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 139 cm). For a sure base, late November to mid-May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How much snow does Les Orres get?

    About Les Orres

    With some 54 km of runs, Les Orres belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,554 m and reach 2,700 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Crévoux (7.6 km, north-east).

    What there is to ski at Les Orres

    Counting every graded piste, there are 83 runs and around 54 km to ski. The largest share is red, with 25 km (47%). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Advanced skiers have about 5.1 km of black or expert runs.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 2,127 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 26 November and keeps it until about 15 May, roughly 25 weeks. The snow is usually deepest around 8 March, with about 139 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 76 cm to 173 cm in mid-February in most winters. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 66 cm, from 28 cm in a poor year to 133 cm in a big one. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. For about 12 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. By mid-April the typical depth is 99 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Les Orres gives 1,146 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,554 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the second highest lift-served point. Getting up the mountain: 7 chairlifts, 7 button lifts, 1 drag lift and 6 moving carpets, 21 lifts in all. 6 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Le Grand Parpaillon at 2,990 m, standing 290 m above the top lift.

    Other resorts close by
