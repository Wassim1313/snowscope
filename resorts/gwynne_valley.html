    About Gwynne Valley

    Compact and local, Gwynne Valley has about 1.0 km of runs, small for Canada West. The slopes start at 703 m and reach 742 m at the top.

    Snow at Gwynne Valley through a normal winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 722 m) rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. The snow is usually deepest around 31 January, with about 22 cm. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 13 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 33 cm in the best years. Winters vary a lot: in mid-February the depth has ranged from 7 cm to 40 cm in most years, so look at the snow before booking. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early January and mid-January.

    How Gwynne Valley is laid out

    The vertical drop is 39 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The base at 703 m is fairly low, so expect the best snow on the upper half of the mountain. The only lift is a T-bar.

    Snow this week

    There is no snow on the ground at Gwynne Valley yet. So far the season is running close to normal, which is about 9 cm at mid-mountain.

    Questions about Gwynne Valley

    When does Gwynne Valley have the most snow?

    Aim for early January and mid-January.

    How much snow does Gwynne Valley have at Christmas?

    Sometimes. Christmas snow at mid-mountain came in every winter, usually about 13 cm, so check before you go.

    How high do the lifts go at Gwynne Valley?

    Its highest lift reaches 742 m; the lowest starts at 703 m (39 m of vertical).

    How big is Gwynne Valley?

    About 1.0 km of pistes and 1 lift.
