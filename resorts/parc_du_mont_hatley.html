    About Parc du Mont Hatley

    Parc du Mont Hatley is a small ski area by the standards of US Northeast, with about 1.0 km of pistes. Skiing here happens between 238 m and 347 m above sea level. There are 4 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Mont-Bellevue (Sherbrooke), 9.2 km to the north.

    Parc du Mont Hatley and the resorts around it

    For more terrain, Mont Sutton (54 km south-west) has about 38 km of pistes against 1.0 km here. Ski de montagne has the longer snow season: around 16 weeks of solid cover, compared with 11 at Parc du Mont Hatley. The highest nearby is Mont Sutton, topping out at 852 m.

    Runs by difficulty

    Parc du Mont Hatley has 3 graded runs adding up to about 1.2 km. Most of the length is black diamond (53%, about 0.6 km). With 53% of the pistes graded difficult, it suits strong skiers looking for steep runs.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 109 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lowest slopes are at only 238 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Of the 4 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Mont Boisjoli (355 m) and Colline Haskell (300 m).

    How much snow Parc du Mont Hatley usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 292 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from early January to late March, about 11 weeks of good cover. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 25 cm and 72 cm. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 23 cm (snow lay here in every winter); a snowy December gave 44 cm, a dry one 7 cm. A normal season tops out near 51 cm, around 16 February. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and late February.

    Quick answers

    When does Parc du Mont Hatley have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and late February; the cover is usually good from early January to late March.

    Can you ski at Parc du Mont Hatley at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 23 cm deep.

    How high do the lifts go at Parc du Mont Hatley?

    Between 238 m and 347 m, so 109 m of vertical drop.

    Is Parc du Mont Hatley good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 0.6 km of its graded pistes (53%) are difficult.

    Is there a larger resort near Parc du Mont Hatley?

    Mont Sutton, 54 km to the south-west, with about 38 km of pistes.
