    Lifts and terrain

    From top to bottom that makes 126 m of vertical, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. With the bottom at 857 m, the lower runs can turn soft or bare in mild weather while the top stays wintry. Its summit is modest for the area; the highest lifts nearby, at Lermoos Grubigstein, reach 2,055 m. To get up there are 4 funiculars, 1 button lift and 1 drag lift. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. The cabin lifts are sheltered on cold or windy days and also take walkers and sightseers up. The highest named summit within 8 km is Thaneller at 2,341 m, standing 1,358 m above the top lift.

    When the snow is good

    Even in a typical winter the snow at about 920 m, the middle of the ski area rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Most winters land between 8 cm and 38 cm by mid-February. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 13 cm, from 1 cm in a poor year to 27 cm in a big one. The snow is usually deepest around 14 February, with about 20 cm. Spring comes early here: by mid-April the middle of the ski area is usually free of snow. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-January and late January.
