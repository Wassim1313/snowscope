    About Sandia Peak

    With roughly 25 km of runs, Sandia Peak sits in the middle of the size table of US West. Skiing here happens between 2,646 m and 3,133 m above sea level.

    Pistes and who they suit

    Sandia Peak has 66 graded runs adding up to about 25 km. Most of the length is blue square (51%, about 13 km). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. Advanced skiers have about 2.6 km of black or expert runs.

    The mountain at Sandia Peak

    Skied top to bottom, Sandia Peak gives 487 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Even the lowest lift starts at 2,646 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. To get up there are 2 cable cars, 3 chairlifts and 1 drag lift. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. The highest named summit within 8 km is Sandia Crest at 3,255 m, standing 122 m above the top lift.

    How much snow Sandia Peak usually gets

    Even in a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 2,890 m rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in 7 winters out of ten; the usual depth was 9 cm, from 0 cm in a poor year to 49 cm in a big one. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 0 cm to 42 cm. A normal season tops out near 17 cm, around 6 January. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early January and mid-January. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April.

    Sandia Peak: common questions

    When is the best time to ski at Sandia Peak?

    Aim for early January and mid-January.

    Is there snow at Sandia Peak at Christmas?

    It is a gamble: only 7 winters out of ten had snow at mid-mountain on Christmas Day (typically 9 cm).

    How high do the lifts go at Sandia Peak?

    Between 2,646 m and 3,133 m, so 487 m of vertical drop.

    Who is Sandia Peak best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 51% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    How big is Sandia Peak?

    About 25 km of pistes and 6 lifts.
