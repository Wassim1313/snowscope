    Saint-Luc in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Saint-Luc (2,328 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 68 cm of cover (52 cm to 87 cm in the middle half of winters).

    On 15 February expect around 115 cm; the middle half of winters range from 96 cm to 132 cm.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 125 cm.

    65 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 87 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 2023-24, peaking at 187 cm. Worst: 2022-23, which never passed 98 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early June, about 196 days.

    This winter so far: 68 cm on 26 November, against a usual 35 cm for the date.

    Questions about Saint-Luc

    Can you ski Saint-Luc at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 68 cm at Christmas, and 87 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 43 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Saint-Luc?

    March: the snow peaks at about 125 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early June.

    How snowy is Saint-Luc?

    About 125 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 98 cm in the leanest winter (2022-23) and 187 cm in the biggest (2023-24).

    When does the ski season start at Saint-Luc?

    The announced opening day is 19 December 2026.

    How high is Saint-Luc?

    The top is at 2,973 m and the base at 1,682 m.

    Who is Saint-Luc best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 48% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    About Saint-Luc

    With some 56 km of runs, Saint-Luc belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,682 m up to 2,973 m. The closest other ski area is Vercorin (6.9 km, west).

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 95 graded runs, about 56 km in total. The largest share is red, with 26 km (48%). Intermediate runs make up 48% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. Experts can count on some 7.3 km of difficult runs. 1.4 km of marked freeride routes are not groomed and need off-piste skills.

    How Saint-Luc is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 1,291 m of vertical, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. With its base at 1,682 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. The lift system counts 18 lifts: 2 funiculars, 3 chairlifts, 2 T-bars, 8 button lifts and 3 moving carpets. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. 3 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Pointe de Tourtemagne at 3,080 m, standing 107 m above the top lift.

    Snow at Saint-Luc through a normal winter

    Count on about 27 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 2,328 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from late November to late May or later. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 68 cm; a snowy December gave 100 cm, a dry one 40 cm. The snow is usually deepest around 4 March, with about 125 cm. Most winters land between 76 cm and 144 cm by mid-February. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 15 weeks. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and late March. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 119 cm, plenty for sunny spring days.
