    What 46 winters say about Valberg

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Valberg (about 1,713 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 40 cm; half of the winters sit between 15 cm and 65 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 82 cm; the middle half of winters range from 40 cm to 113 cm.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 89 cm.

    By 15 December 30 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 41 %.

    2012-13 was the big one (236 cm); 2006-07 the lean one (25 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from early December until late April in a normal winter, roughly 143 days.

    Today's 0 cm compares with a usual 6 cm for 19 November.

    About Valberg

    Around 54 km of marked runs place Valberg among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 109 by length. The slopes start at 1,414 m and reach 2,012 m at the top. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being Roubion-Les-Buisses, 6.5 km to the east.

    Snow at Valberg through a normal winter

    Count on about 19 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at about 1,713 m, the middle of the ski area in an ordinary winter, from mid-December to mid-April. The snow is usually deepest around 23 February, with about 89 cm. Year to year the snow is steady, with 20 cm to 144 cm in mid-February in most winters. The best weeks for snow are usually early February and mid-February. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 31 cm; by then it pays to ski early, before the sun softens it. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 40 cm, from 8 cm in a poor year to 101 cm in a big one.

    How Valberg is laid out

    598 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, runs of a moderate length. The bottom station at 1,414 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. To get up there are 7 chairlifts, 13 button lifts, 1 rope tow and 4 moving carpets. For small children there are 4 conveyor-belt lifts. Above the ski area rises Le Petit Mounier (2,728 m), 7.7 km to the north, about 716 m higher than the top lift.
