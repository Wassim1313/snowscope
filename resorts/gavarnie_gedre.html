    What 46 winters say about Gavarnie-Gèdre

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Gavarnie-Gèdre (2,050 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 33 cm; half of the winters sit between 14 cm and 51 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 73 cm (44 cm–100 cm).

    February is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 78 cm.

    15 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 41 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 2013-14 (181 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2011-12 (33 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from mid-December to mid-April, about 126 days.

    So far this winter: 26 cm on 27 November (usual: 8 cm).

    About Gavarnie-Gèdre

    Neither huge nor tiny, Gavarnie-Gèdre offers about 21 km of pistes (number 30 of 49 in the Pyrenees). Skiing here happens between 1,818 m and 2,283 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Cauterets Pont d'Espagne (17 km, north-west).

    How much snow Gavarnie-Gèdre usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 2,050 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from late December to mid-April, about 16 weeks of good cover. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 27 cm and 122 cm. A normal season tops out near 78 cm, around 7 March. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 25 cm; by then it pays to ski early, before the sun softens it. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and late February. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 33 cm (snow lay here in nearly every winter); a snowy December gave 70 cm, a dry one 3 cm.

    How Gavarnie-Gèdre is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Gavarnie-Gèdre gives 465 m of descent: runs of a moderate length. Even the lowest lift starts at 1,818 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Its 8 lifts break down into 2 chairlifts, 5 button lifts and 1 moving carpet. Drag lifts dominate, so snowboarders new to the sport should expect some practice on the way up. 1 moving carpet serves the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Mallo de Marmorés / Cilindro de Marboré (3,325 m), 6.7 km to the south-east, about 1,042 m higher than the top lift.
