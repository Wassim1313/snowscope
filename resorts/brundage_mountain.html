    The snow at Brundage Mountain, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Brundage Mountain (6,742 ft (2,055 m)), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 37 in (93 cm), with a normal range of 30 in (75 cm)–44 in (112 cm).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 59 in (150 cm) (50 in (127 cm)–68 in (172 cm)).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 69 in (176 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    87 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 96 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1998-99, peaking at 112 in (284 cm). Worst: 2000-01, which never passed 46 in (118 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 12 in (30 cm) typically runs mid-November to early June (202 days).

    Today's 10 in (26 cm) compares with a usual 17 in (44 cm) for 26 November.

    About Brundage Mountain

    The snow season, winter by winter

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 2,055 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 20 November and keeps it until about the end of May, roughly 28 weeks. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 109 cm and 200 cm. For about 19 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. A normal season tops out near 176 cm, around 29 March. For the best snow, aim for early March and late March. By mid-April the typical depth is 154 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 93 cm; a snowy December gave 130 cm, a dry one 60 cm.
