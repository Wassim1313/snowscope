    About Kanc

    About 1.0 km of pistes make Kanc one of the smaller places to ski in US Northeast. The slopes start at 265 m and reach 339 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Loon (2.2 km, east).

    Other resorts close by

    For more terrain, Loon (2.2 km east) has about 46 km of pistes against 1.0 km here. If snow is the priority, Cannon Mountain does better, with about 17 weeks of a good base instead of 8. The highest nearby is Cannon Mountain, topping out at 1,231 m.

    Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, Kanc gives 74 m of descent: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. At 265 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Its summit is modest for the area; the highest lifts nearby, at Cannon Mountain, reach 1,231 m. The only lift is a rope tow. The skyline is dominated by Mount Liberty, 1,353 m high and 7.1 km north of the resort.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 8 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 302 m in an ordinary winter, from late January to late March. Most winters land between 17 cm and 66 cm by mid-February. The snow is usually deepest around 17 February, with about 46 cm. In a normal winter mid-mountain is bare again by mid-April. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in nearly every winter; the usual depth was 20 cm; a snowy December gave 37 cm, a dry one 4 cm. For the best snow, aim for mid-February and early March.

    Questions about Kanc

    When does Kanc have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and early March; the cover is usually good from late January to late March.

    How much snow does Kanc have at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 20 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in nearly every winter.

    How high is Kanc?

    The top is at 339 m and the base at 265 m.

    Which bigger ski area is near Kanc?

    Loon, 2.2 km to the east, with about 46 km of pistes.

    How big is Kanc?

    About 1.0 km of pistes and 1 lift.
