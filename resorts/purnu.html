    About Purnu

    About 2.0 km of pistes make Purnu one of the smaller places to ski in Scandinavia. The slopes start at 115 m and reach 206 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Luoteis-Himos (34 km, north-west).

    What there is to ski at Purnu

    Purnu has 8 graded runs adding up to about 2.2 km. Most of the length is blue (42%, about 0.9 km). Easy slopes dominate, which makes it a good choice for a first ski holiday or for young children. There is little here for expert skiers looking for steep slopes.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 91 m: short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. Among its 3 neighbours within 50 km, it has the third highest lift-served point. Its 2 lifts break down into 1 T-bar and 1 rope tow.

    How much snow Purnu usually gets

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 160 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 9 January and keeps it until about 11 April, roughly 13 weeks. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 46 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early February and early March. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 22 cm, with 35 cm in the best years. Year to year the snow is steady, with 27 cm to 60 cm in mid-February in most winters. By mid-April the typical depth is 24 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy.

    Purnu: common questions

    When does Purnu have the most snow?

    Aim for early February and early March; the cover is usually good from early January to mid-April.

    Is there snow at Purnu at Christmas?

    In general yes, with around 22 cm at mid-mountain on Christmas Day in a typical year.

    What altitude is Purnu at?

    The top is at 206 m and the base at 115 m.

    Is Purnu good for beginners?

    Yes: 59% of the graded pistes, about 1.3 km, are easy.

    Which bigger ski area is near Purnu?

    Luoteis-Himos, 34 km to the north-west, with about 8.0 km of pistes.
