    Cannon Mountain in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Cannon Mountain (2,959 ft (902 m)), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 13 in (33 cm) of cover (7.5 in (19 cm) to 17 in (43 cm) in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 28 in (70 cm) (22 in (56 cm)–33 in (84 cm)).

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 31 in (78 cm) at the peak of a typical winter.

    9 % of the winters had at least 20 in (50 cm) by 15 December, and 24 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 2000-01 (57 in (144 cm) at its peak); the thinnest was 2005-06 (16 in (40 cm)).

    A cover of 12 in (30 cm) or more usually lasts from mid-December to mid-April, about 120 days.

    So far this winter: 0.4 in (1 cm) on 22 November (usual: 1.6 in (4 cm)).

    Cannon Mountain: quick answers

    Does Cannon Mountain have snow over Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 13 in (33 cm), and only 24 % of the past 46 winters had 20 in (50 cm) by 1 January. Today there is 6.3 in (16 cm).

    When is the best month to ski Cannon Mountain?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 31 in (78 cm)). For a sure base, mid-December to mid-April is the usual window with 12 in (30 cm) or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Cannon Mountain?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 31 in (78 cm) in mid-March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 57 in (144 cm) (2000-01), the thinnest 16 in (40 cm) (2005-06).

    When does Cannon Mountain have the most snow?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from late December to mid-April.

    How high is Cannon Mountain?

    Between 574 m and 1,231 m, so 657 m of vertical drop.

    Does Cannon Mountain have steep runs?

    Yes: about 13 km of its graded pistes (34%) are difficult.

    About Cannon Mountain

    With some 39 km of runs, Cannon Mountain belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of US Northeast. The slopes start at 574 m and reach 1,231 m at the top. The closest other ski area is Kanc (14 km, south).

    What there is to ski at Cannon Mountain

    Counting every graded piste, there are 100 runs and around 39 km to ski. Blue square runs make up the biggest part: 20 km, or 51%. Intermediate runs make up 51% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. The steepest pistes add up to about 13 km.

    About 2.3 km of pistes (6%) have snowmaking, mostly the busiest runs and the way back to the bottom.

    Run colours follow the North American system: green circles are easiest, blue squares intermediate, black diamonds difficult.

    How Cannon Mountain is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 657 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. At 574 m the base is low, and the bottom runs depend on cold weather or machine-made snow. Among its 7 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. Its 9 lifts break down into 7 chairlifts, 1 T-bar and 1 rope tow. Chairs and cabins make up most of the network: a comfortable ride for every level. The highest named summit within 8 km is Mount Lafayette at 1,600 m, standing 369 m above the top lift.

    How much snow Cannon Mountain usually gets

    In a typical winter the snow at mid-mountain (about 902 m) reaches a solid 30 cm base around 21 December and keeps it until about 16 April, roughly 17 weeks. Most winters land between 36 cm and 108 cm by mid-February. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 35 cm, enough on top while the bottom gets patchy. For the best snow, aim for late February and early March. The snow is usually deepest around 14 March, with about 78 cm. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 33 cm (lean years 9 cm, snowy years 56 cm).
