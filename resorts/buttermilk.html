    About Buttermilk

    With roughly 40 km of runs, Buttermilk sits in the middle of the size table of US West. Its lifts climb from 2,402 m to 3,012 m. Aspen Highlands is the next resort, 3.3 km away to the south.

    The pistes of Buttermilk

    Buttermilk has 50 graded runs adding up to about 40 km. Most of the length is blue square (37%, about 15 km). The mix is balanced: easy slopes to learn on and enough steeper runs to keep good skiers busy. The steepest pistes add up to about 11 km.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    The vertical drop is 610 m: runs of a moderate length. Even the lowest lift starts at 2,402 m, high enough for the bottom of the resort to keep cold snow longer than most. Among the 7 ski areas within 50 km it is one of the lowest; Aspen Snowmass reaches 3,815 m. The lift system counts 8 lifts: 4 chairlifts, 1 drag lift, 1 rope tow and 2 moving carpets. For small children there are 2 conveyor-belt lifts. The highest named summit within 8 km is Highland Peak at 3,757 m, standing 745 m above the top lift.

    How much snow Buttermilk usually gets

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 2,707 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to early May, about 24 weeks of good cover. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and late March. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 74 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 78 cm and 147 cm. A metre-deep snowpack is normal for around 8 weeks. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 66 cm (lean years 30 cm, snowy years 102 cm). The snow is usually deepest around 7 March, with about 118 cm.

    Questions about Buttermilk

    What is the best month to ski at Buttermilk?

    Aim for early March and late March; the cover is usually good from late November to early May.

    Is there snow at Buttermilk at Christmas?

    Most years, yes. Snow lay at mid-mountain at Christmas in every winter, about 66 cm deep.

    How high is Buttermilk?

    The top is at 3,012 m and the base at 2,402 m.

    Is Buttermilk good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 11 km of its graded pistes (29%) are difficult.

    Which bigger ski area is near Buttermilk?

    Aspen Snowmass, 6.9 km to the west, with about 120 km of pistes.
