    What 46 winters say about Bonneval-sur-Arc

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Bonneval-sur-Arc (2,371 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 80 cm of cover (64 cm to 104 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 130 cm (103 cm–152 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 146 cm.

    70 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 87 % by 1 January.

    2003-04 was the big one (240 cm); 2021-22 the lean one (102 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 189 days.

    This winter so far: 67 cm on 25 November, against a usual 37 cm for the date.

    Questions about Bonneval-sur-Arc

    Does Bonneval-sur-Arc have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 80 cm at Christmas, and 87 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 87 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Bonneval-sur-Arc?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 146 cm). For a sure base, mid-November to late May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How deep does the snow get at Bonneval-sur-Arc?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 146 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 240 cm (2003-04), the thinnest 102 cm (2021-22).

    What altitude is Bonneval-sur-Arc at?

    Between 1,794 m and 2,948 m, so 1,154 m of vertical drop.

    Is Bonneval-sur-Arc good for beginners?

    Yes: 66% of the graded pistes, about 13 km, are easy.

    About Bonneval-sur-Arc

    Bonneval-sur-Arc is a mid-sized ski area with about 20 km of pistes, number 307 of 1,056 in the Alps by length. Its lifts climb from 1,794 m to 2,948 m. Bessans is the next resort, 6.2 km away to the south-west.

    Snow at Bonneval-sur-Arc through a normal winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 2,371 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 17 November and lasts to 24 May: about 27 weeks. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 80 cm (lean years 37 cm, snowy years 130 cm). Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March. Year to year the snow is steady, with 82 cm to 172 cm in mid-February in most winters. For about 13 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. A normal season tops out near 146 cm, around 8 March. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 92 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring.

    This week's snow

    Up high, Bonneval-sur-Arc has roughly 87 cm of snow on the ground today. 14 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. The next days look mild and dry; the cover is expected to settle to about 81 cm by Saturday. That is close to a normal winter for the date (about 44 cm at mid-mountain).

    The mountain at Bonneval-sur-Arc

    1,154 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. With its base at 1,794 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Its top at 2,948 m is the third highest of the 9 ski areas within 50 km. To get up there are 3 chairlifts, 1 button lift, 5 drag lifts and 1 moving carpet. With so many tows and T-bars, the lifts are simple and quick, but tiring for first-time snowboarders. For small children there is 1 conveyor-belt lift. The highest named summit within 8 km is Uia di Ciamarella at 3,676 m, standing 728 m above the top lift.
