    The snow at Val Thorens, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Val Thorens (about 2,498 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 110 cm, with a normal range of 82 cm–147 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 190 cm, between 151 cm and 219 cm in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 213 cm.

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 80 % of winters, and by 1 January in 98 %.

    The deepest winter was 1994-95 (316 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2004-05 (164 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early November to early June, about 210 days.

    Skiing Val Thorens: the practical side

    The pistes run from 1,822 m to 3,175 m (1,353 m of vertical), with 115 km of marked runs and 27 lifts.

    About Val Thorens

    With some 115 km of runs, Val Thorens belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The slopes start at 1,822 m and reach 3,175 m at the top. Val Thorens is the next resort, 0.7 km away to the south.

    Val Thorens today and the next days

    Up high, Val Thorens has roughly 119 cm of snow on the ground today. 48 cm of fresh snow fell in the last 24 hours. More is on the way: about 104 cm of new snow is expected by Sunday.

    How Val Thorens is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Val Thorens gives 1,353 m of descent: a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. A high base at 1,822 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter.
