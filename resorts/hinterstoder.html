    What 46 winters say about Hinterstoder

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Hinterstoder (1,288 m), from 46 past winters.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 24 cm, with a normal range of 14 cm–33 cm.

    By mid-February the usual depth is 41 cm (31 cm–56 cm).

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 44 cm.

    By 15 December 2 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 4 %.

    Best winter on record: 1981-82, peaking at 116 cm. Worst: 2025-26, which never passed 24 cm.

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late December to late April, about 121 days.

    This winter so far: 15 cm on 27 November, against a usual 10 cm for the date.

    About Hinterstoder

    With some 35 km of runs, Hinterstoder belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 589 m up to 1,987 m. The closest other ski area is Wurzeralm (11 km, south-east).

    Runs by difficulty

    Hinterstoder has 72 graded runs adding up to about 35 km. Red runs make up the biggest part: 16 km, or 45%. Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. The steepest pistes add up to about 4.3 km.

    How Hinterstoder is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 1,398 m of vertical, a long top-to-bottom run that tests the legs. The lowest slopes are at only 589 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. Among its 8 neighbours within 50 km, it has the highest lift-served point. Getting up the mountain: 2 gondolas, 2 chairlifts, 7 T-bars, 1 rope tow and 7 moving carpets, 19 lifts in all. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 7 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Warscheneck, 2,388 m high and 6.1 km south-east of the resort.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Halfway up the slopes, around 1,288 m, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 11 January and lasts to 20 April: about 14 weeks. Most winters land between 23 cm and 62 cm by mid-February. By mid-April the typical depth is 31 cm, and the lower runs are often thin by then. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 24 cm, from 5 cm in a poor year to 47 cm in a big one. The snow is usually deepest around 22 February, with about 44 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually early February and mid-February.
