    The snow at Killington Resort, winter after winter

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Killington Resort (about 2,651 ft (808 m)), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 9.1 in (23 cm), with a normal range of 4.7 in (12 cm)–13 in (34 cm).

    Mid-February typically sees 20 in (50 cm), between 13 in (33 cm) and 25 in (63 cm) in a normal winter.

    The snowiest month is February, when the pack peaks at about 21 in (53 cm).

    A 50 cm base was there by 15 December in 2 % of winters, and by 1 January in 9 %.

    2000-01 was the big one (50 in (126 cm)); 2015-16 the lean one (7.5 in (19 cm)).

    Expect 12 in (30 cm) or more from early January until late March in a normal winter, roughly 98 days.

    This winter so far: 0 in (0 cm) on 21 November, against a usual 0.8 in (2 cm) for the date.

    About Killington Resort

    Killington Resort tops the size table of US Northeast with roughly 107 km of runs. The slopes start at 354 m and reach 1,262 m at the top. Its nearest neighbour is Pico, 6.0 km north-west, and 8 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How Killington Resort is laid out

    Skied top to bottom, Killington Resort gives 908 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. Because the lifts start as low as 354 m, the valley runs are the first to suffer in a thaw. No other ski area within 50 km reaches as high as its 1,262 m. Its 22 lifts break down into 4 gondolas, 14 chairlifts, 1 button lift and 3 moving carpets. Most of the lifts are chairs or cabins, so you sit on the way up and beginners rarely struggle. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. First-timers get 3 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. Named peaks within a few kilometres include Killington Peak (1,293 m), Pico Peak (1,206 m) and Little Killington (1,194 m).

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Count on about 11 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) halfway up the slopes, around 808 m in an ordinary winter, from mid-January to late March. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 24 cm and 84 cm. Depth normally peaks in early March at roughly 53 cm. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 23 cm; a snowy December gave 46 cm, a dry one 5 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in early March and mid-March.
