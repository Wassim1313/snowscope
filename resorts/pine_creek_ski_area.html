    About Pine Creek

    With roughly 18 km of runs, Pine Creek sits in the middle of the size table of US West. Skiing here happens between 2,092 m and 2,507 m above sea level.

    Runs by difficulty

    Pine Creek has 29 graded runs adding up to about 16 km. Blue square runs make up the biggest part: 6.9 km, or 43%. Mixed groups do well here, with gentle slopes for learners and steeper pistes for the experts. Advanced skiers have about 4.2 km of black or expert runs.

    Run colours follow the North American system: green circles are easiest, blue squares intermediate, black diamonds difficult.

    Heights, vertical and lifts

    From top to bottom that makes 415 m of vertical, runs of a moderate length. A high base at 2,092 m means the lowest runs rarely turn to slush in midwinter. Its 2 lifts break down into 1 chairlift and 1 moving carpet. 1 moving carpet serves the beginner and children's areas. The highest named summit within 8 km is Coke Mountain at 2,712 m, standing 205 m above the top lift.

    Snow at Pine Creek through a normal winter

    In a typical winter the snow halfway up the slopes, around 2,300 m reaches a solid 30 cm base around 10 December and keeps it until about 25 April, roughly 20 weeks. In mid-April a normal winter still leaves 54 cm: Easter holidays are usually still well covered. Depth normally peaks in late February at roughly 88 cm. For the holidays at the end of December, expect around 41 cm (snow lay here in every winter), with 81 cm in the best years. Snow conditions have usually peaked in late February and early March. The snow is fairly dependable: in mid-February most winters had between 51 cm and 117 cm.

    Pine Creek: common questions

    What is the best month to ski at Pine Creek?

    Aim for late February and early March; the cover is usually good from early December to late April.

    Is there snow at Pine Creek at Christmas?

    Usually: past winters had about 41 cm at mid-mountain on 25 December, with snow in every winter.

    How high do the lifts go at Pine Creek?

    The top is at 2,507 m and the base at 2,092 m.

    Is Pine Creek good for advanced skiers?

    Yes: about 4.2 km of its graded pistes (27%) are difficult.

    How many km of pistes does Pine Creek have?

    About 18 km of pistes and 2 lifts.
