    What 46 winters say about Lake Louise

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Lake Louise (about 2,145 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 78 cm, with a normal range of 61 cm–94 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 99 cm, between 82 cm and 114 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 108 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 83 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 93 %.

    2025-26 was the big one (164 cm); 1992-93 the lean one (85 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early November to early June, about 212 days.

    Skiing Lake Louise: the practical side

    Lifts: 13. Pistes: 81 km. Heights: 1,647 m at the bottom, 2,643 m at the top (996 m of vertical).

    Common questions

    Can you ski Lake Louise at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 78 cm at Christmas, and 93 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 37 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Lake Louise?

    March: the snow peaks at about 108 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early November to early June.

    How much snow does Lake Louise get?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 108 cm in early March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 164 cm (2025-26), the thinnest 85 cm (1992-93).

    What altitude is Lake Louise at?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,643 m; the lowest starts at 1,647 m (996 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Lake Louise have?

    About 81 km of pistes and 13 lifts.

    About Lake Louise

    About 81 km of pistes make Lake Louise one of the ten biggest ski areas of Canada West (number 7). Skiing here happens between 1,647 m and 2,643 m above sea level.

    The mountain at Lake Louise

    996 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,647 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months.
