    About 2.0 km of pistes make Abenaki one of the smaller places to ski in US Northeast. Its lifts climb from 184 m to 244 m. Its nearest neighbour is Gunstock, 14 km south-west, and 3 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    How Abenaki is laid out

    60 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, short runs, good for quick laps and for learning. The lowest slopes are at only 184 m, so snow down to the bottom needs a cold spell; the upper runs hold it far better. It tops out lower than almost all of its neighbours: Waterville Valley Resort, for one, climbs to 1,163 m. All 2 lifts here are rope tows. The highest named summit within 8 km is Moody Mountain at 399 m, standing 155 m above the top lift.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    Even in a typical winter the snow at about 214 m, the middle of the ski area rarely reaches a 30 cm base; the season relies on cold spells, the upper slopes and machine-made snow. Snow here is hit and miss from year to year: mid-February has seen anything from 7 cm to 50 cm. Snow conditions have usually peaked in mid-February and late February. At Christmas there was snow here in nearly every winter, typically 10 cm; a snowy December gave 23 cm, a dry one 2 cm. By mid-April the snow at mid-mountain has usually melted; only the top keeps any. A normal season tops out near 25 cm, around 17 February.

    Questions about Abenaki

    When does Abenaki have the most snow?

    Aim for mid-February and late February.

    How much snow does Abenaki have at Christmas?

    Not reliably. On 25 December there was snow at mid-mountain in nearly every winter, typically 10 cm; the higher slopes do better.

    What altitude is Abenaki at?

    Between 184 m and 244 m, so 60 m of vertical drop.

    Does Abenaki have steep runs?

    Yes: about 0.7 km of its graded pistes (47%) are difficult.

    Is there a larger resort near Abenaki?

    Gunstock, 14 km to the south-west, with about 26 km of pistes.
