    What 46 winters say about La Thuile ski

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at La Thuile ski (2,038 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 88 cm of cover (59 cm to 117 cm in the middle half of winters).

    Mid-February typically sees 169 cm, between 142 cm and 198 cm in a normal winter.

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 178 cm.

    70 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 96 % by 1 January.

    Best winter on record: 1994-95, peaking at 295 cm. Worst: 2010-11, which never passed 138 cm.

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 198 days.

    This winter so far: 79 cm on 21 November, against a usual 26 cm for the date.

    La Thuile ski: quick answers

    Does La Thuile ski have snow over Christmas?

    Most years, yes: around 88 cm on 25 December, and a 50 cm base by New Year in 96 % of 46 winters. Right now: 72 cm.

    When is the best month to ski La Thuile ski?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 178 cm). For a sure base, mid-November to late May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How deep does the snow get at La Thuile ski?

    About 178 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 138 cm in the leanest winter (2010-11) and 295 cm in the biggest (1994-95).

    When does the ski season start at La Thuile ski?

    The announced opening day is 28 November 2026; the last ski day is planned for 11 April 2027.

    What altitude is La Thuile ski at?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,608 m; the lowest starts at 1,468 m (1,140 m of vertical).

    Who is La Thuile ski best for?

    Mostly intermediates: 54% of the graded pistes are of medium difficulty.

    About La Thuile ski

    With some 79 km of runs, La Thuile ski belongs to the largest quarter of the ski areas of the Alps. The ski area spans the mountain from 1,468 m up to 2,608 m. There are 8 other ski areas within 40 km, the closest being La Rosière, 7.6 km to the south-west.

    What there is to ski at La Thuile ski

    In all, 131 marked runs cover roughly 78 km. The largest share is red, with 42 km (54%). Intermediate runs make up 54% of the length, the sweet spot for most holiday skiers. The steepest pistes add up to about 11 km.

    Past winters give a clear pattern halfway up the slopes, around 2,038 m: a 30 cm base is usually there from late November to late May, about 27 weeks of good cover. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 138 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. Most winters land between 106 cm and 220 cm by mid-February. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 88 cm, from 47 cm in a poor year to 140 cm in a big one. For about 16 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more.     Lifts and terrain

    Skied top to bottom, La Thuile ski gives 1,140 m of descent: enough height for proper long runs. With its base at 1,468 m, the lower slopes usually hold their snow from midwinter into March. Getting up the mountain: 1 gondola, 10 chairlifts, 2 button lifts and 4 moving carpets, 17 lifts in all. You sit down on most of the lifts here, which suits children and snowboarders. Enclosed cabins mean non-skiers can ride up too. 4 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. The skyline is dominated by Mont Berrio Blanc, 3,252 m high and 6.8 km north of the resort.
