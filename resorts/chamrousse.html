    Chamrousse in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Chamrousse (about 1,828 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 65 cm; half of the winters sit between 41 cm and 83 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 129 cm; the middle half of winters range from 101 cm to 145 cm.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 138 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 43 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 74 %.

    2017-18 was the big one (224 cm); 1992-93 the lean one (74 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early December to late May (174 days).

    So far this winter: 17 cm on 24 November (usual: 22 cm).

    Chamrousse: quick answers

    Is there snow in Chamrousse at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 65 cm at Christmas, and 74 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 44 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Chamrousse?

    March: the snow peaks at about 138 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to late May.

    How deep does the snow get at Chamrousse?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 138 cm in late March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 224 cm (2017-18), the thinnest 74 cm (1992-93).

    When does Chamrousse have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from early December to late May.

    When does the ski season start at Chamrousse?

    The announced opening day is 5 December 2026; the last ski day is planned for 11 April 2027.

    How high is Chamrousse?

    The top is at 2,246 m and the base at 1,411 m.

    About Chamrousse

    Chamrousse is one of the bigger ski areas of the Alps: about 51 km of pistes put it at number 118 of 1,056. The slopes start at 1,411 m and reach 2,246 m at the top. Chamrousse is the next resort, 0.6 km away to the north-west.

    Snow at Chamrousse through a normal winter

    Past winters give a clear pattern at mid-mountain (about 1,828 m): a 30 cm base is usually there from early December to late May, about 24 weeks of good cover. The snow is usually deepest around 23 March, with about 138 cm. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 65 cm; a snowy December gave 95 cm, a dry one 22 cm. By mid-April the typical depth is 93 cm, so spring skiing usually holds up well. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. For about 11 weeks of a typical winter there is a metre of snow or more. Most winters land between 74 cm and 161 cm by mid-February.

    How Chamrousse is laid out

    From top to bottom that makes 835 m of vertical, enough height for proper long runs. The bottom station at 1,411 m sits at a comfortable height for snow through the main winter months. Getting up the mountain: 1 gondola, 7 chairlifts, 8 button lifts, 2 drag lifts and 2 moving carpets, 20 lifts in all. Cabins keep the ride up dry and warm when the weather turns. 2 moving carpets serve the beginner and children's areas. Above the ski area rises Grande Lauzière (2,741 m), 7.4 km to the north-east, about 495 m higher than the top lift.
