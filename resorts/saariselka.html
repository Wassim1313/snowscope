    The snow at Saariselka, winter after winter

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Saariselka (350 m), from 46 past winters.

    Christmas week typically brings 39 cm of cover (32 cm to 48 cm in the middle half of winters).

    By mid-February the usual depth is 62 cm (52 cm–66 cm).

    March is the deepest month, with the cover peaking near 63 cm.

    15 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 28 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1997-98 (108 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1987-88 (56 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from early December until early May in a normal winter, roughly 160 days.

    So far this winter: 19 cm on 21 November (usual: 21 cm).

    Common questions

    Does Saariselka have snow over Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 39 cm, and only 28 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 22 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Saariselka?

    March: the snow peaks at about 63 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early December to early May.

    How snowy is Saariselka?

    About 63 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early March); between 56 cm in the leanest winter (1987-88) and 108 cm in the biggest (1997-98).

    When does Saariselka have the most snow?

    Aim for early March and mid-March; the cover is usually good from early December to mid-May.

    How high is Saariselka?

    Its highest lift reaches 433 m; the lowest starts at 267 m (166 m of vertical).

    How many km of pistes does Saariselka have?

    About 2.0 km of pistes and 3 lifts.

    About Saariselka

    Compact and local, Saariselka has about 2.0 km of runs, small for Scandinavia. Skiing here happens between 267 m and 433 m above sea level. Its nearest neighbour is Ski Saariselkä, 0.4 km north-west, and 3 ski areas lie within 40 km in all.

    The snow season, winter by winter

    At about 350 m, the middle of the ski area, a base of at least 30 cm usually builds by 4 December and lasts to 11 May: about 23 weeks. Year to year the snow is steady, with 48 cm to 73 cm in mid-February in most winters. Spring skiers should know that mid-April usually has 59 cm, plenty for sunny spring days. Christmas week brought snow on the ground in every winter; the usual depth was 39 cm; a snowy December gave 54 cm, a dry one 27 cm. The best weeks for snow are usually early March and mid-March. The snow is usually deepest around 7 March, with about 63 cm.
