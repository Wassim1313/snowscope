    About Aletsch Arena

    Around 90 km of marked runs place Aletsch Arena among the larger resorts of the Alps, number 56 by length. Skiing here happens between 1,057 m and 2,869 m above sea level. The closest other ski area is Belalp (8.0 km, west).

    Runs by difficulty

    The piste map lists 189 graded runs, about 86 km in total. The largest share is blue, with 42 km (49%). Its heart is intermediate terrain, ideal for skiers who are confident on most slopes and want variety. The steepest pistes add up to about 4.0 km.

    The mountain at Aletsch Arena

    1,812 m separate the highest and lowest lifts, one of the longest descents you can ski in one go. Of the 9 ski areas within 50 km, only 2 reach higher. The lift system counts 43 lifts: 5 cable cars, 3 funiculars, 4 gondolas, 1 chair-gondola, 8 chairlifts, 10 T-bars, 4 button lifts, 2 rope tows and 6 moving carpets. On stormy days the enclosed cabins are the warmest way up. First-timers get 6 moving carpets on the gentle nursery slopes. The skyline is dominated by Geisshorn, 3,740 m high and 7.0 km north-west of the resort.

    When the snow is good

    Count on about 24 weeks of solid cover (30 cm or more) at mid-mountain (about 1,963 m) in an ordinary winter, from early December to late May. Most winters land between 62 cm and 117 cm by mid-February. At Christmas there was snow here in every winter, typically 51 cm, from 29 cm in a poor year to 73 cm in a big one. For the best snow, aim for early March and mid-March. By mid-April the typical depth is 85 cm, which keeps the runs open into spring. The snow is usually deepest around 6 March, with about 98 cm.
