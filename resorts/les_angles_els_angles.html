    What 46 winters say about Les Angles

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Les Angles (1,996 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 14 cm; half of the winters sit between 7 cm and 30 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 28 cm, between 15 cm and 61 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in February: about 38 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    By 15 December 9 % of winters had reached 50 cm; by New Year it was 13 %.

    The deepest winter was 1980-81 (134 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1989-90 (17 cm).

    The skiable season with at least 30 cm typically runs early January to late April (114 days).

    Questions about Les Angles

    Is there snow in Les Angles at Christmas?

    It depends on the winter. The usual Christmas depth is 14 cm, and only 13 % of the past 46 winters had 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 16 cm.

    What is the best time to ski Les Angles?

    February: the snow peaks at about 38 cm then. A base of 30 cm or more usually lasts from early January to late April.

    How deep does the snow get at Les Angles?

    About 38 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early February); between 17 cm in the leanest winter (1989-90) and 134 cm in the biggest (1980-81).

    How high is Les Angles?

    Its highest lift reaches 2,358 m; the lowest starts at 1,635 m (723 m of vertical).

    How big is Les Angles?

    About 35 km of pistes and 21 lifts.
