    Red in past winters

    Here is how the snow usually builds up at Red (1,596 m), from 46 past winters.

    At Christmas the upper slopes usually hold 97 cm; half of the winters sit between 79 cm and 111 cm.

    On 15 February expect around 129 cm; the middle half of winters range from 112 cm to 145 cm.

    The snowiest month is March, when the pack peaks at about 155 cm.

    89 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 98 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1998-99 (257 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 2014-15 (109 cm).

    Expect 30 cm or more from mid-November until late May in a normal winter, roughly 188 days.

    Red: quick answers

    Does Red have snow over Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 97 cm at Christmas, and 98 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 48 cm.

    Which month has the most snow at Red?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 155 cm). For a sure base, mid-November to late May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How snowy is Red?

    About 155 cm at the peak of a normal winter (early April); between 109 cm in the leanest winter (2014-15) and 257 cm in the biggest (1998-99).

    How high do the lifts go at Red?

    The top is at 2,037 m and the base at 1,155 m.

    How many km of pistes does Red have?

    About 69 km of pistes and 8 lifts.
