    Hafjell in past winters

    46 winters of snow depth on the upper mountain of Hafjell (about 625 m), so you can plan with real odds rather than hope.

    On 25 December the usual depth is 56 cm, with a normal range of 43 cm–73 cm.

    Mid-February typically sees 89 cm, between 75 cm and 102 cm in a normal winter.

    The deepest snow comes in March: about 93 cm at the peak of a typical winter.

    54 % of the winters had at least 50 cm by 15 December, and 76 % by 1 January.

    The deepest winter was 1987-88 (159 cm at its peak); the thinnest was 1995-96 (73 cm).

    A cover of 30 cm or more usually lasts from late November to early May, about 173 days.

    Getting to Hafjell and what it costs

    Common questions

    Can you ski Hafjell at Christmas?

    Usually yes. The upper slopes hold about 56 cm at Christmas, and 76 % of the past 46 winters had at least 50 cm by 1 January. Today there is 18 cm.

    When is the best month to ski Hafjell?

    The deepest snow comes in March (about 93 cm). For a sure base, late November to early May is the usual window with 30 cm or more.

    How much snow does Hafjell get?

    In a typical winter the upper-mountain snow peaks at about 93 cm in mid-March. The deepest of 46 winters reached 159 cm (1987-88), the thinnest 73 cm (1995-96).

    How high is Hafjell?

    The top is at 1,058 m and the base at 192 m.

    How big is Hafjell?

    About 39 km of pistes and 17 lifts.
